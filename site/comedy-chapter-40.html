Characters: Catherine Cartwheel

Definition from research

Catherine’s logic is at its most perfectly absurd in the chapter’s closing argument. Asked to choose between a ship’s figurehead and the greatest captain of the greatest boat, as to which knows more of waves and storms, she chooses the figurehead, for the waters roar at its mouth. “For one thing, it always went first.”

It is a joke with a theory inside it. Catherine, who cannot walk and has not left her bed in years, believes the carved wooden woman at the prow understands the voyage better than the man steering it, because she meets everything first and decides nothing. That is her own position exactly, and Young lets her defend it with the confidence of a debating champion.
