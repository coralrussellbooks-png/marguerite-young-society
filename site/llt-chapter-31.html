Chapter 31

Other versions: Chapter hub · Research notes · The Wheel and the Spokes

Characters: Catherine Cartwheel · Cousin Hannah · Joachim Spitzer · Miss MacIntosh

Pillars: Grief Doesn't End · Unfinished Is Honest

The Questions

Cousin Hannah brings Catherine “a mirror from a tomb” to force her to see mortality. Is forcing a sealed person open any kinder than leaving them sealed?

Where Mr. Spitzer would not disturb the dreamer, Cousin Hannah tries to overthrow the dream by force. Is either of them right?

Cousin Hannah “always believed in being direct.” Is directness a virtue here, or her particular blueprint?

Mr. Spitzer cannot finish Cousin Hannah’s epitaph or recall her death date. What does an unfinishable inscription say about her?

Cousin Hannah does to Catherine exactly what Mr. Spitzer would not. Where he refused to disturb the dreamer, she arrives to “carry Catherine away from this dark tower, to rescue her from opium dreams of the dead love,” and she brings, as her instrument, “a mirror from a tomb,” a glass meant to force Catherine to see the transience of her own life. This is the anti-Witness. Cousin Hannah does not stay with the sealed person; she tries to break the seal by force, to hold the mirror up until the dream cracks. And the book, having shown that witnessing without unsealing may be complicity, now shows that forcing the seal open may be its own violence. Neither pole is clean. The mirror from the tomb is as much an assault as Mr. Spitzer’s silence was an abandonment.

Her directness, which the chapter admires, is also her blueprint. Cousin Hannah “always believed in being direct,” believed it the way Miss MacIntosh believed in facts and Catherine in her dreams, as a sealed conviction about how life must be met. Directness is a real strength and it is also a construction she has stopped questioning, a single answer she carries into every room. The book will collect the cost of this at her death, when she confesses that her great public battles were as nothing beside the domestic wars she never fought; here the sealed certainty is still intact, still sure that the mirror and the rescue are the right and only way.

Grief moves under her constant motion. She is nomadic, grief-shadowed, always in transit, and the transit is the tell. Cousin Hannah carries a grief she will not stop long enough to face, and her endless doing, the causes, the travels, the rescues, is partly a way of outrunning it. This is Grief Doesn't End refused in a new key: not walled like Catherine’s, not become-material like Mr. Spitzer’s, but outpaced, kept just behind her by never standing still. The woman who would force Catherine to look in the tomb-mirror cannot hold still in front of her own.

And Mr. Spitzer cannot finish her. He visits her house after she is dead, tries to write her epitaph, cannot settle on one, cannot even recall the date of her death; no one mourned her rightly. The unfinishable inscription is Unfinished Is Honest turned on the most decisive person in the book. Cousin Hannah, who believed in directness and certainty and the final answer, cannot be sealed into a final line. The woman of the finished blueprint gets no finished epitaph, and the failure to complete her is truer than any completed inscription would have been.

What the Chapter Refuses, and the Footing It Offers

The chapter refuses the reading that takes Cousin Hannah’s directness as its answer, the healthy confronter of reality against Catherine’s evasion, and the reading that takes her attempt to rescue Catherine as simple heroism. Neither the healthy realist nor the rescuer is the all of her.

The footing is that Cousin Hannah is the anti-Witness, forcing the seal open where Mr. Spitzer refused to disturb it, and the book shows the forcing as its own violence, no cleaner than his silence was clean. Her directness is a sealed blueprint, a certainty she has stopped questioning, and her constant motion outruns a grief she will not face. And her unfinishable epitaph is Unfinished Is Honest claiming even the most decisive figure: the woman of the final answer cannot be sealed into a final line. Forcing a sealed person open is not kinder than leaving them sealed, and certainty about directness is its own construction. Neither Mr. Spitzer’s silence nor Cousin Hannah’s mirror is endorsed, and her epitaph stays unwritten.

For Reflection

Is forcing someone to face a hard truth kinder than leaving them their comfort, or only a different kind of harm?

What conviction about how life must be met has one stopped questioning, and carried into every room?

What grief does one outrun by never standing still?
