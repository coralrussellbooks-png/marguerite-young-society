Arc 7: The Tavern (Chapters 67–71)

← Miss MacIntosh's Last Month · Story Arcs Home · Esther Longtree →

Spoiler note: This page covers Chapters 67 to 71. If you are reading along, read it after you finish Chapter 71.

Overview

The bus reaches Indiana. Madge and Homer walk out of the novel, and Vera spends a sleepless night at the Tavern among the Christian hangman Mr. Weed, Dr. O'Leary raging at the dead Sarah through the wall, and the remembered Mrs. Hogden, before a grey morning of inventory.

Central figures: Vera Cartwheel · Miss MacIntosh · Catherine Cartwheel · Moses Hunnecker · Justice O’Leary · Mr. Weed

Chapter by chapter

Chapter 67

The bus reaches an Indiana tavern and cannot park between a leaking milk truck and a hearse. Moses curses at old Doc, whom he believes is blocking the space with his ghost car. The passengers rock wearily in their seats. Madge laughs breathlessly, and Homer recalls a fun house of mirrors with no doors, wondering which door Jacqueline found.

Chapter 68

At night the bus finally stops. Vera watches Madge and Homer walk away, never to be seen again. She waits to check into the Tavern, a decaying hotel built in 1827, while the landlord talks at the bar with Mr. Weed, a Christian hangman. Mr. Weed's first wife, Marie, hanged herself from a peach tree for fear of another pregnancy. His second wife, Julia, has the mind of a child. His children play at hanging. He carries a pen given to him by a condemned man, a Jew's harp, and a black poker chip of unknown origin, and he considers himself a humanitarian. No clock strikes.

Chapter 69

Vera's room is marked Fire Escape. It has a corn-husk mattress, a "Forget me not" picture of a church steeple, and a slop jar holding someone else's hair combings. The landlord points out the other guests on the way: Moses, a knitting girl named Tillie, an old woman with enormous canaries, and Mr. Weed's shadow. All night, through the thin wall, old Dr. Justice O'Leary rages at Sarah, his dead sister, lists every disease he knows, and mentions his strangest case, a woman who is always pregnant. Vera finally screams at Sarah to go.

Chapter 70

In the quiet, Vera tells of Mrs. Hogden, whose name keeps shifting and whom Vera admits she never actually saw. Mrs. Hogden sold her Dutch windmill home and arrived at Catherine's house with birdcages, temple bells, and a letter of employment as arithmetic teacher that Catherine denied writing. Lisa Lunde, Catherine's new companion, suspected her at once. Mrs. Hogden lived for months in the abandoned playroom, playing chess against herself. She finally left her wig on a crab at the water's edge and a blackboard message reading "All a's are b's."

Chapter 71

Vera has not slept. She wears her mother's black velvet, meant as Catherine's shroud and never used, along with her lace and her pearls, which Catherine believed multiplied when lost. She thinks of Mr. Weed guarding his children through the night. She enters Dr. O'Leary's room, a clutter of mismatched women's shoes, medical instruments, a Yale diploma, and a calendar twenty years out of date. A woman shouts prejudices on the stairs. The Tavern Landlady, in thick dialect, tells her about everyone in town, including a waitress always expecting a baby. The maid, Tabitha, whispers good morning.
