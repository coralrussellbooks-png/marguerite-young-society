Characters: Cousin Hannah

Allusions: Women's Suffrage

Definition from research

Cousin Hannah, the formidable suffragette, brings a blast of comic force into a household of dreamers. She is the kind of woman who "would give him a whack with her black umbrella," ready to swing that umbrella at an intruder or at the Devil himself, and she is so self-sufficient that at chess she plays "both herself and a gentleman," supplying her own opponent. Her combative certainty and sharp tongue make her a bracing comic contrast to the vague, drifting figures around her, a jolly old campaigner who takes no nonsense from man, ghost, or fiend.

Cousin Hannah's comedy is not only the umbrella but a full mock-heroic military caricature. She is drawn as a battle-scarred general of the vote, "cuirassed by suffrage," girded "like a great Crusader's," the captain of "a lost campaign," her old grey army coat pinned with medals earned in "so many foreign and domestic wars." Attached to her is the chapter's theological-athletic joke, her scorn at the prospect of "Dog races in Heaven as on earth" on the firm ground that "bowling is better," backed by the strong right arm she built up in the bowling alleys. The humor is the collision of the sacred and the sporting, a suffrage Crusader who would sooner bowl her way into heaven than watch the dogs run there.
