Mahound (Ch 35)

From the reading guide, Chapter 35 · Research: Chapter 35

From the guide

Mahound is an archaic and often pejorative English term for Mohammed, used in medieval Christian literature to describe a fearsome foreign deity. Young uses it in Catherine’s question about whether Hannah might still “be riding in a desert place with her Mahound,” which is to say, with the terrifying and magnificent force that drove her through all those years. Hannah’s Mahound is not a person. It is the movement itself, the purpose, the calling that was also her marriage. Young does not explain the word. She trusts the reader to feel its combination of reverence and strangeness.
