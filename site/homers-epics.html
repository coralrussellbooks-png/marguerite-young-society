Definition from research

Chapter 2

  • Troy is a city that was besieged for ten years and eventually conquered by King Agamemnon. The war and siege was written in Homer’s “Iliad”.

Chapter 5

  • Penelope is a character in Homer's Odyssey. The reference to Penelope highlights themes of waiting, fidelity, and steadfastness amid uncertainty and absence. Penelope, the faithful wife of Odysseus in Greek mythology, is renowned for her patience and loyalty, waiting many years for her husband’s return from the Trojan War while warding off numerous suitors.

Chapter 6

  • “She was Morality, and I was Honesty”: Homer Capehorn shares the poet’s name. Homer’s works (the Iliad and the Odyssey) present a complex picture of morality and honesty, reflecting the values and norms of ancient Greek society. The pursuit of honor and glory are central motivations for Homeric heroes. Their actions, especially in battle, are often dictated by what will earn them recognition and respect from their peers. This can sometimes lead to actions that may be considered morally questionable. The decisions of Homeric heroes are guided by a set of values, including courage, strength, leadership, and excellence (aretē). They strive to excel in all areas, and failure to do so can result in shame (aischron). The gods play a significant role in determining the fate of mortals. While heroes are expected to uphold certain moral obligations, their destinies are often tied to the gods’ plans, which may not always align with human concepts of fairness. Homer suggests a type of morality that is dependent on a person’s upbringing and surroundings. Divine laws provide a baseline, but the majority of a person’s moral compass is shaped by their society’s expectations. In the Odyssey, Odysseus frequently uses lies and deception for survival and self-preservation, particularly when disguised. He tailors his falsehoods to specific situations and individuals, demonstrating his cleverness and resourcefulness. While Odysseus’s actions are often portrayed as strategic and necessary for his survival, they can also be viewed as ethically problematic. Despite the prevalence of deception, Homer also portrays honesty as a valuable trait. Odysseus drops his disguise at last to Penelope and to his father, and honesty brings him home.

Chapter 29

  • Diomedes is a prominent figure in Greek mythology. He is known as a brave and skilled warrior who participated in the Trojan War and is renowned for his exploits in Homer’s Iliad.

Chapter 51

  • Nepenthe is a drug described in Homer’s Odyssey as banishing grief or trouble from a person’s mind.

Chapter 82

  • Nepenthe is a mythical, ancient Greek potion or drug used to induce forgetfulness of pain, sorrow, or grief. It is famously mentioned in Homer's Odyssey as a gift to Helen of Troy to ease her suffering.
