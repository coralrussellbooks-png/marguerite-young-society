Definition from research

Chapter 2

  • Troy is a city that was besieged for ten years and eventually conquered by King Agamemnon. The war and siege was written in Homer’s “Iliad”.

Chapter 5

  • Penelope is a character in Homer's Odyssey. The reference to Penelope highlights themes of waiting, fidelity, and steadfastness amid uncertainty and absence. Penelope, the faithful wife of Odysseus in Greek mythology, is renowned for her patience and loyalty, waiting many years for her husband’s return from the Trojan War while warding off numerous suitors.

Chapter 6

  • “She was Morality, and I was Honesty”: Homer Capehorn shares the poet’s name. Homer’s works (the Iliad and the Odyssey) present a complex picture of morality and honesty, reflecting the values and norms of ancient Greek society. In the Odyssey, Odysseus frequently uses lies and deception for survival and self-preservation, particularly when disguised. He tailors his falsehoods to specific situations and individuals, demonstrating his cleverness and resourcefulness. While Odysseus’s actions are often portrayed as strategic and necessary for his survival, they can also be viewed as ethically problematic. Despite the prevalence of deception, Homer also portrays honesty as a valuable trait. Odysseus drops his disguise at last to Penelope and to his father, and honesty brings him home.

Chapter 11

  • “Penelope’s web”: In the Odyssey, Penelope weaves a shroud for Laertes by day and unravels it by night to hold off the suitors.

Chapter 13

  • “We both step and do not step into the same river, we both are and are not” (Heraclitus Homericus, Homeric Allegories)

Chapter 21

  • “A man who is a pig is still a man”: Circe turns Odysseus’s men into swine, but “their minds were as before” (Odyssey 10).

Chapter 22

  • “it was a wise child who knew his own father”: “It is a wise father that knows his own child” (The Merchant of Venice 2.2). Telemachus says, “My mother says I am his son; but I know not, for no man knows his own father” (Odyssey 1).

Chapter 23

  • “Ulysses, if ever he had seen Penelope under electric lights”: Ulysses is the Roman name for Odysseus, a hero in ancient Greek literature. Penelope is the wife of Ulysses and is an example of a faithful wife because she waits for her husband to return and tricks suitors who wish to marry her. Penelope wove a shroud by day and unwove it by night to put off her suitors (Odyssey 2).

Chapter 29

  • “Homeric butterflies which were like persons who had passed through many unknown forms of being”: Io Vanessa is the Peacock butterfly. The Aphrodite Fritillary and the Apollo (Parnassius apollo) are named for gods. Morpheus, “the fashioner,” shapes dreams in Ovid’s Metamorphoses 11. The Atlas moth is among the largest moths in the world. Papilio homerus of Jamaica is the largest swallowtail in the Americas, and Papilio antimachus is the largest butterfly in Africa.
  • “the old Diomedes with planets and half moons”: Diomedes (Deudorix) is a butterfly found in Africa.

Chapter 51

  • Nepenthe is a drug described in Homer’s Odyssey as banishing grief or trouble from a person’s mind.

Chapter 82

  • Nepenthe is a mythical, ancient Greek potion or drug used to induce forgetfulness of pain, sorrow, or grief. It is famously mentioned in Homer's Odyssey as a gift to Helen of Troy to ease her suffering.
