On 'Oh, come in': Young's classroom.

The note in To All My Darlings that Young would say this to students in her classroom at Indiana University, welcoming every arriving idea as a guest, is a key to the novel's deepest structural principle. The infinite house is not only Catherine's mind; it is Young's method. Every philosophy, every mythological figure, every historical allusion is invited in, given a chair, asked to speak. The novel has no bouncer. It is genuinely hospitable to contradiction, to the idea that has no business being in the same room as the idea next to it. This is either a formal achievement or a formal risk, depending on your tolerance for roommates. After thirty-nine chapters, you know which kind of reader you are.
