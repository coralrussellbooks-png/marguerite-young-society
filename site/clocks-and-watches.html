Definition from research

Chapter 2 · The Clock and Time

  • The twelve goblets and chairs represent the passage of time and the ritualized nature of existence.
  • Time is both suspended and fragmented in Catherine’s world.

Chapter 6

  • “She was watching the old town clock very closely now”: Clocks can signify: time, schedules, business, modern man, new beginnings, endings, and the transitory nature of human beings. In Tudor times a clock could mean truth unveiled by time. Clocks in this chapter are not merely instruments measuring time but serve as potent symbols of the instability of human experience, the blending of reality and dream, and the inexorable flow of time that ultimately eludes human control.
  • “She dreamed that she was a sundial in a ruined garden”: Old sundials carry the motto “I count only the sunny hours.”

Chapter 9 · Time and Watches

  • The silver dollar watch worn by Miss MacIntosh symbolizes the relentless march of time and the pressure of discipline.
  • Miss MacIntosh frequently refers to time as deceptive and insists on punctuality and the urgency of preparing for the future, reinforcing the theme of control versus Vera’s dreams.

Chapter 12 · Clocks and Time

  • Clocks stopped at different hours symbolize frozen moments, hesitation, or the past lingering in the present.

Chapter 47 · The Ambiguity of Identity and Time

  • Mr. Spitzer’s reflections reveal confusion about who he is, when he is, and how identity persists or dissolves across time and death.

Chapter 57 · Timepieces (Two Wrist Watches)

  • Represent duality and the conflicting measures of time, cosmic and earthly, highlighting Mr. Spitzer’s fractured perception of time.

Chapter 65 · The Watch and Biblical Quotations

  • Mr. Bonebreaker’s watch symbolizes time running out, the urgency of salvation, and the inevitability of the end.

Chapter 69 · Mr. Weed’s Watch

  • A “world within a world,” symbolizing time, fate, or the inevitability of death.

Chapter 71 · The Calendar and Books

  • Symbols of time, knowledge, and the passage from order to chaos.
  • The calendar stuck 20 years ago symbolizes frozen time and lost opportunity.

Chapter 79 · Symbolic Use of Time

  • The seven-year conception period symbolizes prolonged suffering and anticipation.
  • The anniversary visit to the other woman reflects attempts to reconcile past trauma.
