Allusions: Angels and Archangels

Definition from research

She has "wandered like some penitential palmer with sea shells on his hat," the pilgrim to the Holy Land marked by scallop shells, penance made visible, and a "Crusader's castle" sets her among the medieval holy wars.

"There were halos where there were no moons," a light of holiness that shines from itself, not borrowed or reflected.

"Angels rolling away the stones of tombs" recalls the empty grave of Easter and the stone rolled back.

"a white bird lifted its archangelic wing" lends the highest order of angels to a bird stirring in the wind, and "an old cross-beam breaking upon the waves" carries a faint echo of Noah's ark.

Her long prayer, "Forgive the cloud which knows not what it whispers," on through "I fell from star to star. My life was one long act of falling," gathers divine forgiveness, the Fall, and the downward pull of all things into one plea, a breaking body asking pardon for a secret "none can tell and live."

Around this the chapter also gathers echoes from beyond Christianity, a Roman epitaph, Carthage, a "forgotten Ramadan," a winding horn, but the mourning frame stays Christian, a burial in a "grey New England churchyard."
