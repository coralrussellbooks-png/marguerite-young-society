On Keats's Bright Star.

John Keats wrote Bright Star in 1819, the year before he died. The sonnet addresses a star (constant, patient, watching forever) and expresses the speaker's desire for a similar constancy, but in the arms of his beloved rather than in cold celestial solitude. The paradox is exact: he wants the permanence of the star but the warmth of the human; he cannot have both; the star endures and the beloved dies and the dying knows it. Cousin Hannah's deathbed cry to the bright star of her love is this paradox lived out: she calls to the constancy she chose not to reach for while she could, now that it is too late, from the position of the one who is dying.
