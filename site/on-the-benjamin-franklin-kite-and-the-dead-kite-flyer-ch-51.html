On the Benjamin Franklin kite and the dead kite-flyer.

Young's image, “he had made of a dead kite-flyer his premise,” almost certainly invokes Franklin's famous 1752 lightning rod experiment, in which he flew a kite in a storm to demonstrate that lightning was electrical. Franklin survived. The kite was a conductor. The lightning was drawn down the string. Young's Mr. Spitzer makes this dead kite-flyer his premise: the man who drew lightning from the sky and took it, at controlled cost, into a useful form. The premise is that anything, even lightning, can be channeled if you know what you're doing and use the right string. Mr. Spitzer is not sure he knows what he is doing. He is not sure he has the right string. But he has made the premise anyway.
