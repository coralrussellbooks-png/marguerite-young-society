Pillar: Pillar 3 Grief Doesn't End · Across every book

or On the loss that will not close, and what power does with our refusal to mourn.

We are bad at grief now. We want it to move through tidy stages and arrive at something called acceptance, after which we are restored to normal. The grief that does not cooperate, the kind that simply stays, embarrasses us. We treat it as a wound that has failed to heal on schedule.

Power is very interested in unmourned grief, because grief that cannot rest goes looking for somewhere to put itself. So power offers your loss a destination and a face. Your pain has a cause, it says, and the cause is them. The migrants, the elite, the unbelievers, whoever has been chosen. Let me remove them and you will be whole again. This is grievance turned into a weapon, and it works because the promise underneath it is the one we most want to hear: that the hurt can be ended, that someone can be made to pay, that you can be made new.

Young will not offer that. Her book is full of people carrying losses that never close. A mother lost inside her own dreams. A nursemaid lost to the sea. A search that finds no clean ending. Grief in that novel is not an event you get over. It is the climate you live in. It accumulates, layer on layer, like sediment, until it becomes the ground you walk on rather than a hole in the road.

The third pillar names grief as permanent weather. Not a problem to be solved, not a debt to be collected, but a condition you learn to live inside. And here is its political weight. If you can carry your loss without needing it avenged, without needing someone to pay, you cannot be recruited by the man who promises to heal you by harming somebody else. The unmournable grief that he wants to aim is, in you, simply being carried.

This is the hardest pillar to live, and I will not pretend otherwise. Letting grief stay, without the consolation of a culprit, asks a great deal of a person. But the alternative is to hand your sorrow to someone who will spend it on cruelty and call that healing. Better to carry it yourself. The weather does not have to clear for you to keep living under it.
