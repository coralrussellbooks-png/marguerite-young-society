Definition from research

Chapter 4 · Red Hair and Red Rooster’s Wing

  • Symbolizes vitality, passion, and the vividness of Miss MacIntosh’s presence.

Chapter 5 · Hair

  • Symbolizes identity, resistance, and the last vestige of personal freedom.
  • The bus-driver’s hair grows uncontrollably, reflecting his wild imagination and refusal to conform.

Chapter 12 · Hair

  • Symbolic of protection and sensitivity; also connected to historical and biblical references.

Chapter 17 · Baldness

  • Represents loss, emptiness, exposure, and transformation.
  • Compared to “the egg,” “the rock where nothing grows,” “the rose stripped of all its petals,” and “bare as the winter winds,” emphasizing barrenness and desolation.

Chapter 18 · Baldness of Miss MacIntosh

  • Represents the stripping away of illusion, revealing raw and uncomfortable truth.
  • Symbolizes mortality, vulnerability, and the loss of pretense.

Chapter 19 · Red wig

  • A mask or false identity, perched and ready but separate from her true self.

Chapter 20 · Hair and Baldness

  • Hair symbolizes beauty, identity, and vitality; baldness represents loss, vulnerability, and exposure.
  • Absence of hair is described as a “barren, impoverished landscape without flower or bush or hedge”.

Chapter 21 · Bald Head

  • Symbolizes raw truth, vulnerability, and the stripping away of superficial identity.

Chapter 63 · The Wig and Baldness

  • Miss MacIntosh's wig symbolizes the facade of social norms and appearances, which she ultimately reveals as a protective but deceptive covering.
  • Her baldness, exposed in a moment of vulnerability, represents raw truth, mortality, and the stripping away of illusions.

Chapter 64 · Baldness

  • Represents vulnerability, difference, and mourning. It is a physical manifestation of Miss MacIntosh’s inner loneliness and social alienation.

Chapter 64 · Wig

  • The wig symbolizes the mask people wear to hide their true selves and conform to societal expectations. Miss MacIntosh’s frequent removal and replacement of the wig depict her struggle with identity.

Chapter 65 · Baldness and Hairlessness

  • Represents vulnerability, loss, and the stripping away of pretense.
  • Contrasted with Mr. Bonebreaker’s idealized descriptions of hair and beauty.

Chapter 66 · The Red Wig

  • A mask hiding her baldness, symbolizing concealment of vulnerability.

Chapter 72 · Bald spot

  • Symbolizes the wear and loss Esther has endured.
