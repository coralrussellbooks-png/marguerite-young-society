On bloomers and Amelia Bloomer.

To All My Darlings notes that bloomers, the divided women's garments that became a symbol of women's rights in the 1850s, take their name from Amelia Bloomer, the women's rights activist who advocated for them as a healthful alternative to heavy, constricting dresses. Young places Cousin Hannah's vanity about silken skirts against this history: the suffragette tradition rejected certain feminine garments as instruments of constraint; the delirium gives them back. Cousin Hannah was always more complicated than any single garment's politics.
