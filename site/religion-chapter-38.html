Allusions: Revelation and the Apocalypse

“seven deathless eternities”: “Seven deathless eternities” may recall Dante’s Divine Comedy, where the seven terraces of Purgatory purge the seven deadly sins. Seven heavens are found in Judaism and Islam, and Hindu cosmology has seven upper worlds, the lokas. The seven virtues join the four cardinal virtues of Greek philosophy to the three theological virtues of Paul (1 Corinthians 13:13). See Dante, Judaism, and Hinduism. Earlier in Chapter 18.

"Babylon is fallen" is Revelation's cry over the doomed city, the corrupt empire brought down in judgment.

“the heavenly bridegroom none should see”: “Behold, the bridegroom cometh; go ye out to meet him” (Matthew 25:6). See the Chapter 33 resources and The Bible. Earlier in Chapter 33.

“she had found a stairway to heaven”: “And he dreamed, and behold a ladder set up on the earth, and the top of it reached to heaven: and behold the angels of God ascending and descending on it” (Genesis 28:12). See Genesis. Earlier in Chapters 14, 29 and 36.

“Cousin Hannah must have believed that the dead were silent”: “The dead praise not the LORD, neither any that go down into silence” (Psalm 115:17). See The Bible.

“action was no necessary criterion or measure of life”: Those that take action, do things, and are preoccupied with accomplishments are rewarded. The mid-western, Protestant work ethic is set in these standards. The Protestant work ethic is implicitly reflected through the character of Miss MacIntosh and her attitudes toward life, labor, and morality (Max Weber, The Protestant Ethic and the Spirit of Capitalism, 1905). In 2021 young people in China began “lying flat” (Elsie Chen, “These Chinese Millennials Are ‘Chilling,’ and Beijing Isn’t Happy,” New York Times, July 3, 2021). It recalls Diogenes the Cynic, a Greek philosopher, who lived in a large storage jar in Athens, often called a barrel, scorning comfort and convention. Work is considered “a false idol” and people instead rest, exercise, and relax as a form of “resistance.” See Miss MacIntosh.

“whether he was a man or a butterfly”: Zhuangzi’s butterfly dream. See the Chapter 37 resources and Taoism and Zhuangzi. Earlier in Chapter 37.
