Woman in Young's Magnum Opus

Chapter 24

← Chapter 23 · Woman in Young's Magnum Opus · Chapter 25 →

Other versions: Chapter hub · Research notes · The Wheel and the Spokes

Characters: Catherine Cartwheel · Joachim Spitzer · Miss MacIntosh · Vera Cartwheel

The dream has taken the whole household by this chapter, not Catherine alone. The servants have gone into it to keep their places, the valet "waiting on Wellington," the gardener sure he is Potiphar's spouse, the Irish housemaid silent in company and speaking only to herself as a crowd of "fine ladies" and "fly-catching warblers." This is the servants' survival inside a mad house, and it is plural selfhood spread down through the staff, each of them scattered the way Catherine is scattered, the dream no longer one woman's but the building's, real and dream as one country with a full complement of dreamers.

They keep its rituals, arranging chrysanthemums for the dead, lighting fires for guests who never come, and Catherine at the center works the telephone, placing calls to the dead and the absent on a line that reaches only the servants' quarters. The calls that do not connect are the book's erasure, and the "horizontal lady" who must die inside her dreams to keep from waking into death is the life-and-death boundary held in a single sentence, a woman kept alive by staying dead to the world.

Mr. Spitzer is here too, unreliable, his image displaced by symbolic figures whenever Catherine reaches for him, a companion who cannot be counted on to be one thing or even one man. Vera, watching the chaos, weighs whom to bring her burning questions to and turns from Spitzer toward Miss MacIntosh, the grounded pole, though the reader now knows that pole is coming apart as surely as the rest.
