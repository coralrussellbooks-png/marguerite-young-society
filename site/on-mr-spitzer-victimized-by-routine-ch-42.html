On Mr. Spitzer victimized by routine.

The phrase, victimized by routine itself, is one of Young's most compressed portraits of a certain kind of conservative consciousness. To be victimized by routine is to have submitted so completely to the structures of conventional life that the structures have become the prison. Mr. Spitzer values his dignity more than his life: he will maintain appearances at the cost of everything that appearances are supposed to protect. He cannot break the routine to speak honestly about what he saw at the deathbed, cannot cross the line that decorum has drawn, cannot do the one thing that might actually matter, because to do it would require surrendering the dignity that is all he has left. Young is not unkind about this. But she is clear.
