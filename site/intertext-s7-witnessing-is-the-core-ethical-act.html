Definition from research

"The hardest and most honest stance is to stay in the room with something painful and complicated without rushing to fix it or wrap it up."

Falada, the Grimm horse whose severed head goes on speaking the truth after death, stands behind Vera's pony. At the height of her ride Vera says, "I was the headless rider" (chapter 16).

The novel's own conduct is the fuller example. It reports Miss MacIntosh's disappearance into the sea in chapter 3, then returns sixty chapters later to stay with her again. In chapter 63 she is still "the one person never dreaming where all might dream," even though the reader already knows about the wig, the drowned book, and the empty beach. The novel does not explain her or rescue her. It keeps her company.
