Definition from research

Chapter 7

  • “He searcheth out stones in the darkness”: Job 28:3 speaks of “the stones of darkness, and the shadow of death.” This phrase can be interpreted as reflecting the struggle to find meaning, certainty, or “stones” of reality when surrounded by the unknown, illusions, or the intangible nature of life and death. It is part of the larger motif of searching for stability, truth, or guidance in a world filled with shifting shadows, dreamlike states, and elusive realities.

Chapter 8

  • Job 41:1 This phrase metaphorically questions how one might access or influence the hidden, powerful depths of existence: whether within the self, nature, or the cosmos.
  • “Canst thou draw out leviathan with a hook?”: Moses recites God’s speech on Leviathan from Job 41:1-21 nearly word for word, then adds his own line, “He roareth like a great locomotive.”

Chapter 9

  • “naked as in that day when we came from our mothers’ wombs”: “Naked came I out of my mother’s womb” (Job 1:21).
  • “One died in his full strength. Another died in bitterness of soul. How died the wise man?”: Job 21:23-25, and “how dieth the wise man? as the fool” (Ecclesiastes 2:16).

Chapter 10

  • “this life is very short and full of mortal sorrow”: Job 14:1 reads, “Man that is born of a woman is of few days, and full of trouble.” This phrase serves as a sobering meditation on human finitude, the inevitability of death, and the sorrow that colors a brief life.
  • “his brother worm who was under the ground”: Job says “to the worm, Thou art my mother, and my sister” (Job 17:14), and Hamlet says, “Your worm is your only emperor for diet” (Hamlet 4.3).
  • “Man that is born of woman”: The burial service opens, “Man, that is born of a woman, hath but a short time to live, and is full of misery.” The preacher adds the swollen stomach, from the trial of the jealous husband’s wife in Numbers 5:27.
  • “He cometh up, and he is cut down”: Job 14:2 reads, “He cometh forth like a flower, and is cut down: he fleeth also as a shadow, and continueth not.” This phrase serves as a poignant reminder within the narrative of life’s impermanence and the inescapable presence of death even at moments of joy and union. The preacher quotes the burial service word for word: “He cometh up, and is cut down, like a flower; he fleeth as it were a shadow, and never continueth in one stay.”

Chapter 14

  • “he had spoken to her out of the whirlwind”: “Then the Lord answered Job out of the whirlwind” (Job 38:1).

Chapter 15

  • “man is born to trouble as the sparks fly upward”: “Yet man is born unto trouble, as the sparks fly upward” (Job 5:7).

Chapter 17

  • “man is not born to happiness and is a bubble”: Homo bulla, “man is a bubble,” and “man is born unto trouble, as the sparks fly upward” (Job 5:7).

Chapter 18

  • “wrapped in this house of clay”: “How much less in them that dwell in houses of clay, whose foundation is in the dust” (Job 4:19).
  • “Common sense was the rarest jewel, more precious than rubies”: Wisdom “is more precious than rubies” (Proverbs 3:15), and “the price of wisdom is above rubies” (Job 28:18).
  • “Only the worm is clothed, and the worm is our little brother”: “I have said to corruption, Thou art my father: to the worm, Thou art my mother, and my sister” (Job 17:14).

Chapter 20

  • “grey hair does not signify a necessary wisdom”: “The hoary head is a crown of glory, if it be found in the way of righteousness” (Proverbs 16:31), but “great men are not always wise: neither do the aged understand judgment” (Job 32:9).
  • “as a belt of stars”: Orion’s Belt: “Canst thou bind the sweet influences of Pleiades, or loose the bands of Orion?” (Job 38:31).

Chapter 21

  • “the darkness and the whirlwind”: “Then the Lord answered Job out of the whirlwind” (Job 38:1).

Chapter 29

  • Restorer - signifies God’s ability and desire to bring back to their former state, or even to a better state, what has been lost, damaged or broken, whether spiritually, physically, or relationally. The story of Job, Israel, and Jesus are examples.

Chapter 31

  • “He cometh forth like a flower, and is cut down: he fleeth also as a shadow, and continueth not.” - Job 14:2 This quote poignantly expresses the fleetingness of life and the inevitability of death, reminding readers of the fragile and transient nature of human existence that is central to the novel's meditations on time, memory, and mortality.

Chapter 40

  • Coffin of Stars may refer to Delphinus and the main group of stars called Job’s Coffin.

Chapter 69

  • "Naked, we come into this world, and naked, we go out of it." The quote comes from the Bible, found in both Ecclesiastes 5:15 and Job 1:21. The phrase reflects the inevitability of human vulnerability at birth and death, stripping away all pretenses or material concerns, which is a poignant theme running through the novel’s exploration of mortality, identity, and human frailty.
