Chapter 12. Grief Doesn't End

← Previous · Philosophy of MMMD Home · Next →

See also: Pillar 3 Grief Doesn't End

Characters: Catherine Cartwheel · Cousin Hannah · Esther Longtree · Joachim Spitzer · Miss MacIntosh · Peron Spitzer · Vera Cartwheel

Themes: Grief, Loss, and Mourning

Chapters discussed: 1, 41, 63, 72, 82

Definition from research

Grief is not a stage to pass through on the way to recovery. It is permanent weather. Refusing to perform a tidy healing is a way of staying truthful about what was lost.

The whole novel is an act of grief. Vera has spent years searching for Miss MacIntosh, and she describes the loss as “the sudden, terrible shock of that great loss which had divided my heart against my heart” (ch. 1). She does not recover. She searches. The book’s title is the cry of that search.

Every major character carries a grief that the novel will not resolve. Mr. Spitzer mourns Peron for decades and cannot even choose the words for the tombstone. Cousin Hannah built a crusade over the girl whose hand she let go. Esther mourns the stillborn, who come “back to haunt me and tease me” (ch. 72).

Catherine’s life shows what happens when grief is refused. She arranged her whole world to avoid sorrow, and the novel’s verdict is exact: “The lack of sorrow was a sorrow, too” (ch. 41). Miss MacIntosh, at the other extreme, insisted “There is nothing to grieve over” (ch. 63), and her own disappearance became the grief that shaped Vera’s life. Neither avoidance nor dismissal works.

Honest grief is what remains. It does not pretend that the lost can be found. It does not pretend that the loss has been overcome. It keeps walking, and it keeps looking. At the novel’s end, Vera is “in mourning for three deaths” (ch. 82), and she is also in love and carrying a child. The grief has not ended. It has been joined by other things.
