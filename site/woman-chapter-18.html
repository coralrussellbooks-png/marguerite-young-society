Woman in Young's Magnum Opus

Chapter 18

← Chapter 17 · Woman in Young's Magnum Opus · Chapter 19 →

Other versions: Chapter hub · Research notes · The Wheel and the Spokes

Characters: Miss MacIntosh · Vera Cartwheel

The book says its crossing point outright in this chapter. Inside every woman, the text finds a "secret man"; inside every man, a "woman secret and recessive." This is the constructedness of gender, and it is the pillar spoken plainly, the recessive opposite carried inside each person, the seam the whole novel writes from named for once in the open.

Miss MacIntosh carries it on her body. The "red-headed, plain nursemaid" is now "bald and naked," the "feather of pretense" gone, the "curtain of illusion" pulled back, and the reveal turns into a statement about everyone: "each must wear a mask." The wig was one mask; the plain nursemaid was another; the "inner man" and the "outer man" are the two she names for the layered self. The bald head with its "missing features" and the "missing breast" are the body as the place the self comes apart, and the question that rises off them is the book's own, "Who am I?", asked by a person who has just watched her single self fall away.

The "old rapist" who attacks Vera in the dark is also, somehow, Miss MacIntosh, and the doubling is plural selfhood at its most unsettling, the attacker and the guardian the same figure. Her wisdom runs against the sorting that would keep them separate: "the best are often the worst, the lowest are the highest," "one sin sinks a sinner." The moral categories collapse in her plain mouth the way the physical ones collapse on her plain body, and the igloo house "at the roof of the world" holds the two of them alone with the fact.
