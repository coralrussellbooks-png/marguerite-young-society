Woman in Young's Magnum Opus

Chapter 13

← Chapter 12 · Woman in Young's Magnum Opus · Chapter 14 →

Other versions: Chapter hub · Research notes · The Wheel and the Spokes

Characters: Catherine Cartwheel · Joachim Spitzer · Miss MacIntosh · Vera Cartwheel

Vera crosses a frozen lake without knowing the depth beneath it, and the image is the chapter's method: a surface walked in confidence over a dissolving deep. What she learns, saying goodbye to Miss MacIntosh, is that the woman she washed and ironed for was "the strangest being on earth" and was never known at all, plural selfhood in the character the book keeps building to look most single.

The gardens of the dream open around her. Dr. Galen and Dr. Harvey stand among the guests, the dead present with the living, and the air is full of "wheeling skirts" and "masks" and a chessboard whose squares move under life-sized chessmen. The masks and the moving board are the self as mask and the dreamed world made solid, real and dream as one country. Venus stands "bearded by patriarchal fog," and the bearded Venus is the crossing point in a single image, the female figure with a grown beard, gender coming apart where the reader can watch it happen.

The chapter closes in the "garden of the deaf," where "the ear is dead" and Catherine insists that neither she nor Mr. Spitzer can hear or be heard. A frog screams and no one hears it. "One should see," the passage says, "but one should not hear the song." This is the silencing of women, and it is the book's deepest statement so far of invisibility and erasure, a whole garden built around a severed sense, where silence itself becomes the music, and the unheard song is the one the book keeps playing for anyone deaf to it.
