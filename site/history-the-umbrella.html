The Umbrella: A Leveller's History

← Previous · History in MMMD Home · Next →

Characters: Miss MacIntosh

Themes: Memory and the Past

Symbols: The Umbrella

Chapters discussed: 63

Definition from research

The one historical thread that runs through more than fifty chapters is the umbrella. In Chapter 63, Miss MacIntosh credits Jonas Hanway, who fought for chimney sweeps, with bringing it to England from the East, where it served as "a sign of princely dignity, of the superiority of one man over his fellow man, of the divine rights of rulers and kings." Her verdict is "Balderdash, nonsense," and she praises Hanway as "a fine leveller who lived to triumph over the rich."

This may be the most compact example of Young's method. An object of rank becomes an object of rain. A symbol built to divide people turns ordinary, carried by everyone, and the novel's endless umbrellas become the history of that change. It shows the invented status of royal signs and then brings them down to the actual weather, which is where the philosophy locates meaning.
