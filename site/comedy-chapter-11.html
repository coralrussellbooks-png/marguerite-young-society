Characters: Catherine Cartwheel · Joachim Spitzer

Definition from research

The shortest chapter in the novel is a comic catalogue. Catherine has reached the P’s, Q’s, and R’s of her alphabet of phantom guests, and the best of them is Sir Walter Raleigh, who removes his head along with his white plumed hat and asks to be called by his intimate name, Ocean, or Water. His head, it turns out, presides over a society devoted to atheistic propositions.

The running joke is Mr. Spitzer’s absence. Off on dusty legal errands, tracing a lost heir to no property, he has missed it all: Rimsky-Korsakov, Sir Joshua Reynolds, the songs of Ronsard, Raphael’s tomb, the constellation of the Ram. “What delightful company Mr. Spitzer had missed.” Catherine’s pleasure in reminding him is the chapter’s quiet cruelty, and the alphabet’s relentless order is its comic engine.
