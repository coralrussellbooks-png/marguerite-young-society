Allusions: Judaism · Revelation and the Apocalypse · The Bible · The Book of Job

Definition from research

"the brightest that ever fell with burning hair" is the fall of Lucifer and the rebel angels, the most radiant of the host cast down for pride, a third of heaven swept out with him in Revelation's account. The burning hair is Young's own fiery image, not scripture.

The bus driver's names come out in a "broken jeremiad," a lament named for Jeremiah, the weeping prophet who mourned a doomed Jerusalem.

His litany of names, "Reuben, Gad, Manasseh," on through Dan and Saul, gathers the tribes of Israel and other scriptural figures. The twelve sons of Jacob founded the twelve tribes, and the number itself carries completeness, a whole people named in a breath.

"the children of Ham" are the descendants of Noah's son Ham, from whom the Bible's Table of Nations traces many peoples.

"a stone without hands," "a stone of stumbling," "a living stone," and the rest chain together biblical stone images that all point to Christ: the stone cut without hands that shatters kingdoms (Daniel 2), the rejected cornerstone (Psalm 118:22), the stone that trips the faithless (Isaiah 8:14), the living and tried stone of 1 Peter and Isaiah 28. The closing line, "He searcheth out stones in the darkness," borrows Job 28's miner digging for hidden ore.

"The stork knoweth her times" opens a long litany of biblical roles and objects, most of them names for Christ or his mediators: the daysman who stands between God and man (Job 9:33), the forerunner (Hebrews 6:20), the advocate (1 John 2:1), the strayed sheep and the unjust steward from Jesus's parables, the wedding garment, the candlestick of God's presence. The stork itself comes from Jeremiah 8:7, its migratory instinct shaming a people who will not know God's law.

"Solomon, Bathsheba!" names King Solomon and his mother Bathsheba, once David's wife.

The claim that "twelve is the age when Christ went out to preach to the wise men" garbles the record: the Magi came to the child, the boy of twelve astonished the temple teachers, and the ministry began nearer thirty. In the novel the number matters less as fact than as a threshold, the passage from childhood into responsibility.
