Themes: Identity and the Self

Definition from research

The novel refuses the single, stable self on nearly every page, and it does so most sharply through Mr. Spitzer. He is a twin who survived his brother Peron, and his survival is a kind of partial nullification. He is mistaken for the dead man, mistakes himself for him, and cannot always say which brother he is. Catherine, Vera's mother, is many selves at once, crying out to know "which self of selves was true," setting her own identity against a rival she names Belshazzar, the last king of a falling kingdom. Radical immanence holds that multiplicity is not a fault to be corrected but the actual texture of being alive, and Young stages that truth in the idiom of resurrection and doubled souls. Mr. Spitzer's confusion is not a failure of self-knowledge. It is self-knowledge, honestly rendered.
