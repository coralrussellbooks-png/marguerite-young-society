On Baby Farming and Amelia Dyer

Baby farming (the practice of accepting custody of infants for payment, which in its most criminal form became cover for systematic murder) was a documented practice in Victorian Britain and, to a lesser extent, Australia and the United States. The most notorious practitioner was Amelia Dyer, who murdered infants in her care over approximately thirty years during the Victorian era. Trained as a nurse and widowed, Dyer took in infants and kept their fees while killing the children, disposing of bodies in the River Thames. She was caught when a child’s body was recovered from the river: the same method of disposal Esther uses for the mud baby. Her daughter, known as Polly, assisted her. Young’s “timid little assistant” echoes this detail. The Catholic Bon Secours Mother and Baby Home in Ireland, while not technically baby farming, represents a related institutional horror: children’s remains were later discovered in a septic tank on the property, with documented high mortality rates and abuse of mothers. These are the real histories behind the yellowed newspaper clipping the detective sends Esther.
