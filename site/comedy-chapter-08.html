Characters: Madge Capehorn · Homer Capehorn

Definition from research

The comedy here is a quarrel in which the two lovers describe completely different women. To Madge, Beatrice Fitzgerald is the oldest prostitute in the county, who turns out the light so no one can see her. To Homer, she is the laundress who pulls clean clothes on a little red sled through the snow and whose blue face proves, people say, that she has royal blood.

Every fact Madge offers, Homer turns innocent, down to the arrow that took Beatrice’s eye, which she treated “as if it were a hailstone that fell out of a cloud.” Cornered, he retreats into the three monkeys: see no evil, hear no evil, speak no evil. Madge’s own vanity is part of the joke. She sleeps with the light on and her pale gold hair spread on the pillow, so that no man can say afterward he did not know who it was.
