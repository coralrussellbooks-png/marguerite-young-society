On Saint Anne, Joachim, and the Miraculous Conception

To All My Darlings connects Esther’s unnatural conception to the tradition of Saint Anne and Joachim, the parents of Mary. In the apocryphal Gospel of James and in Islamic tradition (where they are known as Hannah and Imran), Anne and Joachim are elderly and infertile; their sacrifice was rejected at the Temple because childlessness was read as divine displeasure. After fasting and penance, angels appeared to both separately, promising them a child. They met at the city gate of Jerusalem and conceived Mary through their embrace, or, in some traditions, without physical union at all, which is why the conception of Mary is called “immaculate” (free from original sin) rather than literally asexual. The tradition of the child born to parents who could not ordinarily conceive, destined for something the world will not understand, is precisely Esther’s tradition. Rosemary is the dead twin. Esther is the surviving impossible child. She says she is Jesus Christ. Young does not contradict her.
