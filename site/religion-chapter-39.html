Characters: Catherine Cartwheel

Allusions: Angels and Archangels · Greek Mythology · The History of Opium

Definition from research

"memento mori," remember that you must die, is the old Christian charge against forgetting death, one of Catherine's memoirs kept for the living and one for the dead.

Belshazzar, Babylon's last king, haunts her, the doomed ruler of Daniel 5 who read the writing on the wall, a rival self against whom she measures her own.

Her cry to know "which of many selves" is true, and whether to give up the "beautiful false selves" for one true and dull, reads as a strange repentance, a dying to the self she loves for a barer one she does not.

Gabriel, the messenger angel, recurs through her dreams.

"was not God dreaming, too," she asks, dreaming these opium dreams, and if so, "should not Gabriel lose his plume." If God himself only dreams, the whole order of angels and mornings goes uncertain, dream and reality run together.

Her dreams wash up "upon the shores of Hades," the realm of the dead, strewn with drowned ships and crying gulls and a lost love.

She asks whether the drug is "God's drug," crediting even her visions to a divine hand.
