Chapter 43

On Chapter 43 of Marguerite Young’s novel Miss MacIntosh, My Darling (1965). Part of Woman in Young's Magnum Opus.
By Coral Russell · Updated October 29, 2026

Characters: Joachim Spitzer

A man arrives at understanding too late, and that lateness is the pillar. Mr. Spitzer carries the dying suffragette through a storm he cannot help her against, "voicing the music of the dead," and his confusion about whom he mourns, whether he is alive or dead, is the difficulty of witnessing and preserving a feminist life from outside it. His abstention from voting, his line that "the dead cast no vote," folds women's disenfranchisement into the scene, and his understanding that "always came too late" is the culture's chronic lateness to the women who fought for it.

The suffragette's delirium holds the fracture of a feminist self. Her "returning loves," the desires she had "subdued" and "denied," surface at the end and name the tension between private longing and public duty that a life of struggle demands, and her cry "Who am I? Am I myself or another heart?" is the plural, interconnected selfhood the novel keeps opening, identity dissolving under the weight of the cause and the world's expectations. Her weeping at a snowflake, her fear of the "unknown, untried, unknowable," expose the vulnerability beneath the warrior, and the narrator's sense that "few could endure the absolute truth" while "most must be self-deceived" measures the loneliness of seeing clearly in a society that will not.

The chapter reaches across history and race for its scope. Her fragmented invocations of ancient cities and armies place feminism in a long human continuum, and her cries for "Black Moor" and "golden archer" widen it past gender into racial and cultural intersection. Death here is ending and transformation at once, the "lid" she cannot tell is "lowering or raising," the boundary held open. The chapter closes on Mr. Spitzer piecing broken mosaics he grasps only after the fact, and on a feminist life that stays a journey through storm and contradiction, with no clean resolution and a persistent reaching toward meaning.
