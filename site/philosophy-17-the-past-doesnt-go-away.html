Chapter 17. The Past Doesn't Go Away

← Previous · Philosophy of MMMD Home · Next →

See also: Pillar 8 The Past Doesn't Go Away

Characters: Catherine Cartwheel · Joachim Spitzer · Vera Cartwheel

Themes: Time

Symbols: Clocks and Watches

Chapters discussed: 14, 24, 41, 47

Definition from research

The past is not behind us. It is beneath us, pressed into the present like layers of rock.

Catherine’s house has no single century. The servants say it is “Roman in the morning but Greek in the afternoon” (ch. 24), and the guests sleep “with little regard for century.” For Catherine, “The years collapsed to a moment whenever my mother looked back” (ch. 41).

Young’s image for this is the palimpsest. Under Catherine’s present visions lie “old and precious communications faded or illegible, a palimpsest of many hieroglyphic writings which had been effaced” (ch. 14). The old writing has not vanished. It shows through.

Mr. Spitzer keeps his own palimpsest, a “many-written palimpsest or tally sheet” on which he tries to record every “rose, bird, star, horse, barnacle” (ch. 47). But the tally always leaves someone out, “one lost soul never to be recorded.” Sedimentary time is not a complete archive. It is layer upon layer, and something is always lost between the layers.

The novel’s own structure is sedimentary. Vera rides the bus in the present, but most of the book lies beneath that ride, in layers of memory, and in the memories of others that Vera could never have witnessed. The reader moves down through the layers and back up again. The past does not stay past. It presses up through every scene.
