Definition from research

Old Doc, Dr. Justice O'Leary, holds a faith as broken as his mind. He has slipped his grip on reality, caught somewhere between sanity and madness, life and death, and years of watching people die have left him bitter and lost. He is "mad as a March hare," raving and wandering, still clinging to his calling as a healer even as his judgment fails him.

He curses the world and the God who made it. His God is not almighty but sorrowful, a maker filled with remorse for a flawed creation ever since the beginning. He doubts the old consolations. He once claimed to see something leave the room when a patient died, a "mist, a vapor," then admitted he never really heard a soul go. The silent woman he calls Sarah, crawling at his feet, seems to stand for whatever part of his life he can no longer control. He wants to help and cannot, and his failures at the bedside, especially around birth, deepen a crisis that is as much about faith as medicine. He keeps only a frayed hold on a God he pictures as suffering rather than saving, a tragic man at the far edge of understanding.
