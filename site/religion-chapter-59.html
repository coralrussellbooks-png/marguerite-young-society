Chapter 59

On Chapter 59 of Marguerite Young’s novel Miss MacIntosh, My Darling (1965). Part of Christianity and Religion in Miss MacIntosh, My Darling.

Allusions: Genesis · Greek Mythology · The Bible

Charon, the ferryman who carries the dead across for a coin, recurs from Greek myth rather than Scripture.

"de profundis," out of the depths, is Psalm 130, the penitent's cry from the bottom for mercy.

“The darkness was his illumination”: Compare Henry Vaughan: “There is in God (some say) / A deep, but dazzling darkness” (“The Night,” 1650). See Light and Darkness.

“as a ghost may cause the hairs to raise up on one’s hand”: “Then a spirit passed before my face; the hair of my flesh stood up” (Job 4:15, KJV). See The Book of Job. Earlier in Chapter 12.

“should one walk seven times around all existence”: The Battle of Jericho in biblical tradition involved the walls of Jericho falling after the sounding of trumpets and a ritual march around the city which is an event symbolizing the collapse of barriers and the onset of a new order. “And the seventh day ye shall compass the city seven times, and the priests shall blow with the trumpets” (Joshua 6:4, KJV). See On the Battle of Jericho and The Bible.

“Should he not be the leper or the beggar or the blind man”: The leper of Leviticus must “cry, Unclean, unclean” (Leviticus 13:45, KJV); medieval lepers carried a bell or clapper to warn others away. See The Bible.

“Out of a cloud they came, and into a cloud they returned”: “For dust thou art, and unto dust shalt thou return” (Genesis 3:19, KJV). See Genesis. Earlier in Chapters 3, 22, 26 to 28, 36 and 40.

“there was a missing rib”: “And the rib, which the LORD God had taken from man, made he a woman” (Genesis 2:22, KJV). Adam, too, was a sleeper: “the LORD God caused a deep sleep to fall upon Adam, and he slept” (Genesis 2:21, KJV). See Genesis.

"water to water and dust to dust" is the burial service and Genesis 3:19, the body returning to the earth. Earlier in Chapters 3, 22, 26 to 28, 36 and 40.

“these tri-stranded beings comprising the individual”: “And I pray God your whole spirit and soul and body be preserved blameless unto the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ” (1 Thessalonians 5:23, KJV). Christian writers who divide the person into body, soul, and spirit cite this verse. See The Bible.

“He was no trinitarian”: The Trinity in Christianity is the belief that there is one God who exists eternally as three distinct persons: the Father, the Son (Jesus Christ), and the Holy Spirit. These three persons are not separate gods but are one divine essence, each fully God, who share a single being and nature. While the word “trinity” doesn’t appear in the Bible, the doctrine is based on scriptural revelations about God’s nature and actions. See On the Trinitarians and The Bible. Trinitarians were a Holy Order founded in 1198 and dedicated to raising funds for the ransom of Christians held by Muslims during the crusades and piracy along the Mediterranean coast. They also performed charitable acts in the areas where the orders were established and were dedicated to studying the mystery of the Holy Trinity.

“his soul had departed upon the wings of the morning”: “If I take the wings of the morning, and dwell in the uttermost parts of the sea” (Psalm 139:9, KJV). See The Bible.

"all members of one another" is Romans 12:5, the many made one body in Christ.
