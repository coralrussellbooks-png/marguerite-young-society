The great explorers naming their Antarctic (Ch 35)

From the reading guide, Chapter 35 · Research: Chapter 35

From the guide

The great explorers naming their Antarctic discoveries after their wives is historically accurate. James Clark Ross named the Ross Ice Shelf and Ross Sea after himself, but dozens of Antarctic features were named by nineteenth and twentieth century explorers after women who waited at home: wives, mothers, daughters, patronesses. The implication in Young’s text, that it was colder at home than in the ice, is both a joke at the explorers’ expense and a genuine observation about what it meant to be the woman whose name was given to a glacier.
