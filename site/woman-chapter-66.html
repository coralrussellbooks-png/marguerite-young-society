Woman in Young's Magnum Opus

Chapter 66

Other versions: Chapter hub · Research notes · The Wheel and the Spokes

Characters: Miss MacIntosh · Vera Cartwheel

Miss MacIntosh walks into the sea at the end of this chapter, and the dissolving boundary between life and death and the body coming apart carry it there. She meets the day in a whaler's hat and sea boots, braced for storm, regretting she was never a whaler or a fisherman, and the masculine gear and the longing for a man's work are the crossing point worn as clothing and the same refusal of a woman's narrow place she has carried since her story began. Her lessons go on "in spite of death itself," the drilling of Vera against a background of drowned things, suspended time held against the sea's dissolution, and her skepticism of Columbus and Cook and Peary strips the male explorers of their authority even as she prepares to make a crossing none of them charted.

The old Arctic explorer courts her, and his wooden leg set beside her bald head is the shared amputation that binds them, two bodies come apart recognizing each other, the body as the place the self comes apart doubled across a man and a woman. He idealizes the cold, barren, imageless country where there are no calendars and no human faces, and his proposal is for a spiritual rather than a physical union, a marriage past the flesh, and she both feels the pull of his loneliness and refuses to "go with him," choosing self-determination over the escape he offers. The "amputated feet walking by themselves" he imagines are the book's resilience, the self moving forward even stripped of its supports.

She walks into the roaring ocean on a cold morning and does not come back, and the disappearance leaves only fragments, the wig, the corset, the knitting, the practical belongings that outlive her exactly as they did when the book first announced her gone. The sea is her coffin "with no lid but the dream," the boundary between life and death held permanently open the way it has been held open for her since Chapter 3, the law unable to say whether she is dead, the record failing to hold her. The black umbrella moving over water and sand is her enduring, protective presence past her body, and the "wounded doe" standing with a blind eye toward the sea closes the chapter on vulnerability and vigilance together, a woman erased and still, somehow, keeping watch.
