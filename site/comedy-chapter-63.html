Characters: Miss MacIntosh · Vera Cartwheel

Definition from research

Vera discovers in this chapter something she never suspected in all their years together: Miss MacIntosh’s “incorruptible sense of humor,” which can make her laugh even at herself when cornered. Confronted about the pretense between them, she gives her wig “a wicked twirl,” so that it sits definitely crooked, and stares at Vera with a mixture of wild humor and the reluctant respect one gives the mad.

Her retorts are pure Miss MacIntosh. When Vera reaches for biblical language about what has been broken, she answers, “Oh, drat it, child. The Bible exaggerates.” Then, wig off and wiser for it, she ends the discussion by announcing that she has all her clothes closets to clean out. The comedy is the comedy of a person who has been seen at last and decides to be amused about it, and it makes her last month the warmest stretch of the book.
