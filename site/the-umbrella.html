Definition from research

Chapter 1 · Miss MacIntosh’s Whaler’s Hat and Umbrella

  • Symbols of groundedness and practicality against the backdrop of illusion.

Chapter 3 · Black Umbrella

  • Miss MacIntosh’s shield and sword against the elements and the unknown.
  • Represents her protection from illusions and external threats.

Chapter 19 · Umbrella

  • A shield or protective barrier, even when unnecessary.
  • “The heavy-ribbed black umbrella, opened, was on the floor where it had rolled as if, during the night, she might have upheld it for her protection.”

Chapter 22 · Black Umbrella

  • Miss MacIntosh’s protective and practical tool, symbolizing her grounded nature and defense against both literal and figurative storms.

Chapter 32 · The Veils, Parasols, and Skirts as Symbols of Femininity and Defense

  • The chapter richly describes feminine accouterments like “great garden hats decked with billowing plumes,” “secretive veils,” “frilled parasols,” and “great hoop skirts flounced with ribbons and lace”. These symbolize traditional femininity, societal expectations, and also the subtle weapons and shields women use in social and gender conflicts.
  • Cousin Hannah’s urging for women to wield their parasol “as her sword or her shield” and to “fly her skirts as her banners in her war against tyrannical man” transforms these delicate objects into instruments of rebellion. The parasol becomes a metaphor for female agency in a world dominated by men.

Chapter 51 · The Umbrella with Zodiac Signs

  • Represents fate, time, and cosmic cycles, as well as the interconnectedness of personal destiny with universal forces.

Chapter 63 · The Umbrella

  • The umbrella serves as a metaphor for protection against external chaos and confusion, protecting her "sand-colored skin" from the "fog" of deception.
  • It also symbolizes autonomy and control, as she uses it to "scold a wave" or defend herself against phantoms.

Chapter 64 · Black Umbrella

  • Serves as a protective shield against the world’s harshness and symbolizes Miss MacIntosh’s resilience and steadfastness.

Chapter 65 · The Black Umbrella

  • Carried by Mr. Bonebreaker, symbolizes protection, shelter, and religious zeal.
  • When left behind, it signifies abandonment and the collapse of Miss MacIntosh’s hopes.

Chapter 66 · The Black Umbrella

  • Miss MacIntosh’s shield against the world; her barrier protecting her from close human contact.
