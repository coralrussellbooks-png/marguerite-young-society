Pillar: Pillar 5 Build Anyway, Knowing It's Temporary · Across every book

or On the comfort of the natural order, and the vertigo and freedom of knowing it was built.

There is a deep relief in believing that the way things are is simply the way things are. The hierarchy is natural. The arrangement is ancient. This is how it has always been, and your only job is to find your place inside it. The given order asks nothing of you except acceptance, and in exchange it lifts two heavy weights: the weight of responsibility, and the dizziness of having to decide.

Power adores the given. Every cruelty is easier to keep if it can be called natural, traditional, ordained, the way of things. The appeal to how it has always been is among the oldest tools there is, because it turns a choice somebody made into a fact nobody is allowed to question.

Young’s imagination runs the other way. She spent years on the story of New Harmony, two utopias built deliberately on the Indiana frontier by people who believed the order of life could be drawn up fresh. They failed, and Young does not hide the failure. But the point survives the failure: the arrangements people live inside were arranged. They are constructions, made and remade, not facts of nature. Even the self, in her work, is recursive, built out of older buildings, assembled rather than found.

The fifth pillar holds that everything is constructed, including the things that present themselves as eternal. This is vertigo at first. If nothing is simply given, then nothing carries you, and you are responsible after all. But the vertigo is also the door. What was built by hands can be understood, questioned, and built differently. The natural order, looked at closely, turns out to be a set of decisions wearing a costume.

You did not consent to most of what you live inside, but somebody made it, and what was made is not sacred. That is not a license for chaos. It is the return of a power that the word natural was quietly taking from you. The order is not the weather. It is architecture. And architecture can be redrawn.
