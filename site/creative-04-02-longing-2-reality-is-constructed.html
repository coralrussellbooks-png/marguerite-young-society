Pillar: Pillar 2 Reality Is Constructed · Across every book

or On the hunger for the secret truth, and the freedom of living without one.

We are told, constantly, that there is a real story under the visible one. That the news is a cover, that the official account hides the actual machinery, that if you could just pull back the curtain you would see the thing as it truly is. Some of this suspicion is healthy. Most of the time, though, it is a door.

Through that door walks the figure who says he has the real story. He tells it like it is. He names the hidden hand, the cabal, the enemy you could not see. The relief is immediate, because the worst feeling is not being lied to. It is not knowing, and he has cured the not-knowing. He has handed you one explanation that makes the whole churning mess cohere. This is how conspiracy works on a person. It is the hunger for bedrock, answered by a man with a shovel and a story.

Young’s whole novel is built to starve that hunger. Her narrator chases the one person who seemed entirely real, entirely without illusion, and discovers that the realist was masked too, that under the plain practical surface was another concealment. There is no figure in the book who turns out to be the solid ground. It is illusion the whole way down. And the strange thing Young insists on is that you can know this and still go on. You do not need the floor to be real in order to keep walking on it.

The second pillar is exactly that. There is no secret story that will finally explain everything, no curtain that pulls back on the real. It is masks, and construction, and appearance, and you live anyway. Once you have made your peace with that, the man with the shovel has nothing you need. You can sit inside uncertainty without grabbing the totalizing myth he is selling, because you are no longer desperate for bedrock. You have learned to stand on water.

The terror of no floor and the freedom of no floor are the same fact. Which one you feel depends on whether you are still waiting to be saved by the truth, or have decided to live without that particular rescue.
