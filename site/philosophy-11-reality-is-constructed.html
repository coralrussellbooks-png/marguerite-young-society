Chapter 11. Reality Is Constructed

← Previous · Philosophy of MMMD Home · Next →

See also: Pillar 2 Reality Is Constructed

Characters: Catherine Cartwheel · Miss MacIntosh

Allusions: The History of Opium

Chapters discussed: 63

Definition from research

Most of what feels like solid reality is built. Seeing the construction is the first step out from under its spell, but seeing it does not mean escaping it. People go on living in what has been made.

The two great images of this principle are the opium house and the wig. Catherine’s house is invention on a grand scale, a whole world of guests and centuries summoned from a bed. Miss MacIntosh’s wig is invention on the smallest scale, a single disguise worn over a single head. The novel treats them as versions of the same thing. The mother who dreams everything and the nursemaid who dreams nothing both live inside something made.

Miss MacIntosh gives the principle its clearest statement, at the moment her own invention is exposed. “If I warned you against those lies and illusions surrounding us, I meant only those which we ourselves have made. Every man has a porcelain crown like mine” (ch. 63). A porcelain crown is a false tooth, and a crown is also the sign of a king. Every person wears something artificial, and every person is, in some sense, crowned by it.

Young’s own words give the ethic that follows. The mature person, she said, is one who has “examined all the illusions of life” and knows “there isn’t any reality” and yet goes on. Living in the invention is not a failure of nerve. It is the only place there is to live. The difference lies between those who know they are in it and those who mistake it for the granite road.
