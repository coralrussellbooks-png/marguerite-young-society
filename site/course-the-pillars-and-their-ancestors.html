The Pillars and Their Ancestors

A Course in Radical Immanence · Radical Immanence

To trace the entire architecture of Radical Immanence: as outlined in Coral Russell's comprehensive study of Marguerite Young's philosophy, to map a framework that strips away traditional transcendence and fixes reality firmly in the unresolved "here".

The Eleven Pillars can be anchored to distinct philosophical traditions, modern thinkers, and defining conceptual quotes:

Pillar 1 Not One Self

The Concept: The self is constitutively multiple and irresolvable rather than a single unified identity.

The Ancestor/Quote: Walt Whitman in Song of Myself (1855):

"Do I contradict myself? Very well then I contradict myself, (I am large, I contain multitudes.)"

Alternatively, psychoanalyst Carl Jung's concept of the fractured psyche consisting of numerous autonomous sub-personalities.

Pillar 2 Reality Is Constructed

The Concept: Delusions, private mythologies, and hallucinations are not errors of consciousness; they are the primary material of reality.

The Ancestor/Quote: Pedro Calderón de la Barca in Life is a Dream (1635):

"What is life? An illusion, a shadow, a fiction... for all of life is a dream, and even dreams are dreams."

Pillar 3 Grief Doesn't End

The Concept: Loss does not resolve through linear stages toward "acceptance"; it accumulates like geological strata, becoming the unyielding atmosphere we walk through.

The Ancestor/Quote: C.S. Lewis in A Grief Observed (1961):

"Grief is like a long valley, a winding valley where any bend may reveal a totally new landscape."

Pillar 4 Witnessing Is The Core Ethical Act

The Concept: The ethical imperative is not to fix, cure, or conclude, but to actively stay in the room and witness a contradiction without flinching.

The Ancestor/Quote: French philosopher Simone Weil on the concept of Attention:

"Attention is the rarest and purest form of generosity."

It is the flat refusal to look away from suffering even when it cannot be solved.

Pillar 5 Build Anyway, Knowing It's Temporary

The Concept: Reality, language, and memory are built outward by continually looping back into themselves.

The Ancestor/Quote: Danish philosopher Søren Kierkegaard:

"Life can only be understood backwards; but it must be lived forwards."

Pillar 6 Unfinished Is Honest

The Concept: Truth lives inside things that are unfinished; a closed, perfectly completed sentence or system is an artificial lie.

The Ancestor/Quote: Romantic poet John Keats’s concept of Negative Capability:

"...when man is capable of being in uncertainties, Mysteries, doubts, without any irritable reaching after fact & reason."

Pillar 7 The Margins Reveal the Truth

The Concept: The margins and edges are not disposable outskirts; they are where standard categories show their seams and structurally define the entire system.

The Ancestor/Quote: Modern political philosopher Hubert Humphrey (1977):

"...the moral test of government is how that government treats those who are in the dawn of life, the children; those who are in the twilight of life, the elderly; and those who are in the shadows of life—the sick, the needy and the handicapped."

Pillar 8 The Past Doesn't Go Away

The Concept: The past never actually recedes; it gathers in deep layers and presses upward into the present moment.

The Ancestor/Quote: William Faulkner in Requiem for a Nun (1951):

"The past is never dead. It's not even past."

Pillar 9 Language Shapes What's Possible

The Concept: Language does not reflect an already existing world; it builds a provisional world through its own prolonged execution.

The Ancestor/Quote: Philosopher Ludwig Wittgenstein in Tractatus Logico-Philosophicus (1921):

"The limits of my language mean the limits of my world."

Pillar 10 The Sacred Is Here

The Concept: The sacred does not live elsewhere (in heaven or a promised land); it exists wholly within the grit, fog, and unresolved reality of the present.

The Ancestor/Quote: French philosopher Gilles Deleuze in his final text, Pure Immanence (1995):

"A life of pure immanence... does not depend on an object or belong to a subject... The immanent life contains only virtualities, events, singularities."

Pillar 11 The Crossing Point

The Concept: The unique individual consciousness is the localized axis where all these infinite wheels, illusions, and historical strata meet.

The Ancestor/Quote: Boethius in The Consolation of Philosophy (524 AD), reimagined:

"In constancy is my very essence," says Fortune. "Turn my wheel with a rush of wings... but if you try to stop it, you are no longer Fortune's master."

Young moves this wheel entirely inside the human heart.
