In the research: Chapter 1, Chapter 5, Chapter 7, Chapter 8, Chapter 67, Chapter 68

Definition from research

Chapter 1

Listed as: Moses Hunnecker (The Bus Driver)

  • A complex figure mixing spiritual invocation with reckless behavior, embodying ambiguity.

Chapter 5

Listed as: Moses Hunnecker (The Bus-Driver)

  • Central figure, described as wild-eyed, drunken, and somewhat erratic.
  • His long hair and beard symbolize his identity, freedom, and resistance to external control.
  • He is a life-long Republican, openly hostile to Democrats, whom he blames for the country’s decay.
  • He is a caretaker figure for his mother and is caught between delusion and reality, possibly dead or dreaming while driving an old bus.
  • His internal monologue reveals a deep existential crisis and philosophical reflection on life, death, and meaning.

Chapter 7

Listed as: Moses Hunnecker (The Bus Driver)

  • Acts as a skeptical observer, rationalizing Old Doc’s strange behavior.
  • Reflects on Old Doc’s past and present, doubts his sanity, and worries about his own survival.
  • Represents the worldly, pragmatic perspective contrasted with Old Doc’s mystical one.

Chapter 8

Listed as: Moses Hunnecker (the old bus driver)

  • An experienced, somewhat cynical man who knows the terrain well and sees the storm as routine.
  • He stops the bus at a graveyard hill, signaling a crucial moment in the journey.
  • He is skeptical of medical science and doctors, especially “old Doc,” whom he sees as out of touch and ineffective.
  • The bus-driver’s long monologue reveals his mistrust in institutions, the inevitability of death, and a complex view of life, health, and mortality.

Chapter 67

Listed as: Bus-driver

  • A long-haired, possibly drunk or deeply weary man who curses and talks aloud to "old Doc," an unseen figure. He embodies frustration, loneliness, and a struggle against an unyielding obstacle.

Chapter 68

Listed as: Moses

  • A figure representing avoidance and detachment, Moses goes home to a mother who believes he is her bridegroom, but he maintains distance, obeying "traffic rules" metaphorically to avoid emotional entanglement.
  • His “old mother”
  • The bus driver feels obligated to care for his mother, who he says thinks he is "a baby crying in her arms". He made a promise to never leave her. This sense of responsibility, whether real or imagined, weighs heavily on him and contributes to his anxieties.
  • The bus driver's mother exists in a dreamlike state, much like the characters Vera encounters throughout her journey which blurs the lines between reality and fantasy.
  • The bus driver's mother is sometimes depicted as young and beautiful, with breasts filled with milk. This suggests a longing for an idealized past, a time of innocence and maternal comfort. She is associated with the potential, yet ultimately unfulfilled, union.
  • The bus driver's relationship with his mother can be interpreted as a symbol of his internal conflict between his desire for freedom and his sense of obligation. He dreams of escaping his mundane life as a bus driver. However, his commitment to his mother, whether self-imposed or externally enforced, keeps him tethered to his present circumstances.
  • The bus driver says that he will not cut his hair as long as "the Democrats were running us", and that he has made a promise to his mother to never cut his hair. It seems as though the bus driver's long hair is a symbol of his commitment to his mother and his abstinence from the more modern world.
