Allusions: Revelation and the Apocalypse · The Bible · The Book of Job

Definition from research

"we are gathered here in the sight of God" is the marriage rite of the Book of Common Prayer.

"speak now or forever hold his peace" is the old call for objection, once a real legal safeguard against unlawful marriage, now mostly ceremony.

"the end of the world draweth nigh with lightnings and thunderings and voices" pulls apocalyptic imagery from Revelation, joined to Paul's promise that the dead shall be changed "in the twinkling of an eye."

"the sea shall turn to glass" and "the secrets of all hearts shall be revealed" gather the judgment visions of Revelation, the thrones and the sea of glass, with Paul's disclosure of every hidden thing.

"even as ye will answer at the dreadful Day of Judgment" is the marriage charge of the Book of Common Prayer, binding the vow to the final reckoning.

"take this man to be your lawful wedded husband" lets the rite slip its own words, marrying man to man and folding funeral into wedding, so that vow and identity blur, fitting for a man poised between life and death.

"Man that is born of woman hath but a short time to live" is Job 14:1, and the line that follows, "He cometh up, and he is cut down, even like a flower," is Job 14:2, the brevity of a life.

"Spare us, Lord most holy, God most mighty, holy and merciful Savior" is a funeral sentence from the Book of Common Prayer, set to music by Purcell.

"Oh death, where is thy sting? Oh grave, where is thy victory?" is 1 Corinthians 15:55, death undone by resurrection, turned here toward the missing bride.

"Can these bones live?" is Ezekiel 37, the valley of dry bones and the question of whether the dead can rise.

"earth to earth, ashes to ashes, dust to dust," closing on "deep calling to deep," is the burial service of the Book of Common Prayer with Psalm 42 folded in.

"As it was in the beginning, is now, and ever shall be, world without end" is the Gloria Patri, the doxology of God's unending glory.
