Appendix A: The Five Frameworks at a Glance

Part of The Weather You Were Never Allowed to Have, on the psychology in the work of Marguerite Young (1908 to 1995).
By Coral Russell · Updated October 29, 2026

Framework One: The Chorus Model

The core reframe: You are not one man. You are a chorus. The goal is not to achieve the singular coherent self the framework required. The goal is to become a better host to all the selves you carry.

The lie it replaces: There is a correct version of the male self: singular, coherent, resolved. The complexity is the problem. The management is the solution.

The Chorus Model: You are multiple voices, different registers, different histories, all of them yours, none of them the whole of you. The question is not which one is real. They are all real. The question is who is conducting.

The three practices: Name three selves you have inhabited in the last year. Give each one a name that is not yours. Write down what each one wanted without editing or justifying. Which part of you is most in charge right now? How long has it been running things? What is it protecting you from? One sentence for each answer. Write a letter from one of your older selves to the one you are now. Not the self you hope to become. A self you have already been. Read it once. Do not revise it.

Framework Two: The Working Story

The core reframe: The stories you have been handed about what a man is and what a man's life looks like are not facts. They are tools. The question is not whether your story is true. The question is whether it is working.

The lie it replaces: The man with the clear purpose and the consistent performance is the correct story. Deviation from it is failure or weakness.

The Working Story: Not is this story true but is this story working. Is it expanding you or contracting you. Is it yours or did you inherit it. Is it serving your life or serving the systems that handed it to you.

The three practices: Write down a story you are currently living by in its raw form. Ask: is this story expanding me or contracting me? Name a story you inherited. Write one honest sentence about what it would cost to put it down and one honest sentence about what it is already costing you to keep carrying it. Identify a story that stopped working. Ask: who was this story working for? Then write: I carried this story as long as it was useful. It is not useful anymore. I am putting it down.

Framework Three: The Climate of Loss

The core reframe: Grief is not a handling problem. It is not a timeline. It is the weather of a life fully lived. You do not handle it correctly and move past it. You develop a literacy for it.

The lie it replaces: Grief has a timeline. The man who has moved past it has handled it correctly. The grief that keeps coming back is evidence of incomplete handling.

The Climate of Loss: Grief as permanent climate does not mean permanent devastation. It means grief changes form, intensifies seasonally, recedes without warning, and returns. You are not failing when it returns. You are in weather. The practice is building grief literacy: learning to read your own patterns.

The three practices: Name a loss in your life that has no clean endpoint. Write it down without trying to resolve it. Sit with it for one full minute. Map one season of your grief climate. What are the early signals? What makes it worse? What makes it survivable? Identify who or what you are still in relationship with even though the form of that relationship has changed entirely. Call it what it is: the continuing bond. This is not failure. This is grief doing what grief actually does.

Framework Four: The Standing Still

The core reframe: The man who is not affected by things is not the man who has transcended difficulty. He is the man whose window has narrowed so far that the difficulty cannot get in. The witness is not the man above the weather. The witness is the man who is fully inside the experience and has not been taken over by it.

The lie it replaces: The strong man is the one who is not moved. The correct response to difficulty is to handle it without being visibly affected by it.

The Standing Still: The witness is not detachment. The witness is the rooted presence of the tree in the wind: branches moving, roots holding. The standing still is the capacity to be fully present with what is happening without being taken over by it.

The three practices: Identify the last time you lost the witness position completely. Write down the early signals you missed. You are building the map of your own window. Practice the return right now. Find the breath. Notice: I am feeling the weight of this. I am not only this weight. Write one sentence about what the return feels like in the body. Identify one place where you are requiring the experience to be different before you can be fully present to it. Write: I am here. Not when it is better. Now. In this.

Framework Five: The Reef Method

The core reframe: The life that keeps returning to the same territory is not the life that has failed to move on. It is the life that is building something that can only be read from a sufficient distance.

The lie it replaces: Growth is linear. The man who is still dealing with the same things he was dealing with ten years ago has not done the work. Progress is measurable by the distance from the starting point.

The Reef Method: Every return to old territory is a new layer on the structure you are building. Nothing is wasted. Nothing is finished. You are not going somewhere. You are becoming more of what you already are.

The three practices: Do the backward read. Find five moments you have previously understood as setbacks or failures. For each one write one sentence that begins: this added to the structure by. Identify the return you are currently in. Write: what am I bringing to this return that I did not bring before? Identify the witness in your life. The person or community that can hold what comes after: this is hard to say but here it is. Name one thing you have not yet said in their presence that the reef needs you to say.
