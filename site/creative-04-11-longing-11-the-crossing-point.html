Longing 11: The Crossing Point

Pillar: Pillar 11 The Crossing Point · Across every book

or On the wish to outlast your own death, and the true shape of the largeness you are looking for.

This is the deepest hunger and the most human, and I have saved it for last because every other longing is a version of it. We do not want to vanish. We want to belong to something that goes on after we are gone, something large enough to make our small life mean more than its small span. The bloodline. The nation. The thousand-year reach. A name that survives inside the immortal group.

Power offers exactly this, and it is the most seductive thing it has. Give yourself to the nation, the race, the movement, and you will not really die, because the thing you gave yourself to will not die. The catch is in the giving. The immortal collective is always, in the end, hungry for the actual bodies of the people who believe in it. It promises to outlast your death and then arranges your death to feed its outlasting. The abstraction eats the very lives it offered to preserve.

Young’s answer is not to deny the hunger but to locate its true object. You already belong to something vast. You are made of the dead, of their language and their griefs and their choices, deposited in you like silt carried downriver and set down somewhere new. And you are making the ground the not-yet-born will stand on. The line of transmission is real, and it runs straight through you. You are the place where what was received crosses into what is passed on. You are the crossing point.

The eleventh pillar offers that real largeness in place of the murderous abstract one. You wanted to be part of something that outlives you. You are, already, with no leader required and no enemy to purge. The obligation it asks is not to die for the nation but to tend the actual generations, the ones in the ground behind you and the ones in the unformed ground ahead. It is humble, and it is located, and it does not need your blood. It needs your care.

You were looking for immortality. What you have is better, because it is true. You are a moment in a long act of carrying, and the carrying does not stop with you.
