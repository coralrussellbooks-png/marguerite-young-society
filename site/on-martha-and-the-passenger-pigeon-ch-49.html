On Martha and the passenger pigeon.

The passenger pigeon (Ectopistes migratorius) was once so numerous that nineteenth-century ornithologists estimated three to five billion individuals in North America. John James Audubon described flocks that took three days to pass overhead. By 1900 they were effectively gone from the wild; the last confirmed wild bird was shot in 1900. Martha, named for Martha Washington, was hatched in captivity and died at the Cincinnati Zoo on September 1, 1914 (Young's date of September 14, 1918 is slightly different from historical record, a deliberate compression or alteration in service of the narrative). Aldo Leopold's 1947 monument inscription asked whether it was sorrow or shame that a species so numerous could be destroyed so completely. Young's answer, given through Mr. Spitzer's grief, is: both.
