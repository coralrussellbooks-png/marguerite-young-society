Woman in Young's Magnum Opus

Chapter 39

Other versions: Chapter hub · Research notes · The Wheel and the Spokes

Characters: Catherine Cartwheel · Lord Murdock Burdock Thrushmorton · Mr. Res Tacamah

The unstable self is this chapter's pillar, and Young builds it out of a great Bostonian suffragist. The pioneer who fought "distant pinnacles of romance, the ivory towers and minarets," a "phantom" and "creature of the immortal night" with "great Coriolanian eyebrows" fighting "dragons, ogres, basilisks," is feminist resistance raised to myth and it is a self that will not stay singular, "the personal pronoun I" shown to be unstable and shared among many selves. Catherine as social hostess and recluse at once, present and absent, is the plural subjectivity the chapter treats as truth rather than confusion.

Feminism becomes an argument about knowledge and equality. The chapter sets "rational" science and schedules against Catherine's mystical world of "spirit messengers" and refuses the male chronology in which "time should be no more," so that the suppressed, intuitive way of knowing stands as valid against Enlightenment reason. The suffrage captain's demand that "equalities must be verified," her scorn for man as a "blushing coward" who leaves women to fight alone, makes the feminist project a transformation of psychic and cultural reality and not only of law. The many-spiraling house with its infinite rooms is the fragmented female subject given architecture, corridors the woman must negotiate to inhabit her multiple roles at once.

The chapter closes on the embrace of ambiguity against patriarchal order. Catherine prefers "confusion to order," "illusive ambiguities" to fixed truth, and her skepticism toward "simple scientific truth" is a feminist epistemology that values the subjective and the uncertain. The drug bottle personified as Mr. Res Tacamah, and Lord Murdock Burdock Thrushmorton "twittering like a cage of canaries," turn patriarchal authority to absurdity, and the closing question of whether God dreams, whether dream and reality can be told apart, leaves the chapter inside multiplicity, the aching void and the numberless dead crowding rooms that no single "I" could ever hold.
