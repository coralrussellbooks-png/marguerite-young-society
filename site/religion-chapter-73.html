Chapter 73

On Chapter 73 of Marguerite Young’s novel Miss MacIntosh, My Darling (1965). Part of Christianity and Religion in Miss MacIntosh, My Darling.

Characters: Esther Longtree · Joe Goldberg

Allusions: The Book of Job

Esther Longtree's "everlasting pregnancy" and her waiting "little shadow boxer" warp the pregnancy of the Virgin, a birth forever promised and never come.

“worse than the Chaldeans”: In the Bible the Chaldeans are the Babylonians of Nebuchadnezzar, who burned the Temple and carried the people of Judah into captivity (2 Kings 25). Habakkuk calls them “that bitter and hasty nation” (Habakkuk 1:6, KJV), and in Daniel they are the king’s astrologers: “the magicians, and the astrologers, and the sorcerers, and the Chaldeans” (Daniel 2:2, KJV). For a Jewish fighter, no people could be worse. See The Bible and Judaism. Chaldeans may refer to Chaldean Catholics who are adherents of the Chaldean Catholic Church, which originates from the historic Church of the East. The Chaldean community originates from groups of adherents of the Church of the East that entered into communion with the Roman Catholic Church in the 16th and 17th centuries. They were indigenous to the regions of northern Iraq, southeastern Turkey, northeastern Syria and northwestern Iran, many have migrated to Western countries including the United States, Canada, Mexico, Australia, Sweden and Germany. Many of them also live in Lebanon, Egypt, Palestine, Israel, Jordan, Iran, Turkey, and Georgia. (Wikipedia)

“the brave little Jew, hard as rock”: Jewish fighters ruled American boxing in the first half of the twentieth century: twenty-six Jewish world champions between 1910 and 1940, most of them wearing the Star of David on their trunks. Abe Attell held the featherweight title from 1906 to 1912 (he first claimed it in 1901) and was later tied to the fixing of the 1919 World Series. Benny Leonard was lightweight champion from 1917 to 1925, and Barney Ross, raised in Chicago, held titles in the 1930s. See Joe Goldberg and Judaism.

Joe Goldberg's dying quarrel with God echoes Job, a strong man broken and demanding an answer.

“a fighter in the Jewish family, beginning with those who had fought the angels”: “And Jacob was left alone; and there wrestled a man with him until the breaking of the day” (Genesis 32:24, KJV). The man renamed him Israel, “for as a prince hast thou power with God and with men, and hast prevailed” (Genesis 32:28, KJV); Hosea says “he had power over the angel, and prevailed” (Hosea 12:4, KJV). Joe’s last bout, with God on a peak above the clouds, is Jacob’s, and he is losing it. See The Bible, Angels and Archangels, and Judaism. Earlier in Chapters 3, 6, 15, 53 and 65.

“old Phoebe’s statue”: Old Phoebe was a washwoman, and the Phoebe of the Bible was a servant of the church: “I commend unto you Phebe our sister, which is a servant of the church which is at Cenchrea” (Romans 16:1, KJV; the King James spells her name Phebe). The Greek word translated “servant” is diakonos, deacon, and she is the only woman so named in the Bible; Paul trusted her to carry his letter to Rome. Where modern translations of 1 Timothy 3:11 speak of women deacons, the King James Version has “Even so must their wives be grave, not slanderers, sober, faithful in all things.” In Greek mythology Phoebe was a Titaness, grandmother of Apollo and Artemis; the name means bright. See Phoebe, The Bible, Women, Gender, and Marriage, and Greek Mythology.

“cut down in the flower of their youth”: “He cometh forth like a flower, and is cut down: he fleeth also as a shadow, and continueth not” (Job 14:2, KJV). See The Bible and Death and Mortality. Earlier in Chapters 10, 31 and 61.

“she might endure the birth of nothing”: “We have been with child, we have been in pain, we have as it were brought forth wind” (Isaiah 26:18, KJV). See The Bible and Pregnancy, Abortion, and Midwifery.
