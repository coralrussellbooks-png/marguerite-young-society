Definition from research

The book closes on a run of psalms. Esther "lifts her eyes to the hills from whence cometh help" (Psalm 121), calls God "my strong rock and my castle" (Psalm 31), and sings of one who has "numbered the hairs of my head" (Matthew 10) and "closed the wound of my side" (John 19).

She sings "of the Rugged Cross," from the old hymn, and of waters that have drowned us, "the stream has gone over our soul" (Psalm 124), the last images of a soul going under.

The final Christian note is the stone-deaf man's "My God Who art my enchantment, we be in the wrong hotel," a God who is pure enchantment and a life that has wandered into the wrong place, the book ending on misplacement and wonder together.
