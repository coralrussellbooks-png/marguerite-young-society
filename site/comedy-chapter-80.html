Characters: Esther Longtree · Walter

Definition from research

Here the comedy is loud and physical, then goes suddenly cold. Esther's braying laugh, "Hee, haw, my darling," rattles the silverware and shakes the coffee cups. Her humor is called "convulsive," her face turning black as she laughs. Old Doctor supplies the chapter's best absurd flourish, warning that if she does not hurry she will give birth to a full-grown little man "wearing a bowler hat and smoking a black cigar," walking out of her womb "swinging a bright cane," the cigar lit from the coal burning inside her.

The comic bravado is a mask. This is the chapter that reveals Walter, the living, accusing first child, and the mule laughter keeps breaking against real horror. Young lets the two coexist rather than resolve, so the reader is never allowed to settle into either. The verbal comedy of inversion runs through it too, her habit of asking "where is black white, where is white black, where is the dead child living, where is the live child dead," which reads as riddling nonsense and as a genuine map of her broken mind at once.
