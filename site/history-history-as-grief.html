Chapters discussed: 49, 63

Definition from research

Chapter 49 contains the most moving historical fact in the book. "Whenever he thought of the death of an individual, he wept for the death of the last passenger pigeon," Martha, who died at the Cincinnati zoo in 1914 (Young gives 1918), "dying of dying, dying as the leaf falls." Mr. Spitzer arrives too late with his white rose.

Extinction is the one historical event that cannot be revised, reversed, or dreamed back. Young pairs it with Miss MacIntosh's Kirtland's warbler, almost gone, so that the book's history lessons keep circling loss that stays lost. Mr. Spitzer does not heal from Martha. He carries her into every other grief. That matches "Grief Doesn't End" closely: grief as permanent weather rather than a stage. Miss MacIntosh voices the other side in Chapter 63: "How shall I show my grief? Shall I tear my hair?" For a bald woman the conventional gesture is impossible. Performed mourning fails her, and what remains is staying with the loss, the stance the reference calls "Witnessing Is The Core Ethical Act."
