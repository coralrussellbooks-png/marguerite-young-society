8. Borrowed Crowns

Part of Intertextuality in Miss MacIntosh, My Darling, a study of Marguerite Young’s novel Miss MacIntosh, My Darling (1965).
By Coral Russell · Updated October 29, 2026

← Previous · Intertextuality in MMMD Home · Next →

Characters: Esther Longtree · Joachim Spitzer · Mr. Titus Bonebreaker · Mrs. Hogden · Vera Cartwheel

Definition from research

Several characters claim descent from greatness, and Young treats every claim with the same mixture of comedy and sympathy.

Mr. Bonebreaker's grandmother in the Ozarks told him "he was a true king, the king of almost any European country." Only "the bar sinister" kept him from "almost any throne he chose," and "it was all in the Almanach de Gotha and various other almanacs." He "could take the Hapsburgs by storm, sweep the Stuarts off their feet, achieve almost any miracle if he believed in America" (chapter 65). Mrs. Hogden, hired as a companion for Vera's mother, is "a descendant of Daniel Boone and William the Conquerer and Alexander Graham Bell" (chapter 70). Esther Longtree's mother was a "pallid descendant of George Washington's sister" (chapter 78). The two Cleopatras of Boston claim not descent but identity.

Mr. Spitzer's shelves hold the satire of all this in a single invented title, "Burke's Peerage in the Penguin World" (chapter 55). The reference book of British nobility has been moved to the ice floes, where penguins stand in rows as formal as any peerage.

The pattern shows Young's view of American democracy and its borrowed crowns. A country without titles is full of people quietly claiming them. The claims pass through almanacs and grandmothers, not courts. The phrase in Bonebreaker's passage is exact: he could win any throne "if he believed in America." The crown and the republic are joined in one hopeful, impossible sentence. Young does not sneer at the claimants. Each borrowed crown covers a real deprivation: an illegitimate birth, a sterile marriage, a hired companion's small room. The borrowed grandeur is how poor or overlooked people keep some dignity.
