Characters: Miss MacIntosh · Vera Cartwheel

Definition from research

Vera’s first critical look at Miss MacIntosh is also the novel’s best inventory of her eccentricities. She bundles up in hot weather, carries her umbrella open indoors when it is not raining, and leaves the schoolroom windows open all winter, so that the snow drifts in, the pupils wear earmuffs, and ice crystals form on their hair. “Was this some more of her famous common sense,” Vera asks, “this certainty that she would freeze?”

Vera’s rebellion is comic in its own way, because it is still shaped by the routine it rebels against. She foresees the morning’s penance for having danced with nobody: boxing the old sandbag dummy, knocking down bowling pins, pitching horseshoes, learning everything Benjamin Franklin ever said about early to bed and a bird in the hand. Even her defiance is made of Miss MacIntosh’s exercises.
