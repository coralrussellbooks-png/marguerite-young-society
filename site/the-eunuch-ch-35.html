The eunuch (Ch 35)

On Chapter 35 of Marguerite Young’s novel Miss MacIntosh, My Darling (1965). Part of A Guide to Reading Miss MacIntosh, My Darling.
By Coral Russell · Updated October 29, 2026

From the reading guide, Chapter 35 · Research: Chapter 35

From the guide

The eunuch who became a father, dying for love and strangled in a desert courtyard with hens clucking over him: is one of Young’s most compressed images of the unexpected persistence of desire under apparently impossible conditions. Hannah tells it to Catherine as a story of “what even a poor eunuch might be capable of.” Young places it in a chapter about Hannah’s own buried capacity for love that she apparently could never name. The hens clucking over him are Young’s touch: the domestic fowl presiding over the body of the man who loved past the limits of his own nature.
