The Tale of the Shipwrecked Sailor

← Previous · Intertextuality in MMMD Home · Next →

Characters: Catherine Cartwheel · Esther Longtree

Allusions: Ancient Egypt

Definition from research

Category: optional. The Egyptian tale is not named, but its figure, the drowned Egyptian sailor, appears in three chapters, and its pattern returns in the toy salesman.

The Tale of the Shipwrecked Sailor survives on a papyrus from Egypt's Middle Kingdom, nearly four thousand years old. A sailor is the only survivor when his ship goes down in a storm. He is washed up on an island, where a great serpent tells him its own story of loss. A star fell and destroyed its whole family. The sailor returns home and lives to tell of it.

Young brings the figure into Catherine's bedroom as "a drowned Egyptian sailor, gold-turbaned" (chapter 2). His "hieroglyphic language" returns in chapter 40, and in chapter 54 "Some dead Egyptian sailors were unwinding their winding sheets."

The tale's pattern returns fully in Esther Longtree's toy salesman. The drowned were "the captain, the crew, the women, the children, the moon, the sun, the stars, the kittens." Of the salesman the novel says, "He had been saved. He had been the lone eye witness, he who had not wanted to live" (chapter 81). "He had sat safe in a rubber life boat, gently rocking in the vasty deep." Esther cannot forgive him for surviving and calls him "a murderer of women and of little children." Where the ancient sailor comes home to tell his tale, Young's survivor carries it as guilt. Even the falling star returns, among the things that drowned.
