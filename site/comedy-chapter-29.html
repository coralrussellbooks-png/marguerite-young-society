Characters: Joachim Spitzer

Allusions: Deafness and Sign Language

Definition from research

The deaf mute produces one of the novel's purest surreal jokes, the man who seems to have "a frog in his throat" so literally that his hoarse croaking is treated as a small living instrument. Mr. Spitzer's grave astonishment at this croaking, and the mute's "croaked" counsel about betting and about watching out for pickpockets, turn a disability into deadpan whimsy. The comedy is the seriousness with which Spitzer receives musical advice from a man whose only note is a frog's.
