Definition from research

Chapter 11

  • “Sir Walter Raleigh in his marvelous white satin suit embroidered with seed pearls”: Sir Walter Raleigh (c. 1552-1618) was an English statesman, soldier, spy, writer, poet, and explorer. He was famous at Elizabeth’s court for the pearls and jewels on his clothes, even his shoes.
  • Raleigh’s poetry is known as the plain style. His writing contains strong personal treatments of themes such as love, loss, beauty, and time. Most of his poems are short lyrics that were inspired by actual events. In poems Raleigh expresses a contempt-of-the-world attitude more characteristic of the Middle Ages than of the dawning era of humanistic optimism. But his lesser-known long poem, The Ocean’s Love to Cynthia, expresses a melancholy sense of history. The poem was written during his imprisonment in the Tower of London. Raleigh answered Christopher Marlowe’s “The Passionate Shepherd to His Love” with “The Nymph’s Reply to the Shepherd.” Raleigh gives an almost line-for-line refutation of Marlowe’s sentiments. Centuries later, William Carlos Williams added “Raleigh Was Right” (1940) to the conversation. It begins, “We cannot go to the country / for the country will bring us / no peace.”
  • “when he removed his white plumed hat, removed also his head”: Raleigh was beheaded at Westminster on October 29, 1618. Of the axe he said, “This is sharp medicine, but it is a physician for all diseases.” His widow kept his embalmed head for the rest of her life. The poem he wrote the night before begins, “Even such is Time, that takes in trust / Our youth, our joys, our all we have, / And pays us but with earth and dust.”
  • “his intimate name, Ocean or Water”: Elizabeth called him “Water.” Spenser named him “the Shepherd of the Ocean” in Colin Clouts Come Home Againe (1595), and Raleigh wrote The Ocean’s Love to Cynthia, with the Queen as the moon who rules the tides.
  • “a society of night devoted exclusively to atheistic propositions”: “The school of night” comes from Love’s Labour’s Lost (4.3): “Black is the badge of hell, / The hue of dungeons and the school of night.” The phrase became a name for Raleigh’s circle with Thomas Harriot and Christopher Marlowe, men accused of doubting Genesis and God. In 1594 an inquiry at Cerne Abbas examined charges of atheism against Raleigh’s circle.

Chapter 11 · Sir Walter Raleigh

  • Imagined with a white satin suit embroidered with pearls and gems.
  • His decapitation and request to be called “Ocean or Water” suggest themes of transformation, fluidity, and martyrdom or sacrifice.
  • Raleigh was beheaded in 1618.
  • Represents atheistic propositions and philosophical skepticism within the dream.
