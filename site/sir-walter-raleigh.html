Definition from research

Chapter 11

  • Sir Walter Raleigh was an English statesman, soldier, spy, writer, poet, and explorer.

Chapter 11

  • Raleigh's poetry is known as the plain style. His writing contains strong personal treatments of themes such as love, loss, beauty, and time. Most of his poems are short lyrics that were inspired by actual events. In poems Raleigh expresses a contempt of the world attitude more characteristic of the Middle Ages than of the dawning era of humanistic optimism. But his lesser-known long poem, The Ocean's Love to Cynthia, expresses a melancholy sense of history. The poem was written during his imprisonment in the Tower of London. Raleigh wrote a poetic response to Christopher Marlowe's, The Passionate Shepherd to His Love, entitled, The Nymph's Reply to the Shepherd. Raleigh gives an almost line-for-line refutation of Marlowe's sentiments. Years later, the 20th-century poet William Carlos Williams would add, Raleigh was Right, to the conversation. The first line from the poem, “We cannot go to the country, for the country will bring us, no peace…”
