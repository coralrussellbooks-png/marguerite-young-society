Arc 6: Miss MacIntosh's Last Month (Chapters 63–66)

← Mr. Spitzer's Grief · Story Arcs Home · The Tavern →

Overview

The novel returns to Vera and Miss MacIntosh. The wig comes off, and Miss MacIntosh tells her life: the childhood baldness, the orphans, Lorena, and Mr. Bonebreaker, who fled. Then the last lessons through a storm, and the September morning she walks into the sea.

Central figures: Vera Cartwheel · Miss MacIntosh · Joachim Spitzer · Mr. Titus Bonebreaker · Catherine Cartwheel · Moses Hunnecker

Chapter by chapter

Chapter 63

The novel returns to Vera and Miss MacIntosh. Vera confronts her about the hollow pretense between them. Miss MacIntosh takes off her wig and speaks more openly than ever about her past, her body, her loneliness, and her coming end. She tells Vera that "nobody knows anybody," and that she suffers especially when there are rainbows.

Chapter 64

Solomon the turtle is missing. Bald and unguarded, Miss MacIntosh tells Vera her life story: childhood baldness, wigs made of stolen horsehair, boys trying to hook her wig with fishing lines, a phrenologist who admired her skull, a Gaelic prince among her ancestors. As a matron of orphans she cared for a forger, who later wrote to her from Alcatraz, and for Lorena, a dancing girl she loved. Vera sees her as she cannot see herself and begins to compose an elegy. Their last month together begins.

Chapter 65

Miss MacIntosh tells Vera about her one romance, with Mr. Titus Bonebreaker, a street preacher from Missouri who proclaimed the end of the world. They were engaged, but when she showed him the truth of her body, he fled, leaving his umbrella behind. She kept it, and the luminous watch he gave her. She worked among the blind in Chicago, where her differences mattered less, and later stood alone on a boat full of honeymooners, resolved to forget him.

Chapter 66

Dressed for a long voyage in whaler's hat and sea boots, Miss MacIntosh teaches Vera through a storm. She fires questions about geography, finance, and the body, describes her imaginary raids on the Stock Exchange, which she calls a palace of evil, and her Arctic explorer, and speaks of her removed ovaries. Then, one cold September morning, she walks into the sea. Her clothes are found on the beach and her red wig is torn in the sand, while old Friday, the sheep dog, barks at the waves.
