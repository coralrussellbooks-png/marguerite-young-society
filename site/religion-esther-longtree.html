Definition from research

Esther's faith is bound up with grief. She has had her share of religious teaching, but her repeated pregnancies and stillborn children have worn her belief down to doubt and fear. Her own mother was devout and cold, and that distance left its mark. Esther's faith is called "bigger than herself," yet she keeps running up against God's silence, the sense that no help is coming.

Biblical images crowd her story, the womb, the veil, the stillborn, birth, but they bring her little comfort. She is lonely in her faith as in everything else, a figure set among empty churches and bleak shorelines, wrestling alone. And yet she is practical about it. She takes the harshness of life and the certainty of death as given, folding even her losses into the natural order. Her belief mixes Christian teaching with folk feeling and something older, a personal faith that never hardens into doctrine. She is tragic but not broken, still holding on at the edge of what she can bear.
