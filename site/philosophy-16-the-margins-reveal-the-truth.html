Chapter 16. The Margins Reveal the Truth

← Previous · Philosophy of MMMD Home · Next →

See also: Pillar 7 The Margins Reveal the Truth

Characters: Esther Longtree · Joachim Spitzer · The Stone Deaf Man

Allusions: Deafness and Sign Language

Chapters discussed: 61, 62, 82

Definition from research

The people who do not fit the categories see the categories most clearly. The margins are where the seams show.

The novel is full of people at the edges: the deaf, the mute, the addicted, the unmarried, the poor, the half-mad. Young gives them the clearest sight in the book.

Mr. Spitzer states the principle in musical terms. “The man kicked out of an orchestra knew more about music than any playing in an orchestra, for the player was under the powers of the despotic conductor and could play only his own part” (ch. 62). The one inside the system hears only his part. The one outside it hears the whole.

The frog musician, a derelict who haunts Mr. Spitzer’s city, speaks only in scraps. He asks for a light, and “this simple question” seems to Mr. Spitzer “the real miracle” (ch. 61). The mute who stands with Mr. Spitzer in the rain says nothing at all. Esther Longtree cannot spell. The stone deaf man cannot hear. And in the last chapter it is an old Black woman on a country road, a figure at the far margin of the society the novel describes, who gives the deaf boy the words that could stand as the book’s creed: “life is imaginary, and this be an imaginary world” (ch. 82).

In a novel whose memories begin in a New England mansion of inherited wealth and whose present ends among the poor of the American interior, the movement itself is an argument. The truth is not at the center, where the systems are built. It is at the edges, where they fail to reach.
