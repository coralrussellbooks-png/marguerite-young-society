Part Seven: Practices

← Part Six: The Cross · Radical Immanence Collection · Part Eight: The Crossing Point, Seven Days →

These practices are extensions of the Here Is Enough Monday Mirror and the short meditations. They work the vertical axis rather than the horizontal. They can be used alongside the Here Is Enough practices or separately. There is no required order.

The Sediment Inventory

Once, when you have time and quiet, sit and write down everything you know about what was deposited in you before you had a choice about it. The language. The grief. The particular shapes of love and wound in your family and community. The historical injuries that are part of your formation. The gifts too. Write without editing. This is not a document for anyone else. It is an honest accounting for you.

You do not have to do anything with the inventory. You just have to make it. Seeing clearly is the practice. The seeing changes what you carry, even before any action follows.

The Ancestor You Are Most Reluctant to Claim

Think of someone in your line, biological or cultural, whose deposit in you you are most reluctant to acknowledge. Someone whose harm or limitation or complicity you would prefer not to be made of. Sit with them for a few minutes. Not to forgive them or excuse them. To acknowledge that you are, in part, built from them. That their deposit is in you. That knowing that makes you more honest than not knowing it.

The ancestors you are most reluctant to claim are often carrying information you most need. Their presence in your sediment does not make you them. But denying it makes you less able to see clearly what you are working with.

Here Is Enough.

The Deposit You Are Making Right Now

Think of someone in your life who is receiving something from you, a child, a partner, a student, a friend, someone who is being formed in part by their proximity to you. Ask honestly: what am I depositing in them right now? Not what do I intend to deposit. What is actually in motion between us. What are they receiving from me that I may not have chosen to give?

This question is not an invitation to shame. It is an invitation to honest attention. If you see something you would prefer not to be depositing, you can adjust. But you can only adjust what you can see.

Here Is Enough.

The Seven Generations Question

Once a week, pick one decision you are facing and ask the seven generations question: not what will the consequences of this be, but what am I depositing in the sediment. What kind of ground am I helping to form. Let the question sit. Do not rush to an answer. The value is in the orientation shift, in the felt sense of being on the vertical axis rather than only inside the present moment.

This does not have to be a large decision. The seven generations question can be asked of small things: how you speak to someone when you are tired, what you choose to name and what you leave unnamed, how you carry your grief. Every deposit matters. None requires perfection.

Here Is Enough.

Sitting at the Crossing Point

This is a short practice, five minutes or less. Sit still. Feel the weight of your body pressing down, which is the Here Is Enough body practice. Then extend that feeling downward, into the ground beneath you, into the sediment, into everything that made this moment possible. Let the weight of what is below be felt, however briefly. Then extend your attention upward along the same line, into the future you are forming, into the not-yet-born who will stand on what you are depositing. Then return to the center. The horizontal axis, the present moment, your body, this breath. You are at the crossing point. You have always been at the crossing point. Here is enough. Here is also enormous.

You do not have to hold all of this simultaneously for more than a moment. The practice is the movement: down, up, center. Down into what you received. Up into what you are forming. Center into what is here. The crossing point is always available.

Here Is Enough.
