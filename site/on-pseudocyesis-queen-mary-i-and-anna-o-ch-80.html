On Pseudocyesis: Queen Mary I and Anna O

To All My Darlings notes two famous historical cases of pseudocyesis. Queen Mary I of England experienced a well-documented false pregnancy in 1554–1555: she showed physical symptoms, her court prepared for a birth, announcement letters were drafted with blanks for the date and sex of the child. No child came. The experience was devastating to Mary personally and politically. Anna O, the name given to Bertha Pappenheim in Josef Breuer’s case history later published by Freud, experienced what was described as a false pregnancy; she believed she was in labor with her therapist’s child. Pappenheim went on to become a pioneering feminist and social activist; the case that made her famous in psychoanalytic literature reduced her to a symptom. Both cases illuminate the way in which women’s reproductive experiences, real or imagined, have been defined by the medical and legal systems surrounding them, and both cast a shadow over Esther’s everlasting pregnancy.
