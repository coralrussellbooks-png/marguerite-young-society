On the red rose and white rose.

The red and white roses carry the weight of an entire cultural history: the Wars of the Roses, in which the House of Lancaster (red) and the House of York (white) fought for the English throne through the second half of the fifteenth century; the Valentine tradition of the red rose for passionate love; the funerary tradition of white roses for mourning, purity, and the passage beyond. Young assigns the red rose to Peron at his grave (the last recognition, the vitality acknowledged) and the white rose to Joachim himself, marking his own passage into whatever state he inhabits beyond the ordinary living. He is the mourner who is also the mourned. He wears the white rose for himself.
