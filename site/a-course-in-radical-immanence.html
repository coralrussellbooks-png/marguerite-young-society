Radical Immanence: The Eleven Pillars

How Each Lesson Is Built

Every lesson follows the same frame so readers learn to trust the rhythm.

  1. The hook, a single claim that unsettles a common assumption.

  2. The framework, where Young's fiction meets the pillar's ancestor thinker.

  3. The art companion, a poem, painting, piece of music, or film that holds the idea in another form.

  4. Reflection questions, meant for a journal or a conversation.

  5. The exercise, something concrete a reader can do with a pen, a walk, or a conversation.

  6. The signpost, a closing line that hands the reader to the next lesson's pillar.

The Deleuze and Young mapping sits inside Lesson 10, where the plane of immanence meets Young's immanent transcendence point by point.
