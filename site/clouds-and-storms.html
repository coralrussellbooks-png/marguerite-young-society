Definition from research

Chapter 5 · Stormy Petrels, Woodpeckers, Red-winged Blackbirds

  • Represent chaos, disturbance, and the persistence of nature amid decay.

Chapter 37 · Fog and Clouds

  • These represent uncertainty, the unknown, and the boundary between states of being.

Chapter 38 · Clouds, Whirlwinds, and Stars

  • Reflect the fluidity of time, memory, and identity; also Catherine’s dreamlike mental state.

Chapter 40 · Clouds, Stars, and Celestial Imagery

  • Frequently used to evoke dreamlike states, mystery, and the vastness of time and space.

Chapter 43 · Storm, Snow, Desert, Mountains

  • Represent the suffragette’s internal struggle and the vast unknown of death.

Chapter 43 · Skirt of Clouds and Waters

  • Symbolizes beauty, identity, and the suffragette’s fading presence.

Chapter 44 · Mountains, Clouds, Icebergs

  • Evoke the harshness and isolation of her inner world and struggles.
  • The iceberg imagery suggests overwhelming, unseen dangers and emotional coldness.

Chapter 44 · The Whirlwind and Darkness

  • Represent confusion, chaos, and the unknowability of the human heart.

Chapter 45 · Clouds and Snow

  • Ambiguity, transition, death, and the veil between life and afterlife.

Chapter 56 · Clouds, Sea, and Tides

  • Recurring natural motifs representing impermanence, change, and the fluid boundary between life and death.

Chapter 59 · Cloud, Tree, Moon

  • Recurring images representing the transient, mutable, and interconnected nature of existence.
  • Clouds suggest the ephemeral and formless; trees denote rootedness yet vulnerability; the moon symbolizes mystery and change.

Chapter 66 · The Hurricane Lamp

  • Represents fragile knowledge and guidance amid darkness and storms.

Chapter 79 · Nature (Clouds, Stars, Flowers)

  • Contrasts with the harshness of Esther’s birth, representing purity, innocence, and the natural cycle of life.
  • The “clouds filled with holes” evoke fragility and vulnerability.

Chapter 81 · The Storm at Sea

  • A metaphor for chaos, destruction, and the fragility of life.
