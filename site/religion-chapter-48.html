Chapter 48

On Chapter 48 of Marguerite Young’s novel Miss MacIntosh, My Darling (1965). Part of Christianity and Religion in Miss MacIntosh, My Darling.
By Coral Russell · Updated November 24, 2026

No Christian references appear in this chapter.

“had gone through the door of the sun”: In the Upanishads the wise “depart free from passion through the sun to where that immortal Person dwells whose nature is imperishable” (Mundaka Upanishad 1.2.11, trans. F. Max Müller, 1884). The Sanskrit is sūrya-dvāreṇa, “by the door of the sun.” Those who return to rebirth go by the path of the moon (Chandogya Upanishad 5.10). Peron goes by the sun, Mr. Spitzer by the moon. See Hinduism.

“through clouds of eye witnesses”: “Wherefore seeing we also are compassed about with so great a cloud of witnesses, let us lay aside every weight” (Hebrews 12:1). See The Bible.

“drawing all things unto themselves”: “And I, if I be lifted up from the earth, will draw all men unto me” (John 12:32). See The Bible.

“The mother of creation was this great abyss”: “Verily at the first Chaos came to be” (Hesiod, Theogony 116, trans. Hugh G. Evelyn-White, 1914), and “darkness was upon the face of the deep” (Genesis 1:2). See Hesiod’s Night in the Chapter 47 resources. See Greek Mythology and Genesis. Earlier in Chapters 1, 12, 15, 20, 37 and 47.

“certainly not by those who were the living, for they were dead”: “Follow me; and let the dead bury their dead” (Matthew 8:22). See The Bible. Earlier in Chapter 3.

“He had gone down in darkness and in whirlwind”: The reverse of Elijah’s ascent: “and Elijah went up by a whirlwind into heaven” (2 Kings 2:11). See The Bible.

“His star had fallen, never again to rise”: “How art thou fallen from heaven, O Lucifer, son of the morning!” (Isaiah 14:12). Lucifer, Latin for “light-bearer,” is the morning star, the planet Venus, which the Greeks called Phosphorus and which heralds the dawn. The verse was addressed to the king of Babylon, but Christian tradition, reading it in Jerome’s Latin, made Lucifer a name for Satan, the angel who fell from heaven. See The Bible and Angels, Devils, and Myth. Earlier in Chapters 10 and 39.

“the old lamplighter with his trembling ladder of dreams”: “And he dreamed, and behold a ladder set up on the earth, and the top of it reached to heaven: and behold the angels of God ascending and descending on it” (Genesis 28:12). See Genesis. Earlier in Chapters 14, 29, 36 and 38.

“Perhaps he was the stowaway”: “But Jonah was gone down into the sides of the ship; and he lay, and was fast asleep” (Jonah 1:5). See The Bible.

“only by losing life did one win life”: “For whosoever will save his life shall lose it: and whosoever will lose his life for my sake shall find it” (Matthew 16:25). See The Bible. Earlier in Chapter 45.

“Perhaps he was translated”: “By faith Enoch was translated that he should not see death” (Hebrews 11:5); to be translated is to be carried to heaven without dying. “Bless thee, Bottom, bless thee! thou art translated” (A Midsummer Night’s Dream 3.1). See The Bible and Shakespeare.
