The Sarum rite (Ch 10)

From the reading guide, Chapter 10 · Research: Chapter 10

From the guide

The Sarum rite was the liturgical practice of the Diocese of Salisbury in medieval England, and it became the dominant form of worship in England before the Reformation. It is the source of many phrases in the modern Anglican and Protestant wedding ceremony including “to have and to hold, from this day forward, for better for worse, for richer for poorer, in sickness and in health, till death do us part.” When Young’s preacher speaks these words, she is reaching back to the oldest layer of the ceremony in English, the one closest to the original Latin, the one written before the Reformation changed everything. She is marrying her characters in a rite that no one has used for five hundred years.
