Characters: Esther Longtree

Allusions: The Bible

Esther means to teach the toy salesman to "read the Bible," Scripture as the mark of a proper life, with a glancing echo of Bathsheba behind it.

“had been in Bathsheba selling earrings”: Bathsheba is a woman, not a place; he means Beersheba, the desert town of the Negev, where Abraham dug his well (Genesis 21:31). See Genesis. Bathsheba was an Israelite queen consort. According to the Hebrew Bible, she was the wife of Uriah the Hittite and later of David, with whom she had all of her five children. Her status as the mother of Solomon, who succeeded David as monarch, made her the Gebirah of the Kingdom of Israel.

“had been in the Holy Land selling holy water”: The Holy Land is a sacred region in the Southern Levant, primarily located within modern-day Israel and Palestine. It is revered by Jews, Christians, and Muslims as the setting for key biblical events, including the life, ministry, and death of Jesus Christ, as well as the birthplace of the Jewish people.

“the whole world turned into salt”: “But his wife looked back from behind him, and she became a pillar of salt” (Genesis 19:26, KJV). See Genesis. This could be a reference to the atomic bomb which was dropped on Japan during WWII. The whole human race “turned into smoke” may also call up the crematoria of the death camps, where six million Jews were murdered. See Judaism. Earlier in Chapter 18.

“Saturn stood still in the glassy heavens”: “Sun, stand thou still upon Gibeon; and thou, Moon, in the valley of Ajalon” (Joshua 10:12, KJV). See The Bible. Earlier in Chapter 12.

“with antiphon between the antipodes”: An antiphon is a short, often scriptural chant or verse sung as a refrain in Christian liturgy, particularly within Catholic Mass (Entrance, Offertory, Communion) and the Liturgy of the Hours (Lauds, Vespers). It acts as a thematic focal point for a specific day or season, usually accompanying psalms or canticles. Historically rooted in early Christian worship and favored by St. Ambrose, they were originally performed in a call-and-response style between two choirs. Antipodes are points on Earth diametrically opposite to one another, representing the maximum distance apart on the globe. Most landmasses have their opposite point in the ocean, as 70% of Earth is water. The US mainland aligns with the Indian Ocean, Australia with the North Atlantic, and New Zealand with Spain/Portugal.

“a victim of the Jews”: May refer to Arthur Miller’s play Death of a Salesman. The play features a character, Willy Loman, who is a failing salesman. While Arthur Miller was Jewish, the play itself is often considered a universal American tragedy about the failure of the American Dream, and Willy’s Jewish identity is a long-debated topic among literary critics. Some productions and analyses have framed Willy as a Jewish character whose assimilation makes him vulnerable to societal pressures. The rubber hose that Willy hides by the gas heater, planning suicide, is a key symbol in the play; in the end he dies in his car. This is also a representation of the character’s antisemitism, prejudiced and paranoid worldview within the fictional narrative. The play opened in 1949. The concept of a “Jewish conspiracy” is an antisemitic myth with roots in medieval Europe that became the foundation for modern political antisemitism. These conspiracy theories falsely allege that Jewish people secretly manipulate institutions, banks, the media, and governments for malevolent purposes, such as achieving world domination. It is a dangerous and debunked fiction used throughout history to justify discrimination, persecution, and violence against Jews. In the Middle Ages, hostility toward Jews was often rooted in religious prejudice and false accusations, which served as a basis for later conspiracy myths. The antisemitic myth of blood libel emerged in the 12th century with the story of William of Norwich. It falsely claimed that Jews ritually murdered Christian children to use their blood for religious rituals, particularly for Passover matzah. This accusation frequently led to violent massacres and expulsions of Jewish communities. During the Black Death in the mid-14th century, Jews were used as scapegoats and falsely accused of poisoning wells to cause the plague. This led to the destruction of hundreds of Jewish communities across Europe. Due to restrictions that prevented Jews from owning land or joining certain trades, many worked as moneylenders. This created resentment and gave rise to stereotypes of Jews as greedy and obsessed with money, which later fueled secular conspiracy theories about Jewish wealth and control. In the 19th century, antisemitic ideas evolved from religious prejudice into modern political and racial theories of antisemitism, giving rise to more elaborate conspiracy narratives. German novelist Hermann Goedsche published Biarritz, which featured a fictional scene of a secret rabbinical cabal meeting in a Jewish cemetery to discuss a plot for world domination. This work was a commercial success and was later plagiarized to create The Protocols of the Elders of Zion (circa 1903), this fabricated document is the most infamous antisemitic text. It was a forgery created by the Russian secret police to stir up antisemitism and deflect from public discontent with the Tsarist regime. The Protocols claim to be the minutes of secret meetings held by Jewish leaders outlining a plan to take over the world. Though repeatedly exposed as a hoax, it continues to circulate and influence conspiracy theories to this day. Antisemitic conspiracy myths played a central role in the rise of Nazism and the Holocaust. Adolf Hitler and the Nazi Party adopted the Protocols as proof of a “Jewish world conspiracy.” They used it to justify their persecution and extermination of six million Jews. They falsely claimed that Jews were behind communism, capitalism, and Germany’s loss in World War I. The American industrialist Henry Ford distributed massive quantities of antisemitic material, including articles based on the Protocols, through his newspaper, The Dearborn Independent. He later retracted and apologized for his publications following a lawsuit, but the damage was already done. Following the Russian Revolution, an antisemitic conspiracy theory known as “Jewish Bolshevism” or Żydokomuna claimed that Jews controlled the Soviet Union and international communist movements. This false notion was promoted by anti-communist factions and heavily used by Nazi propaganda. In the modern era, antisemitic conspiracy theories have adapted to contemporary fears and platforms, continuing to spread hatred. “ZOG” theory (Zionist Occupation Government) theory, promoted by some white nationalists, claims that Jews secretly control Western governments. “Great Replacement” theory is a white nationalist conspiracy theory, which has influenced mass shootings, and claims that Jews are orchestrating a demographic “replacement” of white people through immigration. At the 2017 Charlottesville rally, participants chanted “Jews will not replace us.” The internet and social media have facilitated the widespread dissemination of antisemitic conspiracy theories, with groups like QAnon promoting versions of the myth. These online forums and platforms amplify old lies and connect them to new global events, continuing the cycle of prejudice and misinformation. Antisemitism remains part of American life. On October 27, 2018, a gunman who blamed Jews for immigration killed eleven worshipers at the Tree of Life synagogue in Pittsburgh, the deadliest attack on Jews in American history; another killed a worshiper at the Chabad of Poway synagogue in California in 2019, and in 2022 a gunman held a rabbi and three congregants hostage in Colleyville, Texas. The Anti-Defamation League, which has tracked incidents since 1979, counted a record 9,354 antisemitic incidents in 2024 and 6,274 in 2025, when assaults rose to 203. That year two staff members of the Israeli embassy were shot dead outside the Capital Jewish Museum in Washington, D.C. (May 21, 2025); a firebombing in Boulder, Colorado, killed an 82-year-old woman; and an arsonist set fire to Pennsylvania Governor Josh Shapiro’s residence on the first night of Passover. The FBI counted 1,528 anti-Jewish hate crimes in 2025, 63 percent of all religion-based hate crimes, though Jews are about two percent of the population. Like many forms of prejudice and bigotry in the United States, antisemitism is still being reckoned with. Flag: after 1964, the events from 2017 to 2025. See Judaism. The history of the Jewish rubber business is marked by individual entrepreneurial success, particularly in products like condoms, alongside the forced seizure of Jewish-owned businesses during the Nazi era. A prominent example is Julius Fromm, a Polish-German chemist who developed a way of making seamless rubber condoms and founded a successful rubber products company, only to have his factory stolen by the Nazis before he fled to England in 1939.

“a voice saying to jump from the high places”: “If thou be the Son of God, cast thyself down: for it is written, He shall give his angels charge concerning thee” (Matthew 4:6, KJV), the devil’s temptation on the pinnacle of the temple. See The Bible.
