Tap a dot to read what the book is, how it meets Young, and which books it connects to on the map.

The threads are the five Young discovered and the lineage after her inherited, as traced in the series What Marguerite Young Built, with a sixth, the con, from America, The Failed Utopia, and a seventh, the sacred here, from Marguerite Young and the philosophical history of Radical Immanence.

    Not her sources, by her own account

    Some names readers expect are missing on purpose. Young knew Gertrude Stein and liked her, but felt “not the slightest empathy with her as a writer.” She admired T. S. Eliot but resented readers who heard him in her work; she went to his sources instead. She told Ellen G. Friedman and Miriam Fuchs that Joyce did not influence her, though in 1983 she said she related herself more to Sterne and Joyce than to Henry James. She rejected the comparison to Virginia Woolf, though Miss MacIntosh’s walk into the sea draws partly on Woolf’s death. When she was compared to Proust, Faulkner, and Thomas Wolfe, she pointed to Jung instead. See Young’s Interviews Analyzed.

    Young’s reading comes from her own interviews, gathered in Young’s Interviews Analyzed. Her circle comes from Susan Neville’s profile, the Ruas interview, and the Society’s home page. The American books come from The Dream That Goes All the Way Down and Seven Essential Books for America. The philosophy comes from Marguerite Young and the philosophical history of Radical Immanence and the Society’s concept pages, with every quotation from the novel checked against its text. The lineage comes from What Marguerite Young Built and The Lineage.
