Introduction to the 2nd edition

Part of Teaching a Writing Masterclass, a study of Marguerite Young’s novel Miss MacIntosh, My Darling (1965).
By Coral Russell · Updated October 29, 2026

Teaching a Writing Masterclass · The Wheel That Turns Around Nothing →

Marguerite Young was first and foremost a teacher. She taught High School English. She taught at the famous Iowa Writer’s Workshop. She taught at the New School in New York City. Her teaching career spanned decades and from the students who have shared reminiscences, she was a beloved teacher.

Young was asked in an interview if she would write a book about writing and her response is that she wouldn’t live long enough to do so.

However, we have her magnum opus, Miss MacIntosh, My Darling plus her poetry and two other novels. Miss MacIntosh, My Darling is unique in that it is much longer than her previous writings and once we dive into it just looking at what she did with the novel, there is a cornucopia of literary devices, character development, style and architecture to learn from and analyze.

My hope with this book is that the structure and writing knowledge from an overlooked author who was a master of her craft will get the attention that it deserves.

To All My Darlings is the definitive exploration of Marguerite Young's magnum opus Miss MacIntosh, My Darling. Inside this massive volume you will find all the research and resources from 2019 to 2025. It is broken down chapter by chapter including: character list, synopsis, themes, Symbols and Imagery, Important Passages, and Resources.

A Guide to Reading Miss MacIntosh, My Darling is a condensed version of To All My Darlings where each chapter entry covers the characters, what happens, the themes and symbols worth tracking, the passages most worth pausing on, and a section called "For the Curious" that opens outward into context and connection. You can read it alongside the novel, chapter by chapter, or come to it after a section when you need someone to say: yes, that happened, here is what I think it means.

The Wheel and The Spokes provides a chapter by chapter guide that argues what we have been missing is a complete philosophical system, embedded in the novel's form and enacted by its prose. I called it Radical Immanence. Radical Immanence is not merely a literary philosophy. The study shows Young to be a systematic thinker whose work speaks directly to our most pressing questions about consciousness, identity, loss, and what it means to be constituted by what came before us while constituting the ground for what comes after.

What this 2nd edition does is correct and expand the material to reflect what this reader believes Young was doing within her novel.
