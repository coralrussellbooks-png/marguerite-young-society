Epilogue: The Wheel Holds

Part of Radical Immanence Collection, on Radical Immanence, the philosophy Coral Russell draws from the work of Marguerite Young (1908 to 1995).
By Coral Russell · Updated October 29, 2026

← Chapter Fourteen: Here Is Also Then · Radical Immanence Collection · A Note on the Eleven Pillars Together →

The Wheel of Fortune turns.

It has been turning for fifteen hundred years in the Western philosophical imagination, longer in the traditions that preceded it. It has been carrying its figures around the rim, rising and falling, reigning and losing the kingdom, moving through the four orientations of the full human life.

The standard reading says: the wheel is a warning. Don't cling to what the wheel can take. Fortune turns without regard for merit or desire. The wise person does not invest in what the wheel carries.

The eleventh pillar offers a different reading, the reading that has been waiting inside the image for the full two thousand years of its circulation.

The wheel is not a warning about the instability of fortune. It is a description of the structure of existence: the two axes and their intersection, the horizontal spread of accumulation and the vertical line of transmission, the crossing point that does not move while the wheel turns around it.

The figures on the rim are not four different people. They are the same person at four orientations: horizontal in the spread of power and accumulation, rotating toward the vertical in the descent, vertical in the stripped-down clarity of the crossing point, rotating back toward the horizontal in the ascent. The rotation is not punishment or reward. It is the description of what it is to be a full person across a full time: sometimes resting in the wide spread of the present moment, sometimes oriented along the long line of what was given and what will be required, always rotating around the same crossing point, which does not move, which holds, which is here.

The wheel holds because the crossing point holds. The crossing point holds because it is not on the rim. It is the axis. It is the intersection of the horizontal and the vertical, the place where the lateral accumulation meets the transmission line, the place where the alluvial self stands in the middle of the carrying.

Boethius found this in the cell. The medieval illuminators encoded it in the manuscripts. Marguerite Young reached toward it across 1,198 pages of recursive construction. The ten pillars were built on it without naming it. The eleventh pillar names it.

The wheel holds. The crossing point holds. Here holds.

The sentence is still going. The carrying continues. The not-yet-born are being formed by what is being deposited now. The ancestors are in the sediment. The wheel is turning around the crossing point, which is here, which is where you are, which has always been where you are.

Here is enough. Here is also enormous. Here is the crossing point.

The wheel holds.
