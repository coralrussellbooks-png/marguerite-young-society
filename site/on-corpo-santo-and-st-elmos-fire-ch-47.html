On corpo santo and St. Elmo's fire.

St. Elmo's fire (the luminous plasma that forms around pointed objects during electrical storms, visible on the tips of ship's masts and church steeples) was called corpo santo (holy body) by sailors, who sometimes took it as a good omen and sometimes as a warning. It is light without heat, present without being substantial, visible without being graspable. Young's use of this phenomenon in the chapter's atmosphere is precise: Cousin Hannah's presence in Mr. Spitzer's consciousness after her death has this quality, luminous, charged, at the tip of every pointed thought, neither entirely present nor entirely gone.
