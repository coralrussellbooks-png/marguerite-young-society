Definition from research

"like Saul or Solomon or Sennacherib in the midst of our corruption" names three fallen powers, Saul the first king undone, Solomon the wise man turned to idols, Sennacherib the proud Assyrian struck down, all figures of greatness gone to decay.

The "itinerary of the children of Israel from Egypt to Canaan," the stations Rameses, Succoth, Etham, and the rest, traces the Exodus route, the long wilderness march toward the promised land.

The questions about "the desert of El-Tih," and whether "some die... on their way from Egypt to Canaan, the blessed land," keep to that same journey and to the many who fell before reaching it.

A litany of images for God, "a fuller's soap," "a refining fire," "rivers of water in a dry place," "a hiding place from the wind," chains Malachi's purifying fire to Isaiah's water and shelter, God as cleanser, refuge, and relief.

A longer litany, "a friend that sticketh closer than a brother," "a root out of a dry ground," "a nail fastened to a sure place," "the shadow of a great rock in a weary land," strings biblical figures for God from Proverbs, Isaiah, and Samuel, loyalty and humble origin and firm footing and refuge, the divine turned over facet by facet.

"the plant not planted by God," "the strayed sheep," "the relapsing demoniac" gather three of Jesus's images, the false teaching uprooted (Matthew 15), the lost sheep sought and found (Luke 15), and the swept house reclaimed by worse spirits (Matthew 12).

"something which none should see and live to relate" reaches for Exodus 33, no one may see God and live, the vision too great to survive.

"baldness as of a babe cast into purgatory" ties Miss MacIntosh's bare head to the limbo of the unbaptized, her lifelong baldness read as a soul suspended, never held against the creature that bears it.
