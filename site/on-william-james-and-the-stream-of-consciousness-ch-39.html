On William James and the stream of consciousness.

James, in his Principles of Psychology, described consciousness not as a series of distinct states but as a continuous flow, a stream, in which past, present, and anticipated future blur together. He also wrote extensively about the self as multiple: we have as many social selves as there are individuals who recognize us, and as many possible selves as the imagination can furnish. Young absorbed this deeply. Catherine's refusal to choose a single self is Jamesian: to crystallize is to impoverish. The many fascinating false selves are not simply delusions; they are the breadth of a consciousness that has refused to narrow itself to fit what the world expects.
