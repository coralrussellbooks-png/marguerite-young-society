On panpsychism's history and its costs.

The philosophical position that consciousness is a fundamental feature of reality, not an emergent property of complex biological systems but a basic aspect of the universe, runs from Thales of Miletus through Spinoza, Leibniz, Schopenhauer, and into twentieth-century philosophy of mind. Its contemporary defenders include thinkers like David Chalmers and Galen Strawson. But Young is not giving Mr. Spitzer a philosophical position. She is giving him a condition: the inability to maintain the ordinary boundary between the animate and the inanimate. This is experienced not as intellectual discovery but as anguish, as the world refusing to stay inert, as consciousness leaking into everything. It is the burden of a sensitivity that has no off switch.
