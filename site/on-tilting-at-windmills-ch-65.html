On tilting at windmills.

Miss MacIntosh says she had been chasing Dutch windmills: a direct reference to Don Quixote, where the knight mistakes windmills for giants and charges them. Young has been in conversation with Cervantes throughout this novel: Vera's search for Miss MacIntosh echoes Quixote's quests, Moses Hunnecker the bus driver is described as a mad Quixote, and now Miss MacIntosh herself admits to her own windmill. The novel is full of people fighting what isn't there, longing for what won't come, attacking what cannot be attacked. Young does not mock them for it. Quixote, after all, is not a fool. He is the highest aspiration of human beings, reaching for something real even when the world insists the giants are only windmills.
