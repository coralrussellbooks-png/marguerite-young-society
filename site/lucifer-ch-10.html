Lucifer (Ch 10)

On Chapter 10 of Marguerite Young’s novel Miss MacIntosh, My Darling (1965). Part of A Guide to Reading Miss MacIntosh, My Darling.
By Coral Russell · Updated October 29, 2026

From the reading guide, Chapter 10 · Research: Chapter 10

From the guide

Lucifer, “who was brightest and fell farthest” the name means “light-bearer” in Latin, and was originally associated with the planet Venus as the morning star. It became associated with Satan through a passage in Isaiah about a fallen Babylonian king, and later through Milton’s Paradise Lost, where Lucifer is the brightest and most beautiful of the angels before his fall. Young’s preacher invokes him in the wedding litany not as a figure of evil but as a figure of extreme contrast from highest to lowest, brightest to darkest. It is the structure of tragedy. It is also, quietly, the structure of Miss MacIntosh’s story.
