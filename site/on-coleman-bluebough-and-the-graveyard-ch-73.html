On Coleman Bluebough and the Graveyard

The graveyard where Joe Goldberg encountered Esther is peopled with specific names: Coleman Bluebough, who died at age three, his small grave in what is now someone’s vegetable garden; Phoebe, the old washwoman, her statue with a bulbous nose and sunbonnet; Abigail Porter. Young gives the dead their names. The graveyard is not an abstraction; it is a specific community of the lost, each one remembered with an inscription, each one part of the local record. Esther’s stillborn children have no inscriptions, no graves in the official record. They are hidden in streams and snow. The graveyard holds the names of children who died but were claimed. Esther’s children are unclaimed by the record, present only in her memory and in the fragments she has buried herself. The graveyard is what the official version of death looks like. Esther’s losses are what death looks like when no one is watching.
