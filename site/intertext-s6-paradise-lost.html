Paradise Lost

See also: Paradise Lost

Characters: Catherine Cartwheel · Joachim Spitzer · Miss MacIntosh

Allusions: Angels and Archangels · Genesis · Milton

Definition from research

Category: optional. Milton and his poem are named once, and several of Milton's figures appear, but the novel does not rest on the poem's story.

John Milton's Paradise Lost, published in 1667, tells of Satan's rebellion and fall and of Adam and Eve's expulsion from Eden. Milton, blind by then, dictated the poem to helpers, among them his daughters.

Young remembers the poem through its author's injury: "blind Milton dictating Paradise Lost to those indifferent daughters" (chapter 2). Milton's figures scatter through the book. A marriage ceremony calls on "Lucifer who was the brightest and is fallen farthest" (chapter 10). Catherine's mind becomes "the medley, the imbroglio, Pandemonium, Saturnalia of her deranged imagination" (chapter 40), using the name Milton coined for the capital of Hell. Mr. Spitzer pictures a light "falling from the wing of the great archangel Uriel" (chapter 49), Milton's regent of the sun. Miss MacIntosh's lessons recall "Man's Fall which came from Eve's eating an apple of the tree of knowledge, and the apple was no sweet mackintosh" (chapter 9).

The great epic of the Fall enters the novel as a handful of names and one image of a blind man and his bored daughters. The essay "The Canon Through Its Wounds" in Section 5 places this among Young's other injured greats.
