Chapter Thirteen: The Ten Pillars and Daily Life

← Part Five: Living in the Architecture · Radical Immanence Collection · Chapter Fourteen: What Cheer: The Youngian Polity →

Philosophy that cannot be lived is philosophy that has failed the test Young's system sets for it. The recursive attempt, the fifth pillar, is not a theoretical commitment. It is a daily practice. The witness, the fourth pillar, is not a philosophical position. It is a way of being in a room.

The ten pillars describe a way of living as well as a way of thinking, and the two cannot be separated in Young's system, because the system is built on the recognition that the form of the thinking and the form of the living are the same thing.

Living the plural self means releasing the demand for consistency as the primary virtue. It means allowing the self that is present in this moment to be different from the self that was present in the last moment, without requiring that the difference be explained or resolved. It means holding the plurality with honesty rather than managing it into the appearance of unity. This is harder than it sounds in a culture that treats inconsistency as a moral failure. The person who has changed their mind is suspected of weakness. The person who holds contradictory positions is suspected of dishonesty. The person who contains multitudes is suspected of containing too much. Young's plural self says: the multitudes are not the problem. The demand that they be reduced to one is the problem.

Living the witness means staying in the room when the room becomes uncomfortable. It means maintaining the quality of attention that the thing in front of you deserves, even when the thing in front of you is contradictory or painful or resistant to the simplifications that would make it easier to process. The witness in daily life is the person who does not look away from the difficult conversation, who does not rush toward resolution when the contradiction becomes uncomfortable, who can hold the wig and the baldness simultaneously without requiring that one of them be more real than the other. This is an ethical practice, not a passive one. The witness is not the bystander. The witness is the one whose presence changes the quality of what is happening, whose attention creates the conditions for honest appearance, whose refusal to conclude keeps the space open for the full complexity of what is there.

Living recursive construction means building without needing the building to be finished. It means returning to the beginning with what you learned on the last pass, incorporating what the previous attempt revealed, building again with the honest acknowledgment that this attempt will also reveal what it didn't know. This is the practice of the honest life: not the life that has found its foundation and built on it, but the life that has stopped looking for a foundation and started building with what is actually available. The plural self. The situated knowledge. The permanent grief. The quality of attention the witness brings. The language that knows it is a construction. The community that holds difference without erasure. The sedimentary time that carries the past forward.

The unfinished that is the only honest form integrity can take.

You build with all of this. You return. You build again.
