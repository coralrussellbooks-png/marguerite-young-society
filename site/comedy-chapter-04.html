Characters: Catherine Cartwheel · Vera Cartwheel

Definition from research

The chapter is mostly grief, but Young lets comedy in at both ends of Vera’s departure. At home, Catherine treats her daughter’s collapse as a reason to prescribe opium, and she is not even serious about it: she twirls her white umbrella in her swan boat, calls to her polar bear to come out of the mirror, and sends for her hairdresser in the middle of the discussion. The comedy is in the mismatch of scale, a mother answering a catastrophe with stage business.

On the train, the fat, soft-faced lady with icicle earrings delivers a comic prophecy of the Middle West. Everyone there is old, everyone is deaf, and they carry ear trumpets they never turn on, “the reason being that they did not wish to hear.” Her warning piles up like a sermon, funeral wreaths on every door and every clock stopped, until it tips into exaggeration. It is also a joke the novel will cash in: the book ends with a stone deaf man.
