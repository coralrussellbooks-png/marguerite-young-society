Characters: Miss MacIntosh · Vera Cartwheel

The Questions

After the night that stripped her, Miss MacIntosh is back to “essential facts, bare and unvarnished.” Is the blueprint restored, or only re-sealed over what showed?

Vera feels “reality was missing” from the rote lessons. What is the plainness leaving out that Vera can now sense?

Miss MacIntosh questions the world endlessly, biblical facts, geography, the news. Is that curiosity, or the blueprint categorizing to keep chaos out?

The house is a “vacant-eyed facade.” Whose facade, and over what?

After the night that stripped her, Miss MacIntosh is back at her blueprint, and the reader now sees it for what it is. She wants “essential facts, bare and unvarnished,” she dismisses the “phantom” as “stuff and nonsense,” she calls for “a practical roofer to keep the rain out of the impractical house.” Before the baldness this looked like bedrock; now it looks like the surface re-sealing over what showed. The plainness is not the answer reasserted but the construction rebuilt, the wig back on, the daylight self reassembled the morning after it came apart.

And Vera can feel the difference, though she cannot yet name it. She moves through the chapter in a “nameless disappointment,” sensing that in the rote lessons and the repeated old voyages “reality was missing.” This is the education of the baldness night doing its slow work. Having seen behind the plain surface, Vera can no longer be fed by the plain surface; the sealed blueprint that once steadied her now reads as thin, and the thinness is what her disappointment is registering. She is outgrowing the answer she was given.

Miss MacIntosh’s endless questioning, the biblical facts, the geography, the obsessive reading of the news and the “confusion of tongues” in the world, is the blueprint at work. It looks like curiosity and it is really containment: she names and categorizes the unknown in order to keep it out, to fix the shifting world into countable facts she can stand on. The questioning is not open inquiry but the sealed construction extending its walls, taking in new territory only to file it.

Around them the built world shows hollow. The house is a “vacant-eyed facade,” an “old, empty hotel”; the ships are sunk, the racetracks abandoned, the past rising through the present in ruins. The facade is the construction seen from outside, kept up and empty, and Miss MacIntosh’s facts are the roofing she nails over it. The chapter sets her practical repairs against a world decaying faster than any roofer can mend, and the mismatch is the quiet argument: no amount of bare unvarnished fact seals a house whose walls are time.

What the Chapter Refuses, and the Footing It Offers

The chapter refuses the reading that takes Miss MacIntosh’s pragmatism as its steadying philosophy, hard work and plain fact as the answer to Vera’s drift. After the baldness, that reading is no longer available; the plainness has been shown built.

The footing is that the pragmatism reasserted here is the sealed blueprint rebuilt over the exposed vulnerability, and Vera’s “nameless disappointment” is her outgrowing it, sensing that “reality was missing” from the surface she once trusted. Miss MacIntosh’s endless questioning is containment dressed as curiosity, the construction extending its walls; the world it tries to fix is a facade decaying faster than fact can mend. The plainness is a construction, and Vera can now feel its thinness. Her disappointment stays nameless, unresolved, the sign of an education outpacing the words she has for it.

For Reflection

What answer that once steadied one now feels thin, and what changed to make it thin?

Is one’s own questioning of the world open inquiry, or containment, naming things to keep them out?

What facade has one kept nailing repairs onto while the walls behind it went on decaying?
