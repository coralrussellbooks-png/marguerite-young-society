Chapter Five. Build Anyway, Knowing It's Temporary: The End Is the Beginning Renamed

← Chapter Four. Witnessing Is The Core Ethical Act: The Awe Is · Be Bak in a Whale · A Closing Note. Where the Philosophy Meets the Person →

Pillar: Pillar 5 Build Anyway, Knowing It's Temporary · Across every book

Characters: Esther Longtree · Miss MacIntosh · Vera Cartwheel

The novel ends where it begins.

Not literally. Not in the way that circular narratives return to their opening sentences and close the loop. Young’s ending is not the same as her beginning. But the ending contains the beginning, the way the Earth contains everything it has been given: present, layered, transformed but not gone. Vera is still on the journey. Esther is going back into the whale. The sentence hasn’t ended. The question the novel opened with, what do you do when the solid ground turns out to be a construction, has not been answered so much as deepened, turned over, returned to from a different angle with everything the journey has taught, and found to contain more than it appeared to on the first pass.

Young does this again and again across 1,198 pages. The images return. The grief accumulates rather than resolving. The characters circle back through their own histories and find them changed by the circling. The end is the beginning renamed. The beginning is the end approached from the other direction. The novel moves the way memory moves and grief moves and the plural self moves: not forward in a line but recursively, each pass finding what the previous pass had walked past, each returning to the beginning discovering that the beginning contains more than it appeared to the first time through.

This is not a formal experiment for its own sake. It is Young enacting the fifth pillar in the structure of the novel itself, before the reader has the language to name it.

You build anyway.

⁂

The fifth pillar of Young’s philosophy is this: you build anyway. Knowing the building is provisional. Knowing the map is wrong before the ink dries. Knowing the finished structure will be less than you imagined and more than you expected and already in need of revision by the time it is complete. You build because the recursive attempt is the only form of integrity the system allows. Because building is what it means to keep going, and keeping going is what the witness does after it has stayed in the room long enough to understand what the room actually contains.

This is where Young’s philosophy becomes livable. The first four pillars are descriptions of a world that is genuinely difficult to inhabit: the self is plural and unstable, reality is constructed and contested, grief is permanent, and the only honest ethical stance requires a quality of attention that most of us cannot sustain for long without relief. It would be reasonable, having followed Young this far, to ask what exactly is being offered.

The fifth pillar is the answer. Not a consolation. Not a resolution. The answer: you build. You return to the beginning with what you learned on the last pass. You build again. The structure is always under construction and that is not its failure. That is its honesty. The recursive attempt is not the second-best version of a finished thing. It is the only form available to a self that is honest about its own condition.

⁂

I didn’t realize it until I wrote this book that my Substack is the personal recursive construction.

The early writings were spaghetti thrown at the wall. I knew I was doing it. I threw them anyway because the alternative was to wait until I understood enough to write with authority, and waiting would have meant not writing, and not writing would have meant not finding out what I understood. The throwing was the thinking. The wall was the test. What stuck was the kernel, and the kernel was the thing worth keeping even when everything around it was imperfect.

I thought about deleting the early work. The impulse is understandable because the early attempts are visibly imperfect, the thinking is incomplete, the language is reaching for something it hasn’t found yet. A reader coming to the Substack now would find the early posts and see the distance between where the work began and where it is.

I let it pile up.

Not because I couldn’t see the imperfections. Because the pile is the argument. The early attempts with three volumes of To All My Darlings and then revising that into a second edition that I thought would make it more manageable that instead expanded it with more information I had mined from multiple readings to taking volume three and expanding each of those into its own book and then exploding my Substack with multiple posts a day because there was so much I needed to get out of my head, just dump it, to try to clarify, until finally settling on a simpler, shorter (still 500 pages) of A Guide to Reading Miss MacIntosh, My Darling and then AND: The Philosophy of Marguerite Young then to this book are all part of the same construction, and the construction only makes sense as a construction, as a thing that is visibly being built rather than a thing that arrived complete. The early kernels are the foundation the current understanding stands on. To delete them would be to pretend the current understanding arrived without the foundation, which would be the same dishonesty Miss MacIntosh committed when she insisted the solid world had always been solid.

The pile is the sediment. The layers are the geological record of a thinking mind working through a problem that keeps revealing itself to be larger than the previous pass had accounted for. Nothing erased. Everything present. The early imperfections and the later precision and all the readings between them, layered, carrying each other forward the way the Earth carries everything it has been given.

⁂

The word recursive is precise and worth unpacking.

A recursive process is one that calls itself as part of its own operation. That uses its own output as part of its input. That loops through its own structure in order to proceed. The recursive story is the one that keeps returning to its own beginning, not to repeat it but to find something new in it on each return, to discover that the beginning contains more than it appeared to the first time through.

Miss MacIntosh, My Darling is a recursive novel. The wig appears and reappears. The grief accumulates rather than processing. Vera’s journey circles through itself. The novel does not move from beginning to middle to end in the linear sense. It moves the way Young’s mind moves, returning to what it cannot let go of, finding new layers on each return, deepening rather than resolving. The end is the beginning renamed because the journey has changed what the beginning means. Vera arriving at What Cheer, if she arrives, will not find the What Cheer she imagined when she set out. She will find what the journey has made of it, which is always more complicated and more honest than the destination imagined before the journey began.

This is what my five readings of the novel are. Five passes through the same recursive structure, each one finding what the previous pass had walked past, each one arriving at a different What Cheer than the last. The first reading produced drowning and the last line and the knowledge that I would come back. The second produced characters and the beginning of the philosophy. The third produced the wig sentence and Roe and the personal foundation collapsing. The fourth produced Jonah’s whale and Esther and the geological carrying. The fifth produced the system, the five pillars, the understanding that Young had built something complete whose completeness was not the destination but the building.

And there will be a sixth reading. There will always be a next pass. The novel keeps generating new surfaces because it was built that way, recursively, each layer placed with the knowledge that the next reader’s return would find something the current reader had walked past. The excitement is real because the discoveries are real.

⁂

Young spent eighteen years building the novel and she did not stop there. Harp Song for a Radical, the Debs biography, grew to 2,400 pages of manuscript at the time of her death. She was still building. Still returning to the beginning with what she had learned on the last pass. Still finding that the territory was larger than the previous map had accounted for.

She did not finish.

That is not a tragedy. It is the fifth pillar enacted at the scale of a life. The building that knows it is building. The map that knows it is wrong before the ink dries and draws itself anyway. Young did not stop when the novel was published because the novel was not the destination. It was the pass. The next pass was already beginning. The sentence had not ended. It had not ended because the world that made it necessary had not ended and the self that was writing it was not singular and stable and therefore finished but plural and ongoing and therefore always in the middle of the next return.

This is what Young means when she says the sentence hasn’t ended. Not that the novel is incomplete. That the recursive construction is the condition of honest work and honest work does not end when a particular structure is finished. It ends when the worker does, and even then the Earth carries what was built into the next pass made by the next witness who finds the thread and pulls it.

⁂

Maybe this book is the final touch.

Finally having the language to express the journey with this book. The reading guide that holds the reader’s hand through the novel’s considerable demands. The philosophy named and placed in conversation with the thinkers it anticipated and surpassed. And now this, the inside account, the record of five readings and what they built. The story of the reader who drowned the first time and came back and kept coming back and found in the coming back the only method adequate to what Young had built. The story of how long it takes to learn a language in order to express what was found and experienced while reading a book.

Maybe this is it.

Maybe there is still more.

Both of those sentences are true simultaneously and Young’s system is the only one I have encountered that does not require me to resolve them into one. The fifth pillar holds them together: the building that names what it has built and leaves the door open and puts it out there and trusts the carrying. Not as a failure to commit. As the honest acknowledgment that the territory keeps revealing itself to be larger than the current map, and the revelation is not a problem. It is the condition of genuine work.

I am perfectly willing to receive more from anyone who starts this journey.

That sentence is the fifth pillar stated as a personal practice. The building released into the carrying. The work is put out there for the next witness who arrives with different tools and a longer reach and finds in the pile the kernel that the current pass had not yet known what to do with. The door left open not from uncertainty but from the understanding that the room is larger than any single occupant, that the work is larger than any single life, and that what you build with full attention and release into the world’s keeping will be carried further than you can follow.

⁂

The five pillars build on each other in a specific direction.

The plural self is the ground: the honest account of what selves actually are, which dismantles the demand for the singular acceptable self and the threat that enforces it. Illusion as primary is the epistemological consequence: if the self is plural then reality is constructed, and if reality is constructed then the question of whose construction dominates is the central political question. Grief as permanent weather is what you live in when you have accepted the first two pillars honestly: the climate of a world where nothing holds still and everything you love is always already changing and the Earth carries what you cannot. The witness is the ethical response: the only honest stance available to a plural self living in constructed reality in the permanent weather of grief is to stay in the room and keep looking and not conclude. And recursive construction is what the witness does with what the room contains: builds anyway, knowing the building is provisional, returning to the beginning with what the last pass taught, finding that the beginning contains more than it appeared to, building again.

The fifth pillar is where Young’s philosophy becomes a life. Not a theory about life. The actual practice of living honestly inside the conditions the first four pillars describe.

Young built a novel across eighteen years. I have been building around that novel across years of reading and writing and returning. The Ohio series (currently in progress) are five fictional novels whose architecture grows directly from the five pillars. The reading guide, chapter by chapter through all eighty-two chapters. Young’s philosophy named and argued for the first time. And this book, the inside account, the record of the journey that the other books were preparing for without knowing it.

Each of those was a pass. Each one found something the previous pass had walked past. Each one is a layer in the sediment, early kernels and later architecture all present simultaneously, none of it deleted, none of it resolved into a cleaner version of itself, all of it the record of a mind working through a problem that keeps revealing itself to be larger than the previous accounting.

That is the recursive construction. That is Young’s fifth pillar enacted in a life rather than argued in a treatise.

⁂

Vera never arrives at What Cheer in any simple sense.

Or rather: what she arrives at is not what she was traveling toward, because the traveling changed what the destination means. What Cheer at the end of the journey is not What Cheer at the beginning. The place is the same. The self arriving at it is not. The journey has built a different reader for the destination, and the destination has to be read again now with the self the journey made.

That is what happens to every honest reader of this novel. You arrive at the last page a different reader than the one who opened the first page, and the difference means the first page needs to be read again, and the reading will produce something the first reading could not have found because the self doing the reading is not the same self. The end is the beginning renamed. The recursive construction has already begun before you know you are inside it.

Young built that into the novel deliberately. She built a novel you have to read more than once not because it is deliberately withholding on the first reading but because it is genuinely larger than any single reading can hold. The first reading gives you what you are ready for. The second gives you what the first prepared you to see. And so on. The novel is the recursive construction made into a reading experience: you keep returning to the beginning and finding it has more in it than it appeared to have the last time you were there.

⁂

The sentence hasn’t ended.

Young said this about her own work and she was right in the way she was right about everything: not as a metaphor for incompleteness but as a precise description of the condition of honest building in a world that does not resolve. The sentence hasn’t ended because the world that made it necessary hasn’t ended. The plural self is still being demanded to simplify. The constructed real is still being presented as the natural order. The grief is still the climate. The witness is still in the room. The building is still under construction.

This book is a pass. It is the pass that finally had the language for the journey, that could stand inside the novel and the philosophy and the personal history and the political present simultaneously and show how Young built the system that holds all of it. It is the pass that found Esther’s last line as the key the other passes had circled without entering. It is the pass that understood the baby rabbits and the unmarked grave and the crab and the geological carrying as Young’s own argument made visible in her own images.

It is not the last pass.

The door is open. The pile is visible. The early kernels and the later architecture are all present and available to the next witness who arrives with different tools and a longer reach. The work is in the world’s keeping now. The Earth has it. The carrying continues.

You build.

You return.

You build again.

The sentence hasn’t ended yet.
