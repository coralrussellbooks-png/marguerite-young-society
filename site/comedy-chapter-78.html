Definition from research

The comedy turns to family caricature, all of it grotesque and oversized. Esther has been called "Little Jackass" since birth for being "born kicking, stubborn, awkward." Her father, the passive policeman, is a masterpiece of comic understatement: so slow and half-asleep that when the bank robber comes, "he showed him the way to the bank." Her tiny mother is "a child with long grey curls" who lives in a house so small and breakable that "if you sighed, you broke something."

The standout comic image is the paternal grandmother, a woman so natural about childbirth that "babies spilled from her like the leaves from the trees," often without her noticing. She steps out of a room mid-visit and returns "naked with a live new baby in her arms." Against this fertile comedy sits the chapter's dark engine, Esther's belief that she murdered her twin Rosemary in the womb by kicking, so that she stands "guilty of infanticide in her mother's womb." The size mismatch of the parents, the physical comedy of a giant daughter and a doll-sized mother, keeps the register absurd even as the accusation is monstrous.
