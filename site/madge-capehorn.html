Chapter 5

Listed as: Madge and Homer Capehorn

Chapter 5

Listed as: Madge

  • Madge is a figure of tragic endurance and profound emotional pain, trapped in a loveless and alienating marriage with Homer, who is depicted as passive, innocent, and disconnected. Their relationship embodies the novel’s exploration of despair, loss, and the elusive nature of human connection, with Madge’s pregnancy serving as a potent symbol of both life and death, hope and despair

Chapter 6

Listed as: Madge Capehorn (Edwards)

  • Central figure, struggling with her pregnancy, marriage, and identity.
  • She is haunted by memories and fears, feeling trapped and betrayed.
  • Experiences deep psychological conflict, torn between her past self and her present reality.
  • Symbolizes fragility, loss of innocence, and the burden of unwanted responsibility.
  • Her sleep is restless, and she cries in her dreams, revealing her subconscious anguish.
  • She is both alive and metaphorically dead, caught between states.

Chapter 7

  • A woman who provides a dark, detailed account of local tragedies and suspicions.
  • Reveals complex family histories involving death, murder, and social decay.
  • Portrays a harsh view of morality and survival in a tough town, accusing Gertrude of murder and describing Musidora’s tragic life.

Chapter 8

  • A female character who comments on a woman named Beatrice Fitzgerald.
  • Madge is outspoken and somewhat judgmental, suggesting that Beatrice tries to hide in darkness because of her ugliness and reputation.
  • Madge’s perspective contrasts with Homer’s more sympathetic view.

Chapter 23

  • “The heart, my dear, was locked from the inside”: Madge and Homer argued about Josh being locked in the closet and dying at the beginning of the book (Chapter 7). Old Josh, the barkeeper, died in a coat closet that Madge insisted “was locked from the outside.” Miss MacIntosh’s heart is locked from the inside.

Chapter 67

Listed as: Madge

  • Laughs breathlessly at Moses's misery, indicating a detachment or acceptance of the surreal situation.
