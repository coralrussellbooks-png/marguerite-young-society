On mortmain.

The legal term appears in Mr. Spitzer's will, and it deserves its moment. From the French mort (dead) and main (hand), mortmain originally described land held in perpetuity by a church or institution: a dead hand continuing to control the living world. In medieval England, statutes were passed specifically to limit this practice, because the dead were accumulating too much property. Mr. Spitzer's bequest is mortmain in reverse: a living man giving his inheritance away in advance, the dead hand not grasping but releasing.
