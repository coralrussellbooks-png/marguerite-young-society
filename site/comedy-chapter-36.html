Definition from research

Mr. Bonebreaker is remembered as the Chicago Gospel salesman who nearly married Miss MacIntosh and then betrayed her, and the comedy around him is rueful rather than loud. Miss MacIntosh mends the very black gloves she wore in his company while insisting she cannot, after all these years, "find it in her heart to blame Mr. Bonebreaker," a woman salvaging dignity from a swindle. The chapter's broader religious comedy belongs to the bungling preacher who "fished up the wrong quotation, caught the wrong fish every time," a spiritual authority who cannot land a single verse. The humor is the smallness and fallibility of the men who offer women salvation and deliver disappointment.
