History in the Body: The Past Doesn't Go Away

Characters: Joachim Spitzer

Themes: Memory and the Past · Time

Symbols: Clocks and Watches

Chapters discussed: 57, 68, 69

Definition from research

In Chapter 57, Mr. Spitzer meets a man reconstructing Appomattox with the outcome reversed, so that "Grant was surrendering to Lee." The man still carries a bullet from the battle in his knee-cap. It aches before rain, and "that was how he knew he lived." Mr. Spitzer quickly calculates that the man would have to be eighty. "There were the impossibilities which Mr. Spitzer recognized in others," Young writes, and the irony lands, since Mr. Spitzer cannot recognize his own.

The scene holds two ideas from the philosophy at once. History is not behind this man. It is lodged inside him, a weather sense in a joint, which is "The Past Doesn't Go Away" made literal. And by flipping the war's result he shows that the outcome was not the only possible one, which is the point behind the warning about calling any arrangement "inevitable." The same layering shows up in the sailor of Chapter 68, who claims, "I was on the Titanic. I was on the Lusitania." In Chapter 69 a man carries "Remember the Marne" tattooed on his chest. The past in this novel is written on skin and in bone.
