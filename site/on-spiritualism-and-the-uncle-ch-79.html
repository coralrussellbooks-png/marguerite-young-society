On Spiritualism and the Uncle

The uncle who told Esther about her unnatural conception, “laughing until his face turned purple,” died of cancer and left his estate to a spiritualist medium: a tall, thin-cheeked man in contact with astral forces and star bodies. American Spiritualism, the movement claiming communication with the dead through mediums, originated in upstate New York in 1848 with the Fox sisters’ claimed spirit rappings, spread rapidly through the nineteenth century, and revived after World War I as people sought contact with those killed in the war. By 1897, it claimed more than eight million followers in the United States and Europe. The uncle’s bequest to a spiritualist medium places him at the intersection of physical violence (pugilism) and metaphysical speculation (astral contact), the same intersection Esther occupies: she is born of a strange physical process and has spent her life in conversation with the spirits of the children she could not keep.
