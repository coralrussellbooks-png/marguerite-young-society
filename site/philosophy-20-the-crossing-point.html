Chapter 20. The Crossing Point

← Previous · Philosophy of MMMD Home · Next →

See also: Pillar 11 The Crossing Point

Characters: Esther Longtree · Vera Cartwheel

Allusions: Deafness and Sign Language

Chapters discussed: 82

Definition from research

Each person stands where everyone who came before meets everyone who will come after.

The novel ends with Vera pregnant, and the last reflective sentence in the book is a statement of this principle: “I do not know the night in which I conceived as I do not know the night in which I was conceived” (ch. 82). Vera stands between the mother she has just lost and the child she has not yet met, and she cannot see the origin of either. She is the crossing point.

The deaf man gives the child a purpose before it is born: “our child will be our ear.” The generation that comes after will supply the sense that the present lacks. And Esther Longtree, the mother of the stillborn, becomes Vera’s future: “Esther Longtree would be me.” The woman whose hope never produced a living child merges with the woman who is about to have one.

This principle gathers all the others. The plural self becomes plural across generations. The invention is handed down. Grief joins with new life. The construction remains open because a child is coming who will build on it. And the time that was sedimentary, layer beneath layer, turns at last toward what is not yet written.
