Penelope and the Odyssey

← Previous · Intertextuality in MMMD Home · Next →

Characters: Catherine Cartwheel · Homer Capehorn · Miss MacIntosh

Allusions: Homer's Epics

Definition from research

Category: obligatory. Penelope is named three times, and her story is needed to see what Young does with it.

In Homer's Odyssey, Penelope waits twenty years for her husband Ulysses to return from Troy. To hold off her suitors, she weaves a shroud by day and unravels it by night. She is the faithful one who stays at home while the hero wanders.

Young gives her part to a man. The bus-driver, guarding his uncut hair, "was Penelope weaving this web, not Ulysses who wandered over the wide seas" (chapter 5). In Catherine's alphabet, "Penelope's web" sits between Admiral Peary and the Pleiades (chapter 11). Miss MacIntosh uses the story to argue against electric light: "Ulysses, if ever he had seen Penelope under electric lights, would never have come home, would have wandered the wide seas forever, and she would always have woven her web" (chapter 23).

Each carrier bends the myth to a need. The bus-driver needs to be the one who waits. Catherine needs a word that begins with P. Miss MacIntosh needs a reason to hate the light bulb. The web that is never finished suits a novel that is never finished either.
