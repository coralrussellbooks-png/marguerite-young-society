On the barber pole.

The red-and-white striped pole that marks barbershops has its origin in bloodletting: barbers in medieval Europe served as surgeons as well as hairdressers, performing phlebotomy (drawing blood with leeches or lancets) alongside haircuts. The red symbolizes blood; the white, the bandage. The pole itself represents the staff patients gripped during the procedure. When Miss MacIntosh visits a barber hoping for some remedy for her baldness and finds instead this ancient symbol of futile medicine (leeches, blood, nothing to be done) Young is placing her in a long history of bodies that sought help from official sources and were told, eloquently, that nothing could be done.
