On the Dark Go-Between and Midwifery

The other woman who facilitates Esther’s conception is described as large, dark, foreign, wearing men’s clothes. To All My Darlings connects her to the tradition of Black midwives in America: figures like Biddy Mason, born into slavery, who became a midwife and advocate; Margaret Charles Smith, who delivered over 3,000 babies in rural Alabama; Mary Francis Hill Coley, whose career was documented in the 1952 film All My Babies. These women (often Black, often working in rural areas with limited resources, often outside the formal medical system) were essential to the survival of communities the medical establishment did not serve. Young’s go-between is their cousin: the dark woman who does the work of bringing life into the world, who is present at origin and at death, who is neither acknowledged nor loved but without whom the child would not exist.
