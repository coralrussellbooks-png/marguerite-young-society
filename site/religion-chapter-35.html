Characters: Cousin Hannah · Joachim Spitzer

Allusions: Revelation and the Apocalypse · Women's Suffrage

Definition from research

Mr. Spitzer holds death "itself a great deceiver," expecting no improvement on the other side and no incorruptible soul, a flat denial of the Christian promise of heaven.

A crusader's cap "covered with shells of mother-of-pearl" marks a completed pilgrimage, the scallop shells of the holy road, the journey to and back from an "Orient paradise."

Cousin Hannah "had seen the burning bush," Moses's sign at Exodus 3, which lends her the air of one called and set apart.

Her vow to fight "until the last woman on earth had been rescued from the last man on earth" turns suffrage into an apocalypse, a final separation and deliverance as absolute as any last judgment.

A figure stands "at arms until he heard the angelus," the Catholic bell that calls the hour of prayer and remembers the Incarnation.
