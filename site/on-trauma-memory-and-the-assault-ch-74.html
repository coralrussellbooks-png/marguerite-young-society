On Trauma Memory and the Assault

Esther describes the detective’s assault with detachment: a whirl, an aching, a dullness, over too soon. This is not Young minimizing the assault. It is Young being precise about how traumatic memory often works. Research on trauma consistently shows that survivors may remember specific sensory details while the event itself becomes fragmentary, dissociated, described in passive or atmospheric terms. The body’s response to extreme threat can produce exactly this quality of observation from a distance, as if it were weather, as if it were happening to someone else. Esther’s detached description is the honest register of a mind that was not able to be fully present for what was done to it.
