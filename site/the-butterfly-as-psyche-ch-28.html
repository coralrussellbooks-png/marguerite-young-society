The butterfly as psyche (Ch 28)

From the reading guide, Chapter 28 · Research: Chapter 28

From the guide

The butterfly as psyche, the Greek word for both butterfly and soul, is one of the oldest symbols in Western tradition. Aristotle used the butterfly as the final stage of metamorphosis in his biological writings. The Stoics used it as a symbol of the soul’s release from the body at death. Young’s Mr. Spitzer, collecting these impalpable creatures, pinning them in glass cases, maintaining “correspondence with other meditative butterfly hunters,” is doing what philosophers have always done with the idea of the soul: trying to catch it still enough to examine it, knowing it will always escape.
