Characters: Miss MacIntosh

Allusions: Ecclesiastes

Definition from research

"the bald heart is so much worse than the bald head whom God hath created" sets spiritual barrenness above the physical, Miss MacIntosh judging the empty heart harder than her own bare head.

"let God not put away with the brown leaves falling, with the stars going out" pleads against being discarded, her baldness tied to the impermanence of all created things.

"the golden bowl was broken" returns from Ecclesiastes, the sign of her lifelong baldness, a beauty broken before she was ever drawn from the well.

"the essential loneliness of every mortal heart," each going alone "upon the tide which does not return," borrows a line close to Aurobindo's Savitri, the solitude of dying, a passage no one shares.

"Vanity, vanity, all is vanity" is Ecclesiastes, set beside her plain worry that "there are no hoods for orphans in cold countries," heaven's futility measured against a child's cold head.

"no one can say I did not try" is her whole works-faith in a sentence, effort held up where feeling failed, faith proved by labor.
