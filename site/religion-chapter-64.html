Chapter 64

On Chapter 64 of Marguerite Young’s novel Miss MacIntosh, My Darling (1965). Part of Christianity and Religion in Miss MacIntosh, My Darling.
By Coral Russell · Updated November 24, 2026

Characters: Miss MacIntosh

Allusions: Ecclesiastes

"Vanity, vanity, all is vanity" is Ecclesiastes, set beside her plain worry that "there are no hoods for orphans in cold countries," heaven's futility measured against a child's cold head.

"no one can say I did not try" is her whole works-faith in a sentence, effort held up where feeling failed, faith proved by labor.

“as seas might give up their dead”: See Revelation 20:13 in the Chapter 61 resources. Earlier in Chapters 6, 49, 52 and 61.

“Bald I came into this world, and bald I will go out of it”: “Naked came I out of my mother’s womb, and naked shall I return thither” (Job 1:21, KJV). See The Book of Job and Hair, Wig, and Baldness. Earlier in Chapter 9.

“the firmament laid upon the firmament”: “Let there be a firmament in the midst of the waters, and let it divide the waters from the waters” (Genesis 1:6, KJV). See Genesis. Earlier in Chapters 22 and 56.

“this was the universal baldness and sign of mourning”: In the Bible, shaving the head is a sign of mourning: “Make thee bald, and poll thee for thy delicate children; enlarge thy baldness as the eagle” (Micah 1:16, KJV). See The Bible, Hair, Wig, and Baldness, and Grief, Loss, and Mourning.

“Whose were these bones?”: “Son of man, can these bones live?” (Ezekiel 37:3, KJV). See The Bible.

“The flesh as grass fading before my eyes”: “All flesh is grass, and all the goodliness thereof is as the flower of the field” (Isaiah 40:6, KJV). See The Bible. Earlier in Chapter 6.

“the outgoing of the morning and the outgoing of the evening”: “Thou makest the outgoings of the morning and evening to rejoice” (Psalm 65:8, KJV). See The Bible.

"the essential loneliness of every mortal heart," each going alone "upon the tide which does not return," borrows a line close to Aurobindo's Savitri, the solitude of dying, a passage no one shares.

“even upon the wings of the wind”: “Who walketh upon the wings of the wind” (Psalm 104:3, KJV). See The Bible. Earlier in Chapter 50.

"the bald heart is so much worse than the bald head whom God hath created" sets spiritual barrenness above the physical, Miss MacIntosh judging the empty heart harder than her own bare head.

"let God not put away with the brown leaves falling, with the stars going out" pleads against being discarded, her baldness tied to the impermanence of all created things.

“the day before God moved over the face of the waters”: “And the Spirit of God moved upon the face of the waters” (Genesis 1:2, KJV). See Genesis. Earlier in Chapters 1, 12, 15, 20, 37, 47, 48 and 52.

“all those old nonconformist Bible quoters”: “nonconformist Bible quoters” can be understood as conservative religious individuals or groups who were critical of anything that deviated from their interpretation of scripture or social norms. They disapproved of the phrenologist’s high praise of Miss MacIntosh, perhaps because it conflicted with their worldview or challenged their authority.

"the golden bowl was broken" returns from Ecclesiastes, the sign of her lifelong baldness, a beauty broken before she was ever drawn from the well.

“She should walk in narrow ways with her head covered”: “But every woman that prayeth or prophesieth with her head uncovered dishonoureth her head: for that is even all one as if she were shaven” (1 Corinthians 11:5, KJV). Paul’s rule fell hardest on a girl born bald. See The Bible, Hair, Wig, and Baldness, and Women, Gender, and Marriage.

“when, as a little child, she was baptized in an icy river”: Baptists and other frontier churches baptized by full immersion in rivers and creeks, breaking the ice in winter if they had to. The near-drowning foreshadows her death at sea. See American Religious Movements.

“by making old God rest on Sunday, the first day of the week”: “But the seventh day is the sabbath of the LORD thy God” (Exodus 20:10, KJV). See Constantine’s Sunday law in the Chapter 63 resources. The Church of Rome is the Roman Catholic Church. Earlier in Chapter 63.

“the macintosh being no fruit in Eden on the Tree of Knowledge”: Eden is the Garden of Eden described as the biblical paradise in Genesis. The Tree of Knowledge is one of two specific trees in the story of the Garden of Eden in Genesis 2-3, along with the tree of life. Alternatively, some scholars have argued that the tree of the knowledge of good and evil is just another name for the tree of life. Genesis never calls the forbidden fruit an apple. See Genesis. Babylon was the capital city of the ancient Babylonian Empire in the Mesopotamian area in antiquity. The Egyptian Book of the Dead is an ancient Egyptian funerary text generally written on papyrus and used from the beginning of the New Kingdom (around 1550 BCE) to around 50 BCE. See Ancient Egypt.
