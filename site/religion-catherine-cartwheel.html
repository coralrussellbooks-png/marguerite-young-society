Definition from research

Catherine, Vera's mother, lives in what the book calls an "opium paradise," and her faith, such as it is, belongs to that world of dreams. She trusts nothing fixed. She assumes "things were not what they seemed" and that "all forms must change their shapes," and religious certainty is just one more solid thing she cannot believe in. She has "placed her faith only in the unreal."

Angels, saints, cherubim, and phantoms drift through her rooms, but she meets them with confusion rather than devotion. Her world is one of "ghostly tides" and fog, of spiritual barrenness rather than hope, and she often plays at death or withdrawal. Cousin Hannah's hard moral certainty only bewilders her further. Where Miss MacIntosh stands on "truth to nature," Catherine stands on nothing at all, expecting no ground firmer than the shifting surface of the evening tide. She belongs to no church and follows no doctrine. Her faith is really faith in unreality, and it leaves her wandering.
