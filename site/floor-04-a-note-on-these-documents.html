An Introduction to the Companion Document Series

The companion documents that follow are the first attempt to describe the territory the constitution points toward. They explain how the rights established in the constitution are put into practice, what the specific provisions mean in specific domains, and what the transition from the current system to the one the constitution envisions would require.

They are not the final word on any of the subjects they address. They were written by people with particular histories, particular blind spots, and particular limits on what they can see from where they stand. Every document in this series will have missed something. Some of what they missed will be obvious to people whose experiences are different from the ones that shaped the writing. Some of it will only become visible as the society these documents are trying to build comes into fuller view. That is the honest condition of any attempt to describe a more just world from inside the unjust one.

These documents are offered as starting points. They are written to be used: argued with, extended, revised, and replaced as the democratic work of building the society they describe proceeds. The provisions in each document are grounded in the best available evidence and the clearest philosophical framework the writers could bring to them. They are not grounded in certainty that every provision is correct or complete. Where they are wrong, they should be corrected. Where they are incomplete, they should be extended. Where they have missed an entire dimension of a problem, that dimension should be added.

The people most directly affected by each domain these documents address are the people best positioned to identify what the documents have missed. The companion document on historical grief was not written by the indigenous communities whose history it describes or the Black communities whose losses it names. The companion document on patriarchy was not written by every woman whose experience of misogyny differs from the experiences the document was able to see. The companion document on criminal justice was not written by the people most directly affected by mass incarceration. These documents are not substitutes for the voices of the people they are about. They are an attempt to build a framework adequate to receive those voices and act on what they say.

The series will grow. Domains not yet addressed will need their own documents. Provisions in existing documents will need revision as implementation reveals what the writing could not anticipate. The spirit of the amendment process in Article VI applies to these documents too.

The building continues. The door is open. Come in.
