Allusions: Deafness and Sign Language

Definition from research

"the lamb had got no fleece, where the little birds had got no feathers" strips the innocent of their protection. In Scripture the lamb carries sacrifice, Christ as the Lamb of God, and the birds carry God's provision, so their bareness reads as vulnerability left uncovered.

"holy Lucifer and Lucifer matches" names the fallen angel. Lucifer means light-bringer, the morning star, a name early Christian writers read into Isaiah's taunt against a proud king and fixed to Satan, the beautiful angel cast out for his pride.

"dead cherry trees blooming with sudden flowers" carries the promise of resurrection, life breaking out of what looked dead.

"Am I not my mother's son?" echoes the moment Jesus redefines family, naming as kin those who do God's will rather than blood alone.

"the setting hens were not setting" recalls Matthew 23:37, Jesus grieving over Jerusalem, longing to gather its people as a hen gathers her chicks and finding them unwilling.

"four angels at four bed-posts, Matthew, Mark, Luke, John" is the old children's bedtime prayer known as the Black Paternoster, angels set to guard a sleeper through the night.

"Conceived on boxwood, born on cedarwood, died on wormwood" is a traditional riddle whose answer is a lute, the instrument's life told through the woods that make and outlast it.

"the angels crying, crying like loons upon the breasts of the waves" sets the sorrow of angels over a fallen world against a human head too empty to hear it. The image gives divine grief a plain earthly voice, the loon's cry over water, and lets it break on deaf ears.

"in the midst of his long hair? Jezebel!" throws out the name of the painted queen of 2 Kings, cast from her window and eaten by dogs, a byword for the harlot and for adornment that ends in ruin.
