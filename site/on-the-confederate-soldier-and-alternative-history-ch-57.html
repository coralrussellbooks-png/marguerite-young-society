On the Confederate soldier and alternative history.

The phenomenon of refusing to accept a historical outcome, constructing an alternative in which the preferred side won, has a technical name in contemporary culture: the Lost Cause narrative, which reframed the Confederacy's defeat as honorable and inevitable rather than the defeat of an immoral cause. The Confederate soldier in the library reading room is living in this narrative to its logical extreme: not merely framing the loss as honorable but insisting the loss did not occur. This is the historical version of what Mr. Spitzer does with his brother's death: not denying it exactly, but living in such intimate relation with the dead man that the boundary between before and after the death has become uncertain. Both men are refusing a fact. The Confederate soldier has built a city in the refusal. Mr. Spitzer has built an interior.
