Woman in Young's Magnum Opus

Chapter 5

← Chapter 4 · Woman in Young's Magnum Opus · Chapter 6 →

Other versions: Chapter hub · Research notes · The Wheel and the Spokes

Characters: Esther Longtree · Miss MacIntosh · Moses Hunnecker

The chapter belongs to Moses, the drunk bus driver, and to the old doctor, both men, and its center is the interrupted birth, reaching a man.

The old doctor runs a "pregnancy practice" in an empty country. He imagines patients, attends deliveries that never occur, tends births that produce nothing. This is the structure of Esther Longtree, the pregnancy that will not deliver, carried here by a male figure, and the wild mares who "imagine pregnancy but bear waters and winds" carry it again in the landscape. The unfinished is not a fact about women in this book. It is a shape consciousness takes, and Chapter 5 is the proof, seeded early, that it takes that shape in men and in the land as readily as in Esther.

Moses supplies the other thread. His refusal to cut his hair, his sense that the hair is his very life and identity, is the detachable self again, the same motif as Miss MacIntosh's wig and the head-hunter's head, and the buried women and infants clutching their hair drag it toward the line between living and dead. And the "elegiac music of the spheres" that the narrator feels but Moses cannot hear is the book's recurring music that goes unheard, the sound at the edge of erasure that returns whenever the book wants to mark what a mind is deaf to. Stated this way, the chapter is not a detour from the argument. It is the argument, reaching past its women for the first time.
