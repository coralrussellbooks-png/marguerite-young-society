History as Catechism: Reality Is Constructed

← Previous · History in MMMD Home · Next →

See also: Pillar 2 Reality Is Constructed

Characters: Miss MacIntosh · Vera Cartwheel

Themes: Memory and the Past

Chapters discussed: 17, 22

Definition from research

Miss MacIntosh, the Scottish nurse who stands for common sense, quizzes Vera every morning over breakfast in Chapter 22. Where is the sole nesting ground of the Kirtland's warbler, "a species almost extinct, seldom seen in this world"? Who was King Canute? Then she turns to "mortal history" to "consider the mistakes of men, their lack of mature judgment." Her favorite target is the Emperor Constantine, who changed the calendar and "got us all off on the wrong day of the week, resting on Sunday which was the day God had not rested."

Young makes a quiet point with this grievance. A calendar is pure invention, an agreement held in place by repetition, and Miss MacIntosh knows it. She sees the construction in Constantine's week. She does not see it in herself. Chapter 17 has already exposed that the woman of plain facts wore a wig and had "only one breast and no hair and no future prospect." Her rote history was a costume over a body she hid. This is "Reality Is Constructed" at its sharpest: the person most certain about what is real is standing inside the most carefully built disguise in the book.
