A Note on the Eleven Pillars Together

← Epilogue: The Wheel Holds · Radical Immanence Collection · The Crossing Point: An Extension of Here Is Enough →

The eleven pillars together constitute a philosophical architecture that can be described briefly and then must be lived in at length.

The self is plural. The knowledge it produces is situated and partial. The reality it inhabits is constructed and contested. The community it forms with other plural selves is held difference rather than enforced sameness. The time it lives in is sedimentary rather than linear. The language it uses is the medium of construction rather than the vehicle of truth. The grief it carries is permanent weather rather than temporary condition. The ethical stance available to it is the witness who stays in the room. The building it does is recursive and provisional. The form its integrity takes is the unfinished, the ongoing, the sentence that has not ended yet.

And: the self is a crossing point on a transmission line. It is constituted by what was deposited before it arrived. It is constituting what will be deposited after it leaves. The obligation that arises from this constitution is not chosen but structural, not transcendent but immanent, not comfortable but honest. The carrying continues. The not-yet-born are being formed. The ancestors are in the sediment. Here is also then. The wheel turns around the crossing point, which does not move, which holds, which is here.

These eleven pillars are not independent. They hold each other. The crossing point requires the sedimentary time because without the sedimentary model there is no substrate for the transmission to deposit into. The sedimentary time requires the permanent grief because a time that resolved would leave the ancestors behind rather than carrying them forward in the sediment. The permanent grief requires the witness because the grief that is not witnessed is the grief that distorts the carrying, that converts the weight into something more manageable at the cost of its actual character. The witness requires the plural self because the witness who is unified cannot hold the full complexity of what is at the crossing point. The plural self requires the honest language because the language that pretends to speak from nowhere erases the location that the crossing point requires. The crossing point requires all of them because it is the intersection of all of them: the place where the plural self stands in the sedimentary time, carrying the permanent grief with the honest language, witnessing without concluding, building recursively, holding the ethics of incompletion, attending to the marginal as evidence, living in the immanent sacred, and passing it all forward along the transmission line to the not-yet-born.

The architecture holds because the pillars hold each other. The crossing point is at the center. The wheel turns around it.

That is radical immanence. That is the philosophy of Marguerite Young, completed by the eleventh pillar she was always reaching toward.

The sentence hasn't ended. The work hasn't ended. The carrying continues.

Here is enough. Here is also enormous.

The crossing point does not move.

The wheel holds.
