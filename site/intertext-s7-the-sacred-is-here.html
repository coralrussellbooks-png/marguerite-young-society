Definition from research

"Meaning is here, in the actual world underfoot, not in a promised beyond."

Bunyan's pilgrim walks toward the Celestial City. Young's pilgrim walks into the September sea. The promised beyond belongs in this novel to Mr. Bonebreaker, the Gospel salesman. He warns that "the Last Day is at hand, and hell lies under us," promises that "there would be stink in Chicago on that Last Day" (chapter 65), and dreams of European thrones. The principle names such appeals as the oldest way to justify sacrifice in the present, and Bonebreaker's promises cost Miss MacIntosh dearly.

Against them stands her own creed, which belongs to the plain American line of common sense and pragmatism: "Common sense is the finest sense." She wants Vera to "lead a useful life" and to face "the clear reality of simple things" (chapter 1). The novel lets both her creed and her failure stand, and in the space between them the ordinary world keeps its meaning.
