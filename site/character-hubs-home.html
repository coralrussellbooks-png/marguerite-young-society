Every character on one staff

All the Darlings

Marguerite Young said the novel has no minor characters, that each one has a complete story. This map draws every character on a single staff of 82 chapters, in the manner of the music diagrams in Boethius’s De institutione musica, where a central column of strings is ringed with arcs that join the notes.

Each loop runs from a character’s first chapter to their last. A character whose whole story sits inside one chapter is a small closed knot tied to that chapter. People Vera remembers coil to the left; people she meets on the road, at the tavern, and in Esther’s diner coil to the right. The staff itself is Vera, the one telling, drawn as a bird, the animal the novel names more than any other.

Spoiler note: The map covers every character’s whole span, including the ending. Tap a loop, knot, or name to light that character’s chapters; each name links to its hub.

    How it maps to Boethius

    Boethius drew the musical scale as a column of strings with arcs looping between them, each arc an interval. Here the strings are chapters and the arcs are lives. Cousin Hannah’s chapters (30 to 52) sit at the exact middle of the book, the place of the mese, the middle string the scale is tuned around. The movements pair off around her in simple ratios: the bus (8 chapters) against its return (16), and Mr. Spitzer (5) against his grief (10), each an octave.

    What the full cast shows

    Exactly half the cast, 48 of 96, are knots: complete stories closed inside one chapter. The bus passengers’ loops open in Chapters 1 to 8 and close when the bus resumes at 67 and 68; the house’s loops open in Chapter 2 and close at 82. And Vera, the staff, goes dark from Chapter 23 to 65 while every other loop passes through the middle without her.
