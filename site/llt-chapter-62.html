Chapter 62

Other versions: Chapter hub · Research notes · The Wheel and the Spokes

Characters: Joachim Spitzer

Pillars: Build Anyway, Knowing It's Temporary · Unfinished Is Honest

The Questions

Mr. Spitzer wonders “if he had ever really slept.” What does it mean that rest itself eludes him?

“The illusion of perfect sleep like the illusion of perfect death was the one illusion which was never possible.” Why is total rest the one thing he cannot have?

His dreams turn a dark river to fire, then to ice. What is a reality that will not hold one state?

If perfect death is as impossible as perfect sleep, what happens to the hope of a final rest?

This last of the Mr. Spitzer chapters turns on the one seal he can never achieve. He wonders “if he had ever really slept,” and concludes that “the illusion of perfect sleep like the illusion of perfect death was the one illusion which was never possible, at least never for him.” Of all the sealed things the book has examined, this is the one it declares flatly unavailable: total rest, complete oblivion, the perfect closure of sleep or death. Mr. Spitzer cannot reach it. There is no final sleep for him, no perfect death, no state so sealed that nothing more stirs in it, and the impossibility is the chapter’s quiet culmination of everything he has been.

This is Unfinished Is Honest carried to its furthest and strangest place, the grave. The book has praised the unfinished and dreaded the sealed; here it finds that even death, the apparent final seal, cannot be perfect, cannot fully close. If perfect death is impossible, then there is no completion anywhere, not even at the end, no state that finishes and stays finished. The man who feared being right, who left his song unfinished, who refused to cohere, turns out to be barred from the last completion too. He cannot even be perfectly dead. Incompletion is not a stage on the way to a final rest; it is the condition all the way down, including down into the grave.

His dreams show a reality that will not hold one state. He dreams of “oaring… along a dark river which had suddenly turned into a river of fire,” and the fire “into a river of ice,” each element becoming the next. Nothing stays what it is; the river is water and fire and ice by turns, and “perhaps all these rivers were one great river of reality burning from light to darkness, darkness to” light again. Reality itself, in his dreaming, is the thing that will not settle, the single river running through every state without resting in any. This is the world as Build Anyway, Knowing It's Temporary at the level of the elements: not a fixed set of things but one shifting substance that refuses to stop transforming.

So Mr. Spitzer’s cluster ends on the refusal of the final seal, and it is fitting that it does. He has been, through all these chapters, the book’s model of the life kept open, and the last thing the book shows him is that even the rest he might long for, the perfect sleep, the perfect death, is closed to him, because closure is closed to him, because he is the one who does not seal. The river burns from light to darkness and back, the sleep never perfects, the death never completes, and Mr. Spitzer goes on, unsealed even against oblivion, the man who could not finish carried past the place where finishing was supposed to be guaranteed at last.

What the Chapter Refuses, and the Footing It Offers

The chapter refuses the reading that takes Mr. Spitzer’s sleeplessness for a symptom, an insomnia to be treated, and the reading that hears in the longing for perfect sleep or death a rest the book means to grant him. Neither the symptom nor the promised rest is what the chapter shows.

The footing is that perfect sleep and perfect death, the final seals, are the one illusion never possible, so that incompletion is the condition all the way down, including into the grave, and there is no completion anywhere that finishes and stays finished; and that reality itself will not hold one state, the single river burning from water to fire to ice, refusing to settle. Even oblivion cannot be sealed, and reality keeps transforming. Mr. Spitzer’s long arc ends unsealed against death itself, and the chapter grants him no perfect rest, which is the truest thing it could refuse him.

For Reflection

Is there a perfect rest or final closure one longs for that may simply not be available?

If even death cannot be a perfect seal, what happens to the hope of a state where nothing more stirs?

What would it be to accept that reality never settles into one fixed state, but keeps transforming?
