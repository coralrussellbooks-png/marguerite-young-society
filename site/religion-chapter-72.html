Allusions: Ecclesiastes

“Eat grass like the wild ass in the fields”: Of Nebuchadnezzar, driven out in his madness: “his dwelling was with the wild asses: they fed him with grass like oxen” (Daniel 5:21, KJV). See The Bible.

"Balaam's ass beheld an angel" recalls Numbers 22, the donkey who saw what the prophet could not.

“the winnowing test”: “Whose fan is in his hand, and he will throughly purge his floor, and gather his wheat into the garner; but he will burn up the chaff” (Matthew 3:12, KJV). See The Bible.

“as if she dropped a handkerchief”: Dropping a handkerchief was also used when describing “...a moment’s passage, was a handkerchief dropped from the hand of God…” by Mr. Spitzer. See the Chapter 50 resources. Earlier in Chapter 50.

“the skies had unrolled like a scroll on the last day”: “And the heaven departed as a scroll when it is rolled together” (Revelation 6:14, KJV). See Revelation and the Apocalypse. Earlier in Chapters 36 and 56.

“the man who was on his way to salty Utah, the Mormon”: “Salty Utah” may refer to Salt Lake City which is the capital and most populous city of the U.S. state of Utah, as well as the seat of Salt Lake County, the most populous county in Utah. (Wikipedia) Mormons are a religious and cultural group, the principal branch of the Latter Day Saint movement started by Joseph Smith in upstate New York during the 1820s. The largest number of members reside in Utah. (Wikipedia) See American Religious Movements.

“a little Jehovah’s Witness”: Jehovah’s Witnesses in America, initially Bible Students, grew from the late 1800s under Charles Taze Russell, faced intense persecution during the 1930s & WWII for neutrality, adopted the name in 1931, expanded significantly with organized campaigns and Gilead training, and solidified doctrines like the 1935 “great crowd” redefinition, all while building their distinctive identity through publications, conventions, and door-to-door work, establishing a strong presence despite opposition. See American Religious Movements.

“the pecan trees that were brought from the old Jerusalem”: Old Jerusalem, a 5,000-year-old city, has been a central hub of religious and political conflict, serving as the Jewish capital since King David (c. 1000 B.C.) and holding immense significance for Christians and Muslims. The current 0.9-square-kilometer walled city, rebuilt by the Ottomans in the 16th century, is divided into Armenian, Christian, Jewish, and Muslim quarters.

“for God loves a silent woman”: “A silent and loving woman is a gift of the Lord” (Ecclesiasticus 26:14, in the Apocrypha of the King James Bible); “Let the woman learn in silence with all subjection” (1 Timothy 2:11, KJV). See The Bible and Women, Gender, and Marriage.

“Martin Luther”: Martin Luther was a German priest, theologian, author and hymn writer. A former Augustinian friar, he is best known among Christians as the seminal figure in the Protestant Reformation and as the namesake of Lutheranism. (Wikipedia) In his last years Luther turned on the Jews in On the Jews and Their Lies (1543), a tract the Nazis reprinted. See Judaism.

"the maternal pelvis was smaller than the needle's eye" bends Jesus's saying about the rich man and the camel toward the hard passage of birth.

“it could pass through as in the twinkling of an eye”: “In a moment, in the twinkling of an eye, at the last trump” (1 Corinthians 15:52, KJV). See The Bible. Earlier in Chapters 37 and 58.

“The wailings, the gnashings of teeth”: “There shall be weeping and gnashing of teeth” (Matthew 8:12, KJV). See The Bible. Earlier in Chapters 10, 17 and 34.

“Did not all people come into the world to die, as the preacher sayeth”: “A time to be born, and a time to die” (Ecclesiastes 3:2, KJV). See Ecclesiastes.

"was there not something stillborn in every stream" leans on Ecclesiastes, a time to be born and a time to die, death folded into every living thing.
