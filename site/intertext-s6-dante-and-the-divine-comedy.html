Dante and The Divine Comedy

Characters: Catherine Cartwheel · Joachim Spitzer

Themes: Faith, God, and the Supernatural

Allusions: Dante

Definition from research

Category: optional. Dante is named in chapters 2 and 40, The Divine Comedy in chapter 29, and Inferno and Paradiso in chapter 54. The links are real, but the novel does not follow Dante's plan.

Dante Alighieri's Divine Comedy, completed around 1321, carries its poet down through the circles of Hell, up the mountain of Purgatory, and into Paradise, guided first by Virgil and then by Beatrice. Its underworld is strictly ordered. Each sin receives a fitting punishment.

In the novel Dante appears as one of the nine names of Catherine's canary (chapter 2) and among her visitors from the realm of the dead, "ageless Dante" (chapter 40). Mr. Spitzer carries him most seriously. He wonders when "he should come to the last infernal circle of this Divine Comedy of life and death" (chapter 29). He imagines an old aunt who "might be an old uncle in Paradiso if this was Paradiso. Yet it might be Inferno, for Mr. Spitzer's was an escalator psychology" (chapter 54). In the doctor's house hangs "an old steel engraving of Dante wooing Beatrice" (chapter 71). Rhadamanthus, the judge of the dead, finds Mr. Spitzer guilty of forgotten crimes in Catherine's imagination (chapters 11 and 14).

Young keeps Dante's geography and loosens its order. Mr. Spitzer rides an escalator where Dante climbed a mountain, and he cannot tell which floor is which. The underworld in this novel is not a place of fitting punishments. It is a crowded, shifting region where the living keep visiting the dead.
