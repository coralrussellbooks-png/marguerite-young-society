Chapter 3

Characters: Miss MacIntosh · Vera Cartwheel

The Questions

Is Miss MacIntosh’s common sense the ground the novel stands on, or a construction the novel has built in order to examine it?

What does her constant labor make: a useful life, or a wall against everything that cannot be shelled, mended, counted, or scrubbed?

When she refuses to believe in things invisible, is that clear sight, or a blueprint sealing itself against revision?

What does the book gain by making its plainest, most grounded figure also its most rigid?

This early, the novel hands the reader Miss MacIntosh as though she were the answer. Her useful work, the hammering and mending, the shelling of bean pods, the gathering of sea shells, is The Sacred Is Here in its most attractive form. Meaning here is, underfoot, in “correct facts, the correct time, the numbers of tons of coal mined by coal-miners in distant mines.” She makes “a useful contribution to human society,” and the novel means that value truly. She enters the book, clean and appealing, before seeing what it costs.

The same figure, though, shows The Sacred Is Here beginning to harden into a finished blueprint. Her skepticism is not an open inquiry. She does “not believe in things invisible or in promising that which could never be realized by poorest mortals here below, perhaps not even by God,” and she sweeps away “bodiless phantoms and monsters, with imaginary nightingales or turrets of ivory.” Honest as she is, “as honest as the day was long,” the honesty is fused, unable to move. What Build Anyway, Knowing It's Temporary would ask of her, the willingness to revise, to let in what her frame cannot yet hold, is exactly what she will not do.

Her trust in “correct facts, the correct time,” the counted tons of distant coal, is Language Shapes What's Possible seen from inside a sealed grammar. She believes in the nouns, the named and the tallied, and calls the naming truth. Young’s own long, moving, verb-driven sentences run against that grammar, and the quarrel between them is part of the argument. The plainest speaker in the book is set against the book’s own way of speaking.

A reader is meant to be tempted here, and the temptation is the point, because the later chapters will reveal Miss MacIntosh’s secrets.

What the Chapter Refuses, and the Footing It Offers

The chapter refuses the reading that crowns Miss MacIntosh as the novel’s moral ground, the destination Vera should reach. The novel is not endorsing her. It is examining what happens when the ordinary is sealed and called final.

The footing it offers is double and honest. Miss MacIntosh genuinely carries The Sacred Is Here, and the useful world she tends is never mocked. Meaning is here, underfoot, in the shelled pod and the mended thing. But the moment that meaning is sealed into a fixed creed it begins to fail, and the failure is already visible in her rigidity and her shut compassion. She is a figure the book loves and warns through, not a lesson it hands down.

For Reflection

Where does the line fall between a grounded life and a sealed one?

What in one’s own certainties is genuine footing, and what is a blueprint frozen because revising it would cost too much?

If the plainest person in the room is also the most rigid, what is the rigidity protecting?
