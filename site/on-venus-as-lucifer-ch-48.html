On Venus as Lucifer.

In Roman mythology, Lucifer (light-bringer) was the name for the morning star: Venus at dawn, the brightest object in the pre-sunrise sky. The name is derived from lux (light) and ferre (to bear). When early Christian writers were translating the Hebrew Bible, they used Lucifer to render a passage in Isaiah describing the fall of a Babylonian king: how art thou fallen from heaven, O Lucifer, son of the morning! Over centuries, this astronomical metaphor for a fallen king was personalized into a fallen angel, and Lucifer became Satan's pre-fall name. Young knows this etymology. The morning star that rises and falls, that brings light and is the herald of dawn, that is also named for the one who fell; this is the image she gives Mr. Spitzer for the twins who were once one.
