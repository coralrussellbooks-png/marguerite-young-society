Definition from research

Chapter 12

  • “perhaps with two Mr. Spitzers, one who had died, one who had lived”: Joachim and Peron Spitzer, the twins.
  • “put his bet on the dark horse”: The phrase comes from Disraeli’s The Young Duke (1831): “a dark horse, which had never been thought of.”

Chapter 14

Listed as: Mr. Spitzer (Joachim and Peron)

Chapter 25

Listed as: Peron (Joachim’s twin brother) Spitzer

  • The deceased twin, described as quick, impetuous, and decisive.
  • Committed suicide by falling through a window; his death is enveloped in mystery and doubt.
  • Contrasts with Mr. Spitzer’s hesitant survival.
  • Represents a life fully lived and a death fully embraced.
  • His legacy haunts Mr. Spitzer, who often thinks in his brother’s voice or perspective.

Chapter 26

Listed as: Peron Spitzer (Joachim’s twin brother)

  • Impulsive, competitive, and distrustful of the law.
  • Impetuous, restless, afraid of death.
  • His relationship with Joachim is that their lives and deaths intertwine metaphorically; Peron’s death haunts Joachim.
  • Represents the chaotic, fleeting nature of life and human memory.

Chapter 28

Listed as: Peron Spitzer (Joachim’s Twin Brother)

  • A loudmouthed, pragmatic man who embraced the cruelty of actual life and seemed indifferent to spiritual or afterlife concepts.
  • His untimely death deeply affects Mr. Spitzer’s psyche and way of life.

Chapter 29

Listed as: Peron Spitzer (twin brother to Joachim who is allegedly dead by suicide)

  • A mysterious, volatile figure who lived a fragmented life.
  • His death profoundly impacts Mr. Spitzer, whose mourning colors his worldview.
  • Peron’s character contrasts with Mr. Spitzer’s logical nature; he is seen as a greater mystery.

Chapter 41

  • A figure symbolizing tragic melancholy and finality in death.

Chapter 48

Listed as: Mr. Spitzer’s Brother (Peron)

  • Contrasts with Mr. Spitzer by his flamboyance and willingness to "burn brightly," symbolizing a different approach to life and death. His early death and vibrant life are a foil to Mr. Spitzer’s cautious and contemplative nature.

Chapter 53

Listed as: Peron

  • Mr. Spitzer’s brother, a gambler and more flamboyant, volatile personality.
  • Peron’s life is described as colorful, many-sided, and seemingly successful, but also transient and elusive.
  • His death creates a “rift” in Spitzer’s life and intensifies Spitzer’s struggle with his own existence and identity.

Chapter 54

  • Mr. Spitzer’s deceased brother, described as wild, gregarious, and reckless, almost the opposite of Joachim. Peron’s life and death haunt Joachim, and his presence is felt as a contrasting force driving Joachim’s reflections and wanderings.

Chapter 55

Listed as: Peron (Mr. Spitzer’s Brother)

  • A gambler, extrovert, and embodiment of chaos, risk, and unpredictability.
  • Lives a life of abandon, irresponsibility, and indifference to consequence.
  • His death leaves Joachim to bear the burdens and debts he left behind.
  • Represents the impulsive, irrational, and destructive forces in life, contrasting sharply with Joachim’s order.
  • His laughter and ghostly presence continue to haunt Joachim.

Chapter 56

Listed as: Peron

  • Peron is Mr. Spitzer’s deceased brother, described as eccentric with a flamboyant lifestyle, many coats and hats, and a lavish wardrobe. Peron’s death triggers Mr. Spitzer’s existential reflections.

Chapter 57

Listed as: Peron

  • Peron, Mr. Spitzer’s deceased brother, is described as a gambler and a man of dignity in death. Peron’s identity is intertwined with mystery and symbolic imagery (black suit, red rose, bright red necktie), representing a life ended but still profoundly present in Mr. Spitzer’s mind.
  • The Great Tailor may have made the black funeral suit for Peron. He symbolizes unresolved debts, both financial and psychological, and the elusive nature of truth and memory.
  • Various strangers and characters Mr. Spitzer encounters, including a Confederate soldier who claims contradictory historical facts, a librarian, and others who contribute to the dreamlike, surreal atmosphere.

Chapter 59

Listed as: Peron Spitzer (Joachim’s twin brother)

  • A contrasting figure who feared life because it was brief.
  • His death deeply affects Mr. Spitzer and acts as a catalyst for many of Spitzer’s reflections.
  • Represents the opposite approach to life compared to Mr. Spitzer’s fear of life’s length.
