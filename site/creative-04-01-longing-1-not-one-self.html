Pillar: Pillar 1 Not One Self · Across every book

or On the self that refuses to be a single thing, and why that refusal keeps you free.

There is a tiredness that comes from being asked to be consistent. To be the same person on Monday that you were on Sunday, to want one thing and not its opposite, to have a story about yourself that holds together. Most of us cannot do it, not honestly. We are several people in one coat, and we spend a great deal of energy pretending otherwise.

Power understands that tiredness better than we do. Its first and kindest-seeming offer is relief from it. Be one thing, it says. You are a patriot. You are one of the real people. You are of the blood, the faith, the nation. Step into the single self we have cut for you and the argument inside you goes quiet. There is enormous comfort in that quiet. It is the comfort of no longer having to hold your own contradictions.

Marguerite Young builds the opposite person. Her narrator goes looking for one solid self, the dependable nursemaid who seemed made of pure reality, and finds instead that even she was many, masked, unfinished. Everyone in that book is more than one. The mother is a dozen women inside a single opium dream. No one resolves into a type. The novel runs past a thousand pages partly because a person does not reduce, and Young refused to pretend otherwise just to make the book shorter.

The first pillar is that refusal made into a stance. You are plural, and the contradiction in you is not a flaw waiting to be ironed out. It is the texture of being someone. When you stop treating your own multiplicity as a problem, the single clean identity loses its pull. There is no one you for the machine to enlist, no smooth surface for the slogan to stick to.

This is not a grand act of resistance. It is small and daily. It is letting yourself want two things, hold two truths, be tender and furious in the same hour, and not reaching for the person who promises to make you simple. The plural self cannot be marched in a single direction, because it was never marching as one.

You were never only one. That was never the failure. It was the whole of you.
