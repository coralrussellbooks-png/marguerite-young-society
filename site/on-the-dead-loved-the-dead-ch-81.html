On “The Dead Loved the Dead”

Esther’s closing thought (“the dead loved the dead, and” perhaps that is how the world goes on) is one of the novel’s most compressed statements of its central metaphysics. Young has spent ten chapters establishing that the dead do not simply leave: they whisper, they return, they haunt, they accuse, they hover with innocent smiles, they reach up from the water with ivory hands. The dead are present in this novel’s world in ways the living cannot fully access or explain. If the dead love the dead, if the connections that produce new life are not only between the living but between the living and the lost, between presence and absence, between what is here and what is gone: then Esther’s everlasting pregnancy makes a different kind of sense. She is not only carrying the little shadow boxer. She is carrying everything. And that is how the world goes on.
