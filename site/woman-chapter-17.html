Woman in Young's Magnum Opus

Chapter 17

Other versions: Chapter hub · Research notes · The Wheel and the Spokes

Characters: Miss MacIntosh · Vera Cartwheel

The wig comes off here. Miss MacIntosh stands at the window, her corset stays out like porcupine quills, braced as a "weird wrestler," and then she turns and she is bald, "coldly, stonily bald," a "monster moving toward" Vera in the dark. The stripping of femininity and beauty is the plain surface of the scene, and under it is the body as the place the self comes apart, the red hair gone and with it the woman Vera thought she knew, the "bald pretender" who, Vera realizes, may never have been "herself."

With the hair go the categories. Vera looks at the bald head and sees "an old man," the crossing point arriving on the body the same instant the body is unmade, gender falling away with the wig. The head is "the naked rock lashed by waves," the sea already at Miss MacIntosh even here.

Then the fight, and the fight is with a self that will not hold one shape. Vera grapples a figure that is a fallen angel, a gladiator, a shadow boxer, and finally "no one," a sandbag dummy with an icy untouchable surface. This is fear and violence and control, and it is plural selfhood turned into an opponent, the illusion of the individual made physical: Vera is wrestling something that is many and then nothing, and cannot pin it because there is no single body there to pin. In the morning her hair has been pulled out without pain and her braids are whole, the night both true and undreamed, the real and the dream as one country holding it in both hands.
