Allusions: Pregnancy, Abortion, and Midwifery

Definition from research

The comedy here is gallows humor built around Esther Longtree, the "stillborn mother," and it works by taking her private guilt and staging it as a courtroom farce. She imagines her own trial and produces an alphabet of evidence, exhibit A the agony, exhibit B the birthday, exhibit C the candle on the cake, all the way down to U for "an umbrella or her uterus." The mock-legal inventory turns anguish into a deadpan catalog, which is one of Young's steadiest comic devices.

The running joke of the chapter is the white detective who will only solve "white" mysteries and bolts the moment anything black enters the case. He drops a double murder because blackbirds ate the same bread crumbs. The absurd literalism of a man who "drew the line between the whiteness and the blackness" is played for grim comedy even as it carries the chapter's real subject, which is racial murder and denial. Two macabre set pieces round it out: the three-hundred-pound abortionist whose whole scheme unravels because she kills a woman who was only pregnant in a dream, and the mother who lets her children play with a skull, sets their father's hat on it, and cheerfully confirms "that their father had come home" when the headless body is finally found. The humor is entirely in the flat, untroubled delivery of horror.
