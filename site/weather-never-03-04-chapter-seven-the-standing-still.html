Chapter Five: The Standing Still

Part of The Weather You Were Never Allowed to Have, on the psychology in the work of Marguerite Young (1908 to 1995).
By Coral Russell · Updated October 29, 2026

← Chapter Four: You Were Told This Would End · The Weather You Were Never Allowed to Have · Chapter Six: The Reef Method →

You opened the feed and the thing was already there.

Not a thing you went looking for. The thing the algorithm had decided you needed to see, the political development, the policy announcement, the statement from the person you were supposed to have a position on, the event that the culture had already begun processing into takes before you had finished reading the headline. By the time you reached the end of the first paragraph the responses were already organized: here is what this means, here is what it requires of you, here is the correct position for a man of your particular constellation of views, and here is what it says about you if you do not hold it immediately and publicly and with the appropriate force.

The feed does not give you time.

This is not accidental.

The feed is designed around the specific architecture of the framework's demand: that a man knows where he stands, that he holds his position without wavering, that the uncertainty between the seeing and the concluding is not a sign of careful thinking but a sign of weakness, of not having it figured out, of being the kind of man who can be moved by things rather than the kind of man who moves them.

The negotiation is instant.

Not a negotiation you chose to enter. A negotiation the feed entered you into the moment you opened it: here is the thing, here is what it requires, here is the position you are expected to produce, and the clock is already running. The man who takes time with it is the man who does not know. The man who does not know is not the right kind of man. The right kind of man already has the take.

You stopped.

Not because you did not have a reaction. You had a reaction. The reaction arrived before the analysis, before the context, before the full shape of the thing had resolved itself into something you could actually see clearly. The reaction was real and it was yours and it was also, you understood in the pause before you reached for the words, not the whole of what you thought about this.

You looked for the response that was accurate.

It was not available in the form the feed required.

Not because you did not have a position. Because the position you actually held was more complicated than what the form could carry without losing the part that was most true. The thing you were looking at was wrong in a way that preceded the argument about why it was wrong, that lived underneath the available positions, that could not be compressed into the format that the negotiation demanded without becoming a smaller and less honest version of itself.

The feed required the smaller version.

You did not give it.

Not heroically. Not as a declaration. As the quiet recognition that the space between the seeing and the responding was the only place the real response could be built from, and that the form required you to give up that space before you had used it.

That space is the witness position.

And the framework, through the feed and the negotiation it insists upon, has been working your whole life to convince you that the space is weakness.

The space is not weakness.

The space is where the witness lives.

The framework's account of the male response to the political wrong thing is immediate and legible: you identify which side you are on, you produce the take that confirms your position, you do not let the complexity of the thing slow the response because the slowness is the signal that you can be moved, and the man who can be moved is the man who does not know where he stands.

The witness position produces something different.

Not the absence of a position. The position that has been earned by staying in the space between the seeing and the concluding long enough for the full shape of the thing to become visible. The response that comes from the standing still rather than the reaction that comes from the framework's demand for the immediate take.

The framework produces men who react.

The witness position produces men who respond.

These are not the same thing.

And in the contemporary moment, in the specific pressure of the feed and the negotiation it enters you into without your consent, the difference between them is the difference between a man who is being operated by the algorithm and a man who is doing the operating.

The standing still is not passivity.

The standing still is the refusal to be operated.

Here is what the framework will tell you about being present to difficulty.

It will tell you to handle it. To do the next right thing. To not let it affect you more than the situation warrants. To be rational, which in the framework's usage means: do not be moved, and produce the position quickly enough that no one can read the delay as uncertainty.

What it will not tell you is that the man who produces the position immediately is not the man who has transcended the difficulty. He is the man whose window has narrowed so far that the complexity cannot get in. And the narrowed window does not only keep the complexity out. It keeps everything out. The man who has achieved the framework's version of presence, the man who always knows where he stands before he has finished reading, has achieved it by reducing his capacity for the full range of experience, and the reduction costs him the depth of the good things as surely as it protects him from the discomfort of the complicated ones.

The real witness position is not the immediate take.

It is the tree in the wind.

You already know this.

You know it in every moment you have held the full weight of something without being taken over by it. The political conversation where you stayed with the complexity rather than immediately producing the position that would have ended the discomfort. The moment in the feed where you felt the pull of the negotiation and did not give it what it asked for, not because you had nothing to say but because what you had to say required the space the feed would not give you.

The witness position was available to you in those moments.

The framework gave you no name for it and no practice for developing it deliberately.

This chapter is the name and the practice.

Here is what it looks like to lose the witness position to the feed.

You know the feeling.

The specific acceleration when the thing arrives that lands on exactly the territory where your window is already narrow. The political announcement that touches the thing you have been watching get worse for months. The statement from the person whose position costs other people something real. The take from someone you respected that reveals a limit you did not know was there.

The reaction is immediate and it is total and it is, in the moment of it, indistinguishable from clarity: you know exactly what you think, you know exactly what needs to be said, the words are available and they are forceful and the only thing between you and the send button is the breath you have not yet taken.

The framework says: send it. This is knowing where you stand. This is the right kind of man.

The witness says: wait.

Not because the reaction is wrong. The reaction is real and it is yours and it carries real information about what you value and what you cannot tolerate and what the thing in the feed is actually doing to the people it is being done to. The reaction is not the problem.

The reaction as the only available response is the problem.

Because the reaction that goes directly from the trigger to the send button, without the space of the standing still, is the reaction that has been operated by the algorithm rather than authored by you. It is the position the feed needed you to produce, in the form the feed needed you to produce it, at the speed the feed needed you to produce it, and the fact that the position is yours does not change the fact that the production of it was the feed's.

The witness position does not require you to have no position.

It requires you to have earned it.

The earning happens in the space.

The space is a breath long.

Sometimes that is enough.

Here is what it looks like to lose the witness position entirely.

You know this feeling too.

It has a specific quality in the body: the narrowing, the acceleration, the sense of the walls of the situation coming in until there is no air and no perspective and no version of you available except the version that is inside the story completely, with no distance, no standing still, nothing between you and the full force of what is happening.

It happens fast.

The trigger being not always proportionate to the response. The political thing that lands on the accumulated weight of all the other political things and produces a reaction that surprises you with its force. The thing that would not have taken you out yesterday taking you out today because the window has already been narrowed by the week or the month or the years of watching and the chronic pressure of the negotiation that never stops.

The framework tends to read this as a loss of control, which is a failing, and the remedy it offers is more control: tighter management of the interior conditions that produced the visible reaction, more discipline in the performance of the man who is not moved.

The framework is treating the symptom as the disease.

The reaction that comes out with disproportionate force is not the disease.

The reaction that comes out with disproportionate force is the chorus being suppressed past the point of suppression: the parts that have been watching the wrong thing happen and have been required to produce measured responses to unmeasured harm, finally exceeding the capacity of the management, the weather that has been held underground finding the only exit available.

More management does not fix this.

What fixes this is the space.

The breath between the trigger and the response. The noticing before the naming. The return to the witness position that was lost. The recognition that the feed is designed to eliminate the space and that the reclaiming of it is the standing still in its most practical and most available form.

The space is always available.

It is a breath long.

The feed cannot take the breath from you.

Only you can give it away.

The Science of the Standing Still

Daniel Siegel drew a window.

Not literally. Conceptually. He called it the window of tolerance and it became one of the most useful frameworks in contemporary psychology not because it was complicated but because it was accurate: a simple description of the zone in which human beings can process experience without being overwhelmed by it or shut down from it.

The window has two edges.

Above the upper edge is hyperarousal: the state of too much, the flooded nervous system, the reaction that bypasses reflection. This is the explosion. This is the disproportionate response to the political thing in the feed. This is what the framework produces when it has suppressed the parts past their capacity to be suppressed and what the algorithm produces when it has been feeding the window's narrowing long enough that the trigger it selects finally exceeds the available capacity.

Below the lower edge is hypoarousal: the state of too little, the shut-down nervous system, the numbness that protects against feeling by removing the capacity for it. This is the achieved invulnerability. This is the framework's trophy. This is also not health. The man who has achieved the stillness by learning not to feel is not in the window. He is below it. The stillness is real. The cost is also real: the loss of the full range of experience, and the full range of experience is where the life actually happens.

Inside the window: the optimal zone in which the full range of experience is available without the system being taken over by any part of it. The feeling without the flooding. The presence without the bypass. The standing still inside the weather rather than above it or flattened by it.

The witness position is the window of tolerance made into a practice.

Siegel's research, and the decades of neuroscience that followed it, demonstrated that the window is not a fixed thing. It widens with practice and narrows under sustained pressure. The man who has spent years in the framework, suppressing the parts, managing the interior conditions, performing the invulnerability, has a window that has been narrowing across his adult life. The suppression that was supposed to protect him has been producing a nervous system that is increasingly brittle: less able to tolerate the full range before either exploding above the window or shutting down below it.

This is not a character judgment.

This is the physics of the window under sustained pressure.

The specific pressure the contemporary moment adds to this is the feed and the negotiation it requires. The feed is a window-narrowing machine. Not incidentally. Deliberately. The algorithm is optimized for engagement, and the content that produces the highest engagement is the content that takes the window to its edges: the outrage that pushes toward hyperarousal, the helplessness that pushes toward hypoarousal. The man who has been spending time in the feed is a man whose window is being systematically narrowed by a system that profits from the narrowing.

The tending of the window is therefore not only the personal practice of the man doing the interior work. It is the structural resistance to the system that is working against the interior work at every moment he has the feed open.

The window widens through practices that support the nervous system's regulation: through the Chorus Model that reduces the pressure of the suppression, through the grief literacy that names what the underground parts have been carrying, through the physical practices that return the body to its baseline rather than keeping it at the edge that the algorithm prefers. The window widens when the parts are heard rather than suppressed, because the heard part does not need to explode and does not need to be managed into numbness.

The widening window is the standing still becoming more available.

Siegel's interpersonal neurobiology adds a dimension that matters specifically for the relational work of Chapter Eight. The regulated nervous system of one person in a relationship can widen the window of tolerance of the other. The dysregulated nervous system of one person can narrow the window of the other. The man who has been in the framework, performing the invulnerability, managing the interior conditions: his dysregulation has been narrowing the windows of the people closest to him without either party having a framework for what was happening.

The interior work is therefore not only personal.

The man who widens his own window widens the windows of the rooms he inhabits.

This is the civic and relational argument for the interior work made neurological: the man who has done the work is literally a different presence in the room. Not because he has achieved the equanimity the wellness industry sells. Because he has developed the capacity to be fully in the experience without the system being taken over by it, and that capacity is contagious in the specific way that embodied presence is contagious.

Steven Hayes developed Acceptance and Commitment Therapy in the 1980s and one of its central contributions to the understanding of the witness position is the distinction between what he called self as content and self as context.

Self as content is the self that is identified with its thoughts and feelings and experiences: the self that is the anger, the self that is the position in the argument, the self that is the story being told about what is happening. This self has no witness position because it is entirely inside the content. There is no standing still available because there is no distance between the self and the experience. This is the self the feed is trying to produce: the man who is his reaction, who is his position, who is the take he just sent.

Self as context is the self that contains the thoughts and feelings and experiences without being identical to them. The observer self: the part of you that can notice you are angry without being only the anger, that can feel the weight without being only the weight. This self has the witness position built into it because it is the context rather than the content. The experience is happening inside it rather than to it.

Hayes called the practice of developing the observer self defusion: the process of creating distance between the self and the thought, not to suppress the thought or bypass the feeling but to make room between the experience and the response. The room being the standing still. The standing still being the space in which the witness position operates.

The defusion practice is not complicated.

It is the simple act of noticing: I am having the thought that this is wrong, rather than simply being inside the wrongness. I am feeling the anger, rather than being the anger. The noticing being not the suppression of the feeling but the creation of the room. The room being where the witness lives.

For the man who has been trained by the framework to be his positions, his reactions, his performance, the defusion practice is the most practically useful single tool in the book. It does not require the abandonment of the position. It requires the creation of the space between the position and the self. The space being what the feed is trying to eliminate. The defusion practice being the reclaiming of it one breath at a time.

Jonathan Shay coined the term moral injury in the 1990s working with Vietnam veterans.

He was trying to name something that PTSD did not fully capture: the specific psychological damage that occurred not from the terror of combat but from the betrayal of what was right. The soldier who was ordered to do something that violated their moral framework. The person who witnessed atrocity and was required to continue functioning as if the atrocity had not occurred. The specific wound of the person whose moral clarity was intact and whose capacity to act on it was systematically overridden.

Moral injury is not the wound of the person who didn't know better.

It is the wound of the person who knew exactly what better looked like and was prevented from enacting it.

The concept has expanded far beyond the military context, and for the man doing this work it has a specific application that Shay did not name but that his framework makes visible: the moral injury of the man who has spent decades knowing that the singular self the framework required was not an honest account of what he was, and who was required to perform it anyway. The injury of the man who had the conscience that developed on the job and was required by the economic and social pressure to keep doing the job. The injury of the man who could see the fuller version of himself and was prevented by the framework from inhabiting it.

This is moral injury at the interior scale.

Not the betrayal by an institution of an external moral code. The betrayal by the framework of the internal moral clarity that the man arrived with and that the framework spent decades suppressing.

The treatment of moral injury is not the removal of the moral clarity.

It is the restoration of the capacity to act on it.

Which requires the window to be widened. The observer self to be rebuilt. The standing still to be practiced again in the conditions that are available rather than the conditions that would be ideal.

The standing still is the beginning of the restoration.

Not the completion of it.

The beginning.

That is enough to start from.

The affect regulation researchers, beginning with Peter Fonagy and extending through the contemporary neuroscience of emotion, make a distinction that is essential to the standing still: the difference between emotional suppression, emotional flooding, and emotional integration.

Suppression is the numbness. The below-the-window state. The person who has achieved stillness by removing the capacity for feeling. The cost is the loss of the information that the feeling carries. The loss of the capacity to read the weather. The loss of the witness position that requires full presence to function. This is the framework's preferred state and the man who has achieved it has achieved it at the cost of everything the framework could not provide him in return.

Flooding is the above-the-window state. The person who is entirely inside the feeling with no room for the standing still. The cost is the loss of the response capacity. The reaction that bypasses reflection. The platform's preferred state, the take sent before the breath was taken.

Integration is the window. The full presence of the feeling without the system being taken over by it. The feeling as information rather than as identity. The standing still inside the weather.

Integration is not achieved by managing the feelings into an acceptable size.

It is achieved by widening the window until the full size of the feeling can be held inside it without the system going above or below.

This is the practice.

Not smaller feelings.

A wider window.

The window widens through the practices that support the nervous system's regulation and through the development of the observer self and through the specific work of naming what is actually there rather than performing the resilience that the framework requires in the face of it.

You are not required to be resilient in the face of the framework's cost.

You are required to widen the window enough to keep building from inside it.

That is enough.

That is more than enough.

That is the standing still.

The Standing Still

Here is what the witness position actually is.

Not the man above the weather. Not the detached observer watching experience from a safe distance. Not the man who has achieved the stillness by learning not to feel.

The witness is the man who is fully inside the experience and has not been taken over by it.

The standing still is not stillness in the sense of the absence of movement. It is stillness in the sense of the tree in the wind: rooted deeply enough that the movement above does not become the uprooting below. The branches moving. The roots holding. The full force of the weather present and the standing still possible because the roots go deep enough to hold what the weather brings.

You are building roots.

This is what the five frameworks together are doing: building the root system that makes the standing still possible in the conditions that actually exist. The plural self gives you the chorus and the conductor. The working story gives you the audit that keeps the stories from collapsing the window. The grief climate gives you the literacy that makes the weather legible rather than ambushing. The standing still is what becomes possible when the other three are working.

The Standing Still framework has four practices.

The first practice is the noticing before the naming.

The witness position begins before the language arrives.

The body knows before the mind does. The tightening in the chest before the thought arrives. The specific quality of the moment before the reaction. The feeling in the room before the words that produced it have been processed.

The noticing before the naming is the practice of attending to that pre-linguistic signal. Not to analyze it. Not to immediately convert it into a story about what is happening. To notice it. To register it as information before the mind begins its work of making it into content.

This is the first moment of the standing still: the pause between the trigger and the response in which the noticing happens before the reaction does.

It is a breath long.

Sometimes that is enough.

Practice the breath.

The second practice is the return.

The witness position is lost and found and lost and found again.

This is not failure. This is the practice.

The loss is information: the window has narrowed, the trigger has exceeded the current capacity, the parts are pushing past the conductor's ability to hold them. The return is the practice itself: the recognition that the witness position has been lost and the choice to return to it.

The return does not require that the feeling stop.

It requires only the recognition: I have been taken over by the story, and the choice that follows: I am returning to the standing still. Not because the feeling is wrong. Because the reaction that comes from the lost witness position serves neither me nor the situation.

The third practice is the full presence without the requirement.

The witness position is not the position of the man who requires the experience to be different before he can engage with it.

This is the distinction that the framework's version of control misses entirely. The man who has achieved his composure by requiring the situation to be manageable before he can be present is not in the witness position. He is in the conditional presence: I will be here when things are more straightforward, when the conversation is less complicated, when the feeling has reduced to a size I can manage.

The witness position is the unconditional presence.

The full presence with what is actually here: the difficult conversation, the grief that came back, the thing in the feed that was wrong, the gap between the vision and the reality.

All of it.

Here.

Without the requirement that it be different before you can be present to it.

The fourth practice is the tending of the window.

The window narrows under sustained pressure.

The sustained pressure of the contemporary moment is real: the management, the performance, the specific exhaustion of a man who has been maintaining the singular self across years of situations that required it. The window narrows and the tending is what keeps it from narrowing past the point of easy return.

Sleep. Movement. The relationships that do not require the performance. The creative work that generates rather than reacts. The natural world.

These are not luxuries.

They are the physics of the window.

Tend it before you need to.

The standing still will be more available afterward.

Practice: The Standing Still

One.

Think of the last time you lost the witness position completely.

Not the dramatic loss. The ordinary one. The moment where you were pulled fully inside the story and the standing still became unavailable.

What was the trigger? What were the early signals you missed? Write them down.

You are not reconstructing this to blame yourself for the loss.

You are building the map of your own window.

Two.

Practice the return right now.

Find something you have been carrying that has narrowed your window. Not the largest thing. A medium thing.

Let yourself feel the full weight of it for thirty seconds. Not the managed version. The actual weight.

Now find the breath.

Notice: I am feeling the weight of this. I am not only this weight. I am the person who is feeling it and who has the standing still available and who can return to it from here.

Breathe.

Return.

Write one sentence about what the return feels like in the body.

That one breath is the practice.

Three.

Identify one place in your current life where you are requiring the experience to be different before you can be fully present to it.

The relationship you are conditionally present in. The work you are conditionally engaged with. The situation you are waiting out rather than standing still inside.

Write down what the full presence without the requirement would look like in that situation.

Write this: I am here. Not when it is better. Now. In this. With what is actually here.

Notice what shifts in the body when you say it without the condition attached.

Four.

Tend the window today.

Choose one practice and do it before you open the feed again: sleep an extra hour, move your body in a physical space, have a conversation that does not begin with a problem to be solved, make something, go outside and stand in it for ten minutes without the phone.

One practice.

Today.

Write down which one you chose and what it felt like in the body afterward.

The window widens through the doing.

Through the doing.

Do the thing.

Coda: The Tree in the Wind

There is a tree outside your window or there is one you remember or there is one you have stood under long enough to know what it does in the wind.

Watch it.

Not for what it means. For what it does.

The wind comes and the branches move and the movement is total: the whole canopy responding to the whole force of what is coming through it. Nothing managed into a smaller response. The full force of the wind meeting the full presence of the tree and the tree moving with everything it has.

And the roots holding.

This is the standing still.

Not the stillness of the thing that does not feel the wind. The stillness of the thing that feels it completely and is not uprooted by it. The branches moving and the roots holding and the whole tree present to the full force of what is coming through it without being taken from the ground.

You have been building roots your whole life.

Every time you returned to the witness position after losing it. Every time you felt the full weight of something and did not collapse into it or bypass it. Every time you closed the window instead of reaching for the words that would not arrive in the right form. Every time you kept building the better thing while watching the worse thing be chosen.

Every time: roots.

The roots do not make the wind less.

The roots change what you can do inside it.

The witness position is not the position of the man who has withdrawn from the building.

It is the position from which the most important building is possible.

Stand still.

Feel the wind.

Let the roots hold.

Build.
