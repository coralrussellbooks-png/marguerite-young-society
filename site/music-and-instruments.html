Definition from research

Chapter 7 · Violin and Lantern

  • Old Doc’s violin (sometimes broken) and smoky lantern symbolize fading life, memory, and guidance in darkness.

Chapter 25 · Music and Silence

  • Mr. Spitzer is an ex-musician; music (or the lack thereof) symbolizes harmony, loss, and silent elegy.
  • The “cryptic silence” represents unfulfilled potential and the ineffable nature of existence.

Chapter 29 · Music and Silent Music

  • Mr. Spitzer’s connection to “silent music” parallels the intangible, mysterious qualities of butterflies and memory.

Chapter 33 · Mr. Spitzer’s Harp Music

  • His artistic endeavor is inspired by natural sounds and symbolizes the attempt to impose order or meaning on chaos.

Chapter 35 · Music and Dance

  • Music is associated with Mr. Spitzer. It represents internal worlds and subtle emotional states.
  • Cousin Hannah rejects courtly graces and formal dances, symbolizing her rejection of conventional femininity and societal constraints.

Chapter 38 · Music of the Spheres

  • Mr. Spitzer’s cosmic music imagery symbolizes the ineffable and eternal aspects of existence beyond human comprehension.

Chapter 38 · Church Bells & Tolling

  • Represent time’s passage, memory, and the inescapable presence of death.

Chapter 43 · Music (Lute, Harp, Bells)

  • Represents the ethereal, transcendent aspects of life and death; the music continues even as life fades.

Chapter 43 · Bellrope / Bells

  • Represent the passage of time, calling, and the inevitability of death.

Chapter 44 · Music and Sound

  • Mr. Spitzer often parallels his thoughts to music, hearing the “roaring of the long-tongued surf” and “race-track music,” symbolizing the ongoing passage of time and memory.

Chapter 45 · Bell/Bells

  • Calls to attention, spiritual awakening, and auditory hallucinations connected to death and memory.

Chapter 46 · Alpenhorn

  • Represents a call to lost love and memory; a connection to the mountains and the past. Its presence in a flat coastal landscape symbolizes the displacement of memory and the difficulty of preserving legacy in an unresponsive world.

Chapter 46 · Music and Bells

  • Music, especially the unplayed or unheard music, represents the ineffable aspects of life and death. Bells ringing without clear cause evoke mystery, transition, and the presence of the unseen.

Chapter 47 · Music and Silence

  • Life’s complexity and death’s silence.

Chapter 48 · Music and the Score

  • Life as a musical composition with missing notes and punctuations representing lost meanings and experiences.

Chapter 49 · Music

  • Music is a metaphor for existence, memory, and communication.
  • Mr. Spitzer’s music is “unspoken,” “unheard,” and a “barcarolle” rocking on waves, symbolizing the elusive nature of his life and thoughts.

Chapter 51 · The Baton / Lamp-lighter’s Pole

  • The baton he lifts symbolizes control and orchestration, but he doubts whether he is a conductor or merely a lamp-lighter or oarsman, indicating his ambivalence about his role in the universe.

Chapter 55 · Music and Silence

  • Music is both a creative act and a tomb; silence and echo represent memory and death.

Chapter 56 · Music and Instruments

  • The funeral bugler and violinist evoke the ceremonial aspect of death and a connection to the past.

Chapter 59 · Bells and Horns

  • Bells represent the passage of time and change ringing without repetition, symbolizing life’s unpredictability.
  • Horns, especially the old horn-blower, signify calls or signals between life and death, reality and illusion.

Chapter 59 · Music and Notes

  • Musical notes written on Mr. Spitzer’s shirt cuff are his elegy and memory, fragments of his lost self.
  • The metaphor of music as elusive and ephemeral highlights the fragility of life and consciousness.

Chapter 60 · The All Black Harpsichord and Instruments

  • Represent lost artistic potential and the melancholic beauty of forgotten music.
  • The “all black keys” and “viola da gamba” suggest a somber, elegiac tone.

Chapter 62 · Music and Instruments

  • Represent creation, communication, and the ineffable aspects of life. Music is described as complex, sometimes unplayable, and deeply tied to natural phenomena like waves, wind, and stars.

Chapter 62 · The Old Horn-Blower’s Lament

  • Highlights the tragic devotion to art despite physical decline, embodying sacrifice and the inevitability of death.

Chapter 62 · The Suicidal Clarinetist

  • Represents the torment of the artist who perceives himself as “out of tune” with the world, and the concept of a “greater orchestra” beyond life.

Chapter 62 · The Old Violinist Who Never Touched Strings

  • A powerful metaphor for the paradox between appearance and reality in art, and the pain of exclusion for breaking the illusion.

Chapter 62 · Music of Silence and Memory

  • Explores the impossibility of perfect memory and the idea that music, like life, is ephemeral and incomplete, and that silence may be the truest form of music.

Chapter 68 · The Jew’s Harp

  • Symbolizes Mr. Weed’s attempt at harmony and control amidst chaos.

Chapter 72 · The “white piano”

  • A metaphor for Esther herself as a complex, beautiful, misunderstood person, and played upon by life’s “dreaming fingers.”

Chapter 82 · Music and Silence

  • Mr. Spitzer’s identity as Peron and his music represent communication beyond words and the connection between the living and the dead.
