On there being no safety.

Young closes the volume with the observation “that there was no such thing as safety—not even for” the great captain, certainly not for Mr. Spitzer. Helen Keller's formulation is the most compressed version of this truth: security is mostly a superstition. It does not exist in nature, nor do the children of men as a whole experience it. Life is either a daring adventure or nothing. Cousin Hannah chose the daring adventure. She paid its costs. The costs were real. The adventure was also real. Neither cancels the other. This is what the first forty-four chapters have been showing you, and what the next thirty-eight will deepen: that reality and its costs are inseparable, that you cannot have the adventure without the superstition of safety being stripped away, and that the stripping is both the loss and the life.
