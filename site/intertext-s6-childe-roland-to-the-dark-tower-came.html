Definition from research

Category: optional. Browning's poem is named once, in a passage whose meaning it deepens.

Robert Browning's "Childe Roland to the Dark Tower Came" (1855) takes its title from a line sung by Edgar in King Lear. A knight crosses a blasted landscape toward a dark tower where others have failed. The poem ends as he sets his horn to his lips and blows.

In the novel, Catherine asks Cousin Hannah to search the Alps for her lost husband. The list of places ends: "search for him where Childe Roland had blown his horn before a dark tower and where no other sound had ever been heard" (chapter 30). Browning's quest becomes one more address in a catalog of places to look, a place of silence where the search goes unanswered.

The link to King Lear runs through the novel too. The bus-driver's circle sees the old doctor as "A fool, Lear in the storm" (chapter 7). Browning found his quest in Lear's storm, and Young finds it in an Iowa night where a drunken doctor drives into a ditch.
