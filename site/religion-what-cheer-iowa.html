What Cheer, Iowa

Characters: Miss MacIntosh

Allusions: Iowa, Indiana, and the Midwest

Definition from research

The faith of What Cheer, Iowa, is plain and practical, close to the land and suspicious of anything ornate. The people are Christian and churchgoing, but they distrust grand ceremony, lavish vestments, and ritual for its own sake. Miss MacIntosh, who comes from there, carries the whole temperament with her: "truth to nature," and no patience for fancy.

Their religion is mostly a matter of conduct. It prizes honesty, self-reliance, hard work, and modesty, and it teaches that labor and suffering are part of God's design. There is little appetite for speculation and real wariness toward dreams and visions. God here is practical and sometimes severe, imagined as a harness-maker or carpenter rather than a distant king. The afterlife is granted but left vague. What matters is the day survived, the season turned, the work done. Even in so close a community, faith stays a lonely, private business, and no one pretends to know more than a person can know.
