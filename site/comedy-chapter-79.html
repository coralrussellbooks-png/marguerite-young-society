Characters: Esther Longtree

Definition from research

This chapter delivers the most sustained piece of absurdist invention: Esther's conception by silver spoon. Because her parents never touched, a third woman was hired to carry "the little spoon with the elixir of life" back and forth between them across the kitchen for seven years. The whole elaborate machinery is described with a straight face, which is what makes it funny. Her uncle, "laughing until his face turned purple," tells her she "came out of a little silver spoon," and explains the choice of silver as "the greatest conductor of heat and cold," mock-scientific reasoning applied to nonsense.

The go-between herself is a comic-grotesque figure in men's shoes, hat, and coat, smoking a cigar, walking a line that was "oblique," a farmer "plowing no furrow." Young repeatedly deflates the cosmic strangeness by insisting it is ordinary, "nothing to talk of," something that happens "in the barns and stables" all the time. That refusal to be impressed by the impossible is itself the joke.
