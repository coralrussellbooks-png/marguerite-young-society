Characters: Miss MacIntosh

Philosophy: Augustine and Christian Theology

Allusions: The Bible

Definition from research

"The road to Hell... is very broad and very wide and is paved with bones and good intentions," and "life is this valley of dry bones," braids two passages: Matthew 7's broad road to destruction and Ezekiel 37's valley of dry bones, the easy way to ruin laid over a landscape of the spiritually dead.

"The Devil does not always wear horns" opens Miss MacIntosh's shrewd theology of evil: the Devil can be charming, can quote scripture as he did to Jesus in the wilderness, once "sat at the right hand of the throne of God," and is easily confused with the brothers he resembles, the one who fell, the one unfallen, the one who died for us.

She holds the King James Bible untrustworthy, "made for the vanity of earthly kings" and the uniting of empire, a political book more than a holy one.

"The Devil... is God's ape," she says, wearing God's suit and a crown of stars, and "better the Devil you know than the Devil you will never know." The Devil can only mimic God, never match him, and a known evil is safer than an unknown one.

"the highway to Hell is paved with more good intentions than shining cobblestones" reworks the old proverb, traced to Bernard of Clairvaux, that good intentions alone pave the way to ruin.
