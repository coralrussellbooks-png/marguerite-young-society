Chapter 50

On Chapter 50 of Marguerite Young’s novel Miss MacIntosh, My Darling (1965). Part of Christianity and Religion in Miss MacIntosh, My Darling.
By Coral Russell · Updated November 24, 2026

Characters: Joachim Spitzer

Allusions: Revelation and the Apocalypse

"make of man a lyre without strings, a trumpet without a throat" asks whether God could be so careless as to make a creature unable to fulfill its purpose, an instrument that cannot sound.

“His breath was his spirit”: Spirit comes from the Latin spiritus, breath. “And the LORD God formed man of the dust of the ground, and breathed into his nostrils the breath of life; and man became a living soul” (Genesis 2:7). See Genesis. Earlier in Chapter 5.

Bees that "sang bright hosannas in the winter air" lift the Palm Sunday cry of praise, hosanna, into the ordinary hum of insects, the holy word set loose in nature.

“the door of truth which was covered with a golden disc”: “The door of the True is covered with a golden disk. Open that, O Pûshan, that we may see the nature of the True” (Isa Upanishad 15, trans. F. Max Müller, 1879). See Hinduism and Keys, Doors, and Locks.

“an empire enduring a thousand years”: The Nazis called their state the Thousand-Year Reich; it lasted twelve years, 1933 to 1945. In Revelation the saints reign with Christ “a thousand years” (Revelation 20:4). See Revelation and the Apocalypse.

“begot this pearl with the light burning within”: A pearl forms when an oyster coats an irritant with layer upon layer of nacre; the grain of sand of legend is rarely the cause, more often a parasite or a bit of tissue. “The kingdom of heaven is like unto a merchant man, seeking goodly pearls: Who, when he had found one pearl of great price, went and sold all that he had, and bought it” (Matthew 13:45-46). See The Bible.

“that earth which swallows the horse and the rider”: “The horse and his rider hath he thrown into the sea” (Exodus 15:1), and “the earth opened her mouth, and swallowed them up” (Numbers 16:32). See The Bible.

Mr. Spitzer is "not the book of life, not even the book of death," casting himself out of both of Revelation's ledgers, too slight to be written in either.

“a handkerchief dropped from the hand of God”: “Or I guess it is the handkerchief of the Lord, / A scented gift and remembrancer designedly dropt” (Walt Whitman, “Song of Myself,” section 6, Leaves of Grass). See American Writers.

“a light upon the water”: “Who layeth the beams of his chambers in the waters: who maketh the clouds his chariot: who walketh upon the wings of the wind” (Psalm 104:3). See The Bible and The Sea and Water.

He is "the mere flyleaf stuck between two passages or testaments, the Old and the New," a blank page between the halves of Scripture, between patriarch and dove.

“between the patriarch and the cloud or water bird”: “Also he sent forth a dove from him, to see if the waters were abated from off the face of the ground” (Genesis 8:8). See Genesis and The Bible. Earlier in Chapter 29.

He is caught "between the millstone above him and the millstone below him," the crushing stones of Matthew 21.

She should have "passed over the borderline without paying a toll," an image drawn from Charon's ferry rather than the gospel, the coin owed for passage to the land of the dead.
