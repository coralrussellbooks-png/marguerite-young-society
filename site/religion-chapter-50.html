Characters: Joachim Spitzer

Allusions: Revelation and the Apocalypse

Definition from research

"make of man a lyre without strings, a trumpet without a throat" asks whether God could be so careless as to make a creature unable to fulfill its purpose, an instrument that cannot sound.

Mr. Spitzer is "not the book of life, not even the book of death," casting himself out of both of Revelation's ledgers, too slight to be written in either.

He is "the mere flyleaf stuck between two passages or testaments, the Old and the New," a blank page between the halves of Scripture, between patriarch and dove.

He is caught "between the millstone above him and the millstone below him," the crushing stones of Matthew 21.

She should have "passed over the borderline without paying a toll," an image drawn from Charon's ferry rather than the gospel, the coin owed for passage to the land of the dead.

Bees that "sang bright hosannas in the winter air" lift the Palm Sunday cry of praise, hosanna, into the ordinary hum of insects, the holy word set loose in nature.
