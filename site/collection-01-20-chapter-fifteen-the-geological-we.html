Chapter Fifteen: The Geological We

← Chapter Fourteen: What Cheer: The Youngian Polity · Radical Immanence Collection · Chapter Sixteen: The Long Con and the Youngian Corrective →

Characters: Miss MacIntosh

There is a problem at the center of Young's grief pillar that the pillar itself does not resolve. If grief is permanent and cumulative and does not process, and if the self that carries it is plural and unstable and finite, then something does not add up. The individual human life lasts decades. The grief Young describes accumulates across centuries. The vessel is too small for what it is being asked to hold.

This is not a flaw in Young's system. It is an invitation to think at a different scale.

Human beings with their little lives, seventy or eighty or ninety years against the age of what surrounds them, cannot hold the full weight of permanent cumulative grief without being destroyed by it. The attempt to hold it all, to be the complete vessel for every loss the world contains, is not integrity. It is a different kind of false foundation, the heroic self that shoulders everything, which is as constructed and as fragile as Miss MacIntosh's wig.

Young does not ask this of us. She asks something more honest and more difficult: that we hold our portion with complete attention, in full awareness that it is a portion, and that the larger carrying is happening in something older and more patient and less easily crushed than any individual self.

That something is the planet we call Earth.

Not the Earth as a metaphor. Not the Earth as poetic consolation, the romantic gesture toward nature as healer, as balm, as the thing that puts human suffering in perspective by dwarfing it. The Earth as the only archive large enough and old enough to hold loss at the scale Young is actually describing.

Hundreds of millions of years of accumulation.

Extinction after extinction.

Transformation after transformation.

Nothing resolved.

Everything carried forward in a different form.

The geological record is permanent cumulative grief made literal: layer upon layer, each one pressing down on the one before, all of it still present, none of it gone, none of it processed into something more manageable than what it actually was.

The Earth does not accept its losses. It sediments them.

This reframes the witness. Young's witness is a human ethical stance, the one who stays in the room with the contradiction and does not flinch and does not conclude. But the Earth is the original witness. It has been staying in the room with contradiction longer than any human philosophical tradition has existed. It does not conclude. It does not move through stages toward resolution. It carries forward, transformed but not healed, bearing in its strata the complete record of everything that has been lost, every species that will not return, every sea that has receded, every landscape that has become something unrecognizable to what it was. The human witness is a brief and partial approximation of a practice the Earth has been sustaining for longer than human language has existed to name it.

The WE that can hold permanent grief at scale is not a human WE. It is a geological one. The geological WE is not a construction. It was not voted into existence or declared by a founding document or ratified by a sufficient majority. It is the condition. The landscape that preceded every human political arrangement and will outlast every one that follows. The Ohio River carried its water before there was an Ohio. The glaciers that shaped the terrain of what is now the American Midwest did their work and retreated and left their record in the land without asking anyone's permission or requiring anyone's acknowledgment.

What does it mean to build a political WE that knows this? Not a WE that worships the land in the romantic nationalist sense, which is its own kind of false foundation and has produced its own catastrophes. A WE that is honest about its scale. That understands the human political arrangement as a temporary and provisional structure built on and within a much older and more patient carrying. That builds its institutions with the awareness that the grief they are managing is not theirs to resolve, that it belongs to a longer story than any human institution can tell, and that the honest political act is not resolution but honest partial carrying, in relationship with the landscape that holds what the human WE cannot.

Young's sentence hasn't ended because the Earth's sentence hasn't ended. The carrying continues. The layers accumulate. Nothing is resolved. Everything is present. That is the geological WE. That is what Young's philosophy makes available when it is thought all the way through.
