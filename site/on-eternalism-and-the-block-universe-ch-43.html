On eternalism and the block universe.

Eternalism is the philosophical view that all times (past, present, and future) are equally real, forming a four-dimensional block that simply exists rather than flowing. Your grandmother at twenty is as real as you are now; she is just located at a different coordinate in the block. The dead are not absent; they are present at their own temporal addresses. Young's novel does not argue for eternalism explicitly, but it inhabits its logic: Catherine's memories are as real as the present moment, Cousin Hannah's life is as present in its dying as in its triumph, and the question of whether the lid is lowering or raising becomes genuinely open when no moment is more real than any other.
