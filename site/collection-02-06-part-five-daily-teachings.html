Part Five: Daily Teachings

← Part Four: Short Meditations · Radical Immanence Collection · Part Six: The Twelve Pillars →

These ten pieces are longer meditations on ordinary difficulty. They are not advice. They do not promise resolution. They are written to sit beside you in the specific, unnamed weights that most of us carry without much acknowledgment.

Read one when something is hard and you cannot find the words for it. Come back to the same one more than once if it is speaking to you. Let it be company, not instruction.

The Weight You Woke Up With

You woke up with something heavy on you this morning. Not a specific problem, necessarily. Just a weight. The kind that was there before you even remembered what day it was.

And maybe you tried to shake it off. Told yourself to get moving, or counted your blessings, or checked your phone to find something to think about instead. And the weight was still there.

Here is what is also true: you do not have to explain it. You do not have to trace it back to its origin or assign it a name. Some mornings the body is just carrying more than usual. That is not a malfunction. That is not a sign that something is fundamentally wrong with you. That is a person, on a particular morning, under a particular weight.

You can be under it and still be here. You can be under it and still be okay in the way that okay actually is, not the performed version, but the real one. The one that just means: still here. Still breathing. Still in it.

The weight is real. And here is also real. Both things at once. That is enough.

What is the weight you are carrying this morning, and what would it mean to simply acknowledge it without needing it to go anywhere?

The Conversation You Keep Replaying

There is a conversation you keep going back to. Maybe it was yesterday. Maybe it was three years ago. You replay it, and each time you find the thing you should have said, or the thing you wish you had not, or the moment where everything shifted and you did not know how to stop it.

You have been the prosecutor and the defendant in that conversation many times now. You know the transcript by heart.

Here is what is worth saying, not to fix it, just to sit with you in it: the replaying is not stupidity. It is not weakness. It is the mind trying to find safety in a moment that did not feel safe. Trying to locate the place where it could have gone differently. That is a very human thing to do.

But the conversation is over. It ended. And you are here now, in a different moment, one that is not that moment, even when it feels like it is.

What is here right now is not that room, not that exchange, not that version of you scrambling for the right words. What is here is this. Just this. And this is enough.

What conversation are you still in? What would it mean to let it have ended?

Here Is Enough.

The Thing You Haven't Finished

There is something you have not finished. You know what it is. It has been sitting somewhere, in a drawer, on a list, in the back of your mind, waiting. And every time you think about it, there is a small contraction. A little pull of guilt, or grief, or just the dull ache of a thing undone.

Maybe you have told yourself a story about what the not-finishing means. That you are not disciplined enough, or committed enough, or that you never follow through. That this is evidence of something wrong in you.

Here is a different possibility. That the not-finishing is information, not indictment. Maybe the thing ran out of season. Maybe your capacity went somewhere it was more urgently needed. Maybe you changed and the thing did not change with you. Maybe it is still waiting and you will return to it. Maybe you will not.

All of that is allowed. A life is not a checklist. You are not a checklist.

What is here right now is not the undone thing. What is here is you, showing up, carrying it honestly. That is not failure. That is enough.

What is the story you have been telling yourself about the unfinished thing? Is the story true?

Here Is Enough.

Loving Someone You Can't Reach

There is someone you love and right now you cannot reach them. Maybe they are gone. Maybe they have moved far away, not in miles, but in the way people move when something has happened between you. Maybe they are right there and still unreachable, and that particular distance is its own kind of grief.

You are holding love that does not have anywhere to land right now. And that is one of the harder human experiences. Love with no clear address.

It does not mean the love is wrong. It does not mean you failed at the relationship. It does not mean you need to do something with it immediately. Love that cannot reach its person does not just disappear. It stays in you. It moves through you. Sometimes it comes out sideways, as anger or silence or a sudden need to do something with your hands.

You do not have to resolve it today. You do not have to know what the love means for what comes next.

You can just hold it, the way you hold anything you are not ready to put down. Here. With it. Still loving. That is enough.

Who is the person you cannot reach right now? What does the love feel like when it has nowhere to go?

Here Is Enough.

The Tiredness Sleep Doesn't Fix

You slept. And you are still tired. Not sleepy, necessarily. But tired in the way that lives below the surface. In your chest, or your shoulders, or just in the effort it takes to get through ordinary things that should not be this hard.

This kind of tired has a different source. It is not about hours of sleep. It is about how long you have been holding something without a break. How long you have been in a situation without resolution. How long you have been the person who keeps going because someone has to.

And you have been doing that for a while now.

This is not a promise that rest is coming. And this is not a suggestion to take better care of yourself, because you probably already know that, and knowing it and being able to do it are different things.

The tiredness is real. It is a completely honest response to what you have been carrying. It does not mean you are failing. It means you have been working hard at something that does not show up on anyone's radar as work.

You are here. Tired and here. And here is enough.

What are you carrying that is not counted as work? What would it mean for someone to acknowledge that weight?

Here Is Enough.

The Grief Without a Name

Something is sitting with you today and you do not know what to call it. It is not quite sadness. It is not quite anxiety. It is more like a low weather system that moved in without an announcement and has not lifted.

Maybe it is cumulative. All the small losses that did not get a ceremony. The version of your life that quietly became impossible. The person you were before a particular thing happened. The relationship that ended not dramatically but just gradually, like a signal going out.

None of those things got a funeral. No one brought food. There was no official moment of grief. And so the grief exists without a container, moving through your days, showing up in the afternoons, sitting on the edge of things.

You do not have to name it to honor it. You do not have to understand the grief to let it be real. You can just say: something is heavy today, and let that be true without solving it.

The heaviness is here. You are here with it. That act of staying, of not running from it, of just letting it be present without demanding it leave, that is not passivity. That is presence. And presence is enough.

What is the unnamed thing that is sitting with you today?

The Morning You Don't Want

You do not want this morning. You would prefer a different one. One where you woke up feeling clear, or rested, or at least not already behind. This is not that morning.

This is the morning where the light is the wrong color, or your body feels wrong in itself, or the day ahead looks like a list of things you do not have the energy to face. And yet here it is, happening, whether you want it or not.

You do not have to want it. That is the thing. You do not have to generate enthusiasm you do not have, or find the silver lining, or decide that today is actually an opportunity. You are allowed to just be in a morning you did not choose and would not have picked.

What is true is: you are in it. The morning is already happening around you. You do not have to perform readiness. You do not have to arrive anywhere other than where you already are.

The unwanted morning is still a morning. You are still here in it. And here, even this here, is enough.

What would it mean to be fully in this morning without needing it to be different?

The Version of You That Didn't Make It

There is a version of you that you thought you would be by now. Maybe it is a career, or a relationship, or a level of peace you imagined you would have reached. Maybe it is just a feeling, a feeling of having figured something out, of being more settled than you are.

That version did not arrive. Or it arrived and then left. Or it is still theoretically coming but the timeline has shifted so many times that you have stopped believing it.

And there is grief in that. Real grief. The loss of an imagined self is a loss. It does not matter that the self was imaginary. The hope was real. The investment was real. The expecting was real.

You are allowed to grieve the person you thought you would be.

And at the same time, and this is not a replacement for the grief: the person you actually are is here. Complicated and tired and still going and not what you planned. That person is real. That person showed up today.

The version that did not make it was a dream. The version that did is enough.

Who is the version of yourself you are grieving? What did that person carry that you miss?

Sitting with Someone Else's Pain

Someone you love is in pain right now and you cannot fix it. You have tried to find the right words. You have offered what you have to offer. And the pain is still there, because it is theirs, and it has its own timeline, and you are not actually able to reach in and remove it.

And that helplessness is its own kind of suffering. To love someone and be unable to do the one thing you want to do, which is make it stop.

Here is something that might be true: your presence is not nothing. Even when you have no words. Even when you sit there not knowing what to do, the sitting is something. The staying is something. The refusal to look away is something.

You do not have to fix the pain to be with the person who is in it. Being with is its own form of help, even when it does not feel like enough.

You are there. You are staying. You care and they know it. That is not nothing. In fact, in the place where fixing is impossible, it might be everything. It is enough.

Where are you staying with someone in their pain right now? What does your presence give them that you have not given yourself credit for?

The Ordinary Wednesday That Is Your Life

This is it. This ordinary Wednesday. The dishes, the commute, the meeting, the meal you made without thinking about it. The evening that arrived before you were ready. The going to bed and the waking up and the doing of it all again.

This is not a rehearsal. You know that. And sometimes that knowledge is crushing, because if this is the actual life, where is the meaning, where is the arrival, where is the moment when it all comes together into something that feels like enough.

The meaning is not somewhere else in your life. It is not waiting at the destination. It is in the doing of the ordinary things with the people you love, or alone if that is what this season is. It is in the noticing. The cup of tea. The window. The way someone said your name today.

It is here. In this unremarkable Wednesday that is, actually, your life. Not a lesser version of it. The real one.

And it is enough.

What is the ordinary Wednesday of your life right now? What in it is actually real?
