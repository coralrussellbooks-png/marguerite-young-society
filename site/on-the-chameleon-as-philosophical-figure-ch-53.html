On the chameleon as philosophical figure.

The chameleon appears in philosophical discourse as early as Plato, who used it to describe the person who has no fixed nature of their own but adopts the color of whatever surrounds them. In the Renaissance it became an emblem of the flatterer, the courtier, the person without principle who reflects back whatever is expected of them. Young uses it differently: Peron's chameleon nature is not a moral failing but an ontological one. He was genuinely multiple, genuinely shifting, not because he was deceptive but because he had no stable center to return to. The freedom and the tragedy of this are inseparable. He could be anything. He was everything. He died by apparent suicide. Young does not explain the connection.
