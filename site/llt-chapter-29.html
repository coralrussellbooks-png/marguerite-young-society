Life, Love, and Truth That Doesn’t Fail

Chapter 29

Other versions: Chapter hub · Research notes · The Wheel and the Spokes

Characters: Catherine Cartwheel · Joachim Spitzer

Pillars: Witnessing Is The Core Ethical Act · Unfinished Is Honest

The Questions

Mr. Spitzer cannot tell the butterfly from the moth. Is that a failure to know, or a refusal to seal a category the world will not hold still?

He calls God a set of “pitiful images” and himself a “dark atheist.” Is that despair, or the refusal to seal even the last construction?

If God is a “castle-building Creator” dreaming the world, is God any different from Catherine dreaming hers?

The butterfly is “immortality within the logic of mortality.” What kind of immortality never finishes becoming?

Mr. Spitzer cannot tell the butterfly from the moth, and the not-telling is not ignorance. The categories will not hold still, the one shading into the other, and he refuses to force a line where the world declines to draw one. This is the Witness again, attending to the ambiguity without sealing it, the same stance that let him leave Catherine’s dream undisturbed and the coachman unconfirmed. Happiness and sorrow are as unsortable in him as butterfly and moth, rarely pure, never cleanly one or the other, and he lets them stay mixed.

The chapter takes this refusal to its largest object: God. Mr. Spitzer is a “dark atheist,” a “believer in nothing,” and it is easy to hear despair in the phrase. But his atheism is the refusal to seal the last construction. God, for him, is a set of “pitiful images,” a “castle-building Creator” dreaming a “chimerical life of butterflies and stars and barnacles and men.” That is to say, God is another builder, another maker of a world, no different in kind from Catherine dreaming hers, and Mr. Spitzer will not seal that builder into the finished, all-explaining, all-good deity whose existence would make the world’s suffering a scandal to be solved. He keeps even God open, and the openness is why the problem of evil does not crush him: there is no sealed omnipotence to hold accountable, only a Creator building castles like everyone else.

This leaves him among “unsoluble mysteries and doubts,” and he does not flee them into a system. The origin of the butterflies, the nature of the soul, the purpose of the chimerical creation, he holds as open questions rather than closing them with an answer. This is Unfinished Is Honest at the scale of the cosmos: the unfinished is not a failure of his understanding to be corrected but the honest shape of what can be known, and a man who sealed it with a theodicy or an atheism-as-certainty would be lying about the size of the dark.

And the butterfly carries the all of it. It is “immortality within the logic of mortality,” a thing that achieves its endlessness precisely by never finishing, caterpillar becoming chrysalis becoming winged psyche and never arriving at a final form. It is the wandering soul, grief’s own emblem, the dead seeking reunion on borrowed wings. The book has been building toward a figure who lives this, forever transforming and never delivered; here it flies through the chapter as an insect Mr. Spitzer cannot classify, immortal exactly because it is unfinished.

What the Chapter Refuses, and the Footing It Offers

The chapter refuses the reading that takes Mr. Spitzer’s “dark atheist” for the book’s despair, a confession that creation is meaningless, and the reading that wants the chapter to solve the problem of evil, to seal God into an answer. Neither the nihilist nor the theodicy is what the chapter offers.

The footing is that Mr. Spitzer refuses to seal even the last construction, holding God as one more builder, a castle-building Creator no different in kind from Catherine dreaming her world, so that the problem of evil dissolves for want of a sealed omnipotence to blame. He keeps the “unsoluble mysteries” open rather than closing them with a system, Unfinished Is Honest at cosmic scale. And the butterfly is immortality that works only by never finishing, the unfinished as the truthful and even the deathless shape. God is another construction, the mysteries stay open, and the unfinished is what endures. Mr. Spitzer classifies nothing, solves nothing, and the chapter leaves the butterfly unnamed on the wing.

For Reflection

What category does one force a line through that the world itself keeps refusing to divide?

If God were understood as one more builder rather than a sealed omnipotence, what problem would stop being a problem?

What in one’s life endures precisely because it has never been allowed to finish?
