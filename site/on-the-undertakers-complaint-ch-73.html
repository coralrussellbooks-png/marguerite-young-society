On the Undertaker’s Complaint

The undertaker complains that Esther brings him no business; she hides her stillborn children in streams and snow and tree stumps rather than delivering them to the graveyard where they belong, where they could be properly recorded, buried, accounted for. This complaint is comic and grotesque and also perfectly calibrated. The undertaker’s business depends on the orderly processing of death: bodies delivered, buried, recorded, charged for. Esther’s grief is disorderly. She refuses to route her children through the system. Her burials are private, dispersed, invisible, which is exactly what makes them beautiful and exactly what makes the detective suspicious. A death that is not processed by the system has not, from the system’s perspective, really been properly mourned. It has been hidden. Esther’s love for her stillborn looks, to the outside, indistinguishable from concealment of a crime.
