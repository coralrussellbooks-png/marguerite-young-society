The lamp-lighter as a figure belongs (Ch 29)

From the reading guide, Chapter 29 · Research: Chapter 29

From the guide

The lamp-lighter as a figure belongs to a vanishing world: gas lamps were being replaced by electric ones throughout the early twentieth century, a transition Miss MacIntosh would have strongly approved of resisting. The old lamp-lighter moving down the dark street “like Jacob with his ladder of dreams reaching to the clouds” is one of Young’s most deliberate anachronisms: a figure from a world without electric light, mourning a man who will never light another lamp, in a novel where Miss MacIntosh has just spent two chapters condemning electric light for destroying the darkness in which certain lives were possible.
