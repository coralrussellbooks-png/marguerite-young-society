Pillar: Pillar 8 The Past Doesn't Go Away · Across every book

or On the two lies about time, and the layered truth that holds you steady against both.

There are two ways to lie about time, and power uses whichever one is nearer to hand. The first lie is the golden past. There was an age when things were whole and pure and good, and it was taken from you, and it can be restored if you follow. The second lie is the clean break. The past is rot, the slate can be wiped, and a new and purified people can begin at year zero with nothing behind them. Nostalgia and revolution look like opposites. They are the same falsehood about time, one facing backward and one facing forward, both promising that the layered, complicated, unromantic past can be either recovered or erased.

Young does not believe in either. Her novel is saturated with the past, but the past in it never recedes and never returns clean. It presses upward through everything, through the mother’s memories and the narrator’s search and the inherited griefs nobody chose. Time in her work is not a road you travel away from or back along. It is sediment. It accumulates. You stand on all of it at once.

The eighth pillar is this sedimentary time. The past is real and it is present, but as accumulation, not as a paradise to recover and not as a slate to clear. You carry all of it, the good and the shameful and the plain, layered in you, without gilding it and without pretending you can start fresh. And that steadies you against both lies. The man selling the golden age cannot move you, because you know the past was never golden. The man selling year zero cannot move you either, because you know the past does not wipe. It only buries, and it buries shallow.

You are made of what came before, all of it, unedited. That is not a cage, and it is not a sin to be cleansed. It is simply the ground you happen to be standing on, and the only honest thing to do with ground is to know what is under your feet.
