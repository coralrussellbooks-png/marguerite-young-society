On the ear as labyrinth.

The inner ear is anatomically labyrinthine: coiled canals, fluid-filled chambers, the semicircular canals that govern balance. Disruption of the inner ear produces vertigo: the world spinning without cause, orientation lost, the ground no longer reliable. Catherine's lostness inside her own ear is both metaphor and phenomenology. She is vertiginous in the deepest sense: she cannot find the stable ground from which to orient herself to what is real, because the very organ of orientation has become the space in which she is imprisoned.
