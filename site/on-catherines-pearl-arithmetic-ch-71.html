On Catherine’s Pearl Arithmetic

Catherine’s belief that lost pearls multiply is not presented as symptom of delusion alone; it is presented as a complete philosophical system, internally consistent if you accept its premises. Young is drawing on a long tradition of associating pearls with eternity and sorrow: pearls form around an irritant, are produced by suffering, and are associated in many traditions with tears. Catherine’s pearls are her lost estate, not what she owns but what she has lost that nevertheless persists. The chauffeur James returning thirteen when twelve were lost is the novel’s version of the loaves and fishes: abundance from subtraction, infinity from loss.
