On the Nuclear Bomb and the Cold War

The little toy salesman’s prophecy (that the bad men will “blow up the world some day of calm just for fun”) and the imagery of burning sunlight blackening his skin, of the whole human race turning into smoke, are read by To All My Darlings as references to the atomic bomb and the Cold War. The United States dropped atomic bombs on Hiroshima and Nagasaki in August 1945. The Cold War nuclear arms race that followed produced the doctrine of Mutually Assured Destruction: if one side launched, both sides would be destroyed. Young was writing this passage in the years between Hiroshima and the Cuban Missile Crisis, when this annihilation was not theoretical but procedurally planned. The little toy salesman has already been through the end of the world. He has seen the ship sink. He knows what the bad men are capable of.
