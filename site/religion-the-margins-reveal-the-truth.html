Themes: Illusion and Reality

Definition from research

The people who do not fit are, in radical immanence, not exceptions but evidence, the places where the seams of the constructed world show. Young crowds her margins with such figures. The Australian headhunter, caught between the bush and the cathedral, sculpts saints out of barnacles and dreams of a Rome he has never seen, and in his liminal confusion the reader watches belief being assembled. The bus driver named Moses prays and drinks at once, a folk faith with none of its seams hidden. Esther, of mixed heritage and endless loss, the hangman who calls himself Christian, the garden built for the blind who read the world by touch, all sit at the edges where the categories fail. They are not problems for the novel to solve. They are how the novel sees.
