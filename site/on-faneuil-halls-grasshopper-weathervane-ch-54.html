On Faneuil Hall's grasshopper weathervane.

The grasshopper weathervane atop Faneuil Hall has been a Boston landmark since 1742, commissioned by Peter Faneuil and made by the coppersmith Shem Drowne. It is four feet long and has hollow sections: used over the centuries to hide documents, including news clippings and time capsule notes, placed there during repairs. In 1974 it was stolen and recovered within days. The weathervane is one of Boston's oldest surviving objects and has become a test of local identity: if you can identify it as Faneuil Hall's symbol, you are from Boston. Young's Mr. Spitzer, navigating a Boston that keeps rearranging itself, is the kind of man who knows the grasshopper. He just cannot reliably find the building it sits on anymore.
