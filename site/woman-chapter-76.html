Woman in Young's Magnum Opus

Chapter 76

Characters: Esther Longtree

Esther hides her "pupa, her Little Tom Thumb" in the mule's ear at the start of this chapter, wishing to disappear "under a small leaf," and the vulnerability she carries is the interrupted birth folded into a longing for erasure, a woman so exposed she would vanish under a flower. She names herself "widow often, an old maid always, a wife never," and the phrase is the social marginalization of a woman who fits none of the roles, and it is the plural, uncontainable self the book prefers, a person who is many things at once and cannot be filed as any single one.

Her stillborn children drift as "snowflakes drifting in eternity," "robes, bloodless," "hovering at the ivory gates between life and death," and the spectral infants are the interrupted birth turned into a haunting, the losses kept present at the threshold the book reserves for its dead. They intrude even on her intercourse, the "small brown hand moving over her, stealing her life," and the violation of past reproductive trauma into present sexuality is the book's boundary between life and death giving way inside the most intimate act.

The detective pursues her toward a trial for "mass infanticide," and Esther's realization that she "would strike at the living men" rather than the shadowy embryos turns the accusation back on the whole arrangement, a woman refusing to be the sole culprit of a violence men set in motion. Her memory that "her mother had killed her" carries the intergenerational transmission of guilt and harm, and the whole chapter is the diffusion of agency the book keeps insisting on, blame that will not settle on one hand because no loss here has a single author.

Esther calls herself the "best detective in the infinite," knitting a "black hood" for a child "white as melting snow," and the reclaiming of the investigator's role is a woman taking authorship of her own story back from the men who would file it. Her life is "monotonous" yet "beautiful and wild," and at the chapter's close she quits her job to "answer her own questions," a self-determination that refuses the surveillance and the trial alike, a woman who will hold her own inquiry rather than submit to the world's, and remain, pregnant always, the body the arrangement cannot close its case on.
