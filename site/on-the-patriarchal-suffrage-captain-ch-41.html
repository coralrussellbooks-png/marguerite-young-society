On the patriarchal suffrage captain.

The oxymoron Young applies to Cousin Hannah in this chapter, patriarchal suffrage captain, is one of the novel's sharpest formulations. A woman who fought against patriarchy but who had to operate within patriarchal structures of authority, who had to make herself into a captain (a rank, a hierarchy, a masculine word) in order to lead, who had to be legible to power in order to challenge it. The suffrage movement contained this contradiction throughout its history: the demand for rights within a system that denied rights, using the language of that system to argue against it. Cousin Hannah embodies this. Catherine, who refuses all legibility, refuses this contradiction by refusing the contest entirely.
