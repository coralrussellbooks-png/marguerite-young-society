Characters: Miss MacIntosh · Mr. Titus Bonebreaker

Allusions: Angels and Archangels · Genesis · Judaism · Revelation and the Apocalypse · The Book of Job

Definition from research

Mr. Bonebreaker's long sermon is a tissue of scripture. He gathers "the stray lamb to the fold" (Isaiah 40, Luke 15), calls God's discipline love, "for whom the Lord loveth, the Lord chasteneth" (Hebrews 12), and promises a man will be "as the shadow of a great rock in a weary land" (Isaiah 32).

His apocalypse is Revelation's: the Last Day "with trumpets blowing and angels shouting," the good divided from the evil "as the sheep from the goats, as the wheat from the tares" (Matthew 25 and 13), the saved riding "into heaven on cherubim" through the ivory gates, the lake turned to blood and to "sea of crystal," the unsaved cast out like "whited sepulchres" (Matthew 23).

He warns that God "is tired of man" and "will wipe old Chicago clean as a plate, just as He did the old Jerusalem, the old Nineveh," cities judged and destroyed, and that "men shall be crushed before the moth" (Job 4), for "the wrath of God is revealed from heaven" (Romans 1).

His figures run through the whole of Scripture: Hagar and her son the "wild ass among men" (Genesis 16), Eve's fig leaf and Cain's murder of Abel, a God who "has reproved kings," the promise "to hoar hairs will I carry you" (Isaiah 46), and "God speaking out of the whirlwind" (Job 38).

He turns even Miss MacIntosh's body into text, quoting the Song of Solomon over her mastectomy, "we have a little sister and she hath no breasts," bidding the barren rejoice and "enlarge their baldness like the eagle" (Micah 1), for "God loves the shorn lamb."

Between prophecies he drops into the language of baseball, urging the crowd to "pitch for God's sake at the eleventh inning," and claims to have "put away childish things" and "triumphed over death," his reward a "heavenly pavilion." His creed is that "the perishing meat of this world" is "nought," and only "love will take away an aspect of death."

His apocalypse keeps its images to the end, "the end of the world... like a shadow running backward," the sun going out and the stars falling and "the serpent bite without enchantment."
