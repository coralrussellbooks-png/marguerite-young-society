Definition from research

Set all of it together and a shape appears. Young is not a Christian novelist in any narrow sense, though Christianity is her deepest well. She gathers every tradition she touches, the Bible and the Quran, the Buddhist illusion of the separate self and the Ubuntu web that binds all souls, the Greek ferryman and the Hebrew prophets, into one religion of her own making. Its creed, if it has one, is the line the book keeps repeating, that "every heart is the other heart," that no one is finally alone. Its God is finite and imagining. Its sacred is close at hand. Its scripture is every scripture, reworked.

And in the end Young uses religion not to prove God or to deny Him but to hold open the search that outlasts both. Vera sets out after "truth that does not fail" and comes home to ambivalence, and that is not the book failing but the book telling the truth. The many references are not answers. They are the record of people asking, believing, doubting, and enduring, in the only words large enough for the asking. Young's own religion was poetic and cosmic and never theological, always on the move, and so is her novel, which turns every faith it touches into one more way of standing in the world and wondering what is real.
