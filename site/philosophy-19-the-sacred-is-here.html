The Philosophy in MMMD

Chapter 19. The Sacred Is Here

← Previous · Philosophy of MMMD Home · Next →

See also: Pillar 10 The Sacred Is Here

Characters: Catherine Cartwheel · Esther Longtree · Joachim Spitzer

Allusions: Deafness and Sign Language

Chapters discussed: 82

Definition from research

Meaning is here, in the actual world underfoot, not in a promised beyond.

The novel’s great systems all point elsewhere. Catherine’s dreams point to the dead. Hannah’s crusades point to distant lands and mountaintops. Esther’s hope points to a birth that never comes. Even the monist vision of the first chapter points to “some omniscient eye” that no one possesses.

Against these, the novel keeps returning to small things. The frog musician’s request for a light. Mr. Spitzer’s respect for damaged trunks. Esther’s crushing handshake. And at the end, the deaf man hearing “the fishes whispering at the bottoms of rivers” (ch. 82).

The final wedding in the last chapter gathers this principle into a single list. The guests are to be “little Johnny-jump-up and little jack-in-the-pulpit and the snapdragon and four o’clock and whippoorwill and bluejay and little jenny wren,” the waters and the clouds, “and the old mule that climbed over the fence” (ch. 82). Earlier in the novel, lists were how experience broke systems. Here the list has become an invitation. Nothing is excluded, and nothing is too small.
