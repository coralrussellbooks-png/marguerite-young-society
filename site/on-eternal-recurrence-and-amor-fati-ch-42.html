On eternal recurrence and amor fati.

Nietzsche introduced eternal recurrence in The Gay Science and developed it in Thus Spoke Zarathustra as a test of how life-affirming a person truly is. If every moment of your life will recur infinitely, can you love it, not merely accept it, but actively want it again? Amor fati, love of fate, is the attitude of a person who answers yes. The Ubermensch, Nietzsche's higher human type, is capable of this love. Young's Cousin Hannah is not quite Nietzsche's Ubermensch, but she is close: a person who lived with such intensity and commitment that the eternal recurrence of her life, for all its pain and sacrifice and the broken heart at the end, would be a life worth choosing again. Mr. Spitzer is the counterexample. He would not choose again. He is not even sure he chose the first time.
