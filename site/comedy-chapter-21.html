Characters: Miss MacIntosh · Vera Cartwheel

Definition from research

After the long night, Miss MacIntosh stands dressed, wig shining, in unusually good humor, and delivers a lecture as if nothing has happened. Vera has been very sick and unusually stubborn. Dancing is never to be encouraged, “not even,” she says, pausing and smiling, “when there is no one, no partner.” Her proverb for the torn dress is slightly bent, the highway to Hell paved with more good intentions than shining cobblestones.

The comedy is dark and deliberate. The cheerful voice, the proverbs, and the verdict that the dress cannot be mended and must go to the ash bin are all a performance of normality laid over what Vera saw. Vera answers with a performance of her own, resolving to treat Miss MacIntosh as an ordinary creature in the daylight. Two people agree, without a word, to act in the same play.
