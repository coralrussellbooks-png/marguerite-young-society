Chapter Twelve: The Architecture of the Full System

← Part Four: The Ten Pillars in Conversation · Radical Immanence Collection · Part Five: Living in the Architecture →

The ten pillars together constitute a philosophical architecture that can be described briefly and then must be lived in at length, because the architecture is the kind of thing that cannot be understood from the outside. You have to walk through it.

Here is the brief description:

The self is plural. The knowledge it produces is situated and partial. The reality it inhabits is constructed and contested. The community it forms with other plural selves is held difference rather than enforced sameness. The time it lives in is sedimentary rather than linear. The language it uses is the medium of construction rather than the vehicle of truth. The grief it carries is permanent weather rather than temporary condition. The ethical stance available to it is the witness who stays in the room. The building it does is recursive and provisional. And the form its integrity takes is the unfinished, the ongoing, the sentence that has not ended yet.

These ten pillars are not independent. They support each other. The plural self requires situated knowledge because a unified self could claim objective knowledge, but a plural self can only ever know from somewhere. The situated knowledge requires the honest language because a language that pretends to speak from nowhere erases the location that situated knowledge requires. The sedimentary time requires the permanent grief because a linear time could leave things behind, but a sedimentary time carries everything forward. The witness requires the community of held difference because the witness who stands alone has no one to witness, and the witness who stands in a community of sameness has nothing to witness. The recursive construction requires the unfinished because a finished building is a building that has stopped being honest about the world it stands in. The immanent sacred requires all the others because it is the quality of attention the witness brings to the plural, constructed, grief-saturated, sedimentary, linguistically honest world that makes that world sacred rather than merely difficult.

The architecture holds because the pillars hold each other.

The full map of conversations the ten pillars convene includes:

On the plural self: William James, Gertrude Stein, Gloria Anzaldua, Hannah Arendt on plurality as the condition of political life.

On illusion as primary: Plato (as the tradition Young refuses), Descartes (as the tradition Young refuses), Peter Berger and Thomas Luckmann on the social construction of reality, Patricia Hill Collins on Black feminist epistemology.

On grief as permanent weather: Walter Benjamin on the accumulated wreckage of history, Judith Butler on grievability and public mourning, the tradition of indigenous philosophy on the ongoing presence of historical loss.

On the witness: Simone Weil on attention as an ethical act, Iris Murdoch on the sovereignty of good, Arendt on the space of appearance as the space of honest presence.

On recursive construction: Alfred North Whitehead on becoming rather than being, the constitutional tradition of democratic revision, the feminist tradition of ongoing political construction.

On the ethics of incompletion: Walter Benjamin on the incomplete project, the tradition of process philosophy, Young herself as the supreme exemplar.

On the ontology of the marginal: Hannah Arendt on plurality and political life, Mikhail Bakhtin on the dialogic, the tradition of coalition politics developed in feminist and anti-racist organizing.

On sedimentary time: Walter Benjamin on the angel of history, John McPhee and the tradition of deep time thinking, indigenous philosophy on the ongoing presence of the past.

On language as construction: Gertrude Stein on the grammar of being, Adrienne Rich on the politics of location, the tradition of feminist language theory from Dale Spender through Judith Butler.

On immanent transcendence: Robin Wall Kimmerer on the intelligence of the living world, the tradition of process theology developed from Whitehead, Young herself as the philosopher of the sacred without the beyond.

This is the community of thinkers that the ten pillars convene.

It is a community that has never been assembled in this form, because the thinker who could convene them, Marguerite Young, was filed under the wrong category and left there for sixty years.

The filing is over.
