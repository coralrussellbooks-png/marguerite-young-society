On Loving v. Virginia and Anti-Miscegenation Law

Anti-miscegenation laws, prohibiting marriage and often any sexual relationship between people of different races, were part of American law from Maryland’s first prohibition in 1691 through the Supreme Court’s 1967 Loving v. Virginia decision, which held them unconstitutional under the Fourteenth Amendment. At the time of the decision, sixteen states still had such laws on the books. The case was brought by Mildred and Richard Loving, a Black woman and a white man who had married in Washington D.C. and were convicted of violating Virginia law when they returned home. Richard Loving’s lawyers sent his statement directly to the Supreme Court: “Tell the Court I love my wife.” Young was writing and publishing in 1965, two years before this decision. Interracial marriage between Black and white Americans was not just stigmatized when she wrote; it was criminal in much of the country.
