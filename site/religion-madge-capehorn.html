Definition from research

Madge believes, but her faith gives her no peace. Loss and betrayal have left her doubting heaven and divine justice both, and her religion is closer to anguish than comfort. She is eaten by jealousy, above all of Jacqueline White, whom she comes to see as a rival in the spirit as much as in love, a ghost disturbing the living. Her faith and her wounds are tangled together past separating.

Death and the afterlife haunt her. She turns over the fates of the people she has lost and keeps asking whether their suffering was justice or cruelty, whether it was God's hand at all. Homer tries to reason her out of it and cannot; comfort slides off her. Her speech runs to lament, half prayer and half accusation, the voice of someone caught between belief and despair. She suspects that earthly injustice is simply the way of things, allowed if not ordained, and her whole troubled faith reflects the book's larger struggle to find truth in a cruel and confusing world.
