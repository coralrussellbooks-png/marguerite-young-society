Definition from research

Category: a possible echo, between optional and accidental. Conrad and his title never appear, but the phrase does, four times.

Joseph Conrad's Heart of Darkness (1899) follows the sailor Marlow up the Congo River toward the ivory trader Kurtz, and turns that journey into a descent into the darkness within.

The words appear in the novel as an ordinary phrase. Mr. Spitzer asks, "How out of the heart of darkness could evolve this creation" (chapter 29). A voice in chapter 44 speaks of love "false as the heart of darkness and whirlwind." Cousin Hannah is an old maid "whose heart was the heart of darkness and mystery" (chapter 45). Mr. Spitzer calls his own life "the heart of darkness" (chapter 53).

The phrase was common English before Conrad and after. Its four appearances make an echo possible, especially in Mr. Spitzer's river crossings and journeys among the dead. They do not make it certain, and the volume treats it that way.
