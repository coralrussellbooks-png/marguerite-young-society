Chapter Two: The Transmission Line

← Chapter One: What the Horizontal Cannot See · Radical Immanence Collection · Chapter Three: The Ancestors Are in the Sediment →

There is a word in geology for what happens when material is carried by a moving medium, water or wind or ice, and deposited somewhere else when the medium slows. The word is alluvial. Alluvial material is material that has been carried and deposited. It is not where it started. It is not the medium that carried it. It is the record of a transmission: the evidence that something moved, was held, was released, came to rest in a new configuration.

The Mississippi River delta is alluvial. It is made of everything the river carried from the upper watershed, from Minnesota and Iowa and Indiana, from the glacial till fields and the eroded limestone bluffs and the topsoil of a thousand farms. None of that material is where it started. All of it is still itself. It has been transmitted, and in the transmission it has become something new: the delta, which is both the record of everything the river carried and a new landscape that did not exist before the carrying.

The self is alluvial.

This is not a metaphor, or it is not only a metaphor. It is a description of the actual process by which selves are constituted. You are made of what was carried to you and deposited in you by the moving medium of time and relation. The language you think in was carried to you by people who are dead. The categories through which you organize experience were carried to you by philosophical and cultural traditions that stretch back further than any individual life. The specific grief you carry was deposited in you by specific losses that were themselves deposited by earlier losses, the grief of your parents and their parents, the grief of the people who stood on the ground before you stood on it, all of it alluvial, all of it carried and deposited, none of it where it started, all of it still itself in a new configuration.

The transmission line is the line along which the carrying happens.

It is not a metaphor for ancestry, though ancestry is one of its most visible forms. It is a structural description of how time actually works when you stop measuring it as duration and start reading it as deposit. Every present moment is a crossing point on the transmission line: the place where everything that has been carried arrives and where everything that will be carried forward begins. The present moment is not a point on a timeline. It is an intersection. It is the place where the horizontal spread of what you are meets the vertical line of what you are inside.

Western philosophy has largely failed to develop an adequate account of the transmission line because it has been committed, at its deepest level, to a model of time as linear duration and a model of the self as a unified subject moving through that duration toward destinations. On the linear model, the past is behind you and the future is ahead of you and the present is the moving edge between them, the knife's edge of now that separates what has been from what will be. The self on the linear model is a traveler on a road: it has come from somewhere and is going somewhere and its obligations are primarily to the journey and to the other travelers it encounters along the way.

The transmission model is not a longer road. It is a different geometry entirely.

On the transmission model, the past is not behind you. It is beneath you, in the sediment, pressing up through the soles of your feet. The future is not ahead of you. It is above you, in the not-yet-formed layers that will be pressed down by the weight of what you deposit. And the present is not a moving edge. It is a crossing point: the place where the vertical line of transmission intersects the horizontal spread of the living moment, the place where what has been deposited meets what is being deposited, the place where the alluvial self stands in the middle of the carrying.

This geometry changes everything about obligation.

On the linear model, obligation runs forward: you owe things to the people ahead of you on the road, the people you will encounter, the future you are moving toward. The past is behind you and its obligations, if any, are to memory and to the honoring of what has been. But memory and honoring are optional in a way that forward obligation is not: you can choose to remember or not to remember, to honor or not to honor, without changing the structure of your forward journey.

On the transmission model, obligation runs in both directions simultaneously, because you are not on a road. You are on a line that passes through you. What was deposited before you arrived shaped the ground you stand on. What you deposit will shape the ground that the not-yet-born will stand on. The obligation is not chosen. It is structural. It arises from the fact of being a crossing point, from being constituted by the deposit of others, from being a carrier of what will constitute the ground that comes after.

You did not choose to be alluvial. You are alluvial. The obligation is in the constitution.
