Chapter Five: The Standing Still

← Chapter Four: You Were Told This Would End · The Weather Inside · Chapter Six: The Reef Method →

You have seen something you cannot unsee.

It appeared in your feed the way things appear there: without warning, without context, without the preparation that the magnitude of the thing required. One moment you were scrolling and the next moment you were inside something that felt wrong in a way that preceded language. Not wrong like an incorrect fact. Wrong like a violation. Wrong like something being done in public that should not be done at all and that the platform was displaying with the same neutral algorithmic indifference it displayed everything else: the recipe, the sunset, the outrage, the wrong thing, the dog.

You stopped.

You read it again.

The wrongness did not resolve into something nameable. It was not the kind of wrong that has a clear argument against it, the wrong that can be addressed with facts or context or the right framing. It was the kind of wrong that lives underneath the argument, in the place where the values are, in the specific register where you know something is a violation before you can explain to anyone else why it is a violation in terms they will accept.

You looked for the words.

They were not there. Not the words that would reach the person who had posted the thing. Not the words that would make the wrongness visible to the people who had responded with agreement or with the specific blankness of people who had scrolled past it without registering the violation. Not the words that would hold the full weight of what you were feeling without collapsing it into the smaller, angrier, more platform-legible version of itself that the form demanded.

The form demanded a response.

You had no response that would not cost you something essential in the making of it.

So you sat with it.

Not because you had resolved it. Because the platform had nothing to offer you in that moment and you knew it and the knowing was its own kind of standing still: the recognition that some things cannot be addressed in the form available and that the attempt to address them in the wrong form produces a smaller version of the truth than the silence does.

You closed the window.

The wrongness was still there.

It is still there.

This is the witness position under the specific pressure of the contemporary moment: not the ancient grief, not the personal loss, not the ambiguous relationship with the parent or the self. The daily, relentless, algorithmically optimized assault on the capacity to stand still inside what you are seeing without being pulled into reaction or paralysis or the specific exhaustion of a person who can see clearly in a medium that is designed to make clear seeing impossible.

The witness is not the person who doesn't feel this.

The witness is the person who feels it fully and does not let the feeling become the only thing available.

This chapter is about how.

Here is what the self-help industry will tell you about this.

It will tell you to be present. It will tell you to observe your thoughts without judgment. It will tell you that the goal of mindfulness practice is equanimity: the stable, unruffled state of the person who has meditated their way to a place above the weather.

It will show you the person sitting in lotus position with the expression of someone who has achieved something.

What it will not tell you is that the equanimity being sold is a form of bypass. The spiritual bypass, the therapeutic bypass, the wellness bypass: the use of presence practice to go around the feeling rather than through it. The meditator who has achieved equanimity by learning not to feel the wrongness is not in the witness position. They are in the numbness position. The numbness being more comfortable than the feeling and less useful than the standing still.

The witness is not above the weather.

The witness is in it.

The difference between the witness and the person who has been taken over by the weather is not the absence of feeling. It is the presence of the standing still inside the feeling. The capacity to be fully in the experience without the experience becoming the only thing you are.

This is harder than equanimity.

It is also more honest.

And it is the only form of presence that produces the clarity the situation requires.

Before we go further there is something to name about the platform.

The online environment is not a neutral medium for human communication. It is a system designed with specific intentions that are not your intentions and not in your interest. The algorithm is not interested in your witness position. The algorithm is interested in your reaction. The engagement metric does not distinguish between the reaction that comes from standing still inside what you are seeing and the reaction that comes from being fully taken over by it. Both produce the click. Both produce the scroll. Both produce the data point that the platform monetizes.

The platform profits from the lost witness position.

This is not an accident.

The outrage, the helplessness, the specific exhaustion of the person who has seen too much wrongness in too short a time without the language to address it or the form to hold it: these are features, not bugs. The platform is designed to produce them because a person in that state is more likely to keep scrolling, more likely to react, more likely to produce the engagement that the platform sells.

The witness position is an act of resistance in the contemporary moment not because it is politically motivated but because it is structurally opposed to what the platform requires.

The standing still is the thing the algorithm cannot monetize.

This is worth knowing before you try to practice it online.

The practice is not primarily for online.

The practice is for you, in your body, in the life you are living. The online environment is where you will most frequently lose it and most urgently need it. But the building of it happens elsewhere: in the quiet, in the body, in the practices that develop the capacity before the platform demands it.

You cannot build the witness position inside the medium that destroys it.

You build it elsewhere and bring it there.

Here is what it looks like to lose the witness position entirely.

You know the feeling.

It has a specific quality in the body: the narrowing, the acceleration, the sense of the walls of the situation coming in until there is no air and no perspective and no version of you available except the version that is inside the story completely, with no distance, no standing still, nothing between you and the full force of what is happening.

It happens fast.

The trigger being not always proportionate to the response. The small thing that lands on the accumulated weight of all the other things and produces a reaction that surprises you with its force. The comment that would not have landed that way yesterday landing that way today because the window of tolerance is already narrowed by the week or the month or the years of watching the worse thing be chosen repeatedly.

The window of tolerance is not a fixed thing.

It narrows under sustained pressure.

The person who has been watching the wrong thing happen for long enough, who has been carrying the knowledge of what better looks like while the worse is being chosen repeatedly, who has been standing still in the gap between the vision and the reality for years: that person's window of tolerance is under a specific kind of pressure that the acute crisis does not produce. Not the sudden storm. The chronic weather. The sustained low-grade assault on the capacity to stand still that eventually narrows the window until the thing that would not have taken you out yesterday takes you out today.

This is not weakness.

This is the physics of the window.

The window narrows under sustained pressure and the narrowing is information: the information being that the capacity needs tending, that the witness position needs rebuilding, that the person who has been standing still in the hard place for a long time needs the practices that restore the window before they can stand still there again.

The witness is not a permanent achievement.

It is a capacity that requires maintenance.

The maintenance being the practice.

Here is what it looks like to hold the witness position under sustained pressure.

You have been watching something being destroyed.

Not a person. A system. A set of structures and norms and possibilities that were imperfect, that were built on foundations you could see clearly were not solid, that had failed in specific and documented ways, but that also contained within them the seeds of what could have been better: the mechanisms, however flawed, for the kind of governance and care and collective decision-making that a more just world requires.

You can see the better thing clearly.

You have been building it. In the documents and the fiction and the philosophical framework and the constitutional architecture that this book is part of. You have been building the better thing with the specific intentionality of someone who has looked at what exists and understood both its failure and its potential and chosen to build toward the potential rather than simply grieve the failure.

And then you watch the worse thing be chosen.

Again.

Not once. Repeatedly. With the specific relentlessness of a system that has decided the worse thing is what it wants and is implementing that decision with more efficiency than you anticipated and more damage than the models predicted and more normalization than you thought was possible in the time available.

The witness position under this pressure is not the equanimity of the person above the weather.

It is the standing still of the person who is fully inside the weather and has not been destroyed by it yet and is working every day to maintain the capacity that the sustained pressure is working every day to erode.

It requires grief literacy. The grief of the repeatedly chosen worse thing is real and it accumulates and it needs to be read and named and held in the ways that Chapter Four described. The grief that is not named becomes the thing that narrows the window.

It requires the working story. The story that the better thing is being built and that the building matters even when the gap between what is and what could be is at its widest. The story that is not yet verified by the evidence and is absolutely necessary to maintain the capacity to keep building.

It requires the plural self. The part of you that is watching the destruction and the part of you that is building the alternative and the part of you that needs rest from both and the conductor who can hear all three without being taken over by any of them.

All five frameworks are in the witness position.

The witness is not a separate capacity.

The witness is what the other four frameworks make possible when they are all working together.

This is what the Standing Still actually is.

Not the absence of feeling.

Not the achievement of equanimity.

Not the spiritual bypass that mistakes numbness for peace.

The Standing Still is the full presence of a person who has done the interior work: who knows their chorus and has learned to conduct it, who has audited their working stories and knows which ones are expanding them and which ones have stopped working, who has built the grief literacy to read the climate without being destroyed by it, who can feel the full weight of what is happening and remain in the standing still long enough to respond from the witness position rather than react from the lost one.

This is not a small thing.

This is the work of a lifetime.

And it is available to you.

Not as the permanent achievement.

As the daily practice.

The standing still.

One moment at a time.

One breath at a time.

One choice to remain present rather than be taken over.

Over and over and over.

Until the capacity builds.

Until the window widens.

Until the standing still becomes the place you return to rather than the place you reach for.

You are already building it.

You have been building it your whole life.

Every time you closed the window instead of reaching for the words that would not arrive in the form the situation required: that was the standing still.

Every time you felt the full weight of the wrongness and did not collapse into it or bypass it but held it in the space between feeling and reaction: that was the standing still.

Every time you kept building the better thing while watching the worse thing be chosen: that was the standing still at its most sustained and most costly and most necessary.

You know how to do this.

You have always known how to do this.

The framework gives you the name for what you already know.

The Standing Still.

Here is how to practice it.

The Science of the Standing Still

Daniel Siegel drew a window.

Not literally. Conceptually. He called it the window of tolerance and it became one of the most useful frameworks in contemporary psychology not because it was complicated but because it was accurate: a simple diagram of the zone in which human beings can process experience without being overwhelmed by it or shut down from it.

The window has two edges.

Above the upper edge is hyperarousal: the state of too much, the flooded nervous system, the reaction that bypasses reflection, the anger or the fear or the grief that has taken over the whole system and left no room for the witness position. This is the state the platform is designed to produce. This is what happens when the wrongness lands before the standing still is available.

Below the lower edge is hypoarousal: the state of too little, the shut-down nervous system, the numbness that protects against feeling by removing the capacity for it. This is the state that the wellness industry mistakes for equanimity. The person who has achieved the stillness by learning not to feel is not in the window. They are below it. The stillness is real. The cost is also real.

Inside the window: the optimal zone of arousal in which the full range of experience is available without the system being taken over by any part of it. The feeling without the flooding. The presence without the bypass. The standing still inside the weather rather than above it or flattened by it.

The witness position is the window of tolerance made into a practice.

Siegel's research, and the decades of neuroscience that followed it, demonstrated that the window is not a fixed thing. It widens with practice and narrows under sustained pressure. The person who has developed the practices that support the nervous system's regulation has a wider window: more can come in before the system is overwhelmed. The person whose window has been narrowed by chronic stress or trauma or the sustained pressure of watching the worse thing be chosen repeatedly has less room before the edges are reached.

This is not a character judgment.

This is the physics of the nervous system under load.

The window narrows under sustained pressure the way any system narrows under sustained pressure: not as failure but as the natural response of a system that has been asked to hold more than its current capacity allows. The response is information. The information is: the capacity needs tending. The window needs rebuilding. The practices that widen the window need to be applied before the person can stand still in the hard place again.

The practices are not complicated.

They are consistent.

Sleep. Movement. The specific quality of attention that comes from being in a body in a physical place rather than in a screen in an algorithmic environment. The relationships that do not require the witness position to be performed but allow it to be replenished. The creative work that is generative rather than reactive. The practices that return the nervous system to its baseline rather than keeping it at the edge of the window where the platform wants it.

The window widens with the practices.

The standing still becomes available again.

Steven Hayes developed Acceptance and Commitment Therapy in the 1980s and one of its central contributions to the understanding of the witness position is the distinction between what he called self as content and self as context.

Self as content is the self that is identified with its thoughts and feelings and experiences: the self that is the anger, the self that is the grief, the self that is the position in the argument, the self that is the story being told about what is happening. This self has no witness position because it is entirely inside the content. There is no standing still available because there is no distance between the self and the experience.

Self as context is the self that contains the thoughts and feelings and experiences without being identical to them. The observer self: the part of you that can notice you are angry without being only the anger, that can feel the grief without being only the grief, that can hold the position without being only the position. This self has the witness position built into it because it is the context rather than the content. The experience is happening inside it rather than to it.

Hayes called the practice of developing the observer self defusion: the process of creating distance between the self and the thought, not to suppress the thought or bypass the feeling but to make room between the experience and the response. The room being the standing still. The standing still being the space in which the witness position operates.

The defusion practice is not complicated.

It is the simple act of noticing: I am having the thought that this is wrong rather than this is wrong. I am feeling the anger rather than I am angry. The noticing being not the suppression of the feeling but the creation of the room. The room being where the witness lives.

This is the ACT contribution to the standing still: not a technique for managing feelings but a practice of creating the context in which feelings can be fully present without the self being entirely consumed by them.

Jonathan Shay coined the term moral injury in the 1990s working with Vietnam veterans.

He was trying to name something that PTSD did not fully capture: the specific psychological damage that occurred not from the terror of combat but from the betrayal of what was right. The soldier who was ordered to do something that violated their moral framework. The person who witnessed atrocity and was required to continue functioning as if the atrocity had not occurred. The specific wound of the person whose moral clarity was intact and whose capacity to act on it was systematically overridden.

Moral injury is not the wound of the person who didn't know better.

It is the wound of the person who knew exactly what better looked like and was prevented from enacting it.

The concept has expanded far beyond the military context. The teacher who is required to implement policies they know harm children and who must continue teaching anyway. The healthcare worker who cannot provide the care the patient needs because the system will not allow it and who must continue working in that system. The person who has spent years building the vision of what better governance looks like and who must watch the worse thing be chosen repeatedly while continuing to function and continuing to build.

This is moral injury at the civic and political scale.

It is the specific exhaustion of the witness position under sustained pressure: not the acute crisis but the chronic one. Not the single wrong thing but the repeated choice of the worse thing by a system that has the capacity for the better thing and declines to use it.

Moral injury produces specific symptoms that differ from trauma and from burnout and that require specific responses. The cynicism that is not cynicism but the protective adaptation of a person whose hope has been repeatedly disappointed. The withdrawal that is not apathy but the conservation of the capacity that sustained engagement was depleting. The anger that is not dysfunction but the appropriate response of a person whose moral framework has been violated repeatedly over time.

The treatment of moral injury is not the removal of the moral clarity.

It is the restoration of the capacity to act on it.

Which requires the window to be widened. The observer self to be rebuilt. The standing still to be practiced again in the conditions that are available rather than the conditions that would be ideal.

And one more thing that the clinical literature is only beginning to address.

The affect regulation theorists, beginning with Peter Fonagy and Mary Main and extending through the contemporary neuroscience of emotion, have made a distinction that is essential to the standing still: the difference between emotional suppression, emotional flooding, and emotional integration.

Suppression is the numbness. The below the window state. The person who has achieved stillness by removing the capacity for feeling. The cost is the loss of the information that the feeling carries. The loss of the capacity to read the weather. The loss of the witness position that requires full presence to function.

Flooding is the above the window state. The person who is entirely inside the feeling with no room for the standing still. The cost is the loss of the response capacity. The reaction that bypasses reflection. The platform's preferred state.

Integration is the window. The full presence of the feeling without the system being taken over by it. The feeling as information rather than as identity. The standing still inside the weather.

Integration is not achieved by managing the feelings into an acceptable size.

It is achieved by widening the window until the full size of the feeling can be held inside it without the system going above or below.

This is the practice.

Not smaller feelings.

A wider window.

The window widens through the practices that support the nervous system's regulation and through the development of the observer self and through the specific work of naming the moral injury rather than performing the resilience that the system requires in the face of it.

You are not required to be resilient in the face of the repeatedly chosen worse thing.

You are required to stay in the window long enough to keep building the better thing.

That is enough.

That is more than enough.

That is the standing still.

The Standing Still

Here is what the witness position actually is.

Not the person above the weather. Not the detached observer watching experience from a safe distance. Not the meditator who has achieved the stillness by learning not to feel. Not the person who has bypassed the grief and the anger and the moral injury and arrived at a place of serene non-attachment that the wellness industry sells as the goal.

The witness is the person who is fully inside the experience and has not been taken over by it.

The standing still is not stillness in the sense of the absence of movement. It is stillness in the sense of the tree in the wind: rooted deeply enough that the movement above does not become the uprooting below. The branches moving. The roots holding. The full force of the weather present and the standing still possible because the roots go deep enough to hold what the weather brings.

You are building roots.

This is what the five frameworks together are doing: building the root system that makes the standing still possible in the conditions that actually exist rather than the conditions that would be ideal. The plural self gives you the chorus and the conductor. The working story gives you the audit that keeps the stories from collapsing the window. The grief climate gives you the literacy that makes the weather legible rather than ambushing. The standing still is what becomes possible when the other three are working.

And then the recursive construction, which is the next chapter, is what happens when the standing still has been practiced long enough to become the place you return to rather than the place you reach for.

But first the standing still itself.

The Standing Still framework has four practices. Not stages. Not a sequence. Four practices that develop the witness position and maintain it under the specific pressures of the contemporary moment.

The first practice is the noticing before the naming.

The witness position begins before the language arrives.

The body knows before the mind does. The tightening in the chest before the thought arrives. The specific quality of the morning that tells you the window is narrow today before you have named what narrowed it. The feeling in the room before the words that produced it have been processed.

The noticing before the naming is the practice of attending to that pre-linguistic signal. Not to analyze it. Not to immediately convert it into a story about what is happening. To notice it. To register it as information before the mind begins its work of making it into content.

This is the first moment of the standing still: the pause between the trigger and the response in which the noticing happens before the reaction does.

The platform is designed to eliminate that pause.

The notification, the scroll, the appearance of the wrong thing in the feed: these are designed to move the trigger directly to the reaction without the pause. The noticing before the naming is the practice of inserting the pause back into the sequence. The pause being where the witness lives. The pause being the standing still in its smallest and most essential form.

It is a breath long.

Sometimes that is enough.

Sometimes the breath is the difference between the reaction that comes from the lost witness position and the response that comes from the standing still.

Practice the breath.

Not as the solution to everything. As the insertion of the pause. As the moment in which the noticing before the naming becomes possible. As the smallest available practice of the standing still in the conditions that actually exist.

The second practice is the return.

The witness position is lost and found and lost and found again.

This is not failure. This is the practice.

The person who expects to maintain the standing still permanently has misunderstood what the standing still is. It is not a permanent achievement. It is a capacity that is exercised through the losing and the returning. The losing being the information that the window has narrowed or the trigger has exceeded the current capacity or the sustained pressure has accumulated past the point the current practice can hold. The returning being the practice itself: the recognition that the witness position has been lost and the choice to return to it.

The return does not require that the feeling stop.

The feeling can continue.

The return requires only the recognition: I have been taken over by the story. I am fully inside it. There is no standing still available from here. And the choice that follows the recognition: I am returning to the witness position. Not because the feeling is wrong. Because the reaction that comes from the lost witness position serves neither me nor the situation.

The return is not dramatic.

It is the quiet choice made in the middle of the flooding: to step back from the edge of the window. To find the breath. To reestablish the observer self. To return to the standing still that was available before the trigger arrived and that will be available again when the return has been practiced enough times that it becomes the natural movement of the system.

You have already been practicing the return your whole life.

Every time you closed the window instead of reaching for the words that would not arrive in the right form. Every time you felt the full force of the wrongness and waited before responding. Every time you recognized that you were inside the story and found your way back to the standing still: that was the return.

You know how to do this.

The framework gives you the name for what you already know.

The third practice is the full presence without the requirement.

The witness position is not the position of the person who requires the experience to be different before they can engage with it.

This is the distinction that the toxic positivity version of mindfulness misses entirely. The mindfulness that requires the meditator to achieve a certain state before they can be present is not the witness position. It is the conditional presence: I will be here when things are better, when I have processed enough, when the feeling has reduced to a manageable size, when the platform has given me something less difficult to look at.

The witness position is the unconditional presence.

The full presence with what is actually here: the wrongness in the feed, the worse thing being chosen, the gap between the vision and the reality, the grief that is permanent weather, the ambiguous loss that has no clean endpoint, the moral injury of the repeatedly violated moral framework.

All of it.

Here.

Without the requirement that it be different before you can be present to it.

This is the hardest practice in the framework because it runs directly against the instinct of the person who has been in the weather long enough to be exhausted by it. The exhausted person wants the conditional presence. Wants to be able to say: I will be fully here when this is less. When the destruction has stopped. When the better thing has been built and installed and the gap has closed.

The full presence without the requirement does not mean the acceptance of what is wrong as what should be.

It means the willingness to be in the reality of what is before moving to the response that the reality requires.

You cannot respond accurately from a conditional presence.

You can only respond accurately from the full presence of the person who is standing still inside what is actually here.

The response that comes from the standing still is different from the reaction that comes from the lost witness position and different from the withdrawal that comes from the conditional presence.

The response from the standing still is the one that serves the situation rather than the nervous system's need to escape it.

This is what you are building toward.

The fourth practice is the tending of the window.

The window narrows under sustained pressure.

The sustained pressure of the contemporary moment is real and it is not going away and the person who is building the better thing while watching the worse thing be chosen is under a specific kind of sustained pressure that requires specific tending.

The tending is not complicated.

It is consistent.

It is the practices that return the nervous system to its baseline before the next assault arrives. Not the practices that prepare you for more assault. The practices that restore the capacity that the assault has depleted.

Sleep is the first practice. Not the optimization of sleep. The permission to sleep. The specific permission that the person who is watching the worse thing be chosen and building the better thing simultaneously often denies themselves: the permission to stop, to rest, to let the nervous system do its repair work in the dark without the screen and the feed and the ongoing accumulation of the things that cannot be fixed tonight.

The body that has been restored by sleep has a wider window than the body that has not.

This is not a metaphor.

This is the neuroscience of the window.

Movement is the second practice. The body in space, in gravity, in the physical world that is not the screen. The specific quality of attention that returns when the body has moved through space rather than through the feed. The nervous system resetting through the practice of being in a body rather than in a profile.

The relationships that replenish rather than deplete. The people who do not require the witness position to be performed but allow it to be restored. The conversations that do not begin with the wrongness in the feed but with the specific reality of two people in relationship with each other in a place. The belonging that is not contingent on the position or the performance or the consistent legibility that the platform requires.

The creative work that generates rather than reacts. The building of the better thing as its own form of window tending: the specific quality of the person who has spent time making something rather than responding to something. The capacity restored through the act of creation rather than depleted by the act of consumption.

And the natural world.

The specific quality of attention that returns when the body is in a landscape rather than a feed. The witness position that is easiest to find in the place where the weather is literal rather than algorithmic. The standing still that is most available in the places where the standing still is also what the place is doing: the tree in the wind, the river moving toward the sea, the grass doing its winter work underground.

The natural world is the oldest teacher of the standing still.

It has been practicing it for longer than consciousness has existed to name it.

You already know this.

You have been knowing it every time you went outside and felt something restore that the screen had depleted.

The restoration is real.

The practice is returning to it before the window narrows past the point of the easy return.

Tend the window.

Before you need to.

The standing still is available to you.

It has always been available to you.

The tending is what keeps it that way.

Practice: The Standing Still

One.

Think of the last time you lost the witness position completely.

Not the dramatic loss. The ordinary one. The moment in the feed or the conversation or the family dinner or the meeting where you were pulled fully inside the story and the standing still became unavailable.

What was the trigger? Not the content of it. The quality of it. Was it the wrongness that exceeded the available language? The accumulated pressure of too many similar moments landing on the same place? The specific thing that found the narrow part of the window and came through it?

Write down the early signals you missed. The body's warning before the mind registered what was happening. The tightening, the acceleration, the specific quality of the moment before the loss.

You are not reconstructing this to blame yourself for the loss.

You are building the map of your own window. The map that will let you see the narrowing before it becomes the loss. The early signal being the practice. Write it down. The writing is the noticing. The noticing is the beginning of the standing still.

Two.

Practice the return right now.

Not in a crisis. In the quiet. In the reading of this page.

Find something you have been carrying that has narrowed your window. Not the largest thing. A medium thing. The recurring frustration. The situation that has not resolved. The wrong thing that keeps being chosen in a domain you care about.

Let yourself feel the full weight of it for thirty seconds. Not the managed version. The actual weight.

Now find the breath.

Not to make the feeling stop. To insert the pause between the feeling and the reaction. To create the room in which the noticing before the naming becomes possible.

Notice: I am feeling the weight of this. I am not only this weight. I am the person who is feeling it and who has the standing still available and who can return to it from here.

Breathe.

Return.

Write one sentence about what the return feels like in the body. Not what you think about it. What it feels like. The physical quality of the window widening even slightly. Even one breath's worth.

That one breath is the practice.

The practice is made of one breath at a time.

Three.

Identify one place in your current life where you are requiring the experience to be different before you can be fully present to it.

Not the catastrophic situation. The ordinary one. The relationship you are conditionally present in because the other person has not yet changed in the way you need them to change. The work you are conditionally engaged with because the conditions have not yet become what they need to be. The situation you are waiting out rather than standing still inside.

Write down what the full presence without the requirement would look like in that situation.

Not the acceptance of what is wrong as what should be. The willingness to be in the reality of what is before moving to the response the reality requires.

Write this: I am here. Not when it is better. Now. In this. With what is actually here.

Read it once.

Notice what shifts in the body when you say it without the condition attached.

Four.

Tend the window today.

Not tomorrow. Not when things are less. Today.

Choose one practice from the following and do it before you open the feed again: sleep an extra hour, move your body in a physical space, have a conversation that does not begin with the wrongness in the feed, make something, go outside and stand in it for ten minutes without the phone.

One practice.

Today.

Write down which one you chose and what it felt like in the body afterward.

The window widens through the doing.

Not through the understanding of why the doing matters.

Through the doing.

Do the thing.

The standing still will be more available afterward.

That is not a promise of the permanent.

It is the physics of the window.

Tend it.

Coda: The Tree in the Wind

There is a tree outside your window or there is one you remember or there is one you have stood under long enough to know what it does in the wind.

Watch it.

Not for what it means. For what it does.

The wind comes and the branches move and the movement is total: the whole canopy responding to the whole force of what is coming through it. Nothing held back. Nothing managed into a smaller response. The full force of the wind meeting the full presence of the tree and the tree moving with everything it has.

And the roots holding.

This is the standing still.

Not the stillness of the thing that does not feel the wind. The stillness of the thing that feels it completely and is not uprooted by it. The branches moving and the roots holding and the whole tree present to the full force of what is coming through it without being taken from the ground.

You have been building roots your whole life.

Every time you returned to the witness position after losing it. Every time you felt the full weight of the wrongness and did not collapse into it or bypass it. Every time you closed the window instead of reaching for the words that would not arrive in the right form. Every time you kept building the better thing while watching the worse thing be chosen. Every time you tended the window before the narrowing became the loss.

Every time: roots.

The roots do not make the wind less.

The wind is the wind. The wrongness in the feed is real. The worse thing being chosen is real. The moral injury of the repeatedly violated moral framework is real. The gap between the vision and the reality is real and it is wide and the width of it is not going to close on a timeline the urgency of the moment finds acceptable.

The roots do not change any of that.

The roots change what you can do inside it.

Marguerite Young stood still for eighteen years.

Not in the sense of not moving. She traveled and she taught and she lived the full life of a person in Greenwich Village in the middle of the twentieth century. But inside the writing she stood still for eighteen years inside the most complete and most demanding witness position available to a novelist: the full presence with twelve hundred pages of human consciousness without the requirement that it resolve into something smaller than it was.

She did not bypass the grief or the illusion or the plural self or the recursive return to the same territory. She stood still inside all of it. She let the full force of what consciousness actually is move through the writing without managing it into a size the market could accept or the reader could finish in a weekend or the critics could categorize without discomfort.

The standing still produced the most complete philosophical account of consciousness in the Western literary tradition.

Not despite the eighteen years.

Because of them.

The witness position is not the position of the person who has withdrawn from the building.

It is the position from which the most important building is possible.

The person above the weather cannot see what is actually here. The person destroyed by the weather cannot build anything from inside the flooding. The person who has found the standing still, who has built the roots deep enough to hold while the branches move, who has tended the window and practiced the return and developed the full presence without the requirement: that person can see clearly enough to build something that will last.

You are building something that will last.

Not in spite of the wind.

In the wind.

The roots are holding.

The branches are moving.

The standing still is available.

It has always been available.

It is available right now.

Stand still.

Feel the wind.

Let the roots hold.

Build.

Before you start the next chapter: stand up.

Find your feet on the floor. Feel the specific weight of your body in gravity, the contact between the soles of your feet and the ground beneath them. This is the root system. This is what holds.

Now sway.

Not dramatically. The way a tree sways in a light wind: a gentle movement from the center, the body moving with what comes through it, neither resisting nor collapsing. The movement being the full presence with the wind. The feet staying on the ground being the roots holding.

Feel both simultaneously: the movement above and the rootedness below.

This is the standing still.

Not the absence of movement.

The rooted movement.

The branches moving and the roots holding.

You can do this anywhere. In the thirty seconds before you open the feed. In the moment after the wrong thing has landed and the window has narrowed and the reaction is building. In the middle of the building when the gap between the vision and the reality is at its widest.

Stand.

Sway.

Feel the roots.

Return to the standing still.

The tree has been doing this for longer than you have been watching it.

It knows how.

So do you.
