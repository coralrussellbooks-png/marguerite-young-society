An Invitation to Work Here

Part of Art When Here Is Enough, on Radical Immanence, the philosophy Coral Russell draws from the work of Marguerite Young (1908 to 1995).
By Coral Russell · Updated October 29, 2026

← What This Tradition Knows · Art When Here Is Enough · How to Submit →

This publication is open to any artist, in any medium, working in the tradition of Radical Immanence.

You do not have to have read Young. You do not have to have read this document all the way through. You do have to be familiar with the concepts above so you can incorporate them into your work. The tradition does not require membership. It requires the work.

What it means to work here is different in every medium, and the following is not a checklist but a set of questions to ask of your work as you make it.

If You Are a Writer

Is the form honest about what the form is holding? If you are writing about grief, does the structure of the piece enact grief's actual logic, which accumulates and returns and does not resolve, or does it enact the conventional literary arc, which moves from loss through stages toward arrival? The form is not decoration. The form is the argument.

Does the sentence do the work the sentence needs to do, regardless of what conventional length says? Young's sentences are long because the thought they are carrying cannot be finished without being falsified. Some truths require that length. Some require the fragment. What does your truth require?

Is the narrator honest about being a narrator, which is to say, about being a plural self with situated knowledge and constructed reality and permanent grief? The witness does not pretend to objectivity. The witness knows where they are standing and names it.

Does the work stay in the room? The reader should not be consoled at the cost of the truth. The difficulty is the point. The difficulty is where the work is doing its work.

If You Are a Visual Artist

Does the work give the same quality of attention to what it is attending to that the witness gives? This is different from composition and different from skill, though it may include both. It is the quality of looking that does not reduce the thing to what you already know about it.

Does the form enact what the work is about? If the work is about the plural self, is it formally plural? If it is about sedimentary time, does it work in layers? If it is about the sacred ordinary, is it made from ordinary material attended to with extraordinary care?

Does the work resist the demand to be legible at a glance? The tradition is suspicious of the image that gives itself up immediately, because what the tradition is trying to hold is not the kind of thing that gives itself up immediately. The work should require time.

Is the incompletion honest? Some works are formally complete and philosophically incomplete, which is the right relationship. Some works are formally incomplete and that incompletion is the argument. Know which you are making and why.

If You Are a Musician or Sound Artist

Does the work use time honestly? The tradition is resistant to conventional musical time, to the progress from tension to resolution that mirrors the philosophical mistake of treating experience as a problem that gets solved. What does your material require in terms of time? Give it that time.

Does the work stay in the material? The tradition is wary of the transcendent gesture, the musical moment that points beyond itself to something more significant. The sacred is here. The sound is here. The weight of what the sound is carrying is in the sound itself, not in what the sound is pointing toward.

Does the work witness without consoling? Music that resolves its difficulty at the cost of the truth is dishonest in exactly the way the tradition defines dishonesty. The difficulty should be present at its full weight when you are done.

If You Are a Filmmaker or Video Artist

Does the camera stay? The witness stays in the room. The camera that moves away from difficulty because the difficulty is uncomfortable is the camera that fails the witness test. Akerman's camera stays. Stay.

Does the film give ordinary life the attention it deserves? The sacred ordinary pillar is available to cinema in a way it is available to almost no other medium, because cinema can give 201 minutes of real time to a woman washing dishes. What are you giving time to that the culture has decided is not worth time?

Does the film earn its length? Both questions are real: does the work need more time than it takes, and does the work need less time than it takes? The tradition does not mean long. It means honest about time.

If You Are a Performer or Choreographer

Does the body stay in the difficulty? The performer in this tradition does not manage the difficulty for the audience's comfort. The body holds what the body is holding and the audience is asked to witness, not to be made comfortable.

Is the repetition doing work? The recursive construction pillar is formally present in repetition, but repetition that does not deepen is not recursion. Each return should find something new in the material, should add a layer rather than simply restating what was there before. What does your repetition reveal that a single performance would not?

Is the incompletion honest? Bausch's works were never resolved in the conventional sense. They accumulated. If your work is accumulating rather than arriving, is that accumulation honest, or is it avoidance of the formal work required to earn the arrival?

Across All Mediums

Three questions that apply everywhere:

What are you carrying into this work that you have not yet named? The tradition begins with honesty about what the maker is bringing. The plural self, the situated knowledge, the permanent grief: these are not confessions. They are the material. Name them.

What does honesty require of the form? Not what is conventional, not what is expected, not what will be most legible to the largest audience. What does the truth of what you are making require in terms of how it is made? That is the formal question. Answer it honestly.

Where is the crossing point in this work? Where does the horizontal axis of the present moment, the thing you are making now, in this life, in this body, meet the vertical axis of what you received and what you are depositing? Every work that lives in this tradition has a crossing point. Finding it is part of the making.
