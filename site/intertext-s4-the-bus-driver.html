Allusions: The Bible

Definition from research

The bus-driver turns Scripture into oaths and barroom litany. "Ach, God and the angels, jumping Jesus, jumping toads, holy Lucifer and Lucifer matches" (chapter 5) is typical of his speech. He recites the tribes of Israel as a lament. He turns the Gospel into a joke about the old doctor: "Render unto Caesar the things that are Caesar's! Render unto Doc the things that are Doc's!" (chapter 8).

His one extended myth is a reversal. Guarding his uncut hair, he "was Penelope weaving this web, not Ulysses who wandered over the wide seas" (chapter 5). He jokes about the dead having no coin for Charon. He is the novel's first ferryman, driving his passengers through fog toward a river town.
