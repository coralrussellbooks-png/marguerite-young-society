On the perpendicular I and the horizontal mother.

Young's sentence, “as if the perpendicular pronoun I had come to see my horizontal mother,” is one of the novel's great geometric formulations. In grammar, the subject pronoun I is vertical in the sense that it stands alone, upright, self-supporting. The horizontal is the position of rest, sleep, illness, death. Young is mapping an existential difference onto a spatial one: Vera moves through the world under her own power; Catherine has given the vertical axis over. Their relationship is conducted across this gap, which is not only a difference in health but a difference in orientation to existence itself.
