Characters: Joachim Spitzer · Catherine Cartwheel

Definition from research

Mr. Spitzer is “ridiculously proud” of his little accidents, and the best of them is the glass wall. Visiting a prosperous lawyer’s office on behalf of a lost heir whose own mother, a famous actress of the gay nineties, had denied he was her son, he walked straight through a glass wall without noticing. Bills for the wall followed, along with threatening letters.

When he finally lost patience and tried to collect damages for his own discomfort, an obtuse desk clerk told him that Mr. Spitzer was dead, having departed this mortal life several years ago. The chapter’s comedy is bureaucratic, and it lands on its deepest theme. With equal good humor he concedes that Catherine may be right about everything: the table may be a coffin of stars, the rose in his buttonhole a lady’s ear, and he himself a tower of water or a basket of peaches.
