Woman in Young's Magnum Opus

Chapter 41

← Chapter 40 · Woman in Young's Magnum Opus · Chapter 42 →

Other versions: Chapter hub · Research notes · The Wheel and the Spokes

Characters: Catherine Cartwheel · Joachim Spitzer

Catherine holds male-coded power in this chapter, and her seizure of it is the pillar. She is the "great engineer" and "mathematical genius," commanding stars, tides, and spectral figures, and the woman claiming mastery over construction, number, and nature reclaims exactly the domains patriarchy reserved for men. That the power lives mostly in dreams and illusions is the gap between women's capacity and their permitted conditions, the reach real and the world too narrow to let it land. Her refusal of beauty ideals runs alongside it, the fascination with "symmetry and asymmetry," with beauty and deformity together, and the covering of "blemishes with beauty spots," which expose the performance a woman is asked to keep up and the bodily reality beneath.

Plural selfhood carries the chapter's inner life. Catherine is "everywhere at once because she was nowhere," "swifter than her own mind," "pale as death" yet everywhere, and this multiplicity is the fluid identity the novel prefers to any fixed self, a woman resisting the categories that would contain her. Her wanderings "inside her ear," lost in a labyrinth Aristotle called the "doorway to the soul," and her train journeys where she "travels" yet "never moves," are the socially limited mobility of a woman whose interior is vast and whose outward motion is denied.

The chapter's gravity is the greater unexpressed love. "The love which had never come into being was a greater love than love" makes restraint and self-possession into feminist virtues, the unlived feeling weightier than the lived, and it answers Mr. Joachim Spitzer's unrequited longing with a woman whose power exceeds his repetitive devotion. The door and key that would finally free her, the sense that she "would be happy" only upon finding the key in death, hold entrapment and liberation in one image. The chapter ends with Catherine talking to winds and walls, commanding her environment despite invisibility, a woman who vanishes and becomes many persons and rules the room she cannot be seen in.
