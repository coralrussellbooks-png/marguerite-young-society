Definition from research

Absalom, "hanged by his hair" after rebelling against his father, recalls 2 Samuel 18, where his pride, his famous long hair, snares him in an oak and finishes him.

The garden "created for one whose eyes were as missing" is a garden for the blind, and in the book's hands blindness reads as the soul's want of light as much as the eye's, with light standing, as in John's gospel, for God himself.

"flowers of the old Jerusalem, flowers of the new Jerusalem" sets the earthly, grieving city against the heavenly one of Revelation, the fallen world against the redeemed.

"the forms without color and void as when darkness moved on the face of the deep" borrows Genesis 1:2 again, the formless dark before creation.

"the greatest remorse ever since the day of the beginning" reads creation as flawed from the start, a God grieving his own work, the Fall pushed back to the first day.

The line that "the wisest man was a fool in God's eyes," with "a fool bound in the heart of every child," joins 1 Corinthians 3:19, the world's wisdom foolishness to God, with Proverbs 22:15, folly bound up in a child.
