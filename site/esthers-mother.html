In the research: Chapter 78, Chapter 79

Definition from research

Chapter 78

Listed as: Esther’s mother

  • She blames her for the death of her twin sister, Rosemary, who was born prematurely and dead. Esther is accused of killing this twin by kicking her in the womb, an act that haunts her throughout her life. This accusation shapes Esther’s self-perception as a murderer even before birth and contributes to her lifelong feelings of guilt and alienation.
  • She hit Esther for screaming about the bee, tied her by a rope in the yard, and “had never nursed her at those narrow breasts.”
  • “Her mother used to tie her by a rope”: Vera narrates the harsh and painful parental abuse Esther Longtree endured from a very early age. Esther’s mother is portrayed as a cold, narrow-hearted woman who blamed Esther for the death of her twin sister, Rosemary, even before Esther was born. Her mother repeatedly accused her of having “killed a little child before she was ever born,” labeling her guilty of infanticide in the womb, which created a deep sense of shame and guilt in Esther throughout her life. This accusation was a source of constant reproach and emotional cruelty, with her mother both laughing and crying as she reminded Esther of this supposed crime. The mother also expressed disappointment and resentment toward Esther, comparing her unfavorably to the lost twin, Rosemary, who was idealized as perfect, delicate, and the daughter her mother wished she had. This kept Esther in a state of embarrassment and alienation, burdened by the shadow of the lost child and her mother’s cold judgment. Esther’s mother physically abused her as a child, exemplified when she once hit Esther for screaming about a bee that had flown up her skirts, not believing her and dismissing her cries. Esther was also tied by a rope in the yard under a tree by her mother, indicating neglect and harsh treatment. The mother is described as childish in mentality and size, living in a house too small for both her and Esther, filled with fragile objects that seemed to symbolize the fragile and oppressive environment Esther was subjected to. The emotional abuse extended to lack of nurturing; Esther’s mother never nursed her at the narrow breasts or stayed to comfort her tears. She also mocked Esther’s awkwardness, physical deformities, and stubborn nature, further isolating her emotionally. The mother’s coldness was a constant presence, leaving Esther to grow up lonely and unloved, intensifying her suffering. Esther’s maternal abuse was a complex mixture of emotional cruelty, neglect, physical punishment, and harsh judgment rooted in the tragic loss of her twin and her mother’s inability to love her fully, which deeply affected Esther’s sense of self and her life trajectory.
  • “Her mother had accused her of murder”: Esther’s mothers behavior is a form of severe emotional abuse with devastating psychological consequences for the surviving child. The parent’s actions are often rooted in their own inability to cope with the trauma, grief, and feelings of helplessness surrounding the death. Individuals with narcissistic or antisocial traits may lack empathy and view their children as objects to be used to regulate their own emotions. In this context, blaming the child can serve the parent’s emotional needs rather than the child’s.

Chapter 79

  • A small, delicate, and fragile woman who yearned for a child but was unable to conceive naturally.
  • She endured the long, torturous process of conception mediated by the “other woman.”
  • Often blames Esther for the barren landscape of their lives and the shadow of Rosemary.
