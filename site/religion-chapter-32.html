Characters: Cousin Hannah

Allusions: Women's Suffrage

Definition from research

Cousin Hannah, the book says, "should have died... a martyr to her cause," raising her suffrage fight to the level of religious martyrdom, a death for belief.

Her "death-bed confession" invokes the Christian practice of final confession, and the doubt cast on it, whether it happened at all, recalls the uncertainty around any late repentance, the Good Thief's grace turned questionable.

She claims "the greatest battles were fought in the domestic wars," making the home, not the battlefield or the crusade, the true holy war, with woman at its center.

"weddings are funerals, and funerals are weddings," she says, and "when one marries, a part of one must die." The paradox draws on the Christian dying-to-self of marriage (Ephesians 5) and the marriage of the soul to God in death, union and loss folded together.
