On Epizeuxis

The rhetorical device Esther begins using in this chapter (the tripling of words in immediate succession (baby, baby, baby; gone, gone, gone)) is called epizeuxis, from the Greek for “fastening upon.” It is one of the oldest and most emotionally direct devices in the rhetorical tradition, used in scripture, in incantation, in the cries of grief across cultures. The tripling carries a ritual quality: three repetitions constitute a charm, an oath, a summoning. Esther is not merely emphasizing. She is attempting, through language, to make something real, to hold something still. The repetition also has the quality of a stutter: the word catching, unable to clear, the mouth returning to the same unresolvable sound. Young gives Esther this voice as she deepens into her most painful material. The language breaks along the same fault line as the experience.
