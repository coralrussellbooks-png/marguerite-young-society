Characters: Catherine Cartwheel · Miss MacIntosh

Themes: Death and Mortality

Definition from research

More references gather around death than around anything else: the valley of dry bones, the thief in the night, Abraham's bosom, the last judgment, the resurrection, funeral after funeral. The book keeps returning to the one question religion exists to answer, and it refuses the comforting answers. Miss MacIntosh will not romanticize dying; it is a plain end. Spitzer expects "no improvement on the other side." Catherine turns death into more dreaming. Even the wedding is a funeral, the vow bound to "the dreadful Day of Judgment," the bride already a corpse. Young raises the promise of heaven on nearly every deathbed, and nearly every time lets it dissolve into doubt or dream. The consolation is offered and withdrawn, and mortality is left sharp.
