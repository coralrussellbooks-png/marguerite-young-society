On Einstein and time.

The chapter engages, obliquely but seriously, with Einstein's observation that the distinction between past, present, and future is only a stubbornly persistent illusion. For Young, this is not a physicist's curiosity but a lived condition. Catherine experiences time as non-linear; she cannot reliably sequence what has already happened from what she is imagining, or what is memory from what is dream. Einstein gave this a scientific name. Young shows you what it feels like from the inside.
