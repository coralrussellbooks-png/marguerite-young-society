Pillar: Pillar 4 Witnessing Is The Core Ethical Act · Across every book

or On the urge to look away, and the quiet power of refusing to.

It is exhausting to keep looking. There is too much, it is too much, and the most natural thing in the world is to turn the eyes somewhere easier. Power knows this and offers you several ways not to look. It offers spectacle, an endless bright churn of things to watch instead. It offers a single enemy, so that all your looking can be aimed in one safe direction. And it offers, at the rally, in the feed, the rare and intoxicating feeling of being seen yourself, which is its own way of stopping you from seeing.

What it cannot survive is a person who stays in the room and keeps their eyes open.

Young’s narrator is a witness above all else. She does not solve the people she loves. She does not look away from the mother in her drugged paradise or the nursemaid in her concealment. She stays, she watches, she keeps faith with what is actually in front of her, even when it offers her no resolution and no reward. The whole book is an act of refusing to leave the room.

The fourth pillar is that refusal. The witness is the one who does not flinch, does not change the channel, does not accept the spectacle in place of the thing, does not let the designated enemy absorb the whole of their attention. It sounds passive. It is not. To witness clearly, over time, without looking away, is one of the few things a person can do that power cannot easily undo, because you cannot be argued out of what you saw with your own eyes. The lie needs you to have not been looking.

You do not have to fix what you witness. That is a different pillar, and a relief. You only have to stay, and see, and refuse the comfort of the turned head. Most of what gets done in the dark is done counting on the fact that we would rather not look. Be the one who looks. Stay in the room.
