Doc O'Leary

Part of Intertextuality in Miss MacIntosh, My Darling, a study of Marguerite Young’s novel Miss MacIntosh, My Darling (1965).
By Coral Russell · Updated November 23, 2026

Chapters discussed: 7, 68, 77

The old country doctor, Doctor O'Leary (chapter 68), carries a broken cry. As the bus-driver talks of him on the night road, he becomes "A fool, Lear in the storm" (chapter 7). The weightiest philosophical line in the novel is his. In Esther Longtree's memory, after delivering a real baby he believed was a dream, he declares, "Oh, I see it all now! Life is a dream, and I have been wrong and much wronged, and I must give up this practice!" (chapter 77).
