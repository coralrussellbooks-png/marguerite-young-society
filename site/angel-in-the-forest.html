Angel in the Forest: A Fairy Tale of Two Utopias

← Miss MacIntosh, My Darling · The Marguerite Young Society · Harp Song for a Radical →

Published: first published in 1945; reissued by Scribner in 1966 and now available from Dalkey Archive Press.

In the late 1930s Young visited New Harmony, Indiana, a small town that had been the site of two nineteenth-century attempts to build a perfect society. She moved there and stayed for years, and this book came out of that time.

The first utopia was founded in 1814 by Father George Rapp, a German mystic whose followers practiced a Scriptural communism while they waited for the New Jerusalem. The second began in 1825, when Robert Owen, one of the founders of British socialism, bought the settlement and tried to build a perfect community on rational lines. Both failed.

Young braids history and imagination into a study of people who tried to build heaven on earth and how their dreams came apart in their hands. The book won her a Guggenheim Fellowship, and its subject stayed with her for the rest of her life.
