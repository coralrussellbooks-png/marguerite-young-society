Reality Is Constructed

Part of Intertextuality in Miss MacIntosh, My Darling, a study of Marguerite Young’s novel Miss MacIntosh, My Darling (1965).
By Coral Russell · Updated October 29, 2026

See also: Pillar 2 Reality Is Constructed

Characters: Esther Longtree · Mr. Titus Bonebreaker · Mrs. Hogden

Allusions: Iowa, Indiana, and the Midwest

Definition from research

"Most of what feels like solid reality is built: money, borders, laws, status."

The novel begins with the inheritance of the West literally built beside an Iowa road: "a windmill, a leaning tower, Noah's Ark, the old woman who lived in the shoe" (chapter 1). Status is built the same way. Mr. Bonebreaker's grandmother assures him that only an illegitimate birth kept him from a European throne, "and it was all in the Almanach de Gotha and various other almanacs." He could claim it, she says, and "achieve almost any miracle if he believed in America" (chapter 65).

Other borrowed crowns follow: Esther Longtree's mother is a "pallid descendant of George Washington's sister" (chapter 78), and Mrs. Hogden claims three illustrious ancestors at once. The essays "The Roadside Canon" and "Borrowed Crowns" follow these claims. What they show is that in Young's America rank is printed and repeated into being, and her characters half know it.
