From the guide

On the 1929 crash and the windows.

When Miss MacIntosh says her death will be natural, not suicide, not like the gamblers jumping from high windows after the crash, she is placing herself, and her death, against one of the defining images of the Great Depression. Whether men actually jumped from Wall Street windows in 1929 is historically contested (far fewer did than the myth suggests), but the image was real in the cultural imagination of Miss MacIntosh's era. She distinguishes herself from those men deliberately: she is not frenzied by loss, not destroyed by the market, not driven to despair by financial ruin. She walks into the ocean calmly, on her own terms, on a cold September morning, as she has always done everything.
