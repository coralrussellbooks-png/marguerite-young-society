On the Detective Confessing Imaginary Crimes

The detail that the detective gets confessions from murderers “by confessing his own imaginary crimes” is one of Young’s most compressed ironies. The interrogation technique (the interviewer fabricating sympathy, claiming shared experience, manufacturing false solidarity to draw out the suspect) is real, and its use to extract confessions (sometimes false ones) has been documented in miscarriages of justice throughout American legal history. Young’s detective uses this method professionally. His imaginary crimes are not so imaginary. He has confessed to nothing real. He is still looking for Esther.
