Definition from research

Catherine's opium paradise is the novel's great study of a made world. She "placed her faith only in the unreal," dwelt where forms change their shapes and death is only more dreaming, and the house filled with her constructions until the servants ran in all directions after phantoms. Set against her is Miss MacIntosh, who insists on a shared, sober reality of hard facts and common sense. The book does not simply take Miss MacIntosh's side. It lets the two women stand as the argument itself, over who builds the world and by what right one construction gets to call itself the truth. The most vertiginous lines belong to neither pole and to both. There is, one voice says, "no God but what we dreamed." Another concludes that it was all a dream, and that in another dream we die. Reality here is an agreement, powerful and binding, and the novel keeps asking who authored the version everyone has agreed to inhabit.
