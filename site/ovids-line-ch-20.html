Ovid’s line (Ch 20)

On Chapter 20 of Marguerite Young’s novel Miss MacIntosh, My Darling (1965). Part of A Guide to Reading Miss MacIntosh, My Darling.
By Coral Russell · Updated October 29, 2026

From the reading guide, Chapter 20 · Research: Chapter 20

From the guide

Ovid’s line (“ugly is a field without grass, a plant without leaves, a head without hair”) appears in the Amores, Ovid’s early collection of love elegies. Young cites it almost as a footnote to Vera’s wondering, then immediately follows it with the Greek proverb: “hair adds beauty to a good face and terror to an ugly one.” She is placing Miss MacIntosh’s baldness in the longest possible context of what hair has meant to human beings as a sign of beauty, status, and identity, and then showing you a woman who, stripped of all of it, is still a sad masterpiece.
