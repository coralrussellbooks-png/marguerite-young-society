On Marie Marvingt, the fiancée of danger.

Marie Marvingt was a French athlete who climbed mountains, flew balloons and airplanes, swam the English Channel, served as a combat pilot in World War I, and invented the air ambulance. She was called 'the fiancée of danger' by a journalist in 1903, and the nickname followed her for the rest of her life. Young built Cousin Hannah from women like Marvingt: the women of the late nineteenth and early twentieth century who claimed every domain of physical courage and daring that was supposed to belong to men, and did it without relinquishing their womanhood, or while permanently renegotiating what their womanhood meant. The fiancée of danger never married the danger. She was its perpetual intended.
