Definition from research

Chapter 5

  • She is a figure embodying deafness and detachment from reality, both literally and metaphorically.
  • Her physical deafness also isolates and makes her unable to engage fully with the world around her.
  • This deafness symbolizes a disconnection from the living world and the ongoing turmoil and a futile or incomplete attempt to connect or listen.
  • Her perceptions and experiences are filtered through dreams or illusions rather than reality which further emphasizes her detachment and perhaps a coping mechanism against the bleakness surrounding her.
  • She complains to the desk clerk about being kept awake by noises that are in fact spectral or imaginary. These hauntings reflect a world where the boundaries between life and death, reality and illusion, have blurred, and Miss Pebbles exists trapped in this liminal space.
  • Her character serves as a poignant contrast to the bus-driver’s chaotic, vivid awareness of the world’s decay and the surreal events transpiring around them. While the bus-driver wrestles with his drunken consciousness and the metaphysical implications of his journey, Miss Pebbles is insulated, unable or unwilling to fully confront these realities. Her state can be seen as symbolic of denial or the human tendency to retreat into subjective realities when faced with overwhelming despair and madness.
