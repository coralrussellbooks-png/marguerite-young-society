On Diogenes and the lantern.

Diogenes of Sinope, the founder of Cynicism, was famous for walking through the streets of Athens in broad daylight with a lit lantern, telling anyone who asked that he was looking for a man: meaning an honest man, a genuine human being. He found none he considered adequate. He lived in a large storage jar, rejected conventional values, and argued that virtue was the only good. Young's Mr. Spitzer, wandering the fog-bound city asking strangers if they have seen the dead suffragette, is Diogenes's opposite: Diogenes carried his own light and found no one worthy of it; Mr. Spitzer is looking for light in others and finding only the pigeon-colored dawn. Both searches are honest. Both fail to find what they are looking for.
