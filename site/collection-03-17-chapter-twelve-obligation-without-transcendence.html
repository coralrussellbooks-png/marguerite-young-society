Chapter Twelve: Obligation Without Transcendence

← Part Four: What This Requires · Radical Immanence Collection · Chapter Thirteen: The Witness Across Time →

The hardest philosophical problem the eleventh pillar creates is also the most important one: how do you ground intergenerational obligation without transcendence?

Every major framework for grounding obligation to future generations has relied, in the end, on something outside the immanent world. Natural law grounds it in a moral order that transcends human construction. Religious ethics grounds it in divine command or divine purpose. Utilitarian calculation grounds it in a principle of impartiality that requires treating all persons, past, present, and future, as equally worthy of moral consideration, which is a principle that cannot itself be derived from any particular person's interests and so requires a standpoint outside any particular person's perspective, a view from nowhere that is itself a form of transcendence.

The eleventh pillar cannot use any of these grounds. It is committed to radical immanence: the sacred without the beyond, the ethical without the transcendent anchor, the obligation that is real without being grounded in anything outside the world it operates in.

The ground the eleventh pillar offers is structural rather than moral. The obligation to future generations does not arise from a moral principle that transcends the present moment. It arises from the fact of being a crossing point: from being constituted by what was deposited before you arrived, from being a carrier of what will constitute the ground that comes after. The obligation is not chosen. It is constitutional. It is in the structure of what you are.

This is a different kind of obligation than Western ethics has generally recognized. Western ethics is largely built on the assumption that obligation is either chosen, as in contractual obligation, or derived from a moral principle that applies to all rational agents, as in Kantian duty, or calculated from the consequences of actions for sentient beings capable of suffering, as in utilitarian obligation. All of these models assume a self that stands outside the obligation and either chooses it or is subject to it from a position of prior independence.

The crossing point is not outside the obligation. The crossing point is constituted by the transmission that generates the obligation. You cannot step outside the obligation any more than you can step outside the sediment you are standing on. The obligation is not something that applies to you from outside. It is something you are made of.

This is the most radical thing the eleventh pillar says, and it is also the most honest. The pretense of standing outside obligation, of being a self that chooses its commitments from a position of prior freedom, is the pretense that the ten pillars have been dismantling from the beginning. The plural self cannot stand outside its own plurality and choose which parts to be. The sedimentary self cannot stand outside its own sediment and choose which layers to acknowledge. The alluvial self cannot stand outside its own constitution and choose which deposits to carry.

The obligation is not a burden added to a self that would otherwise be free. It is a dimension of what the self actually is: a crossing point on a transmission line, constituted by what was given and constituting what will be deposited, holding the weight in both directions not because it chose to but because that is the structure of where it is.

This does not mean the obligation is unlimited. The crossing point is finite. The life that stands at the crossing point is seventy or eighty or ninety years against the age of what surrounds it. The obligation is not to carry everything. It is to carry honestly: to acknowledge the weight, to not flatten it into a more useful shape, to pass it forward with the full recognition of what it is and where it came from.

The crossing point does not resolve the tension between the finite self and the infinite transmission. It holds the tension. That is what crossing points do. They hold the intersection of what cannot be reconciled into a single axis, the horizontal and the vertical, the finite and the geological, the present moment and the seven generations. The obligation is to hold that intersection honestly, without pretending it is lighter than it is, without pretending the ground is neutral, without pretending you are not already inside the transmission.

That is obligation without transcendence. That is what the eleventh pillar requires.
