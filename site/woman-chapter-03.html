Woman in Young's Magnum Opus

Chapter 3

Other versions: Chapter hub · Research notes · The Wheel and the Spokes

Characters: Catherine Cartwheel · Miss MacIntosh · Vera Cartwheel

Miss MacIntosh arrives as the anti-dream, and the chapter builds her carefully as the one thing in the book that will not melt. Her hair is a helmet, her cheeks are frosty apples, her body is armored against weather and nonsense. She believes life is "hard and intended to be hard," refuses ghosts and phantoms outright, and turns her strength toward the coal miners and laborers of the real world rather than the phantoms of Catherine's. It is a feminist archetype of the grounded, self-reliant woman, and it is aimed too low.

The armor is the point, because the book is going to take it off. The helmet of hair pays off when the wig comes away and the bald head is underneath. The corset and the guarded body pay off when the amputation is revealed. Her introduction as the character who most refuses dissolution is the setup for her becoming, later, the character who carries dissolution on her own flesh, the body as the place the self comes apart. Even the head-hunter who wants her head belongs to this thread: he treats the self as a detachable, collectible thing, which is exactly what the wig and the severed identity will confirm.

Her disappearance closes the chapter and opens the book's central question. She is gone, only practical belongings left, and the law cannot say whether she is dead. It is society failing to recognize a woman's personhood and something stranger. It leaves the boundary between living and dead standing open and hands Vera a search with no possible end. The book will not close either.
