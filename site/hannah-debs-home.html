The Suffragette and the Socialist: Cousin Hannah and Eugene Debs

Part of a reading of Harp Song for a Radical (1999), Marguerite Young’s life of Eugene Victor Debs.
By Coral Russell · Updated November 25, 2026

Harp Song for a Radical · The Marguerite Young Society

A study in five parts of the two crusaders at the center of Young’s late work: Cousin Hannah, the suffragette at the middle of Miss MacIntosh, My Darling, and Eugene Victor Debs, the socialist at the center of Harp Song for a Radical.

The parts

  1. The Suffragette and the Socialist: Cousin Hannah, Eugene Debs, and the two crusaders at the center of Marguerite Young’s late work
  2. The Bell and the Blind Leader: Hannah leads the army. Debs refuses to be Moses.
  3. Terre Haute, 1880: The real history that connects Debs to Hannah’s cause
  4. Married to the Movement: What each crusade cost at home
  5. The Vote, the Eight-Hour Day, and the Unfinished: Partial victories and open endings
