On Aldo Leopold's monument to the pigeon.

Leopold erected his monument in 1947 at the University of Wisconsin Arboretum, marking the spot where the university had once planted trees to attract passenger pigeons before the species was extinct. The monument inscription mourned not only the bird but the speed and completeness of the annihilation: a species that darkened the sky reduced to nothing in a human lifetime, through hunting, habitat destruction, and the disruption of communal nesting behavior so extreme that the birds could not reproduce without the thousands of others they required for the ritual. Young would have known this monument. Mr. Spitzer's mourning for Martha draws on exactly the tradition Leopold's inscription embodies: the obligation to remember what we destroyed, not simply as environmental record but as moral fact.
