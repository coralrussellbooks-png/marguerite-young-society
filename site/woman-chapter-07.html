Chapter 7

On Chapter 7 of Marguerite Young’s novel Miss MacIntosh, My Darling (1965). Part of Woman in Young's Magnum Opus.
By Coral Russell · Updated October 29, 2026

Characters: Gertrude Hathaway Tidings · Joachim Spitzer · Madge Capehorn · Moses Hunnecker · Musidora Tidings

The chapter opens on Moses and "old Doc" again, and the doctor as a failed male authority. He is also better understood as the continuation of Chapter 5's central image. He answers calls that never come, drives a car that cannot move, and presides over a country where, as the text says, there are no births and no deaths. This is the interrupted birth widened into a whole landscape, and the boundary between life and death held permanently open: a place where nothing completes, neither the arrival nor the departure.

Gertrude, who married two brothers and is said to have "killed two brothers with one stone," is the first appearance of a figure the book will make central in Mr. Spitzer, the two brothers who are versions of one another, so alike that a single act reaches both. The doubling is plural selfhood, worn by minor characters before the major one arrives.

Musidora, Gertrude's sister, mute and childlike and sold by her mother for sixteen dollars as the neglected poor woman. She is that and she is also the book's recurring figure at the edge of erasure, the person present and almost unseen, almost unheard, who returns as the literal mute of the later chapters. When Madge says Musidora "will be always with us," the line is not only social pity. It is the book naming what it does with the erased: it keeps them, present and unaccounted for, at the threshold. The children eating in the graveyard hold that threshold too, life carrying on among the graves.
