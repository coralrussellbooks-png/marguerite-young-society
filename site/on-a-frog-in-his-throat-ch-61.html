On "a frog in his throat."

The idiom was first documented in America in 1847, in a book for young boys called How to Be a Man: advice on building character through honesty, hard work, and self-discipline. The phrase described the inability to speak from overwhelming emotion, especially fear. Young has taken this idiom and made it perfectly, terribly literal: the mute's frog in his throat was his only speech, and now it is dead.
