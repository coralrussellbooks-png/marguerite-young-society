Character: Miss MacIntosh

Definition from research

What the cluster holds

Bunyan, Franklin and Poor Richard, Ecclesiastes and Exodus, the Farmer's and World Almanacs, Moody Bible genealogies, the Hebrew calendar and the Exodus stations, Tower of Babel, Admiral Dewey corset, Constantine, Caesar, Edison, Garfield.

Pattern

She carries reference books, not stories. Her allusions are drills, almanac maxims and lists to memorize. She treats great men as personal adversaries she would scold if she met them (Franklin, Edison, Constantine), and her rebuke always turns on time and light: Constantine changed the calendar, Franklin preached early to bed and then brought on electric light. In chapter 63 the rebuke turns to pity ("my poor Canute") and her guidebook turns into its own negation.

Key references (chapters)

Pilgrim's Progress (3, 17, 18, 63, 68); Slough of Despond (3, 23); Franklin (15, 23, 63); Constantine (22, 23, 63, 64, 66); genealogies (9, 18, 22); Hebrew calendar and Exodus stations (20); "Vain man" litany (23).

Line from the novel

"ours is a Pilgrim's Progress from nowhere to nowhere" (ch. 63)
