Introduction

This is the unabridged collection of all the research that was done from 2019 to 2025 by myself in my attempt to understand what Young was doing and how she built her epic novel. Miss MacIntosh, My Darling by Marguerite Young is available to purchase from Dalkey Archive Press, on Amazon as a new edition in 2022 (print and Kindle), and to borrow from the Open Library. This is a labor of love. There is no publishing house backing this work. It is just a person who fell in love with a book because it became the ultimate reason for having spent a lifetime reading. Marguerite Young in an interview was interested in “obsession” so this person decided to become obsessed with Miss MacIntosh, My Darling. There are oceans and mountains of material to explore in one of America’s greatest literature epic novels. With Miss MacIntosh, My Darling I have found my reason for reading. I hope you find your book and your reason for reading.

Miss MacIntosh, My Darling is an epic coming of age story of a young girl, Vera Cartwheel. It culminates in a long bus ride across the Midwest to find her nursemaid, Miss Georgia MacIntosh, from What Cheer, Iowa. On the bus she encounters: the bus driver, Moses Hunnecker; the old doctor, Justice O’Leary; and a married couple, Madge and Homer Capehorn. The bus driver, Moses, has never been married and has superstitions like not ever cutting his hair. The old doc, O’Leary, was a local physician who made house calls but now that he is older is suffering from dementia according to Moses. The old doc believes he is still delivering babies even though he is retired now. Madge and Homer Capehorn are a newly married couple and Madge is pregnant. She implies it is not his child and she is not marrying the man she wanted. They are returning to their small town to live with her mother-in-law since her parents are dead. Vera overhears all the small town gossip and rumors. She pays particular attention to Madge and her views on pregnancy and marriage. During the trip Vera, shares her story of how she grew up and the adults she had access to: her mother, Catherine; her father, Jock Cartwheel; her nursemaid, Miss MacIntosh; the family lawyer, Joachim Spitzer and his twin brother, Peron. These adults were supposed to teach Vera what she needed to know about living. Since she is the omniscient narrator of the story, she examines the lives of each one and decides in the end how she will live her life. Her mother, Catherine, is an opium addict and has taken to her bed. She never leaves the house and only lives in an imaginary world made up of her drug induced hallucinations. Vera is looked after by a governess, Miss Georgia MacIntosh. Mr. Spitzer, lawyer of Catherine’s estate, hired her after Catherine took to her bed. Miss MacIntosh is the ideal example of practicality. She has Vera memorize facts and adheres to a strict schedule in order to establish a sense of discipline, work ethic, and reality in Vera that directly counters Catherine’s illusions. Miss MacIntosh harbors deep scars of her own and when Vera turns fourteen, Miss MacIntosh sabotages Vera’s birthday party. Vera, while staying up and reveling in the beautiful night in the garden on her birthday, wanders around the estate and enters Miss MacIntosh’s room and finds her deepest secret. Miss MacIntosh assaults Vera and in the morning pretends nothing has happened. Vera’s innocence is lost and she confronts Miss MacIntosh about what happened that night. Miss MacIntosh hints at how she will die. It is implied that Miss MacIntosh is dead, having committed suicide by walking into the ocean weighted down by rocks in her pockets. Mr. Spitzer assumes she has just left. Her body is never found. Vera is inconsolable and her mother Catherine can only offer her an escape through drugs to ease her grief. Mr. Spitzer, Joachim, is a lawyer who is the manager of Catherine’s and Cousin Hannah’s estates. As the last close adult that may help Vera navigate growing up, he is unable to because he is consumed with grief at the early loss of his twin brother, Peron, by suicide. A large part of the story centers around Mr. Spitzer and his attempts to make sense of life, death and love through philosophical questioning. He was involved in a love triangle with Catherine. Joachim loved Catherine but she did not love him. Catherine was in love with his twin brother, Peron. Peron did not love Catherine. He died young and Joachim stayed by Catherine’s side the rest of her life. Mr. Spitzer dresses like Jock, Catherine’s late husband, Peron and other characters in the book. When Vera is old enough she escapes her mother, Catherine. Since Miss MacIntosh’s body was never found Vera believes she may still exist and decides to go to Miss MacIntosh’s birthplace, What Cheer, Iowa. In Vera’s mind, What Cheer, Iowa has become a magical place that will help her make sense of life. Vera never makes it to What Cheer, Iowa and instead ends up in Indiana, considered the heart of the Middle West. Vera spends the night in a hotel. During that stay she encounters more adults at the tavern and hears their stories. Conclusions are given for Mr. Spitzer after Catherine’s death and parts of Miss MacIntosh’s life. In the end Vera decides to marry a stone deaf man. There is no such thing as perfection or a perfect moment in this life. Vera realizes that “Whatever is average is the dream. Whatever is hidden is the revealed.”
