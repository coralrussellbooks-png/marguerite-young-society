Definition from research

Cousin Hannah carries conquest and the failed quest. Her references come from the East as a campaign ground: Saladin, "Solymon the Magnificent" (chapter 30), Babylon and Nineveh, a white horse "named El-Burraq after the horse which Mohammed the Prophet had tethered at the Wailing Wall" (chapter 30). She crosses "Al Sirat, delicate as a human hair" (chapter 36), the bridge to paradise in Islamic tradition.

Nearly every reference casts her in a man's heroic part. Catherine sees her as "a Captain Derring-Do" (chapter 30). She stands "one arm crossed before her bosom like Napoleon" (chapter 42). "The Swiss had called her the Black Knight or blackamoor" (chapter 52). Her quests do not end in triumph: "she had not found the Holy Grail or any object of her quest. She was that Jason who had not found the golden fleece" (chapter 42). At her death the conquests return as delirium: "Is that Ur of the Chaldees ... Is that Nineveh, Crete, Babylon?" (chapter 43).

Much of Hannah reaches the reader secondhand, through Catherine's dreaming and Mr. Spitzer's memory. The conqueror is always seen by someone who stayed home.
