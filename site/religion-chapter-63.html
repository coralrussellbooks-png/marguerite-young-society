Characters: Miss MacIntosh

Allusions: Ecclesiastes · Genesis · The Bible

Definition from research

"the Rock of Ages in a land where all things crumbled" borrows Toplady's hymn, God the one firm rock while everything else gives way.

"if ever the golden bowl is broken, or the wheel at the cistern," she says, then waves it off, "the Bible exaggerates. You cannot break that which was always broken," turning Ecclesiastes' images of death against the Book itself.

"I may be sailing, sailing without a shroud" glances at the burial cloth, the linen that wrapped Christ.

"Heaven preserve you from a beardless man," and "from a hairy woman," is an old proverb of distrust, a warning against appearances that lie.

"the mad man leading the mad to his destruction" reworks the blind leading the blind of Matthew 15.

"ours is a Pilgrim's Progress from nowhere to nowhere," and "we must walk through seas as if they were flowering cherry orchards," takes Bunyan's pilgrimage and empties it of any destination, faith reduced to a willed pretense of solid ground.

"Who can help me but God Himself Who has already failed and is not?" is Miss MacIntosh's bleakest cry, a God who has failed and does not exist appealed to all the same.

"Live each day as if it were the last. For it will be the last for some" turns the old carpe diem toward plain mortality.

"What was brought to Noah by a dove?" recalls the olive leaf of Genesis 8, the sign that the flood had passed.

"Here's one who was never born and yet has died" names the stillborn, the unlived life, one of the chapter's litany of the dead, the man dead far from home, the man dead intestate.

"I am killed by the oldest preacher, he with the flaming hair" is Miss MacIntosh's figure for her own undoing, a preacher cast as a kind of John the Baptist, and a death that is emotional rather than literal.
