On Wittgenstein's silence.

The final proposition of Ludwig Wittgenstein's Tractatus Logico-Philosophicus (whereof one cannot speak, thereof one must be silent) is one of philosophy's most famous closures. Wittgenstein spent the Tractatus drawing the limits of what language can meaningfully say, and then, having drawn those limits, acknowledged everything that lay beyond them: ethics, aesthetics, the mystical, the meaning of life. He did not say these things were unreal. He said they were beyond the reach of language to express. Silence is not dismissal; it is the appropriate response to what exceeds the sayable. Young's novel is built, in part, on the territory Wittgenstein marked as silent: the territory where circling prose and accumulated image take over from direct statement.
