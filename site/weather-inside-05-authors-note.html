I came to Marguerite Young in 2019.

Not looking for her specifically. Looking for the thing that was large enough, the way I had been looking my whole life, moving through every tradition and framework and community that came close and then showed me its ceiling. I found her the way you find the thing that was always waiting: by reading to the end when every reasonable person would have stopped.

Miss MacIntosh, My Darling is twelve hundred pages. It has no plot in the conventional sense. It does not resolve. It does not arrive at a destination. It builds, recursively and excessively and with complete commitment to the truth of what consciousness actually is, a philosophy that I had been living inside without a name for it my entire life.

I have read it five times.

Each time it gave me something the previous reading could not give me, because I was not the same person who had read it before. That is not a metaphor. That is the plural self and the reef and the working story and the grief climate and the standing still, all of it, enacted in the relationship between a reader and a text across years of a life.

This book is the translation of what Young built into a framework you can use. The philosophy is hers. The application is mine. The hope is that it reaches the people who have been living these truths without a name for them, the way I was, for longer than they should have had to carry the weight of the unnamed thing.

Even if to no one else: I found it, and it changed my life.

That is enough to build from.

That is everything.
