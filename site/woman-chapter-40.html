Chapter 40

On Chapter 40 of Marguerite Young’s novel Miss MacIntosh, My Darling (1965). Part of Woman in Young's Magnum Opus.
By Coral Russell · Updated October 29, 2026

Characters: Catherine Cartwheel · Cousin Hannah

Voice is the pillar, and Young gives it to Cousin Hannah as a spirit who speaks languages "no living person had ever spoken." Armored, with a "Gothic cathedral voice" and a "husky baritone," fluent in dead and mystical tongues, she is the reclamation of silenced knowledge and the "proud suffragette" whose speech itself shakes cloud ceilings and cracks mirrors, so that a woman's voice becomes a force that unsettles the built world. Catherine's wonder at why Hannah should speak at all registers how thoroughly women have been silenced, and how radical the plain fact of her voice is.

Non-linear time is the pillar's second face. Hannah's fury at archaeologists and "believers in linear time," the sense that "all of space could be wound, unwound like a bolt of silk," refuses the straight patriarchal chronology, and Catherine's fluency in languages she never studied is the innate, intuitive knowledge a male education system withheld from women. The disruption of natural order, echo before song and death before life, is the deconstruction of the sequences that hold women in place, and the mingling of living and dead is women's particular relation to memory and loss.

Hannah learns by observing rather than by instruction, greeting strangers as relatives, keeping "silent conversations" with figures like Daniel Boone and an opossum "feigning death," and her adventures may be spiritual and mental as much as physical, the inner life patriarchal history overlooks. Catherine's mind, an "iridescent jumble" of unthought thoughts and unlived dreams, and her uncertain physical presence, whether she has walked out into the clouds, are the fluid, uncontainable feminine selfhood the chapter refuses to pin down. It ends on water flowing over "mooned sand" and the figurehead who knows more than the captain, a woman seen as ornament possessing the deeper knowledge, and the voice that was never supposed to be heard still speaking.
