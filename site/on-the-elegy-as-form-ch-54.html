On the elegy as form.

The elegy has a long history as a poetic and musical form: in ancient Greece it was simply verse written in elegiac couplets, regardless of subject; in Latin literature it became associated with lament; by the Renaissance it was specifically the form for mourning the dead. The great elegies (Milton's Lycidas, Shelley's Adonais, Tennyson's In Memoriam) share a structure: the poem moves from grief through its processing toward some form of consolation or acceptance. Young's Mr. Spitzer has been writing his elegy for decades and has never reached the consolation. The form is right for him. He is in the grief section, permanently. The wind takes the music before it can resolve.
