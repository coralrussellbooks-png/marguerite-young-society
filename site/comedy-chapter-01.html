Definition from research

The bus driver, later Moses Hunnecker, is the novel's first comic engine, a grotesque of a man wholly at peace with his own recklessness. He drives through blinding mist so erratically that Vera fears their "dismembered heads rolling in a corn field," yet he greets each near miss by whistling and turning back "with a kind of serene triumph." He drinks from a whiskey bottle "quite openly, yet with many calls upon God, the angels, the archangels, angel Gabriel," imitating birdcalls between swigs and guessing aloud that his old woman will take his head off when he gets home. The humor is the gap between catastrophe and his complete unconcern, a man out of control who behaves as though he were the safest driver alive.
