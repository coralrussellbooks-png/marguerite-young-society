Definition from research

"Grammar carries a worldview." The principle adds: "Nouns freeze things into fixed identities; verbs keep them moving."

Catherine's catalogs are streams of nouns, yet nothing in them stays frozen. Her alphabet moves: "The P's were crowding upon her" (chapter 9). Her guest rooms fill until "Spinoza and Shakespeare and Mrs. Sydington were already in the green" (chapter 24). Ordering the world by first letter, she lets no name outrank another.

The principle also distrusts any voice that claims to speak from nowhere, in pure objectivity. Miss MacIntosh's voice of plain fact seems to be that voice. She says she never dreams, and "never would she secede her common sense to the illusion" (chapter 63). Then her disguise is uncovered, and she cries, "We each must die and know that this life was all a dream" (chapter 18). The voice from nowhere turns out to stand somewhere after all.
