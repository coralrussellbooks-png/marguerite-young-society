Woman in Young's Magnum Opus

Chapter 8

Other versions: Chapter hub · Research notes · The Wheel and the Spokes

Characters: Beatrice Fitzgerald · Moses Hunnecker

Two roads run through this chapter, and the difference between them is the book's difference. On one, the graveyard, the family names worn off the stones, the "dead ear," and "no sound but the past." On the other, a "beautiful life" where children dance and youth and age are suspended. It is a feminist utopia outside time's cruelty and suspended time, the book's dream-country where the clock does not run, set against the effaced names of erasure on the road that death still governs.

Beatrice Fitzgerald, gossiped over for her reputation and her illegitimate son, is going blind, "losing the other eye." She is a marginalized woman rendered invisible and also the deaf-and-blind motif again, erasure through a severed sense, the same thread as the deaf villagers of Chapter 4 and the blindness that runs through the book. Moses's tirade against "old Doc" and against aging, the "change of life" and "mortal corruption," carries the body coming apart in a lower, comic register.

The spectral village that closes the chapter, its "dead souls," its "men whose heads do grow beneath their shoulders," its "dream within the dream," is real and dream as one country at its densest so far. The rearranged bodies are plural selfhood in a grotesque key. It is an image of women's social erasure and the dissolving ordinary shape of a person and a place, and it is doing it to everyone in view.
