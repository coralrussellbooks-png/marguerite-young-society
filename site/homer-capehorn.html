Definition from research

Chapter 1

Listed as: Madge Capehorn/Edwards and Homer Capehorn

  • Symbols of innocence and conflict, youth and resistance to natural processes.

Chapter 5

Listed as: Madge and Homer Capehorn

Chapter 6

  • Physically strong but emotionally absent and oblivious to Madge’s suffering.
  • Described as sleeping at her side, dreaming of himself rather than her.
  • Represents failed masculinity and emotional neglect.
  • Not the father of Madge’s child, their marriage is hollow and unfulfilling.

Chapter 7

  • Madge’s husband, often skeptical and somewhat detached.
  • Exhibits a more hopeful or resigned attitude toward death and life’s hardships, believing in an afterlife and the persistence of spirits.
  • Struggles to accept Madge’s harsher truths and prefers rational explanations.

Chapter 8

  • Offers a more nuanced and sympathetic view of Beatrice Fitzgerald, describing her as hardworking, loyal, and even "royal" because of her name’s lineage.
  • He recounts stories of her past hardships and suggests a tragic background, including the loss of an eye and a son who joined the Navy.

Chapter 67

Listed as: Homer

  • Impatient and absent-minded, dreaming of a bride he fears his wife might never know.
