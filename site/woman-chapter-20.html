Woman in Young's Magnum Opus

Chapter 20

← Chapter 19 · Woman in Young's Magnum Opus · Chapter 21 →

Other versions: Chapter hub · Research notes · The Wheel and the Spokes

Characters: Miss MacIntosh · Vera Cartwheel

Dawn comes as revelation and as harm at once, a light that cleanses and strips "bare as roses of their petals," and what it lays open is Miss MacIntosh's hairlessness, so complete the narrator thinks she might as well wear a beard. The beard is the crossing point again, the bald woman crossing toward the "old man" of the earlier chapter and the bearded Venus of the garden, and the "intolerable vacancy" where the breast should be is the body as the place the self comes apart, held now in full daylight.

The chapter's turn is quiet and total: Vera means to hide her own "secret baldness" from Miss MacIntosh. Vera is bald too. The dissolution has crossed from the guardian to the girl, and the two women become versions of each other, plural selfhood doubled across a bedroom, each resolving to protect the other from the knowledge of a shared condition. Vera will "pretend that we were simple," will iron and recite the genealogies and the ancient measures and calendars, the patriarchal order of names and dates and hours laid down like suspended time over two bald heads that no longer fit inside it.

She resolves to "hold to every moment as if I lived with a dying man" and to keep Miss MacIntosh from the "knowledge of herself," and the tenderness is the mask turned into an act of love, the life-and-death boundary tended by a girl who will not let the dying woman see that she is dying, or that they are the same. The image the chapter leaves is "the tree stripped bare of leaves before the tree was," a stripping that comes before its own cause, order running backward, the book's world keeping none of the sequences the reader brought to it.
