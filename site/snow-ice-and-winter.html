Definition from research

Chapter 4 · Frozen Iowa and Dead Star

  • Iowa represents the harsh, ordinary, and ultimately unwelcoming origin.
  • The "dead star" metaphor suggests a point of no return or a lost opportunity.

Chapter 13 · Frozen Lake Constance

  • Symbolizes impossible journeys, transition from ignorance to painful knowledge, and the shattering of illusions.

Chapter 14 · Imagery of Water, Snow, and Night

  • Recurring references to water, foaming seas, snowflakes, and darkness evoke a liminal, otherworldly atmosphere.
  • These natural elements symbolize change, decay, and the boundary between life and death.

Chapter 23 · Winter and Ice

  • Metaphors for death, hardship, and the passage of time.

Chapter 29 · Winter and Hibernation

  • The mourning cloak butterfly hibernating symbolizes endurance, waiting, and the hope of rebirth.

Chapter 30 · Sword of Ice

  • Represents Cousin Hannah’s ruthless clarity and decisive action cutting through illusions.

Chapter 32 · Winter, Snow, and Cold Imagery

  • The persistent references to coldness, snow, frostbite, and icicles create a chilling atmosphere that underlines both Cousin Hannah’s harshness and isolation and the emotional coldness surrounding the characters. For example, the barometer dropping "colder than a sea of the icebergs," and “great icicles hanging from the ceiling” during Cousin Hannah’s visits symbolize the emotional frost and the chilling impact she has on Catherine.
  • Snow and ice also symbolize purity, whiteness, and unattainable beauty, reflecting Cousin Hannah’s idealized vision of womanhood as “unsullied honor” and beauty “as white as virginal snow”.

Chapter 37 · Black Snow / Sable Snow

  • Symbolizes corruption, darkness, and the inversion of purity.

Chapter 38 · Whistling Sound & Snow

  • Cousin Hannah’s entrances marked by whistling and snowstorms symbolize her power and otherworldly nature.

Chapter 38 · Frozen Beauty & Marble

  • Catherine’s beauty is likened to cold, unchanging marble, symbolizing her emotional and physical stasis.

Chapter 41 · Snow and Winter

  • Snow imagery recurs throughout, symbolizing purity, coldness, death, and mystery.
  • Catherine walks naked through snow and blizzards that are warm and opalescent, blending opposites.

Chapter 42 · Snow and Ice

  • Metaphors for death, stillness, and the crystallization of time and memory. The persistent snow on Cousin Hannah’s face and body symbolizes the inevitability of death and the fading of life’s passions.

Chapter 43 · Storm, Snow, Desert, Mountains

  • Represent the suffragette’s internal struggle and the vast unknown of death.

Chapter 43 · Iceberg / Frozen Elements

  • Emphasize coldness, isolation, and the slow crumbling of life.

Chapter 44 · Snowdrops and Snow

  • Represent purity, fragility, and the coldness of death. The melting snowdrops in her hand symbolize fading life and lost love.

Chapter 44 · Mountains, Clouds, Icebergs

  • Evoke the harshness and isolation of her inner world and struggles.
  • The iceberg imagery suggests overwhelming, unseen dangers and emotional coldness.

Chapter 45 · Clouds and Snow

  • Ambiguity, transition, death, and the veil between life and afterlife.

Chapter 46 · Mountains and Snow

  • Mountains stand for the ultimate challenges and heights of spirit and consciousness. Snow and ice symbolize purity, death, and timelessness, as well as the coldness and isolation that often accompany great achievements or sacrifices.

Chapter 47 · Mountains and Snow

  • Death’s tomb, purity, and coldness.

Chapter 49 · Water and Ice

  • Recurring images of frozen waters, ice domes, and watery graves symbolize stasis, death, and the frozen moment between life and death.
  • Water also represents fluidity, dream states, and the passage between worlds.

Chapter 62 · Dream of the River of Fire and Ice

  • Reflects the shifting states of reality and consciousness, the tension between light and darkness, life and death.

Chapter 66 · Iceberg and North Pole Imagery

  • Symbolize isolation, the unknown, and the emotional coldness of the characters.

Chapter 68 · Winter Weeds in Spring

  • Symbolize resilience and survival in a decaying environment.

Chapter 74 · The Seasons (Winter and Autumn)

  • Reflect desolation, death, and the cyclical nature of Esther’s suffering.
