Woman in Young's Magnum Opus

Chapter 28

← Chapter 27 · Woman in Young's Magnum Opus · Chapter 29 →

Other versions: Chapter hub · Research notes · The Wheel and the Spokes

Characters: Catherine Cartwheel · Joachim Spitzer · Peron Spitzer

Metamorphosis is the pillar of this chapter, and Young routes it through Catherine's narcotic mind and Mr. Spitzer's butterflies. Catherine's dreams throw up "Russian bishops, queens, duchesses, birds, and serpents," a feminine interior that is at once richly generative and drugged into passivity, so that the same visionary power which makes her a maker of worlds also holds her still. Her "obtuse faith" in Mr. Spitzer's return is maternal persistence, the woman who keeps life and memory going while incapacitated, and the chapter treats that persistence as a real force rather than a symptom.

The butterflies carry the metamorphosis outright. Delicate, ephemeral, transforming, they are the feminized emblem of renewal, and Mr. Spitzer's patient devotion to them is a mode of knowing that runs against his brother Peron's engagement with "the cruelty of actual life." Contemplative aesthetic attention sits on one side and brute pragmatism on the other, feminine and masculine by the old coding, and Young's sympathy stays with the butterflies. When Peron dies and Mr. Spitzer abandons the collecting, the abandonment reads as the way a patriarchal reality suppresses the feminine, spiritual attention it cannot use.

The return to Catherine's "enchanted" room, its "ethereal gleamings" and "semi-faces," is the return of a masculine wanderer to a feminine source, and it names interiority and liminality as sites of both power and vulnerability. The butterfly's indistinguishable beginning and end, birth folded into death, gives the chapter its final figure: cyclical time against the straight line, the interconnection of living and dying that patriarchal chronology cannot admit. Mr. Spitzer's "sporadic butterfly memories" are his intermittent access to that cycle, and the chapter keeps the feminine principle, generative, transformative, resilient, at its center even as it follows a man contemplating wings.
