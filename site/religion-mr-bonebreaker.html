Definition from research

Mr. Bonebreaker is the book's apocalyptic preacher, and he believes every word he shouts. He warns of the coming end, the wrath of God, the day when the wicked fall and the righteous are carried up, and he salts his sermons with Cain and Abel, with death, with the promise of eternal life. He casts himself as God's servant, ready to give up comfort and standing to call others to repent.

The zeal does not spare him. He is worn thin, ragged, half broken by his own labors, often mocked, often alone. Still he holds to salvation and to the kingdom he believes is coming. His theology is literal and severe, hard on sin and worldly pleasure, and narrow about who will be saved. In the novel he stands for conviction itself, the hope and the harshness of it both, a man clinging to certainty in a world that offers him almost nothing.
