Definition from research

Miss MacIntosh’s breakfast tirade picks the least likely villain in American history: “Vain man, Benjamin Franklin!” It was he, harnessing the lightning of the heavens without being asked, who paved the way for electric lights, which now keep everyone up all night, make the nights like the days, and deny a man the privacy of his own bedroom. Old President Garfield, she points out, saw well enough without them.

The joke has a hidden target. A woman seen bald by moonlight a few hours earlier is arguing, with great energy and no apparent connection, that darkness is a blessing and light a violation. Her proverbs come out slightly crooked, giants who may dream they are dwarves, crabs and cranes keeping the same time as her silver dollar watch. Vera hears the comedy and understands the plea under it.
