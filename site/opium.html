In the research: Chapter 4, Chapter 11, Chapter 27, Chapter 40

Definition from research

Chapter 4 · Opium Paradise and Dream States

  • Represents escape from pain and reality.
  • The "sleeping wakefulness" blurs lines between life and death, reality and illusion.

Chapter 11

  • “her opiate voyage through nowhere”: Thomas De Quincey’s opium dreams in Confessions of an English Opium-Eater (1821) carried him to Egypt and the East: “I was buried, for a thousand years, in stone coffins, with mummies and sphinxes.”
  • “Mr. Res Tacamah who was the drug bottle”: His name may join Rx to tacamahac. Rx stands for the Latin recipe, “take,” at the head of a prescription, and tacamahac is an aromatic resin used in ointments and incense. In Chapter 2 he was “the drug bottle with ears to hear her ravings.”

Chapter 11 · Mr. Res Tacamah

  • A symbolic figure representing a drug bottle and shifting identities, suggesting notions of altered consciousness or dependency.

Chapter 27 · Opium Dreams and Fantasies

  • Catherine’s opium-induced visions illustrate altered perception and escapism.
  • These dreams blur the line between reality and imagination.

Chapter 40 · Opium and Somnolence

  • Represent altered states of consciousness, blurring reality and dream.
