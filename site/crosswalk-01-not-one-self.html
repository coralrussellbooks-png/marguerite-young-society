Christianity and Religion in MMMD

The novel refuses the single, stable self on nearly every page, and it does so most sharply through Mr. Spitzer. He is a twin who survived his brother Peron, and his survival is a kind of partial nullification. He is mistaken for the dead man, mistakes himself for him, and cannot always say which brother he is. …

Intertextuality in MMMD

Young's catalogs are the grammar of this principle. Catherine's single yellow canary answers to "Juniper, Tulip, Cellini, Solomon, Methuselah, Marco Polo, Admiral Peary, Shackleton, Dante" (chapter 2). One bird carries nine names, and none of them is the true one. Mr. Spitzer, listing the names of God, concludes that "God was many things to many people" (chapter 29).

The History in MMMD

This is the clearest statement of "Not One Self" in the novel, and it arrives disguised as a mummy. A historical name, which the list files as one person, turns out to be a stack of selves dreaming each other. The same pattern runs through the whole cast. Mr. Spitzer cannot be sure whether he is himself or his dead twin Peron. …
