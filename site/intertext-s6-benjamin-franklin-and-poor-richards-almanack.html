Benjamin Franklin and Poor Richard's Almanack

← Previous · Intertextuality in MMMD Home · Next →

Characters: Miss MacIntosh · Vera Cartwheel

Definition from research

Category: obligatory. Franklin is named repeatedly, and Miss MacIntosh's quarrel with him cannot be followed without his maxims.

Benjamin Franklin published Poor Richard's Almanack from 1732 to 1758. Its maxims of thrift, industry, and early rising became part of American speech. Franklin was also the scientist who flew a kite in a thunderstorm, the inventor of the Franklin stove, and a founder of the republic.

Miss MacIntosh made Vera learn "everything that old Benjamin Franklin had ever said about early to bed and early to rise" (chapter 15). She honors him as the author of "Poor Richard's Almanack, these simple homilies" (chapter 63). She also cannot forgive him. "Vain man, Benjamin Franklin!" she cries (chapters 23 and 63), because the man who preached early rising also opened the age of electric light and "it was he who had written that early to bed makes us wise" (chapter 23). She dreams of "a lightless night before Benjamin Franklin flew his kite" (chapter 63).

The almanac outlives her. In the old doctor's house, Vera finds "Poor Richard's Almanack, which I knew so well" (chapter 71). Every reference to Franklin by name in the novel belongs to Miss MacIntosh. He is her model and her enemy at once, the practical American who could not stop improving the world.
