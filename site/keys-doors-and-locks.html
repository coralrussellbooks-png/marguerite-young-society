Definition from research

Chapter 2 · The Clock and Time

  • The twelve goblets and chairs represent the passage of time and the ritualized nature of existence.
  • Time is both suspended and fragmented in Catherine’s world.

Chapter 7 · The Locked Door & Fire

  • Symbolizes inescapable fate, hidden crimes, and the destructive power of secrets.

Chapter 8 · The Locked Door and the Turning Key

  • Suggest mysteries, secrets, or supernatural forces at play.
  • The locked door that “just locked by itself” hints at unseen powers or fate.

Chapter 12 · Clocks and Time

  • Clocks stopped at different hours symbolize frozen moments, hesitation, or the past lingering in the present.

Chapter 19 · Barred door with seaweed

  • Symbolizes isolation and self-imposed confinement.

Chapter 33 · The Blackbird and the Door

  • Catherine talks to a human-sized blackbird and instructs to open a door that is actually closed.
  • These images portray the overlap of the real and the imaginary.

Chapter 34 · Iron Keys

  • Worn by Cousin Hannah, symbolize access, control, and the discordant music of life’s complexities.

Chapter 38 · Iron Keys & Jeweled Sword

  • Represent authority, battle, and mystery.

Chapter 48 · Keys and Locks

  • Symbolize access to hidden knowledge or secrets of existence; the absence of a master key reflects the mystery of death.

Chapter 50 · Doors and Thresholds

  • Emblems of transition and uncertainty, with repeated imagery of doors opening, closing, or leading to voids.
