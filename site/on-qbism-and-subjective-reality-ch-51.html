On QBism and subjective reality.

To All My Darlings introduces QBism (Quantum Bayesianism), a contemporary interpretation of quantum mechanics proposed around 2010, which holds that quantum states represent not objective reality but an agent's subjective beliefs about the outcomes of measurements. In QBism, reality as we experience it emerges from the collision of all the individual subjective mini-realities. Each person constructs a set of beliefs about the world based on their interactions with it. The big reality in which everyone lives is what comes out of the collisions. Young was writing half a century before QBism, but she was working in the same conceptual territory: a world where what you see is determined by where you stand, and where the shared reality is what emerges from all those partial views overlapping. Mr. Spitzer's one great dream is Young's version of the emergent reality that QBism describes. On the instrument not played. “Man might be the finest musician, but when laid away and not played by anyone, he was a poor instrument, indeed.” This formulation captures the chapter's deepest claim about what it means to be a self: the self is not self-sufficient. It requires an audience, a listener, a player. Left unplayed, even the finest instrument is merely potential. Mr. Spitzer, the musician of silence, “the spectre dreaming upon the spectre,” is an instrument that has spent its life waiting to be played by someone who could hear what it was capable of. The music is in him. The player never arrived. The silence is not nothing; it is the music the instrument would have made. But silence is not music either.
