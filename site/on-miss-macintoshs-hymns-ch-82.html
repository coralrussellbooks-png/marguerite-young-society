On Miss MacIntosh’s Hymns

The hymns Miss MacIntosh sings in this final chapter include “Rock of Ages,” “The Old Rugged Cross,” and hymns derived from Psalms 121, 59, and 130. They are the hymns of someone lost, of someone looking upward from the valley, calling out from the depths of the water, lifting eyes to the hills from which help might come. They are not the hymns of certainty. They are the hymns of the barely surviving, the walking-wounded, the ones who are still here after everything. Miss MacIntosh dismissed religious belief as human exaggeration and sang these songs while scrubbing floors. Young holds both things without resolving them: she is the woman who disbelieved and still sang, who insisted on the cold surface of things and drowned in the depths. Her character is the novel’s spine: the person who stands for one thing and is also entirely something else.
