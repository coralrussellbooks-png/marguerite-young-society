From the guide

On false modesty as a category of failure.

The folk song echo, “for false was my true love’s heart,” gives Cousin Hannah's confession its musical antecedent, but her version is more self-implicating. The folk ballad blames the beloved: the love was false. Cousin Hannah's dying confession locates the failure in herself: the modesty was false, and it killed the love. This is a harder thing to say, and a more honest one. She did not say what she felt. She performed the self that did not need to say it. The performance was successful, and the love died. Young knows this failure intimately; it is one of the central failures the novel catalogues, in Catherine and Mr. Spitzer and Vera and every character who has a real feeling and cannot find the form to express it.
