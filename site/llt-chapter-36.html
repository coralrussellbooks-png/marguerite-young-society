Chapter 36

Other versions: Chapter hub · Research notes · The Wheel and the Spokes

Characters: Catherine Cartwheel · Cousin Hannah · Joachim Spitzer

Pillars: Not One Self · The Sacred Is Here

The Questions

Mr. Spitzer’s face “passed for his face and yet was not his identical face.” What is a self that is a counterfeit of itself?

Catherine keeps Cousin Hannah alive years after her death by imagining her. If the “thinnest line” divides memory from imagination, is she remembering Hannah or making her?

Mr. Spitzer carries “the hidden man, that man he might never be.” Is the self only who one is, or also who one didn’t become?

He finds the daily routine an “imitation of life.” What routine is that, and why is it hollow?

Mr. Spitzer is a “counterfeit of himself,” his face one that “passed for his face and yet was not his identical face,” and the phrase pushes Not One Self past anything the book has said yet. It is not only that he is doubled with his dead brother, or shaped by Catherine’s gaze, or an aggregate of impressions. It is that the self he presents is a copy of a self, a counterfeit with no original in the drawer, a passing-for that never quite is. He lives at a “second remove from reality,” reacting to events after they occur, always one beat behind his own life, and the remove is the same remove between him and himself: he is a counterfeit trailing an original that may never have existed.

Catherine keeps Cousin Hannah alive by imagining her, and the chapter names the deep claim under it: “the thinnest line sometimes dividing” imagination and memory. To remember Hannah and to invent her are nearly the same act. Years after the death, Catherine conjures her, “aggrandized by the pathos of distance,” and there is no way to say where the recalled Hannah ends and the imagined one begins. Memory is not retrieval but construction, built each time from need and distance, and the dead are kept alive exactly by this building. The line is thin because there is barely a line: to remember is already to make.

And the self includes the selves one never became. Mr. Spitzer carries “the hidden man, that man he might never be,” and the book counts him as part of the self, not outside it. A person is not only who they are but who they failed to become, the unlived lives pressed in beside the lived one, the crowd including its own might-have-beens. This is Not One Self at its most spacious: not just many present selves but the ghosts of the absent ones, the hidden man walking beside the visible one, never quite either.

The hollow routine is the sealing’s opposite failure. Mr. Spitzer finds “the routine of daily continuing life” to be “meaningless, an imitation of life,” and this is not The Sacred Is Here but its counterfeit, the mechanical rote that goes through the motions of living without the living. The Sacred Is Here tends the world underfoot; this routine merely repeats it, an imitation. Mr. Spitzer, unmoored in his shifting time, feels the difference sharply: the empty repetition is not the sacred thing but its dead copy, and his sense of its hollowness is not a failure to value the ordinary but a refusal to mistake the imitation for it.

What the Chapter Refuses, and the Footing It Offers

The chapter refuses the reading that takes Mr. Spitzer’s “counterfeit of himself” as a breakdown to be pitied, and the reading that takes Catherine’s imagined Hannah as false memory to be corrected. Neither the broken man nor the failed rememberer meets the chapter.

The footing is that the self can be a counterfeit of itself, a passing-for with no original, Not One Self pushed to where there is no bare self even to double. Memory and imagination are divided by the “thinnest line,” so that to remember the dead is already to make them, and Cousin Hannah lives by being built, not retrieved. The self includes the lives one never lived, the hidden man beside the visible one. And the hollow daily routine is not The Sacred Is Here but its dead imitation, rightly felt as empty. To remember is to make, and the self includes its counterfeits and its might-have-beens. Mr. Spitzer never resolves into an original, and Cousin Hannah is never fixed into a single remembered truth.

For Reflection

If the self one shows is a counterfeit, is there an original anywhere, or only the passing-for?

When one remembers the dead, how much of them is recalled and how much made?

Is who one didn’t become part of who one is?
