The Ending: The Sacred Is Here and The Crossing Point

← Previous · History in MMMD Home · Next →

See also: Pillar 10 The Sacred Is Here · Pillar 11 The Crossing Point

Characters: Esther Longtree · Miss MacIntosh · Vera Cartwheel

Allusions: Deafness and Sign Language

Chapters discussed: 63

Definition from research

Miss MacIntosh states the book's turn plainly in Chapter 63: "imagination is nothing. The life is what counts." The final chapter carries this out. Vera, sleeping beside her deaf lover, hears him say, "There be words of ours, he said, not in the alphabet of the stars. Flowers spell them out on the earth." After a thousand pages of pharaohs, czars, crusades, and emperors, meaning is found in grass and flowers underfoot. That is "The Sacred Is Here" almost word for word.

Vera also says, "I do not know the night in which I conceived as I do not know the night in which I was conceived." She stands exactly at "The Crossing Point," between the generation that made her and the one she carries, with neither origin fully known. Set against Esther Longtree's endless pregnancy that never delivers, Vera's conception is the book's one real opening toward a future.
