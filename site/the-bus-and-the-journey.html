Definition from research

Chapter 1 · The Bus

  • A vessel moving through uncertain, misty landscapes, symbolizing the journey of life and the search for meaning.

Chapter 5 · The Bus and the Journey

  • The bus is a metaphor for life’s journey, fraught with obstacles, detours, and a lack of clear direction.
  • The journey may represent the soul’s passage through life and death or a psychological odyssey.

Chapter 6 · The Bus and the Road

  • The journey on the bus symbolizes the passage of life, the inescapable path Madge must follow.
  • The driver’s drunken sleep parallels Madge’s own unconscious navigation through life and fate.

Chapter 8 · The Broken Windshield Wiper and Flooded Road

  • Symbolize vulnerability and the overwhelming forces of nature or fate that the characters cannot control.

Chapter 11 · Alphabetical Journey (P’s, Q’s, R’s)

  • Represents a symbolic progression or mental voyage.
  • P’s include Pericles, Admiral Peary, Penelope’s web, the Pleiades, and formal penguins.
  • Q’s include Queen Charlotte Islands, Queen Maud Mountains, and naval battles.
  • R’s include a wide array of figures and concepts: Mr. Res Tacamah (a drug bottle, Catherine’s opium/laudanum), Egyptian pharaohs (Rameses I-III), runes, roulades, rubrics, royal masts, rose windows, Roman numerals, riddles, red-caps in Grand Central Station, Roland, Rimski-Korsakov, Sir Joshua Reynolds, the Great Ram constellation, Rhadamanthus, Sir Walter Raleigh, and more.

Chapter 27 · Traffic and Crossings

  • Recurring images of Mr. Spitzer being caught in dangerous or confusing traffic symbolizes his existential confusion and vulnerability.

Chapter 29 · Butterflies as Psyche and Their Cosmic Journeys

  • The belief that butterflies wander seeking immortal love and may journey through the stars.

Chapter 32 · The Journey and the Northwest Passage

  • Cousin Hannah’s life is described in terms of vast, almost mythical journeys through “desert cities,” “impregnable Asiatic fortresses,” “the great Wall of China,” “Manitoba, Hudson’s Bay,” and “ten thousand singing islands of snow and ice”. This geographical imagery symbolizes her relentless quest for freedom and truth, as well as the impossibility of fully conquering the patriarchal world.
  • The reference to the “Northwest Passage” metaphorically represents the ultimate, elusive goal of life or freedom or “that which cannot be found”. It connotes a spiritual or existential journey, echoing themes of quest and discovery.

Chapter 60 · Traffic and Cityscape

  • The contrast between natural sounds and the urban environment highlights the tension between civilization and nature.
  • The “old cars” and “traffic streams” serve as a backdrop to the surreal, almost supernatural events.
