On Emerson's immortal beauty.

Ralph Waldo Emerson, in his essay Nature, describes beauty as not merely sensory but spiritual: a reflection of the underlying harmony of the universe, accessible to the intellect that is willing to look past appearances to essence. His phrase immortal beauty captures the idea that what is truly beautiful is not subject to decay: it participates in the eternal order, the same order that governs stars and tides. Young gives Catherine a version of this (her immortal beauty, her imagination as a power that touches something permanent) and then places it alongside the bed, the opium, the inability to walk. The immortal beauty is real. The body is also real. Both of these things are true at the same time, and neither cancels the other.
