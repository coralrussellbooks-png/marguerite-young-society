Chapter 29

On Chapter 29 of Marguerite Young’s novel Miss MacIntosh, My Darling (1965). Part of Woman in Young's Magnum Opus.
By Coral Russell · Updated October 29, 2026

Characters: Joachim Spitzer

Boundaries will not hold in this chapter, and their refusal is the pillar. Mr. Spitzer cannot fix the line between butterfly and moth, day-flier and night-flier, and the exceptions to every rule keep multiplying until classification itself looks like a wish rather than a fact. The blurred categories carry the weight of gender: the same skepticism that dissolves butterfly and moth dissolves the fixed binary of man and woman, and Young's natural history becomes an argument against essentialism, the fixed positions patriarchy calls natural shown to be exceptions all the way down.

Metamorphosis is the pillar's second face. The caterpillar's passage through the chrysalis, "likened to sleep or death," is transformation and rebirth, the protected interval a self needs to grow, and it reads as the confinement a woman passes through before she emerges freer. The sexual dimorphism Mr. Spitzer notes, the perfumed male and the scentless female, restages the old arrangement in which men are active and women passive or unseen, the male wing scented to draw a female who waits; his sense that the perfume is "the perfume of his thoughts" turns the arrangement into something internalized, a gendered order living inside perception.

Against that order Young sets the feminine divine. The butterflies named for goddesses, for "the goddess of the moon, goddess of wisdom," recover a female power the dominant religious frame has buried, and the cosmic butterflies that predate earthly life make female creativity foundational rather than secondary. Mr. Spitzer's solitary, introspective attention to these creatures is the inner life patriarchal culture overlooks, and his oscillation between light and darkness, life and death, is the liminal ground the chapter prefers to any settled category. The refusal of fixed categories and the reclamation of feminine power are one motion, and the chapter ends inside that motion, in a world of multiplicity and change rather than fixed and hierarchical kinds.
