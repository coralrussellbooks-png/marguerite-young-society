On the Automat.

The Automat was a form of restaurant popular in New York and Philadelphia from the early twentieth century through the 1960s: coin-operated food dispensed through small glass-windowed compartments in a wall, the epitome of democratic, efficient, anonymous eating. You did not interact with a server. You put your coin in the slot, turned the knob, and the compartment opened. The Automat was where the anonymous middle of the city ate: office workers, the lonely, the traveling, the poor who could afford coffee and a piece of pie. Young's Mr. Spitzer, wandering the city in his silk hat and threadbare suit, is an Automat figure: the person who eats alone in the democratic space, the person whose presence is not remarked upon because the transaction requires no interaction.

On Oliver Wendell Holmes and 'the table always set for the wanderer.' To All My Darlings traces the phrase to Holmes's 1872 The Poet at the Breakfast Table: the table set with silver and gold plates and oyster forks for whoever arrives, the wanderer always welcome. Young invokes this tradition of the perpetually set table as an image of the dreamlike suspension of ordinary social time: in the novel's logic, the wanderer is always expected, the setting is always maintained, the meal is always ready for someone who may never arrive and would not be turned away if they did. Mr. Spitzer is this wanderer. The table in Catherine's household was set for him for decades. He never quite sat down.
