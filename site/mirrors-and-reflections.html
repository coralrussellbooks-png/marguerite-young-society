Definition from research

Chapter 6

  • “She was breaking into two, herself and the other, this face unknown”: Young seems to be describing through Madge a form of depersonalization-derealization that is now labeled a disorder and can be brought about by stress or trauma such as rape.
  • “She was painting her lips with a cupid’s bow”: The cupid’s-bow mouth, painted into a sharp double curve, was made famous by the actress Clara Bow in the 1920s.

Chapter 17

  • “a great mirror where one should see one’s self if one looked closely”: Her bald head as a mirror.

Chapter 18

  • “She had never seen it herself except in a mirror”: Until this night, only a mirror had seen her baldness.

Chapter 20

  • “a face which should break all mirrors”: The old saying is of a face that would crack a mirror.

Chapter 22

  • “For many years now, she had never looked into a looking glass”: In Chapter 18 she “had never seen it herself except in a mirror.”

Chapter 25 · The Glass Wall Incident

  • Symbolizes Mr. Spitzer’s fragile boundary between reality and illusion.
  • His walking through the wall without injury suggests his partial nullification and disconnection from the physical world.
  • The unnoticed shattering of glass reflects his invisible wounds and fractured self.

Chapter 27 · Invisible Glass Wall

  • Metaphor for Mr. Spitzer’s barriers between himself and the external world, also referencing an incident where he “walked through a wall of invisible glass,” symbolic of his fragile state.

Chapter 28 · The Candle Flames and Venetian Mirrors

  • Reflect the delicate, transient nature of perception and memory.

Chapter 29 · Reflections on God and Creation

  • The multifaceted nature of God and the mystery of creation, mirrored in the symbolism of butterflies.

Chapter 34 · Mirrors and Reflections

  • Symbolize self-perception, illusion, and the fragmented nature of identity.

Chapter 36 · Mirrors and Reflections

  • Mirrors signify distorted or partial reflections of identity and reality.
  • Catherine’s face in an inaccurate mirror hints at the elusive self and the failure of memory.

Chapter 37 · Mirrors and Reversals

  • The recurring image of mirrored realities (ceiling as floor, trees growing upside down) suggests the instability of perception and reality.

Chapter 40 · Mirrors and Reflections

  • Suggest duality, multiplicity of identity, and blurred boundaries between self and other.

Chapter 41 · Mirrors and Reflections

  • Used to explore identity, illusion, and self-recognition.

Chapter 42 · Mirrors and Reflections

  • The house with rooms of mirrors and endless reflections represents the multiplicity of perspectives and the elusive nature of truth and self.

Chapter 43 · Crystal Casket / Mirror

  • Symbols of self-reflection, identity, and the enigma of existence; the mirror is sought but elusive.

Chapter 48 · Mirrors and Reflections

  • Question identity and self-recognition, blurring the line between reality and illusion.

Chapter 49 · Shadows and Reflections

  • Shadows emphasize duality, absence, and the elusive nature of identity.
  • Reflections question reality versus illusion, with Mr. Spitzer sometimes chasing shadows or reflections rather than reality.

Chapter 50 · Mirrors and Prisms

  • Symbols of reflection and distortion, suggesting fragmented perception and the multiplicity of selves.

Chapter 54 · Mirrors and Reflections

  • Mirrors symbolize identity and illusion. Mr. Spitzer sees his shadow or reflection change or move independently, emphasizing his fractured self.

Chapter 55 · The Twin House and Mirroring Rooms

  • Symbolize the divided self and fractured identity; also the attempt to impose order on life’s chaos.

Chapter 56 · The Mirror and Reflection

  • Symbolize self-perception and identity confusion; often Mr. Spitzer sees distorted or absent reflections.

Chapter 62 · Mr. Spitzer’s Final Reflections

  • Contemplate the nature of truth, memory, and existence, emphasizing that all experiences may be repetitions or illusions, and the loneliness intrinsic to human life and creativity.
