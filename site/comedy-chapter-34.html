Characters: Catherine Cartwheel · Cousin Hannah

Definition from research

Catherine’s weddings grow stranger with each telling. Sometimes she was married by a false preacher, sometimes by a false priest, and more often, “usually at ten o’clock in the morning,” by a St. Bernard, an Alpine retriever who came to her in a prophetic dream. The dog licked her, spoke in church Latin, or possibly delivered Caesar’s message to the Gauls, and gave her the Viaticum, though whether provisions for a journey or the last rites she could not say. Mr. Alexander Pope, meanwhile, was holding her hand.

The precise time of day is the comic touch, a detail of routine fastened to pure hallucination. Under the comedy the dog’s prophecy is true, since Catherine’s husband will vanish in the mountains, and the chapter then turns to Cousin Hannah in her room, keys at her belt, contemptuous of mortal men and wholly indifferent to Mr. Spitzer.
