Characters: Esther Longtree · Vera Cartwheel

Pillars: Unfinished Is Honest

This companion is not a set of answers to memorize. Held up against Radical Immanence, that would be the worst thing it could become, since Unfinished Is Honest warns against exactly the finished blueprint that a book of answers would offer. The companion is instead a way of reading alongside Young: a practice, kept open and revised, the way she keeps her own constructions open.

Each chapter opens with the questions the novel raises, because the questions are where the work lives. Each chapter closes with questions posed to the reader, and these are not a decorative flourish. They are the method itself. The point of the novel, and of this book, is not to settle life, love, and truth on the reader's behalf but to hand the reader the practice of asking after them. Where a chapter names what Young is doing, it names a construction the novel is exposing and the provisional footing it offers in its place. It does not crown a winning philosophy, because there is no contest, and there never was.

A reader can move through the companion in order, following the system as it develops from Vera's first questions to Esther's last, or can open to a single chapter and read alongside it. Either way, the eleven terms above are the shared language, and they will do more work than any borrowed ism ever did.

Vera's Question

Vera Cartwheel sets the book in motion with a single need. “What motive in this quest but the search for life, for love, for truth that does not fail? I had come because of my own heart’s need for an answer.” Everything that follows is Young's response to that heart's need, and readers have long assumed the response must be an answer of the ordinary kind, a truth to arrive at and hold.

It is not. It is most emphatically not.

Young's reply to Vera is this: no fixed truth can avoid failing, and the only truth that holds is one kept under revision.
