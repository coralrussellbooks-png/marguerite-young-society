Definition from research

Vera's mother holds history as a crowd of houseguests. From her opium bed she receives "crippled Mr. Alexander Pope," Lord Byron, and "blind Milton dictating Paradise Lost to those indifferent daughters" (chapter 2). Her house fills room by room until "Spinoza and Shakespeare and Mrs. Sydington were already in the green" (chapter 24). Her reveries run in alphabetical order, so that "The P's were crowding upon her" (chapter 9), and Pericles stands one step from the penguins (chapter 11).

Much of her cluster is made of calls that go unanswered. "She had called the Bishop of Hippo, but he had always just stepped out" (chapter 24). She left her messages for the Emperor Hadrian, "but Hadrian had stepped out into his garden," and "King Canute had gone on a long voyage." The dead are always almost at home. Her single yellow canary answers to nine names, from Juniper to Dante (chapter 2), which makes her the novel's clearest carrier of a self that is many selves.

Her references keep grief at a distance. Her Latin tag "Sunt lacrimae rerum" (chapter 41) comes with the cold note, "But her tears were never for persons." One exception breaks through. When she asks Cousin Hannah to search the Alps for her lost husband, the list of places ends at the spot "where Childe Roland had blown his horn before a dark tower and where no other sound had ever been heard" (chapter 30). For once the catalog holds a person she misses.
