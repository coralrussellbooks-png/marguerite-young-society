Pillar 10 The Sacred Is Here

Every book's treatment of this pillar, side by side.

The sacred is here, inside the ordinary world, pointing deeper into the immanent rather than outward to a beyond. The grey bus, the permanent grief, the plural self in all its unresolved reality: this is where the sacred lives.

The Philosophy in MMMD

The novel’s great systems all point elsewhere. Catherine’s dreams point to the dead. Hannah’s crusades point to distant lands and mountaintops. Esther’s hope points to a birth that never comes. Even the monist vision of the first chapter points to “some omniscient eye” that no one possesses.

Intertextuality in MMMD

Bunyan's pilgrim walks toward the Celestial City. Young's pilgrim walks into the September sea. The promised beyond belongs in this novel to Mr. Bonebreaker, the Gospel salesman. He warns that "the Last Day is at hand, and hell lies under us," promises that "there would be stink in Chicago on that Last Day" (chapter 65), and dreams of European thrones. …

The History in MMMD

Vera also says, "I do not know the night in which I conceived as I do not know the night in which I was conceived." She stands exactly at "The Crossing Point," between the generation that made her and the one she carries, with neither origin fully known. Set against Esther Longtree's endless pregnancy that never delivers, Vera's conception is the book's one real opening toward a future.

Life, Love, and Truth That Doesn’t Fail

Meaning is here, in the actual world underfoot, not in a promised beyond. The appeal to a distant elsewhere, a golden age, a grand destiny, a final arrival, is the oldest tool of anyone who wants to justify sacrifice in the present. Miss MacIntosh's plain sea shells and bean pods matter as much as any vision, and often more.
