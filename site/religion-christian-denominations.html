Definition from research

The novel takes no side among the denominations, but it eyes organized religion with a satirist's coolness and saves its sharpest look for Rome. It names Presbyterians, Free Methodists, Shouting Baptists, and Jehovah's Witnesses in a breath, and makes something of small differences, the immersion of one church against the sprinkling of another, as if the scale of conviction were the real question.

Miss MacIntosh carries the book's temper. She comes from Iowa's "primitive Christians of great works and little faith," a plain, doing faith suspicious of ornament, and she loathes Catholic Rome, "a heathen and monstrous city idealizing the dead," to be fled "as from a plague," with its graven images and vestments of pearl and gold. Against ritual the novel sets private, homemade belief: Catherine's father keeps a fantastic personal religion, his house run like a church with himself its only member, a place laid at his table each night for a wandering angel he would not risk turning away. The book's sympathy leans toward that odd, personal faith and away from the institution.
