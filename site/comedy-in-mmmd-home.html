The other chapters

These pages cover the 29 chapters where comedy carries the most weight. The other chapters are not without it. Young’s humor runs through almost the whole novel, often in the same sentence as grief. Here are the comic moments in the chapters without their own page.

  • Chapter 4: A fat, soft-faced lady on the train warns Vera that Midwesterners are deaf because they do not wish to hear.
  • Chapter 5: Moses rails against the New Deal though he never has time to vote, and swears he will never cut his hair.
  • Chapter 7: Moses mocks old Doc, who drives a Model T with no wheels to patients who are ghosts. After Madge’s darkest gossip, Homer announces that he is hungry.
  • Chapter 8: Madge and Homer quarrel over Beatrice Fitzgerald: common prostitute, or hard worker who lost an eye to a boy’s arrow.
  • Chapter 9: Vera declares she is dancing with Death and is sternly corrected. Solomon the turtle drags a geranium pot across the schoolroom.
  • Chapter 10: A dream wedding with Mr. Spitzer as the veiled bride and Miss MacIntosh as the bridegroom.
  • Chapter 11: Sir Walter Raleigh removes his head along with his plumed hat, and Mr. Spitzer, as always, misses it all.
  • Chapter 12: Catherine suspects Mr. Spitzer of betting on phantom horses as his dead brother.
  • Chapter 15: Vera’s first critical look at Miss MacIntosh: the umbrella carried indoors, the windows thrown open in winter, the day-long lecture of proverbs.
  • Chapter 16: Vera rides her pony up the grand staircase, past mosaic emperors and a funeral gondola.
  • Chapter 21: Miss MacIntosh, wig shining, scolds Vera for dancing without a partner and sends the birthday dress to the ash bin.
  • Chapter 23: Miss MacIntosh’s breakfast tirade against Benjamin Franklin, whose lightning experiments put an end to privacy.
  • Chapter 25: Mr. Spitzer walks through a glass wall without noticing, pays for the wall, and is told when he sues that Mr. Spitzer died years ago.
  • Chapter 26: Two Boston spinsters who each believe they are Cleopatra, and Siamese cats who may live forever on an inheritance.
  • Chapter 27: Calling cards and a passport so the dead man will be identified as himself, and a man who says no while nodding yes when asked if he wants to die.
  • Chapter 30: Cousin Hannah crosses deserts disguised as an Arab king, and Eustace, the invisible visitor, takes many shapes.
  • Chapter 32: Hannah beats the bridegroom with her white umbrella, and Catherine remembers marrying a sailboat, a porpoise, and a star.
  • Chapter 34: A dog recites the wedding ceremony in Latin and a St. Bernard gives the last sacrament.
  • Chapter 39: The perpendicular pronoun I comes to visit the horizontal mother.
  • Chapter 40: A ship’s figurehead knows more than its captain, because it always goes first.
  • Chapter 43: Hannah, champion of bloomers, longs on her deathbed for silk skirts and parasols.
  • Chapter 46: Mr. Spitzer asks strangers in the fog whether they have seen Hannah, and a steeplejack answers him in paradoxes.
  • Chapter 51: A mouse eats Mr. Spitzer’s chords, he lists himself as a lawyer to starfish, and he meets a woman who believes she is a birdcage.
  • Chapter 53: The timid twin finds he knows boxing records and speakeasies that only his brother knew.
  • Chapter 54: Accused of stealing a rajah’s jewel, Mr. Spitzer turns out to prefer glass jewels to real ones.
  • Chapter 55: Peron forges his brother’s checks and leaves him the debts, and Joachim ends up in a beachcomber’s shack wondering whose funeral it was.
  • Chapter 56: Peron goes to his grave with poker chips and dice while a racetrack bugler plays.
  • Chapter 57: A bill keeps arriving from a tailor who cannot be found, and a Confederate soldier in the library believes the South won.
  • Chapter 58: A violinist plays an imaginary violin, and Mr. Spitzer recognizes the silent piece at once.
  • Chapter 59: Mr. Spitzer tosses coins to beggars and musicians without knowing who is rich or poor, living or dead.
  • Chapter 60: Moose calls practiced in the city are answered by a moose in the fog, and wild ducks follow him for days.
  • Chapter 61: A mute’s trained frog recites the Lord’s Prayer.
  • Chapter 62: Mr. Spitzer is startled to learn that he is in Boston and not Philadelphia.
  • Chapter 64: Boys fish for Miss MacIntosh’s wig with hooks, and a phrenologist admires her skull.
  • Chapter 65: Mr. Bonebreaker, prophet of the end of the world, flees and leaves his umbrella behind.
  • Chapter 66: Miss MacIntosh describes her imaginary raids on the Stock Exchange, her palace of evil.
  • Chapter 67: The bus cannot park between a leaking milk truck and a hearse, and Moses curses old Doc’s ghost car.
  • Chapter 69: Vera’s room is marked Fire Escape, and Dr. O’Leary rages all night at his dead sister until Vera screams at her to go.
  • Chapter 72: Esther, the immortal medical case, is pregnant with the little shadow boxer by traveling salesmen.
  • Chapter 73: The undertaker complains that Esther brings him no business.
  • Chapter 76: The detective solves every mystery except his own.

In a few chapters comedy steps back almost entirely: 13, 17, 19, 20, 28, 37, 41, 45, 47, 49, 63, 74. These include the morning Vera discovers what Miss MacIntosh has hidden (Chapters 17, 19, and 20), Mr. Spitzer’s quietest meditations, and Esther’s account of the detective in Chapter 74.
