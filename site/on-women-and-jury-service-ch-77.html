On Women and Jury Service

To All My Darlings notes that when Young envisions Esther’s trial in these chapters, the jury would consist of men. This was historically accurate: it was not until 1968 that all U.S. states were required to allow women to serve on juries, and not until the 1975 Supreme Court decision in Taylor v. Louisiana that the right was made federal law. A woman accused of crimes related to her reproductive life, tried before an all-male jury, with no legal precedent protecting her situation, in a country where her reproductive choices were themselves criminal; this is Esther’s legal reality. The justice system she fears is not neutral. It was designed by and for men, applied to a woman’s body, and has no language adequate to what has happened to her.
