On Penelope and the Unraveled Work

Esther’s knitting and unraveling of the black hood echoes one of the oldest female acts of resistance in Western literature: Penelope’s weaving. In the Odyssey, Penelope keeps her suitors at bay by promising to choose one when she finishes weaving a burial shroud for Odysseus’s father, and then unraveling it each night to begin again. The work that is done and undone to prevent a wrong ending, to keep the future open, to preserve the space in which the right person might still return. Esther’s black hood operates in the same logic: she cannot finish it because finishing it would mean the child is here, and the child is not here yet. She knits to keep the faith. She unravels to keep from being wrong.
