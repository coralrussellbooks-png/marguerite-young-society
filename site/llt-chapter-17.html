Chapter 17

On Chapter 17 of Marguerite Young’s novel Miss MacIntosh, My Darling (1965). Part of Life, Love, and Truth That Doesn’t Fail.
By Coral Russell · Updated October 29, 2026

← Chapter 16 · Life, Love, and Truth · Chapter 18 →

Other versions: Chapter hub · Research notes · The Wheel and the Spokes

Characters: Catherine Cartwheel · Joachim Spitzer · Madge Capehorn · Miss MacIntosh · Vera Cartwheel

Pillars: Not One Self

The Questions

When Miss MacIntosh’s plain surface comes apart, what is revealed: that she was lying, or that the plainness was always built?

Vera insists Miss MacIntosh “had never been herself at all but someone else.” Is that a monster, or the plural self showing in the last person Vera expected?

Why does Vera meet the unsealing with horror and violence rather than recognition?

If even Miss MacIntosh is not one fixed thing, is anyone?

This is the chapter the early ones were building toward: the plain surface of Miss MacIntosh comes apart. The baldness beneath the wig, the “bald pretender,” the sudden monstrous strangeness of the most familiar face, all of it lifts the plainness to show what was under it. And what was under it is not a lie so much as a construction. The groundedness that looked like bedrock was made, tended, kept up, a built surface like any other. The finished blueprint the reader was tempted to trust is unsealed here, and the unsealing is violent because the trust was deep.

What the unsealing reveals is Not One Self in the last person one expected it. Vera insists Miss MacIntosh “had never been herself at all but someone else,” a man beneath the woman, a stranger beneath the familiar. This reads as horror, a monster where a guardian had been. But it is the same truth the book has been stating all along, that a person is a crowd and cannot be sealed into one fixed identity. It has held for Catherine, for Mr. Spitzer, for Madge; it holds for Miss MacIntosh too, and the shock is only that Vera had exempted her.

The naming comes apart with the surface. Is Miss MacIntosh a woman or a man? The surreal, transforming descriptions will not let the categories hold, the muscles and the boxing set against the expected “feminine beauty,” and the nouns that were supposed to fix her identity float loose the way they did at the dream-wedding. Language built the plain woman, and language cannot hold her once the surface lifts.

Vera’s horror is the chapter’s most honest note. She has spent the last chapters unfreezing, saying farewell to certitude, doubting fixed truth, turning toward the open sea. But when the unsealing comes for the person she had kept exempt, she meets it with dread and violence, not recognition. This is the same terror Madge felt at her own plurality: the plural self is easy to affirm in the abstract and terrible to meet in the flesh, especially in someone one had needed to be one solid thing. Vera’s education is real, and it is not finished, and the gap between them is exactly her horror here.

What the Chapter Refuses, and the Footing It Offers

The chapter refuses the reading that treats the unsealed Miss MacIntosh as a revealed fraud, a liar caught out, and the reading that treats Vera’s horror as the correct response, a monster rightly recoiled from. Neither the fraud nor the monster is what the chapter shows.

The footing is that the plainness was a construction, not a lie, and its unsealing reveals the plural self in the last person Vera had exempted from it. The categories that named her, woman, guardian, the true one, were built and cannot hold once the surface lifts. Vera’s horror is honest and unfinished: she has learned to affirm the open in the abstract and cannot yet bear it in the flesh of someone she loved as solid. Even the plainest person is built and plural, and no one is exempt. Vera does not arrive at recognition here; she recoils, and the recoil is left standing as the true measure of how hard the truth is to hold.

For Reflection

When a person one trusted as solid turns out to be built, is that betrayal, or arrival?

Why is the plural self easy to grant in the abstract and terrible to meet in someone one needed to be simple?

Who has one exempted from being a crowd, and what would it cost to stop exempting them?
