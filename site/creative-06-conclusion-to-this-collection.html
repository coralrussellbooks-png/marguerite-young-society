Conclusion to this Collection

Part of The Creative Radical Collection, on Radical Immanence, the philosophy Coral Russell draws from the work of Marguerite Young (1908 to 1995).
By Coral Russell · Updated October 29, 2026

← Closing Page · The Creative Radical Collection

You have reached the end of the collection, which means you carry something you did not have when you started.

You have seen the mechanisms taken apart, the ways a mind gets pried loose from its own perception. You have met the eleven pillars, the ways of standing that a captured mind cannot hold. You have followed me through the more personal reckoning, where the target moved from the strongman to the hunger he feeds on. And you have done the exercises, or you will, or you will come back to them, catching that hunger while it is still small and practicing the alternative until it becomes a habit. I want to be honest, at the end, about what all of it can and cannot do.

It cannot make you invulnerable. The hungers are real, and they do not disappear because you have named them. You will still be tired. You will still grieve losses that refuse to close. You will still, some evening, want one clean answer and someone to follow, want to be lifted out of the ordinary grey into something that finally means more. That wanting is not a flaw in you. It is the most human thing there is, and every system in these books was built by people who understood it well enough to engineer against it at scale.

What changes is not that the wanting stops. What changes is that you now have somewhere to put it other than the hands of whoever is selling a cure.

That, in the end, is the whole of Young's medicine. She did not offer a better paradise or a truer leader. She offered the refusal of the entire structure, and the discovery underneath the refusal that the ordinary, unfinished present is enough to stand on. She said, more or less, that once you have looked at every illusion and understood there is no solid reality waiting beneath them, and you go on living anyway, you have become a grown person. Not arrival. Not rescue. You see that it is all a mask, and you stay, and you keep living in the here that is left.

The defense these books describe is not dramatic, and it will not save you all at once. It is the opposite of the thing it resists. Where capture is fast, this is slow. Where capture promises arrival, this stays in the present. Where capture wants you certain, this asks you to hold your doubt carefully, as a tool rather than a wound. It looks like writing down what you saw before the authorized version arrives to replace it. It looks like sitting with a question you were told not to ask. It looks like staying in the room with a person rather than reaching for the phone and the facts. It looks like leaving a thing unfinished on purpose and calling that honesty. Small, daily, unglamorous, and yours.

The systems arrayed against your attention are large and patient and well funded. They win the way water wins, through small surrenders that each seem reasonable in the moment and only add up in the dark. The good news, the only news these books have really been carrying, is that the defense works the same way. It is also incremental. It is also daily. And unlike the machinery on the other side, it asks no one's permission but your own.

There is one more thing, and it is the reason for the last line of every piece in this collection.

You are not the end of this. You are a place it passes through. The language you think in, the griefs you carry, the capacity to stay present in your own mind, all of it was handed to you by people who came before, and all of it can be handed on. That is the real version of the largeness the strongman counterfeits. He offers you a kind of immortality through the crowd, and then asks for your life to feed it. Young offers something smaller and true. You are a moment in a long act of carrying, and the carrying does not stop with you.

So do the small things. Keep your mind unfinished. Refuse the clean story that would flatten it. And when you meet someone standing at the edge of something they cannot name, the way you once were, tell them a truer story, and hand them the tools.

Here is enough. Here is enormous. The sentence has not ended.

Pass it on.
