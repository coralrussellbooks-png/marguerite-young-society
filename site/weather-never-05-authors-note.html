Author's Note

I am not a man. I have been married to the same man for (as of this publishing) 28 years. That is not nothing. The embracing of these pillars has produced yet another level to our relationship. We are still building.

This seems worth saying directly because it is the most obvious thing about this book and the thing most likely to make the reader who needs it hesitate.

I came to this work through Marguerite Young, which is to say I came to it through the most precise and most demanding account of consciousness available in the Western literary tradition, an account that makes no distinction between the male and female plural self because the plural self is the plural self. Young wrote men with devastating accuracy from the outside. Her work holds male consciousness with the same fullness and the same refusal to simplify that it brings to every other register of human experience.

The witness does not require shared identity.

It requires the quality of attention.

What I have brought to this book from the outside is what the outside can see that the inside sometimes cannot: the cost of the framework as it registers in the people who are not inside it but who are close to the men who are. The specific quality of the distance the framework creates. The grief of the people who can see the fuller version and cannot reach it. The children who learn, from watching, what the interior life is worth.

I have also brought my own experience of plurality: of learning to inhabit the full chorus rather than the permitted parts, of finding in Young's framework the name for what I had always been, of the specific relief of the accurate map. The framework I was handed was different from yours. The cost of the suppression and the relief of the permission to put it down: these are not experiences unique to any gender.

I write from the Mahoning Valley, Ohio, which has its own long relationship with the question of what men are when the systems that defined them have changed. The factories that are gone. The roles that were built around them. The men who had to become something other than what they were told they were and who did it, in this particular geography, with the specific combination of stubbornness and resourcefulness and quiet that this place produces.

This book is for them too.

The permission I am offering is not mine to give in the sense that it comes from me. It comes from Young. It comes from the research. It comes from the simple fact that the plural self is the true account of what consciousness is and the framework was always wrong and you were always more than it allowed.

I am only the one saying it directly.

You already knew it was true.

That is why you picked up the book.
