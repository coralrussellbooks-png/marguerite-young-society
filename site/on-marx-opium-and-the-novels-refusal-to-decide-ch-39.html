On Marx, opium, and the novel's refusal to decide.

Marx's formulation, religion is the opium of the people, was intended as a critique: the drug that prevents the patient from addressing the underlying condition. Young takes this analogy seriously but refuses to let it settle. Catherine's opium-induced visions are both a narcotic and a genuine form of perception. Miss MacIntosh's counter-argument, face the bare face of reality, is also both a moral position and a form of its own limitation. Young has read too much Plato, too much James, too much Augustine to believe that the veil between appearance and reality lifts cleanly on either side. The drug and the sobriety are both partial. The fog and the clear sky are both showing you something.
