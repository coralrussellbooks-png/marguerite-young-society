Characters: Joachim Spitzer

Definition from research

Offered a carbuncle from a maharajah’s crown for the price of a glass bauble, Mr. Spitzer can see at a glance the jewel is real, and so he refuses it: he would have bought it only if it had been glass. He prefers glass jewels “for the reason that glass jewels were glass,” whereas real jewels are real and therefore of no value to him. A real jewel would need insurance. It might make him conspicuous, or even the object of envy.

His reasoning is airtight and entirely upside down, which is the chapter’s comedy. The same embarrassment greets the grandiose claims of a stranger who calls himself heir to a jeweled mace: Mr. Spitzer is skeptical, until he finds Cracow stamped on his much-stamped passport, though he has never been there in this life, “just as he had never visited the kingdom of the dead.”
