In the research: Chapter 3, Chapter 13, Chapter 14, Chapter 19, Chapter 35, Chapter 57, Chapter 60, Chapter 68

Definition from research

Chapter 3 · Two Pennies

  • Found in her purse, symbolizes her humility and the little she possessed.
  • Also a poignant reminder of her mortality and disappearance.

Chapter 13 · Chessboard and Chessmen

  • Symbolize the complexity and hidden order of life’s movements and events despite apparent confusion.

Chapter 14 · Playing Cards (Aces)

  • The four aces that drop from Mr. Spitzer’s sleeve symbolize the four brothers and the layered selves.
  • The cards also represent fate, chance, and the game of life and death.

Chapter 19 · Playing cards (deuce of clubs)

  • Represents the “test of tears” and omen of death; a paradox considering her moral stance against gambling.

Chapter 35 · Cards and Games

  • The metaphor of cards and poker reflects strategy, risk, and deception.
  • Cousin Hannah is a “mean game” player; Mr. Spitzer distrusts her, believing she cheats.
  • This imagery symbolizes life’s gambits and the characters’ differing approaches to fate and challenge.

Chapter 57 · The Penny with Heads on Both Sides

  • A talisman of luck and uncertainty, symbolizing Mr. Spitzer’s gamble with fate and his desire for control in an uncontrollable world.

Chapter 60 · The Ace of Clubs / Playing Cards

  • Symbolize chance, fate, and the gamble of life.
  • The falling ace and the spots on cards evoke the randomness and structure of existence.

Chapter 68 · The Black Poker Chip

  • A meaningless yet persistent clue symbolizing the confusing and elusive nature of truth and justice.
