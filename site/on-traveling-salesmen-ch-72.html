On Traveling Salesmen

The drummer, the American traveling salesman, is one of the defining figures of the mid-twentieth century. Salesmen evolved from frontier peddlers carrying wagons of goods to men with sample cases selling encyclopedias, sewing machines, and brushes, house by house, town by town. They were essential in connecting rural America to commerce before mass advertising took over, and they were also figures of suspicion: the con man, the seducer, the man who is here today and gone tomorrow. The Fuller Brush Company, founded in 1906, was famous for its door-to-door sales force. Young fills Esther’s history with these men (the piano-tuner, the soap salesman, the rubber salesman) precisely because they embody the transient, commerce-driven America that passes through women’s lives and rarely looks back.
