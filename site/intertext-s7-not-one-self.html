Not One Self

Definition from research

"A person is a crowd, not a single fixed identity."

Young's catalogs are the grammar of this principle. Catherine's single yellow canary answers to "Juniper, Tulip, Cellini, Solomon, Methuselah, Marco Polo, Admiral Peary, Shackleton, Dante" (chapter 2). One bird carries nine names, and none of them is the true one. Mr. Spitzer, listing the names of God, concludes that "God was many things to many people" (chapter 29).

The same plurality runs through the people. Two Boston spinsters each claim to be the reincarnation of Cleopatra (chapter 26). Mr. Spitzer's brother's house holds "the twin George Washingtons fording the twin Delawares" (chapter 55). Looking at Cousin Hannah in death, Mr. Spitzer cannot even settle her part in the old romance: "Was she Tristram, or was she the white Yseult?" (chapter 52).

The essays "The Catalog and the Alphabet" and "Roles Worn by the Wrong People" trace these patterns in detail. The novel states the principle outright in its first chapter: "Every heart is the other heart. Every soul is the other soul." Seen this way, doubling in Young is not confusion but the ordinary condition of a self.
