Characters: Miss MacIntosh · Vera Cartwheel

Definition from research

When Vera, dancing alone at her birthday party with no guests, announces that she is dancing with Death, Miss MacIntosh refuses the poetry entirely: “I thought I had cured you of lying, for Death is not yet.” Then she buries the moment in proverbs, the canker in the rose and the sailor swallowed by the waters, and sends Vera to bed. The comedy is common sense meeting romance and flattening it.

Solomon the turtle is the chapter’s quieter joke. He drags a clay pot of red geranium across the schoolroom on his meditative constitutional, scraping on Miss MacIntosh’s nerves. She disapproves of his name, which Catherine chose: Solomon had too many wives, too many tables of gold, too many ostrich eggs, more worldly goods than anyone in his right mind should enjoy. She can see no wisdom in a turtle.
