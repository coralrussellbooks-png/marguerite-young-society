On the epithalamium and the bridegroom.

An epithalamium is a poem written for a bride on her way to the marital chamber: a genre going back to Sappho, practiced by Catullus, revived at every period when poets needed a form for celebrating the threshold moment between one life and another. The bridegroom Cousin Hannah left at the church door deserved one. It was never written. Young's mention of this undelivered poem is one of the novel's most delicate gestures: naming the form that was never filled, the ceremony that was never completed, the life that was turned away from at its threshold. The epithalamium exists in the novel as pure absence.
