Definition from research

Chapter 71

Listed as: Tavern Landlady

  • A large, fat woman with a goiter, representing a figure rooted in reality amidst chaos.
  • She speaks of the hotel’s management, the old watch dogs, the landlord, and the lack of interference in the hotel’s strange happenings.
  • Her speech is laced with local dialect and vivid imagery, adding texture to the scene.
  • Symbolically, the Tavern Landlady can be interpreted as representing the fading old America or traditional American society.
  • As a former circus fat lady, she recalls a form of popular entertainment and spectacle that had its heyday in the earlier part of the 20th century and before, symbolizing a bygone era of American culture rooted in simpler, rural, or small-town life.
  • The Landlady’s association with the old watch dog, who was a devout Catholic attending church even during harsh weather, and the mention of the priest who died in the landlord’s arms, indicates a decline of traditional religious and communal values. This suggests a metaphor for the waning influence of organized religion and community cohesion in mid-20th century America.
  • The Tavern Landlady is a symbol of resignation, decay, and perseverance. She embodies the physical and moral deterioration of the hotel and its inhabitants but also carries a quiet intelligence and a grudging acceptance of her circumstances. Her speech and presence vividly evoke the liminal, haunted atmosphere of the hotel lobby and the larger community, where life persists amid ruin and silence.
