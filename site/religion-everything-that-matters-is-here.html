Philosophy: Augustine and Christian Theology

Allusions: Genesis · Revelation and the Apocalypse

Definition from research

Radical immanence is the name for a philosophy built on that single conviction, that everything which matters is right here, in this world, and not in some beyond that would redeem or explain it. It is offered in these pages as a reading lens rather than a claim about Young's stated beliefs, though her own creed, poetic and cosmic and not theological, sits easily beside it. Read through this lens, the novel's enormous inventory of Christian reference, its thousands of allusions running from Genesis to Revelation, stops looking like the furniture of doctrine and starts looking like raw material, something the book keeps pulling down out of the sky and setting on the ground where people can handle it.

What follows takes the ten ideas of radical immanence, and the exact crossing point that completes them, and reads Young's religious imagination through each in turn. The novel answers back at every step, not because Young set out to illustrate a philosophy she never named, but because her instinct and its instinct are the same instinct: the child's turn toward what is standing just behind her.
