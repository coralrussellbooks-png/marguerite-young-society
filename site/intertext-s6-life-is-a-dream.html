Life Is a Dream

← Previous · Intertextuality in MMMD Home · Next →

See also: Life is a dream

Characters: Esther Longtree · Miss MacIntosh

Themes: Dreams and Imagination

Definition from research

Category: obligatory for the line, though Calderón is never named. The line is quoted twice, and the novel's argument depends on it.

Pedro Calderón de la Barca's play La vida es sueño, first performed in 1635, tells of a prince, Segismundo, imprisoned in a tower from birth. He is brought to court, then drugged and returned to his tower, and told that his day of power was only a dream. He concludes that all of life may be a dream, and that one must act well within it anyway.

Young quotes the line twice and gives it to unlikely speakers each time. Miss MacIntosh, stripped of her disguise, cries, "We each must die and know that this life was all a dream" (chapter 18). Later, in Esther Longtree's chapter, comes "a crazy old doctor who, when he was called out one night in what he thought was a dream, delivered a real baby." Afterward he says, "Life is a dream, and I have been wrong and much wronged, and I must give up this practice" (chapter 77).

Calderón's line belongs to a prince in a tower. Young hands it to the realist who swore she never dreamed and to a drunken country doctor. The line stays true, becomes a little ridiculous, and keeps both qualities. The essay "Roles Worn by the Wrong People" in Section 5 places this among Young's other transfers of famous words.
