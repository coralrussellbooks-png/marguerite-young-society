A note on sources: this book draws on philosophy, clinical psychology, neuroscience, developmental research, and ecological psychology. Where possible, the most accessible version of a source is cited. Full bibliographic information for all sources appears in Appendix B.

Chapter Two: The Chorus Model

William James's argument for the social self appears in The Principles of Psychology (1890), Chapter Ten. The specific formulation cited, that a person has as many social selves as there are individuals who recognize them, is from pages 294-295 of the original edition.

Richard Schwartz's Internal Family Systems framework is developed across Internal Family Systems Therapy (1995) and No Bad Parts (2021). The capital-S Self concept and the goal of psychological health as relationship among parts rather than unity appears throughout both works.

Dan McAdams's narrative psychology framework appears in The Stories We Live By (1993). The argument that the self is a story rather than an object is developed in Chapters One and Two.

Chapter Three: The Working Story

Shelley Taylor's positive illusions research is synthesized in Positive Illusions (1989). The specific findings on slightly inflated self-assessment and resilience appear in Chapters Two and Three. The original research appeared across multiple journal articles from the 1980s, the most cited being Taylor and Brown, Illusion and Well-Being: A Social Psychological Perspective on Mental Health, Psychological Bulletin 103(2), 1988.

Michael White and David Epston's narrative therapy framework appears in Narrative Means to Therapeutic Ends (1990). The externalizing of the problem and the unique outcomes concept are developed in Chapters Two and Three.

Terror management theory is developed across the work of Jeff Greenberg, Shelley Solomon, and Tom Pyszczynski. The most accessible synthesis for general readers is The Worm at the Core (2015). The foundational research appeared in Greenberg, Pyszczynski, and Solomon, The Causes and Consequences of a Need for Self-Esteem: A Terror Management Theory, in Baumeister, ed., Public Self and Private Self (1986).

Chapter Four: The Climate of Loss

The critique of the stage model's misapplication is not a critique of Kübler-Ross's original observation. On Death and Dying (1969) describes what Kübler-Ross observed in dying patients; the prescriptive application to grief generally was a cultural elaboration rather than her argument.

Continuing bonds theory is developed in Klass, Silverman, and Nickman, eds., Continuing Bonds (1996). The challenge to the detachment model is made explicitly in the introduction and in Klass's opening chapter.

Pauline Boss's ambiguous loss framework appears in Ambiguous Loss (1999). The both-and resolution concept is developed in Chapter Five.

The dual process model appears in Stroebe and Schut, The Dual Process Model of Coping with Bereavement: Rationale and Description, Death Studies 23(3), 1999.

Joanna Macy's work that reconnects framework appears in Coming Back to Life (1998, revised 2014). The argument that suppression of ecological grief is itself harmful appears in Chapter Two.

Glenn Albrecht's solastalgia concept was first published in Albrecht et al., Solastalgia: The Distress Caused by Environmental Change, Australasian Psychiatry 15(S1), 2007. The fuller development appears in Earth Emotions (2019).

Chapter Five: The Standing Still

Daniel Siegel's window of tolerance concept is developed across The Developing Mind (1999) and Mindsight (2010). The interpersonal neurobiology framework and the research on how the regulated nervous system of one person can widen the window of another appears in The Developing Mind, Chapters Three and Four.

Steven Hayes's observer self and defusion concepts appear in Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (1999) and are made accessible in A Liberated Mind (2019). The self-as-content versus self-as-context distinction is developed in Hayes et al., Chapter Four.

Jonathan Shay's moral injury concept appears in Achilles in Vietnam (1994). The definition of moral injury as the damage done by the betrayal of what is right by a legitimate authority appears in the introduction.

The affect regulation framework drawing on the distinction between suppression, flooding, and integration draws on Fonagy et al., Affect Regulation, Mentalization, and the Development of the Self (2002), and on the broader literature on emotion regulation that follows from it.

Chapter Six: The Reef Method

Clare Graves's spiral development model is synthesized in Beck and Cowan, Spiral Dynamics (1996). Graves's original research appeared across multiple academic publications from the 1960s through the 1980s.

Richard Tedeschi and Lawrence Calhoun's post-traumatic growth research appears in Trauma and Transformation (1995) and is updated in Posttraumatic Growth: Theory, Research, and Applications (2018). The careful distinction between post-traumatic growth and the minimization of the wound's cost is made explicitly in the 2018 volume, Chapter Two.

Esther Thelen and Linda Smith's dynamic systems approach to development appears in A Dynamic Systems Approach to the Development of Cognition and Action (1994). The argument that development proceeds through iteration rather than program execution is the book's central claim.

The creativity research on constraint and breakthrough draws on multiple sources in the psychology of creativity. The most relevant synthesis for the argument made in this chapter is Csikszentmihalyi, Creativity: Flow and the Psychology of Discovery and Invention (1996), particularly the chapters on the incubation phase and the conditions for creative breakthrough.

Chapter Eight: The People Around You

John Bowlby's attachment theory is synthesized for general readers in A Secure Base (1988). The argument that the self develops in relationship from the beginning, rather than the individual developing a self and then entering into relationships, appears in Chapter Two.

Daniel Siegel's interpersonal neurobiology and the relational dimensions of the window of tolerance appear in The Developing Mind (1999), particularly Chapter Three on attachment and the developing brain.

Murray Bowen's differentiation concept appears in Family Therapy in Clinical Practice (1978). The distinction between fusion, cutoff, and the differentiated position is developed across Chapters Two and Three.

Chapter Nine: What We Build From Here

The research on standardized testing and multiple intelligences draws on Howard Gardner, Frames of Mind: The Theory of Multiple Intelligences (1983), and on the broader literature on assessment and learning difference that has developed from it.

The research on medical research bias and the generalization of findings from narrow populations draws on Caroline Criado Perez, Invisible Women: Data Bias in a World Designed for Men (2019), particularly the chapters on medicine and healthcare.

The restorative justice framework referenced in the legal systems section draws on Howard Zehr, Changing Lenses: Restorative Justice for Our Times (1990), the foundational text of the restorative justice movement.

The cooperative economics and community wealth models referenced in the economic life section draw on Gar Alperovitz, America Beyond Capitalism (2005), and on the Democracy Collaborative's ongoing research into community wealth building.

The deliberative democracy and ranked choice voting research referenced in the political life section draws on James Fishkin, Democracy When the People Are Thinking (2018), and on the Electoral Reform Society's research on proportional representation systems.

The Marguerite Young Society is dedicated to the work of Marguerite Young (1908 to 1995).
