Harp Song for a Radical: The Life and Times of Eugene Victor Debs

← Angel in the Forest · The Marguerite Young Society · Inviting the Muses →

Published: Alfred A. Knopf, 1999, edited by Charles Ruas after Young's death. 624 pages.

For the last long stretch of her life Young worked on a biography of Eugene Debs, the labor leader and five-time candidate for president, a man whose fierce and unfashionable idealism clearly spoke to her. She did not live to finish it.

True to its subtitle, the book ranges across Debs's whole era as much as his life, through figures such as Wilhelm Weitling, Heinrich Heine, Karl Marx, Susan B. Anthony, Mary Todd Lincoln and Allan Pinkerton, and through movements like the Mormons. Dreamers, radicals and the long American argument about how to live together were her lifelong subjects, and here they meet a socialist who ran for office from a prison cell.
