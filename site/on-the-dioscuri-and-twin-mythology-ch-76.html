On the Dioscuri and Twin Mythology

The twins Esther invokes, one who murdered the other and then accused the dead one of being the murderer, echo the mythological twin pair of Castor and Pollux, the Dioscuri. They are an unusual case: heteropaternal superfecundation, meaning they were born of the same mother but different fathers (one divine (Zeus), one mortal (Tyndareus)) making one immortal and one not. They are patrons of sailors and travelers, figures who intervene at moments of crisis. Their twin sisters are Helen of Troy and Clytemnestra: one who caused a war, one who ended a marriage by murder. The mythological structure of twins splits the human into its two possible directions (the divine and the mortal, the destroyer and the redeemer) and Young gives Esther this split as the question of her own existence. Which twin is she? She does not know. Neither do we.
