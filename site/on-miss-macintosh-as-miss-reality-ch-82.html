On Miss MacIntosh as Miss Reality

Throughout eighty-two chapters, Miss MacIntosh has been the novel’s pole of reality: the woman who kept the rain out, who believed in facts and schedules and the cold honest surface of things, who was named for waterproofed cloth and wore a rubber mackintosh coat and was the solid thing in Vera’s childhood. She was also wearing a wig. She had a bald head under the red hair. Her plainness was a performance and also a conviction and also a failure: she believed in the surface and the surface held for years and then she went into the ocean. The thing that made her Miss MacIntosh was the same thing that drowned her. Young has spent the entire novel demonstrating that there is no simple reality, no cold clear surface that is not also unfathomably deep, no Miss MacIntosh who is not also the water she drowned in. The search for the person who would tell you the truth turns out to be a search for the person who taught you that the truth and the dream are the same water. Vera finds this in the end. She finds Esther Longtree. She finds herself.
