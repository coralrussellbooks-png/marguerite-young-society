Definition from research

The novel's true subject is the search for reality in a world of dreams, and religion is where that search is fought out. Belief is the thing that either anchors a person or sets them adrift. Miss MacIntosh's plain faith is her grip on the ground; Catherine's is her flight from it. The same word, faith, names both the discipline that keeps Vera facing hard facts and the haze that dissolves her mother. Young uses each character's religion as an answer to the one question, what is real, and weighs the answers against each other without naming a winner. Scripture itself is pulled into the doubt. Miss MacIntosh prefers Pilgrim's Progress to the Bible, calls the Word so much "human exaggeration," and distrusts the King James as a king's political book. The novel reads its own inherited scripture the way it reads everything else, skeptically, as a made thing.
