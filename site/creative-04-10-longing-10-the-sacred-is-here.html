Pillar: Pillar 10 The Sacred Is Here · Across every book

or On the longing to be lifted out of the ordinary, and the danger in the height.

The ordinary is hard to bear sometimes. The grey Tuesday, the unremarkable life, the sense that the real and shining thing is happening elsewhere, higher up, and that you are stuck down here in the small and the dull. Into that ache comes the oldest offer of all. Let us lift you out. Here is a cause larger than your little life, a height worth climbing toward, a glory worth dying for.

And, in the same breath, worth killing for. This is where the longing turns. Power sacralizes itself. The nation becomes holy, the movement becomes a higher purpose, and once the cause lives up there in the sacred, any cruelty down here can be justified by it. The bodies become acceptable because the thing they are spent on is so exalted. Every transcendent cause keeps a ledger of the ordinary lives it burned to reach the height. The promise to lift you out of the grey is also a permission to do terrible things in the grey, for the sake of the bright thing above it.

Young points the other direction, downward and inward. The sacred in her work is not above the ordinary. It is inside it. It is in the dim room, the long bus ride, the unredeemed afternoon, the grief that does not resolve and the self that will not simplify. She called the central scene of her great book an opium addict’s paradise, a heaven located inside a small bedroom, and the irony is exact. The transcendence people reach for is right here, misrecognized, in the very life they are trying to escape.

The tenth pillar collapses the height into the present. If the sacred is already here, in the grey, then you do not need the cause to lift you to it, and no one can hand you a glorious reason to harm the actual life in front of you. There is nothing higher than this Tuesday to sacrifice this Tuesday for. The bus is not the waiting room for the sacred. The bus is where the sacred turned out to be.
