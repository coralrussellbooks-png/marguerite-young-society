Definition from research

Chapter 68

Listed as: Mr. Weed (The Christian Hangman)

  • Central to the chapter, Mr. Weed is a professional executioner who views his work as a mission to provide a painless death.
  • He is a deeply complex character: a good father, haunted by personal tragedy (his wife Marie’s suicide), and tormented by memories and dreams.
  • He discusses his profession with a mix of bitterness, humor, and philosophical insight, revealing his tolerance and humanity despite his grim role.
  • Mr. Weed recounts various cases of crime, murder, and the inefficiency or cruelty of some executions, highlighting the moral ambiguities of justice.
  • He is associated with symbolic objects (a Jew’s harp, a Parker fountain pen, and a black poker chip) each with narrative significance.

Chapter 69

Listed as: Mr. Weed and His Shadow

  • Mentioned as mysterious figures, Mr. Weed is a man of few words, while his shadow is more outspoken and unsettling. The landlord distinguishes between the two, adding layers to the mythos of the hotel.

Chapter 71

Listed as: Mr. Weed (The Christian Hangman)

  • A man who has hanged many murderers but refuses to hang women or those far gone in pregnancy.
  • His wife, Marie, hanged herself during pregnancy, which haunts him deeply.
  • Mr. Weed is portrayed as a tragic figure, burdened by his past and haunted by ghostly children of the hanged.
  • Vera contemplates whether Mr. Weed might hang himself but concludes he waits for dawn to protect his children from danger 892.
