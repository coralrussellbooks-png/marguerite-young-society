On Deafness and Inner Sound

The stone deaf man’s account of his inner world is consistent with what is documented about acquired deafness: those who lose hearing after having heard often continue to experience internal sound, musical ear syndrome, auditory memory, phantom sounds. The world of silence is not silent from inside. Young gives her stone deaf man a rich interior acoustic life: music louder than what he heard before, voices that will not stop, the sounds he knew before they stopped. He hears with his eyes and his fingers. He is closer to death because he cannot hear the horn of judgment. He is also, Young suggests, closer to a truth that the hearing cannot access: that the world’s real sounds are not the ones that reach the ear.
