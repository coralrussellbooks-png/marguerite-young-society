Pillar: Pillar 11 The Crossing Point · Across every book

Or, Why You Are the Center of Time

There is something the first ten pillars have been circling without naming.

Look back at what we have built. A self made of crowds. A reality that is constructed. Grief as permanent weather. The discipline of witnessing. The open structure, the unfinished work, the view from the edges, the sediment beneath our feet, the located language, the sacred ordinary. Ten pillars, each one a refusal of a different lie that tyranny depends on.

They stand. They hold weight. But when you step back from the architecture and look at it whole, you notice something: they are all rotating. They are rotating around a center that none of them, on their own, fully names.

That center is what this final pillar is about.

It is not a new rule added to the ten. It is not a bonus chapter or a coda. It is the hidden axis the entire structure has been organized around from the beginning, the point that was always already there, waiting for the rest of the building to make it visible. Young does not announce it directly in her novel. She never names it. But it is present in every sentence, in the way the narrator stands, in the way time moves in the book, in the way the past and future press simultaneously on every present moment.

You have been standing at this axis the entire time you have been reading.

What you have not yet done is look down and see what you are standing on. What you have not yet done is look up and understand what you are carrying. What you have not yet done is feel the full weight of the intersection itself: the place where everything that came before you crosses everything that will come after, where the sediment of history meets the unformed ground of the future, where the ancestors in your layers meet the not-yet-born who will walk on what you are forming right now.

You are not just a person living in the present. You are the crossing point itself.

That is the largest claim this philosophy makes. And it changes everything about where your power lives.

The Map Has a Second Axis

To understand this final pillar, we have to look at a basic geometry trick. Every single one of the first ten pillars described your life on a horizontal axis.

The horizontal line is the axis of accumulation. It tracks the wide, lateral spread of who you are right now in the present moment: your shifting inner crowd, your social relationships, and the daily weather of your grief. The horizontal axis is beautiful, and it is where we spend most of our daily energy.

But the horizontal axis cannot see the vertical axis.

The vertical line is the axis of transmission. It is the deep pipeline running straight through you, connecting the ancestors who came before you to the people who will inherit the earth long after you are gone.

Young's philosophy reminds us that we are alluvial. In geology, alluvial material is the rich soil and clay carried by a moving river and deposited in a delta to form a brand-new landscape.

You are a human river delta. You are made entirely of what was carried to you and deposited in you by the moving currents of time and human relation. The language you use to think, the cultural traditions you follow, and even the generational grief you carry were all deposited in your sediment by people who came before you.

You are not just a traveler walking down a straight timeline. You are the exact crossing point where the horizontal spread of today meets the vertical line of history.

The Ancestors under Your Boots, the Kids over Your Head

The eleventh pillar completely changes how we relate to the past and the future, stripping away abstract academic theories and making time feel intensely physical.

First, look downward. The ancestors are in the sediment.

Secular philosophy usually treats the dead like an ancient archive, a collection of old texts and data to look up if we feel like it. But Young says the dead are present as a literal, geological weight. They are in the soil that grew your food, the water flowing through your local aquifer, and the mental grooves that shape your thoughts. You are walking on top of their choices, and you are made of their deposits.

Now, look upward. The not-yet-born are in the ground you are forming.

We often talk about the future as a sentimental concept, but the eleventh pillar treats the future as a structural reality. Every single decision you make today, whether you are paying attention or not, acts as a fresh geological deposit. You are actively packing down the dirt that the next seven generations will have to stand on.

The Stable Center of a Turning Wheel: Boethius

To show us what it feels like to experience this, the text looks back at a famous moment in history. In the year 524 AD, a brilliant statesman and philosopher named Boethius was thrown into a prison cell and sentenced to death.

Before prison, Boethius was at the absolute top of the world. He held power in the court of a king. He was oriented on the horizontal axis: spreading his influence wide, accumulating honors, offices, and social power. He was a public man, connected to the machinery of the state, dependent on the wheel of fortune turning in his favor.

He wrote books. He held office. He commanded respect. He lived for the horizontal expansion of his status and his work.

Then his luck ran out. The king turned against him. He was accused of treason. He was imprisoned in a tower, stripped of his offices, separated from his family, waiting for execution. The medieval "Wheel of Fortune" turned him upside down.

Sitting in that cold cell with all of his lateral success stripped away, with nothing but a blank wall and the certainty of death, Boethius could have broken. Instead, he experienced a total rotation.

He was forced off the horizontal axis of accumulation and dropped straight onto the vertical axis of transmission. He stopped asking what he could build or acquire, and started asking what he was truly made of and what he was carrying forward. He began to write The Consolation of Philosophy, a work that asked: when everything external has been taken from you, when you have no office, no status, no power to spread horizontally, what remains? What is the ground beneath you?

In prison, Boethius discovered a beautiful secret: the crossing point does not move. The wheel of life can spin, your fortune can rise and fall, and the systems around you can erupt into total chaos. But the axis at the center holds perfectly stable.

He realized that he was not his office. He was not his status. He was not the accumulation of honors and influence. He was something deeper: he was a human being standing at the intersection of past and future, carrying something infinite, responsible to something vast.

When he dropped his attachment to the horizontal axis, when he stopped trying to climb back onto the wheel, he discovered a stability that no wheel could take from him. He was the still point around which the wheel turned. He was the crossing point where the weight of the past met the responsibility of the future.

He was enough, exactly as he was, stripped down to nothing but his mind and his mortality.

Jamming the Playbook: The Ultimate Accountability Shield

So, how does standing at the crossing point help us fight back against authoritarian control?

Tyranny relies on two major lies, and the eleventh pillar exposes both of them:

The Lie of the Neutral Ground: Authoritarian systems love to pretend that the current setup of society is neutral and natural. They want you to believe that poverty, exclusion, and their own massive power are just "the way things are". But a sedimentary mindset reads the ground honestly. It proves that the current landscape is actually an alluvial deposit created by specific historical choices, systemic extractions, and the long con of past regimes. Once you see the layers, the dictator can no longer lie to you about the soil.

The Lie of the Present Moment: Destructive systems operate entirely inside a short-sighted present-moment frame. They accelerate extraction, dismantle public institutions, and burn up resources because it benefits their current horizontal power, treating the future as if it doesn't exist. The crossing point enforces an unyielding intergenerational accountability. It forces us to ask: what kind of layer are we depositing into the ground for the kids who come next?

When you live at the crossing point, your obligations are no longer optional commitments you can pick up and drop for political convenience. They are built directly into your constitution. You recognize that you are a temporary carrier of an infinite human transmission, making you fiercely protective of the sediment beneath you and deeply responsible for the ground that will be built above you.

A person who understands that they stand at the crossing point of past and future cannot be bribed with short-term power. A person who recognizes that their children's children will walk on the ground they are forming today will not burn it for immediate profit. A person who feels the ancestors in their sediment and the unborn in their choices becomes unyieldingly protective of what matters.
