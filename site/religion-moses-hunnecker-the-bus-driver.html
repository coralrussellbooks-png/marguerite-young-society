Definition from research

Moses drives through fog and darkness calling on God, the angels, and the archangel Gabriel by name, but his faith owes little to any church. It comes out in singing, in whistled imitations of birdcalls, in muttered talk aimed half at himself and half at heaven. His religion is something he lives rather than something he was taught.

He drinks as he drives, which sits oddly beside the prayers, and that contradiction is the point. He is afraid and reckless by turns, yet he keeps asking for protection and keeps thinking of someone waiting for him at home, as if faith and domestic hope were the same rope he is holding onto. His whole trip through the mist reads like a passage through uncertainty, a man caught between life and death who uses God to make sense of a road he cannot see. His belief is a patchwork of Christian habit, private superstition, and plain searching, and it holds him up.
