Allusions: The Book of Job

"This was not the angel singing in the whirlwind" glances at Job 38, God answering out of the storm, only to set against it a "singing moth," the divine voice traded for a small, humble one.

“weavings of the threads of sound upon the loom of time”: In Goethe’s Faust, Part One (1808), the Earth Spirit weaves the living garment of God at the roaring loom of Time.

“shepherd, if his cane had been a staff”: “Thy rod and thy staff they comfort me” (Psalm 23:4, KJV). See The Bible. Earlier in Chapters 6, 43 and 45.

“fishing for lost souls”: “Follow me, and I will make you fishers of men” (Matthew 4:19, KJV). See The Bible. Earlier in Chapter 6.

“the lacrosse player’s crosse or the flowering crosier, perhaps flowering with hawthorn”: Lacrosse grew from games played by the Haudenosaunee and other nations; French missionaries named it for the stick, which looked to them like a bishop’s crosier. A flowering staff is an old sign of grace. Joseph of Arimathea’s staff, planted on Wearyall Hill at Glastonbury, was said to grow into the Glastonbury Thorn, a hawthorn that flowers at Christmas; in Wagner’s Tannhäuser (1845) the pope’s barren staff bursts into leaf.

“killing the song sparrow in his fall”: “There is special providence in the fall of a sparrow” (William Shakespeare, Hamlet, 5.2), after “one of them shall not fall on the ground without your Father” (Matthew 10:29, KJV). See Shakespeare and The Bible. Earlier in Chapters 2, 6, 14, 18 and 44.

"of what avail now were loud Te Deums" names the ancient hymn of praise and thanksgiving, raised here only to be doubted, set against sobbing and the whisper of ghostly wings.

“a hollow sound like a last trumpet blowing”: “In a moment, in the twinkling of an eye, at the last trump: for the trumpet shall sound, and the dead shall be raised incorruptible” (1 Corinthians 15:52, KJV). See The Bible. Earlier in Chapter 37.

“the pearl-mantled loon singers on the back of the old glider whale”: Compare the voyage of Saint Brendan, whose monks landed on an island to celebrate Easter, lit a fire, and found the island was a whale named Jasconius (Navigatio Sancti Brendani, ninth century).

“dying that he might live”: “As dying, and, behold, we live” (2 Corinthians 6:9, KJV). See The Bible.
