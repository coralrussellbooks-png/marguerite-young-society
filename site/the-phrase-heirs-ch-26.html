The phrase heirs (Ch 26)

From the reading guide, Chapter 26 · Research: Chapter 26

From the guide

The phrase “heirs who had been heiresses, heiresses who had been heirs” appears in Mr. Spitzer’s meditation on the properties of the dead, delivered with Mr. Spitzer’s particular careful vagueness. Young places it among confused features and makeshift identities and properties of a dream. The legal language of inheritance and the language of embodied identity are treated as the same kind of uncertainty. Who inherits what is always, in this novel, a question that cannot be cleanly resolved. Mr. Spitzer’s belief that “perfect life should be perfect death,” and that “one should not die while one should live. One should not live while one should die,” is the chapter’s most concentrated statement of his condition. He has been dying since his brother died. He has been living in his brother’s life. He cannot tell anymore which one is which. Perfect life would be the moment when the confusion ends, not because one of them wins, but because both of them finally stop.
