Chapter 12

On Chapter 12 of Marguerite Young’s novel Miss MacIntosh, My Darling (1965). Part of Woman in Young's Magnum Opus.
By Coral Russell · Updated October 29, 2026

Other versions: Chapter hub · Research notes · The Wheel and the Spokes

Characters: Joachim Spitzer · Miss MacIntosh · Vera Cartwheel

Two kinds of clocks run in this chapter. Old Reliable, which Miss MacIntosh winds by hand, keeps the ordinary hours of order and discipline. Around it stand the frozen enchanted clocks, stopped, holding their single moments forever. This is the halting of oppressive social time, and it is suspended time, the two clocks running at once, one measuring the day and one refusing to, in the same house.

Vera slips out into the night, past the "veil of oblivion," toward the "faces pulsing in the vacancy" and the statues poised between sleep and waking, and the passage is the real and the dream as one country, the ordinary house opening onto a dreamed one without a seam. At its center is the "garden of the blind," made by touch and scent for Vera's grandmother, who could not see. It is the alternative perception granted a marginalized woman, and it is the book's motif of the severed sense, invisibility and erasure worked into a garden, the same thread as the deaf villagers and the blindness the book keeps returning to.

The chapter dwells on eyelashes and eyebrows and hair, on Absalom's hair and on the Mona Lisa's missing eyebrows, which it says add "beauty to the mystery." These are the protective features of a face, and they are the body's removable parts, the body as the place the self comes apart, and the missing eyebrows point straight ahead to Miss MacIntosh, whose own are gone. Miss MacIntosh anchors the chapter as the pole that restores Vera from "childhood madness" and dismisses the visions, her face "bristling with needles and pins," guarded like armor. Against her, Vera's mother senses that Mr. Spitzer is not entirely "substantial," a self growing thin and fluid, which reaches past gender toward the same bottom the women are finding.
