Pillar: Pillar 7 The Margins Reveal the Truth · Across every book

or On the wish to be at the center, and the contempt it quietly requires.

Everyone wants to matter. To be in the main story, part of the real action, one of the people who count. It is a clean and human wish, and power knows precisely how to use it. It builds a center and a margin. It tells you that you belong at the center, with the real people, the true citizens, the heartland, the core. And the price of that belonging, never stated but always understood, is your contempt for whoever has been placed at the edge.

That is the quiet machinery of it. To be told you are central, you must agree that someone else is not. The us is manufactured out of the them. Every politics of purity runs on this exchange, offering you the warmth of the center in return for your willingness to despise the margin.

Young’s attention goes the other way, almost stubbornly. Her novel is full of people the world would file under minor. A waitress, a spinster cousin, a small-town nursemaid, an addict in a dim room. She treats them not as background but as the place where reality is densest. The overlooked figure, in her hands, carries as much being as any great man. She grants full reality to exactly the people a politics of the center would tell you do not finally count.

The seventh pillar is that grant. The marginal is not lesser being. The person at the edge is as real as the person at the podium. Once you hold that, the whole architecture of center and enemy loses its floor, because you cannot be sent to clean out a margin you know to be where life actually lives. The contempt the bargain required of you is simply no longer available.

You wanted to matter. You do, but so does the person you were being invited to look down on, and exactly as much. That symmetry is inconvenient for anyone trying to build a pure center. Which is precisely why it is worth keeping.
