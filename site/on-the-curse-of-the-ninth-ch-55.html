On the curse of the ninth.

The superstition that completing a ninth symphony is fatal to the composer arose partly from the deaths of Beethoven, Schubert, and Dvorak after their ninths, though Dvořák actually completed more symphonies afterward. Mahler, aware of the curse, tried to circumvent it by labeling Das Lied von der Erde (not a symphony number) as his ninth. He died after completing his Ninth Symphony proper without completing his Tenth. Bruckner died with his Ninth unfinished. The superstition has enough historical backing to take seriously as a cultural phenomenon, even if the causality is obviously illusory. Joachim's opus as a tomb is an attempt to create an immortal structure in sound; the curse of the ninth is the superstition that says the structure will kill him before he can complete it. The race is ongoing.
