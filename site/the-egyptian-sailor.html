Definition from research

Chapter 2

Listed as: An Egyptian Sailor

  • Depicted vividly as "gold-turbaned, his face glistening like clouded water," immediately evoking an exotic and otherworldly presence.
  • His arrival is marked by a sense of ambiguity and displacement.
  • He is described as a lost traveler, both literally and metaphorically, crossing boundaries between worlds and times.
  • The sailor’s purpose is to find Mr. Spitzer.
  • Despite the confusion and mistaken identity that ensues, the Egyptian's quest is sincere and urgent. He carries a piece of papyrus, which is later revealed to be a race dope sheet, connecting him to earthly concerns such as horse racing and victory, yet his demeanor and context suggest he is more than just a mundane visitor.
  • His presence introduces a collision between the "real world" and the mysterious, spectral realm inhabited by Catherine and her guests.
  • The Egyptian Sailor symbolizes the crossing of boundaries: life and death, reality and dream, the past and the present.
  • His lost voyage, caused by "ambiguities" such as misread stars and winds, mirrors Catherine’s own disorientation in time and space, encapsulating themes of fate, chance, and the unknowable.
  • His dance of ecstatic excitement upon seeing Mr. Spitzer, and the subsequent confusion over identity, emphasize the fluidity of existence in this narrative.
  • His interaction with Mr. Spitzer underscores the tension between the tangible and the ethereal, as Mr. Spitzer tries to convince him of the death of his brother, while Catherine embraces the coexistence of these two realms.
  • The Egyptian’s presence unsettles Mr. Spitzer, reflecting a resistance to the intrusion of the supernatural or the past into his orderly, if somewhat diminished, life.
  • Catherine’s support of the Egyptian Sailor, defending his reality and expressing fury at the servants’ neglect of him, reveals her own deep immersion in the dream world and her rejection of mundane reality.
  • This dynamic portrays the Egyptian as a catalyst for revealing Catherine’s fractured state and the complex interplay between sanity and madness.
  • He may also be a reference to "The Shipwrecked Sailor". It is an ancient Egyptian tale, likely composed during the Middle Kingdom (c. 2040-1782 BCE), found on a papyrus scroll. It tells the story of a sailor who is shipwrecked and marooned on a mythical island, where he encounters a giant serpent and eventually returns home with tales and treasures.
