Definition from research

Mr. Bonebreaker, the Gospel salesman of Miss MacIntosh's past in Chicago, carries Scripture as merchandise and threat. He warns that "the Last Day is at hand, and hell lies under us." He promises that "there would be stink in Chicago on that Last Day" (chapter 65), borrowing Isaiah's threat. He preaches that the old creation "was wrecked by the Emperor Constantine," which seems to be where Miss MacIntosh's lifelong grudge began.

His Bible turns into a courtship. "I will make you my wife in Christ's name," he tells her. He is also the novel's clearest claimant to a borrowed crown: his grandmother promised that he could "sweep the Stuarts off their feet" if he believed in America. In Miss MacIntosh's memory he was "poor as Tobit's dog." He ends as the salesman "who had sold no goods to her," and in the end "Bible literature fell out of his coat as he fled" (chapter 65).
