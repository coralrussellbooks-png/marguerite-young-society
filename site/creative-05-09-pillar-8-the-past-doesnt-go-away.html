Pillar: Pillar 8 The Past Doesn't Go Away · Across every book

Are you gilding your past into a golden age, or trying to wipe it clean and start at year zero?

The hunger is to fix time, either to restore a golden past that never was or to wipe the slate at year zero. Power uses whichever lie is closer to hand. This pillar says the past is accumulation, not a paradise to recover and not a slate to clear. You carry all of it, layered, without gilding or erasing it.

Notice

Notice, over the next few days, when you reach for a story about your own past that is either too golden or too erased. A version where everything used to be better, or a version where you have supposedly moved past something completely.

Practice

Draw or describe a simple timeline of your life as layers, like sediment, rather than a straight line. Note what still presses upward from underneath, the old griefs and choices that are still active in you now.

Reflection

Where are you tempted to mythologize your past as better than it was, or to pretend you have wiped a part of it clean?

What would it mean to just carry it, unedited, as ground rather than as a verdict?
