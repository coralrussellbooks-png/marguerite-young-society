Pillar: Pillar 9 Language Shapes What's Possible · Across every book

or On the word that ends thought, and the language that keeps the world open.

The most useful word to power is the one that settles the matter. The slogan, the clean phrase, the line you can repeat without thinking, the name that forecloses the argument before it starts. We reach for these words because ambiguity is tiring and a good slogan relieves the tiredness. It tells you what to feel and saves you the labor of working it out. That relief is exactly the opening.

Propaganda is built for this appetite. It renames things until the new name covers the old reality. It repeats a phrase until the phrase begins to feel like a fact. War becomes security. Cruelty becomes order. A whole false world can be assembled out of words and then closed over the real one, and the closing is the point. The finished phrase is a door swinging shut on thought.

Young was a writer who refused the closed phrase on principle. Her sentences open and open and open, qualifying, doubling back, declining to land on the tidy summary. People find the book difficult for exactly this reason, and the difficulty is the argument. She knew that language builds the world, and she refused to build a world that pretended to be finished. Her prose stays accountable to how much it is leaving out.

The ninth pillar holds language as construction, always, and that honest language is the kind that knows it. If you understand that words make worlds, you can hear a slogan as a piece of construction rather than a piece of reality. You can notice the door swinging and put your foot in it. The renamed thing keeps its old name in your mind. The repeated phrase does not harden into fact, because you can see the hands that are doing the repeating.

Honest language keeps the world open, which is tiring, and that is the cost of it. The closed phrase is a rest you cannot afford. Keep the sentence open. The open sentence is where you are still allowed to think.
