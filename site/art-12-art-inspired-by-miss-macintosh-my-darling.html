Art When Here Is Enough

Art Inspired by Miss MacIntosh, My Darling

← Appendix: Daily Studio Affirmations for the Radical Immanent · Art When Here Is Enough

Coral Russell began making art after she retired, when her daughter sat down beside her and taught her how. The lessons were practical, pastel and pencil and acrylic, but the subject kept finding its way back to the same book. These pieces come from years spent inside Marguerite Young’s novel, where a bald woman hides under a wig, an opium dreamer lies in her room, and every object seems ready to turn into something else.

Where a title carries a line from the novel, the page and line number come first and the words from the book sit inside quotation marks. Select any picture to open it full size.

1. 2-32 “all was imagination in this life”

Pastel. A red and orange flame sits inside a brown husk, held in rings of green that open onto a purple dark.

2. 14-45 Catherine and the Black Coachman

Acrylic. A blue cone and a black cone ride a smeared gray carriage of strokes while a white point drifts ahead of them across a field of fire.

3. 18-3 “the knowledge that the external world”

Pastel and charcoal. A gray figure lies with the head tipped back and the mouth open, alone on a ground of rose and red.

4. 21-2 “the inarticulate mediocrity which covers over such dead and vanished glory”

Acrylic. Stripes and a single star with all the color drained out, a flag left in the sun until only its shape remains.

5. 22-54 “Ah, this poor fiction of life”

Charcoal and pastel. A bald head seen from behind, facing a wall of rust-colored marks that look almost like handwriting.

6. 29-56 “butterflies with Latin sayings on their wings”

Colored pencil and graphite. A gray butterfly with watching eyespots and lettered wings, set against rays of red, violet and blue.

7. 46-18 “before the great fiction of death”

Pastel. A red and yellow sun burns at the left edge of a deep blue field, throwing green rays into the dark.

8. 48-11 “the mother of creation was this great abyss”

Pastel in a sketchbook. Inside a green peak, a blue teardrop holds a pink face with a wide dark mouth, under a sky of coral and gold.

9. 50-8 “a handkerchief dropped from the hand of god”

Colored pencil and pastel. A red cone hangs from a line while a crumpled gray handkerchief lands below, falling through pale aqua light.

10. 51-28 “three minutes of happiness”

Graphite and colored pencil. A pocket watch marked Tempus Fugit, cut through by a band of red, yellow and blue.

11. 54-31 “the worm is the all-devouring mother”

Pastel. A pink worm curls around a black opening on a violet ground, half guarding it, half about to go in.

12. 63-71 “I suffer especially when there are rainbows”

Graphite, pastel and colored pencil. A bald gray figure seen from behind, facing a wide blue sky with a rainbow bending around the shoulders.

13. 82-84 “mediocrity has also its power”

Acrylic. A textured white mass is swept along by gray and black wind, with small marks of red caught inside it.

14. “ancient lace”

Graphite. Dense marks, spots and hatching pile up like worn lace or spotted fur, with dark shapes moving underneath.

15. Catherine Cartwheel

Pastel and charcoal. A gray figure lies wrapped on an orange bed under a heavy dark ceiling, eyes not quite closed.

16. 12 “we were the masterpieces of nature”

Acrylic. A pale bald head rises at the bottom edge of the canvas, turned toward a sunset that opens into stars.

17. “Forget, forget, forget”

Acrylic. Pink and orange meet along a rough seam of silver, plum and black, each side holding one small round mark.

18. “life is fragile”

Sculpture and acrylic. A white globe with a blue lake and a single green tree, set before a painted sky that runs from orange to stars.

19. “lunar hysteria”

Graphite. A full moon worked in close detail, rays and craters and dark seas laid down in pencil.

20. Madge Capehorn

Graphite and colored pencil. A quiet face under a hat that is also a large yellow bird, which looks out of the frame while the face looks back at the viewer.

21. Moses Hunnecker

Charcoal and pastel. A great egg of violet and gray darkness holds one small, worn, bearded face near its top edge, peering out from inside.

22. Mr. Res Tacamah

Pastel. A violet bottle stands alone on a pale, streaked surface, its light caught in a single soft window of glass.

23. Mr. Spitzer

Pastel and charcoal. A man in a black top hat and long coat stands with a blue face and blue hands, holding out a small green sphere, while a red shadow follows him through a swirl of orange and violet.

24. 81 “four snails crawling across the open page”

Acrylic. Four snails with red, violet, pink and orange shells travel in a line along a dark ledge, under water lit from above.

25. “the dream was always the lie”

Acrylic. A single black bar crosses a murky field of violet, olive and blue, with a red form pressing in from the right edge.

26. The Egyptian Sailor

Pastel. A figure in a gold kilt stands in profile like a tomb painting, held inside a great sweep of violet and blue, with red marks rising beside the arm.

27. The Great Frog Prince

Pastel. A crowned green frog sits on a golden fruit, legs crossed, eyes closed, perfectly pleased with the throne he found.

28. “Time marches on. There is no time.”

Acrylic. White shapes scatter upward like birds or torn paper over a band of pale light, a green field and black strokes, all set inside a frame of gray.

29. Vera Cartwheel

Pastel and graphite. A woman with dark curls smiles out from a green sweater against a loose blue sky.
