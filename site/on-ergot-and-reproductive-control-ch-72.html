On Ergot and Reproductive Control

Ergot, the fungus mentioned in To All My Darlings, had a complicated history in American reproductive medicine. In the 1930s, companies marketed ergot-derived products to women whose pregnancies were “not progressing,” as abortion, contraception, and birth control devices were still illegal under the Comstock Laws. The FDA’s predecessor deemed these pills unsafe and ineffective and halted their sale. Midwives had used ergot to ease delivery since the 18th century. This background is relevant to Esther: she exists in an era when women had almost no legal control over their own reproductive lives. Her body is subject to forces (cosmic, as O’Leary says, but also legal and social) that she has not consented to and cannot entirely resist.
