On Nellie Melba and the musicians.

Dame Nellie Melba was the Australian soprano who became the most famous opera singer of the late Victorian and Edwardian eras; she gave her name to Melba toast and peach Melba. Jenny Lind, the Swedish Nightingale, was P.T. Barnum's great American touring success in the 1850s: he promoted her US debut so effectively that the phenomenon of managed celebrity was partly invented around her. Ole Bull was the Norwegian virtuoso violinist who toured the United States and became a romantic figure of the sort that the nineteenth century adored: the artist-hero, the man of genius. Young places them in Mr. Spitzer's musical imagination as figures of the immortality he is trying to achieve: the artists who became names, whose music survived them. He is trying to be this. He has lost the house that was supposed to support the attempt. The work continues in a beachcomber's cottage.
