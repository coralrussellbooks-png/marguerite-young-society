Pin these above your mirror, your script, or the door to the room. Read them out loud when the noise of the market starts to flatten what you feel.

My contradictions are my texture. I am not a brand or a type. I am a living, plural maker.

I will not trade a true, frightening complexity for a simpler character just to please a casting room, a feed, or a critic.

I will stay in the moment when it gets uncomfortable. I will witness it. I will not rush to resolve or perform over what is honestly broken.

The work is unfinished. The mask does not fully cover the face. That is not a failure. That is my lifelong work.

There is no final finished performance. The market floor is gone, and I am standing at the crossing point.

Play anyway.

LIVING IN THE HERE

A Closing Section: Radical Immanence for Anyone

You do not have to be an artist to read this part. You do not have to make anything anyone would call art. You do not have to be good at a single one of the six crafts in this series. This closing is for the person who picked up a manual out of curiosity and thought, that is not me, I do not paint or dance or write, I just live my life. Good. Your life is where all of this was headed anyway.

The whole series has been saying one thing in six different rooms: the world does not wrap up neatly, and that is not a defect to fix. It is the truth of being alive. The manuals took that truth into the studio and asked what it does to cloth, to sound, to paint, to mass, to movement, to language, to a role. But none of that was ever really about being a professional. It was about a way of paying attention and being honest, and that is available to anyone, in any life, with no talent required and nothing to buy.

Because here is the thing the market would rather you not notice. You are already making something. You are making a life. You are shaping your days, your rooms, your relationships, the way you move through a grocery store, the way you sit with someone who is hurting, the way you carry what you have lost. That is a medium as real as clay. And you have been told the same lies about it that the artists were told about theirs. Find your one true self. Heal first, then live. Build a clean, finished, successful life story that photographs well and can be approved by everyone watching. Resolve everything. Arrive.

Radical Immanence says none of that is true, and the pressure to pretend it is true is a quiet kind of violence against how your actual life feels. So this closing is a short invitation to live the eleven ideas, not as an artist, just as yourself.

You are not one self. You are a crowd. The you at work and the you at three in the morning and the child you were and the person you are with your oldest friend are all real, all at once. You do not have to pick one and call it the true you. You get to be all of them. When you feel like you contradict yourself, that is not a problem to fix. That is just you, being honest about being a person.

The way things are is not the way things have to be. The schedule, the roles, the whole shape of a normal life were built by people, for reasons, and often for someone else's benefit. You are allowed to notice that. You are allowed to ask who a rule is for before you obey it.

Grief does not end. It does not move through tidy stages and finish. It piles up, and you learn to carry it. You are not failing because you still miss someone years later. You are being accurate. You do not have to get over it. You are allowed to carry it and keep living at the same time.

You can stay in the room with what hurts. When you cannot fix a thing, for yourself or for someone you love, you can still stay present with it and not look away. That is not nothing. That is one of the hardest and kindest things a person can do. To witness, and not rush to a tidy answer, is a whole practice, and you can do it at a kitchen table.

You build anyway. You make plans, relationships, homes, knowing they are temporary and will change and some will end. That is not a reason to hold back. It is the only honest way to love anything. Build it knowing it will need to be built again.

Unfinished is honest. Your life is not a story with an ending you are behind schedule on. It is unfinished because you are still alive, and that is exactly right. You are allowed to be in the middle of things. You are allowed to not have arrived.

The people at the edges show you the truth. Whoever does not fit the neat categories, including you when you do not fit, is not a mistake in the system. They are where you can see how the system was built and who it was built for. Pay attention to the edges. That is where the real picture is.

The past is under your feet. What came before you, in your family, your history, the ground you stand on, did not go away. It presses up into your present, into how you move and what you fear and what you were handed. You are allowed to know what is underneath you. It does not have to run you, but pretending it is not there is how it keeps running you.

Words shape what feels possible. The way you talk about your own life, the labels you were given and the ones you gave yourself, quietly decide what feels allowed. You can notice the language. You can refuse a word that was put on you. You can describe your own life in your own terms.

The sacred is right here. You do not need a somewhere else, a beyond, an afterward, to find meaning. It is in the ordinary thing you actually look at: the light in the afternoon, the face across the table, the plain task done with full attention. Nothing has to be more than what it is. This is already it.

And the last one holds all the others. You are the crossing point. You are the exact place where everything that came before you meets everything that will come after. Everything you were handed, your language, your losses, your people, the whole long past, runs up through you right now, and what you do with your one ordinary day becomes part of the ground the next person stands on. You did not choose this. It is just what a person is. You are small and specific and located, here, now, in this body, on this day, and that is not a small thing at all. That is the whole thing.

So here are a few ways to practice, and not one of them requires any skill.

Let the crowd have the day. Notice which self is running you right now, and let a different one take a turn. Let the tired self rest without guilt. Let the child self do something with no point. Let the grieving self be slow. You do not have to be consistent. You are allowed to be many.

Witness one ordinary thing. Once today, stop and give your full attention to something plain, a cup of coffee, a person talking, the walk to your car, and want nothing from it. Do not photograph it. Do not improve it. Just be there with it. That is the practice the whole series was built on, and you can do it in a minute.

Leave something unresolved. Let a conversation end without a neat conclusion. Let a feeling be complicated. Let a day end unfinished. Notice the small discomfort of not wrapping it up, and let it be there. That discomfort is the exact texture of living honestly.

Carry the grief without fixing it. When a loss comes up, do not rush to feel better or make it mean something tidy. Just carry it for a while, the way you would carry anything heavy and precious. You are allowed.

Find the crossing point in a plain moment. Once, in an ordinary moment, feel the whole thing at once: everything that made you, running up through you now, and the small choice in front of you that the future will stand on. Making dinner. Answering a child. Being kind when it is hard. That is not a small moment. That is you, at the crossing point, making the world that comes next.

None of this asks you to become something you are not. It asks you to stop pretending your real, plural, grieving, hopeful, unfinished life is a rough draft of a cleaner one that is coming. It is not a rough draft. It is the work. It was always the work.

You do not have to make art. You are already making a life, and that is the most Radical Immanent thing there is. Your contradictions are your texture. You are not a brand. You are a living, plural, unfinished person, standing exactly at the crossing point, with everything passing through you.

Live it anyway. That is the whole invitation. That was always the whole invitation.
