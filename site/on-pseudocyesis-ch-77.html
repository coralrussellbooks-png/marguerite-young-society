On Pseudocyesis

Pseudocyesis, false pregnancy, is a real medical condition in which a person develops clinical signs of pregnancy, including abdominal enlargement, breast changes with secretions, delayed menstrual periods, and the felt sensation of fetal movement, without carrying a fetus. It has both physiological and psychiatric components and can be caused by physical trauma, hormonal imbalance, or intense psychological desire for or fear of pregnancy. The abortionist in the newspaper clipping kills a woman whose pregnancy is purely psychosomatic, and the impossibility of the murder, there is no baby to conceal, exposes her method. To All My Darlings notes that Esther’s own condition may be consistent with pseudocyesis, though Young never resolves this. The felt movement “of the little shadow boxer, the” two hearts, the swollen belly: all could be symptoms of a body that has been pregnant so many times and lost so much that it has learned to produce the experience of pregnancy independent of its biological preconditions. Young does not choose. The uncertainty is the truth.
