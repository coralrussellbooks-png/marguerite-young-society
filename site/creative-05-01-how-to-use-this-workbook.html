How to Use This Workbook

Part of The Creative Radical Collection, on Radical Immanence, the philosophy Coral Russell draws from the work of Marguerite Young (1908 to 1995).
By Coral Russell · Updated October 29, 2026

← The Longing Under Each Pillar: A Workbook · The Creative Radical Collection · Pillar 1 Not One Self →

Each of the eleven essays names a hunger that power feeds on, and a pillar of Radical Immanence that answers it. Reading about the shift is one thing. Feeling it happen in your own body and your own week is another. This workbook is for the second kind of knowing.

The chapters describe an idea. The exercises here ask you to catch the hunger while it is active, usually in something small, and to practice the alternative until it stops being a concept and starts being a habit.

A few ways to work through it

  • One pillar a day, or one a week. Eleven pillars, eleven sessions. There is no prize for finishing fast, and the ethics of incompletion is one of the pillars, so take that as permission.

  • Read the short recap at the top of each section, then move straight into the exercises. You already read the full essay. This is the part where you test it against your actual life.

  • Each exercise has two parts. A Notice exercise, which asks you to catch the hunger in daily life before you try to answer it. And a Practice exercise, which is something to actually do, not just think about.

  • Write by hand if you can. The lines are there for that. If a question does not fit your week, skip it and come back later.

  • Nothing here needs to be shown to anyone. The only person this workbook is accountable to is you, on the page.

One last note before you start. Power's whole strategy in each pillar is to offer relief from a real ache. The exercises will not make the ache disappear. They will just show you that there is a way to hold it that does not require handing yourself over to someone selling a cure.
