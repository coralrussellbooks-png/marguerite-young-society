On Dido's Carthage.

Virgil's Aeneid gives us Dido as the founder of Carthage who loved Aeneas and died when he left. But Dido's story predates Virgil; she appears in Phoenician and Roman tradition as a historical figure, a queen who led her people from Tyre to North Africa and built a city through intelligence and will. The Rome-centric version of the story makes her death the collateral damage of Aeneas's divine mission. The Phoenician tradition gives her more dignity: a woman who built something magnificent and was destroyed not by romantic weakness but by the demands made on women who create things in a world that does not protect them. Young's Cousin Hannah is Dido in this fuller sense: the builder, the founder, the woman whose Carthage outlasted her love and whose love outlasted her.
